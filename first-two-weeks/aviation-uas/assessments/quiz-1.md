## Multiple Choice

**1.** A small unmanned aircraft system, or sUAS, is defined as one weighing:

A. Less than 5 pounds
B. 55 pounds or less at takeoff, including everything on board
C. Less than 25 pounds, aircraft only
D. Any weight, as long as it is unmanned

**2.** Who is the final authority for the operation of an sUAS?

A. The visual observer
B. The FAA representative on site
C. The Remote Pilot in Command
D. Whoever owns the aircraft

**3.** Class G airspace is:

A. Controlled airspace requiring authorization
B. Uncontrolled airspace, meaning nobody is separating traffic
C. Restricted airspace
D. Airspace above 18,000 feet

**4.** Under Part 107, the minimum flight visibility is:

A. 1 statute mile
B. 3 statute miles
C. 5 statute miles
D. There is no visibility minimum

**5.** Under Part 107, you must remain at least:

A. 500 feet below and 2,000 feet horizontally from clouds
B. 1,000 feet below and 1,000 feet horizontally from clouds
C. Clear of clouds by any distance
D. 2,000 feet below and 500 feet horizontally from clouds

**6.** Which of the four forces is produced by the motors?

A. Lift
B. Weight
C. Thrust
D. Drag

**7.** You add a heavier battery to gain flight time. What else changes?

A. Nothing, a bigger battery is strictly better
B. Weight increases, so the motors work harder and some of the gain is lost
C. Drag decreases
D. The center of gravity is unaffected

**8.** A METAR is:

A. A forecast for the next 24 hours
B. A coded weather report from an airport, updated hourly
C. A notice about a temporary flight restriction
D. A chart showing airspace boundaries

**9.** During preflight you find a small nick on a propeller. The correct documentation is:

A. "Looks fine"
B. "Prop damage"
C. "Left front prop, small nick on the trailing edge, about 2 mm"
D. No entry is needed for damage that small

**10.** Why does an experienced pilot read a checklist out loud on their thousandth flight?

A. It is required by the manufacturer
B. Checklists are memory aids for beginners
C. Checklists exist to stop experienced people from skipping steps because they are confident
D. It saves time

## Short Answer

**11.** What airspace class is our school in, what is the ceiling, and how would you find that out for a
location you have never flown?

**12.** A METAR reports wind at 14 knots gusting to 22, visibility 10 statute miles, sky clear. The flight is
legal under Part 107. Would you fly it? Give one sentence of reasoning either way.

---

## Answer Key

1: B, 2: C, 3: B, 4: B, 5: A, 6: C, 7: B, 8: B, 9: C, 10: C

**11.** Class and ceiling per what you found on the FAA UAS Facility Map for the school. Full credit needs the
method: check the sectional or the UAS Facility Map, and request authorization through LAANC if it is
controlled airspace.

**12.** There is no single right answer, and say so when you review it. Full credit needs the recognition that
legal and smart are different questions, plus a specific reason. Gusts at 22 knots against the aircraft
limitation, pilot experience, and what is underneath the flight area are all valid reasoning. "It is legal so
I fly" with nothing else earns half credit.
