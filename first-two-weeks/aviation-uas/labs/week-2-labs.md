Five labs, each written to paste straight into Classroom. Labs category, on the Lab rubric.

## Lab 1: Four Forces

**Steps**

1. Label a multirotor diagram with lift, weight, thrust, and drag.
2. Label a fixed wing diagram with the same four.
3. Answer three questions in complete sentences.
   - What happens when thrust exceeds weight?
   - What does a headwind do to a multirotor trying to hold position?
   - Why is a bigger battery not free performance?

**Turn in:** both diagrams and the three answers.

## Lab 2: Airspace Reading

**You will need:** the printed sectional excerpt, the legend, and a highlighter.

**Steps**

1. Identify the airspace class at the five marked points.
2. For each point, say whether a Part 107 pilot could fly there without authorization, and what altitude limit
   applies.
3. Find the nearest airport to the school. Name it, its class, and its distance.
4. Answer the scenario: a farmer three miles from a Class D airport wants field imagery. What does the pilot
   check before saying yes?

Paper chart first. Look it up in the app afterward to confirm. Students who only learn the app cannot answer
the test questions.

**Turn in:** the marked up chart and your written answers.

## Lab 3: Go / No Go

**What this is about.** The legal call and the smart call are two different questions, and a professional
answers both before every flight.

**The limits, from Part 107**

- At least 3 statute miles of visibility from the control station.
- At least 500 feet below the clouds and 2,000 feet horizontally from them.
- Daylight, or civil twilight with anti collision lighting.

**Steps**

1. Decode five real METARs, field by field. Wind, visibility, sky condition, temperature, dew point, altimeter.
2. For each one, state whether the flight is legal under Part 107 and give one sentence of reasoning.
3. Then the judgment case: a report that is legal but unwise, gusting wind under the limits. What does a
   professional do, and what would you do?
4. Pull today's actual METAR from the nearest reporting station and make a real call for right now.

**Turn in:** five decoded reports with calls, your answer to the judgment case, and today's real call.

**How it is graded:** 20 points on the Lab rubric. The judgment case is worth as much as any two of the
decodes. "It is legal so I fly" is not an answer.

## Lab 4: Preflight Inspection

**Setup.** Aircraft on the tables, unpowered, props removed or clearly secured. Nothing gets powered on today.

**Steps.** In pairs, one reads the checklist out loud and one performs the inspection, then swap.

1. Work the full checklist: airframe, arms, motors, props, battery, gimbal and camera, controller, firmware,
   environment.
2. Document every item. No blanks.
3. Some aircraft have a deliberate discrepancy in them. Finding it is part of the lab.
4. Report anything found the way you would report it for real.

"Looks fine" is not documentation. "Left front prop, small nick on the trailing edge, about 2 mm" is.

**Turn in:** the completed inspection log, both roles noted.

## Lab 5: Weight and Balance

**What this is about.** Every gram you add is a gram the motors have to lift, and the aircraft does not care
that your payload was a good idea.

**Steps**

1. Weigh the aircraft with no battery and no payload. Record it.
2. Weigh the battery. Weigh a payload, a camera or a small sensor or a taped on weight.
3. Calculate the takeoff weight of each configuration:
   - Airframe plus battery
   - Airframe plus battery plus payload
4. Look up the manufacturer maximum takeoff weight. How much margin is left in each configuration?
5. Answer three questions.
   - If the aircraft flies 22 minutes at its bare weight, what would you estimate with the payload on, and why?
   - Where is the center of gravity, and what happens if the payload is mounted off center?
   - You are 40 grams over the maximum takeoff weight. Name two things you could do about it and say which one
     you would choose.

**Turn in:** the weight table, the margin calculations, and the three answers.

**How it is graded:** 20 points on the Lab rubric. Show the arithmetic. A number with no work behind it is a
guess, and in flight planning a guess is a hazard.
