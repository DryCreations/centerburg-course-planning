## What This Course Is

This is an aviation course that happens to use small aircraft. You will learn how the airspace system works,
how weather affects flight, how aircraft fly, what the federal regulations require of a remote pilot, and how
to plan and run a safe flight. You will also see what drones actually get used for: mapping, inspection,
agriculture, public safety, and film work.

No experience needed.

## What You Will Walk Out With

- The knowledge base for the FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot certificate
- The ability to read a sectional chart and tell what airspace you are in
- Weather reading: METARs, TAFs, and what actually grounds a flight
- Preflight planning, checklists, and emergency procedures
- Hands on mapping and imaging work
- A competition entry through BPA or our local competition

### Late Work

I do not plan to take points off for being late.

If an assignment will not accept late work, the assignment itself says so. That is the only place that rule
lives, so read the assignment first.

Here is the part that matters. I close submissions on an assignment once I finish grading what came in. So if
you want to turn something in late, check that the assignment does not say no late submissions, then reach out
and ask me to reopen it. I will.

What I need from you is the asking. An assignment that sits closed and never gets mentioned stays a zero, and
that is the only way late work hurts you in this class.

Quizzes, tests, and flight days are the assignments most likely to be marked no late submissions. Flight days
depend on weather, airspace, and a crew that shows up, so they cannot be recreated on request.

## Safety

Aviation runs on procedure. That is not paperwork, it is how people avoid getting hurt.

1. Nobody touches an aircraft until we have covered the procedure for it, and then only when I say so.
2. Checklists get used as written, every time, including the times it feels unnecessary.
3. The person flying is responsible for everyone in the area, not just the aircraft.
4. Anyone in this class can call a stop to an operation for a safety concern. Nobody gets questioned for it in
   the moment.
5. Report damage, malfunctions, and near misses right away. Reporting is expected.

You will sign a flight safety agreement before the first time we fly. In the first weeks all of our aircraft
work is on the ground.

## Classroom Expectations

4. Precision counts. In this field close enough is how incidents happen.
5. Ask questions. Pilots ask questions constantly.

## AI Use

Learning is about the process. AI can help you, or it can let you skip the part that was the point.

Fine: having a concept explained a different way, generating practice questions, checking your understanding
of a regulation after you have read it.

Not allowed: AI produced flight plans, chart readings, weather analysis, or written reflections turned in as
your work. The FAA test is you, alone, with no help. Every shortcut now is a gap then.

If you are not sure, ask me.
