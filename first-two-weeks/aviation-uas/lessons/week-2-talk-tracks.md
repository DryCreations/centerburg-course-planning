# Aviation UAS: Week 2 Talk Tracks

Teacher facing. Week 1 ran the same Express assignment as the design courses, so this is where the aviation
course starts sounding like an aviation course. Keep each one under ten minutes.

---

## Monday: What Actually Holds It Up

- Take the bell ringer guesses seriously before correcting them. Half the room will say the propellers push
  air down, which is close enough to build on.
- Four forces, with an aircraft in your hands and unpowered. Where each one acts. What changes between a hover
  and forward flight.
- Connect it to something they already have a body memory of. A bike, a car, swimming, sticking a hand out a
  car window.
- The line that lands: a bigger battery is more flight time and more weight at the same time, so it is not
  free. Every payload decision in this course is that trade.
- Say where this goes. Aerodynamics is about 9 percent of the WebXam and it shows up all over the Part 107
  test.

## Tuesday: TRUST, and What Makes a Flight Legal and Safe

The safety-baseline talk. Everyone earns TRUST today and we start pointing at Friday.

- TRUST is the recreational safety test. It proves you know the rules. It is not a Part 107 certificate and it
  does not let you fly for work. We do it because it is the honest baseline for putting a real aircraft up.
- Say the core rules out loud with the aircraft in hand: under 400 feet, keep it in sight, not over people,
  give way to crewed aircraft, know your airspace first. Tie each back to Monday's airspace lookup.
- Sell the week's finish line: preflight and controls Wednesday, pretest Thursday, we fly Friday. Nobody flies
  who skips Wednesday's preflight drill. That is a rule, not a threat.
- The deep sectional chart-reading work moves to Unit 1.1. Today it is enough to answer "could we legally fly
  at the school Friday?"

## Wednesday: What We Actually Do to Get It Up and Back Down

Preflight, controls, and Quiz 1 (moved here from Thursday).

- Quiz first, 15 minutes, Part 107 question style. Tell them the format is part of the training.
- Checklists are not memory aids for beginners. They are defenses against experienced people skipping steps
  because they are confident. Walk one full preflight out loud, aircraft in hand: airframe, arms, motors,
  props, battery, gimbal, controller, firmware, environment.
- Model the documentation. "Inspected, no defects" versus "left front prop, small nick, about 2 mm." One is a
  record, the other is a shrug.
- Plant a discrepancy in about half the aircraft. The pairs who report all clear on a bad unit learn the most
  useful thing in these two weeks. Handle it without embarrassment.
- Then the controls, controller in hand, tied to the four forces and three axes: throttle for altitude, yaw to
  rotate, pitch and roll to move. Rehearse the launch/hover/land sequence dry. Getting up and back down on the
  same square is the whole skill Friday.
- Nothing powers on today. The procedure is complete only when the flight training unit is, and that is Friday.

---

## Thursday: The Pretest, Then the Plan

- Pretest is not graded and most of it has not been taught. Low is expected and it is the point. The strand
  data drives what gets emphasized all year, so record it the same day.
- End the period with the flight briefing, everyone off the machines: the location and boundaries, the
  per-pilot sequence, the weather-abort plan, and the ground roles. Walk it so Friday has no surprises.
- The Part 107 certificate is real, it costs them nothing, and it is a thing a seventeen year old can hold that
  most adults do not have. Preview where the year goes: industry and careers, aerodynamics, regulations,
  weather, and more flight.

---

## Friday: The First Flight

- Go/no-go first, out loud, from real weather. If it is marginal, scrubbing is the right call and a real
  lesson. A safe flight is the goal, not a long one.
- One rule above all others: anyone may call an all-stop for any reason and the pilot lands immediately, no
  argument.
- Short flights, rotate pilot, observer, and logger so everyone flies and everyone watches. A clean
  thirty-second flight beats a long shaky one.
- No TRUST proof, no sticks: those students run observer and logger today and fly once the proof is in.
- Debrief every scrub or anomaly the same day. The first time a real thing goes sideways is the best lesson of
  the unit.

---

## Things to Watch For This Week

- Students who learn the app and skip the paper chart. They will pass the flight and fail the exam.
- Anyone reading the preflight checklist by skimming instead of out loud. That habit predicts flight day
  readiness better than any quiz score.
- Vague documentation. Push for a measurement or a location every time.
- If the pretest login is not ready Thursday, run the Weight and Balance lab instead and schedule the pretest
  into Unit 1.1 rather than letting it drift. Do not trade Friday's flight for it.
- Confirm every student's TRUST proof is on file before Friday. Chase stragglers Tuesday and Wednesday, not
  flight morning.
