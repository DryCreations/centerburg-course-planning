# Aviation UAS: Week 2 Talk Tracks

Teacher facing. Week 1 ran the same Express assignment as the design courses, so this is where the aviation
course starts sounding like an aviation course. Keep each one under ten minutes.

---

## Monday: What Actually Holds It Up

- Take the bell ringer guesses seriously before correcting them. Half the room will say the propellers push
  air down, which is close enough to build on.
- Four forces, with an aircraft in your hands and unpowered. Where each one acts. What changes between a hover
  and forward flight.
- Connect it to something they already have a body memory of. A bike, a car, swimming, sticking a hand out a
  car window.
- The line that lands: a bigger battery is more flight time and more weight at the same time, so it is not
  free. Every payload decision in this course is that trade.
- Say where this goes. Aerodynamics is about 9 percent of the WebXam and it shows up all over the Part 107
  test.

---

## Tuesday: Somebody Is Responsible for Separating Traffic, or You Are

The airspace talk. This is the hardest visual skill in the course, so start with the idea and get to the chart
second.

- Controlled means somebody is responsible for keeping aircraft apart. Uncontrolled means nobody is, which
  means you are.
- Classes B, C, D, E, and G in one sentence each. Do not do the whole upside down wedding cake diagram on day
  one, it loses people.
- Then the chart. Magenta and blue, airport symbols, the numbers in the boxes.
- Look up our own school together on the FAA UAS Facility Map. Find the actual ceiling. Say out loud what a
  pilot would have to do to fly here legally.
- Mention LAANC and that authorization in most controlled airspace is close to instant now. The rule is not
  "you cannot fly there," it is "you have to ask."

Pair deliberately for the lab. The survey flagged who is nervous about technical material, and chart reading
is where that shows up first.

---

## Wednesday: Legal and Smart Are Two Different Questions

The go / no go talk, and the first real judgment lesson of the year.

- Part 107 weather minimums, plainly: 3 statute miles visibility, 500 below and 2,000 horizontal from clouds.
- METAR decoding field by field, using today's actual report from the nearest station. Real data, not a
  textbook example.
- Read two more as a class, one obviously flyable and one obviously not.
- Then the one that matters: gusting wind that is under the limit. It is legal. Is it smart? What does a
  professional do?
- The point to land: the regulation is the floor, not the answer. Every accident report you will ever read
  involves somebody who was technically allowed to be there.

---

## Thursday: Why a Pilot Reads a Checklist on Their Thousandth Flight

- Checklists are not memory aids for beginners. They are defenses against experienced people skipping steps
  because they are confident.
- Walk one full preflight in front of them, out loud, with the aircraft in hand. Airframe, arms, motors,
  props, battery, gimbal, controller, firmware, environment.
- Model the documentation. "Inspected, no defects" versus "left front prop, small nick on the trailing edge,
  about 2 mm." One of those is a record and the other is a shrug.
- Plant a discrepancy in about half the aircraft. The pairs who report all clear on a bad unit learn the most
  useful thing anybody learns in these two weeks. Handle it without embarrassment.
- Nothing gets powered on today, and say why: the procedure is not complete until the flight training unit is.

---

## Friday: Where the Year Goes

- Quiz is written in Part 107 question style on purpose. Tell them that. The format is part of the training.
- Preview what is coming: aviation industry and careers, then aerodynamics properly, then regulations,
  meteorology, and eventually flight.
- The Part 107 certificate is real, it costs them nothing, and it is a thing a seventeen year old can hold
  that most adults do not have.
- Collect the "what do you want more practice on before we fly" answers. That list is your flight readiness
  checklist and it is more useful than anything you would have written yourself.

---

## Things to Watch For This Week

- Students who learn the app and skip the paper chart. They will pass the flight and fail the exam.
- Anyone reading the preflight checklist by skimming instead of out loud. That habit predicts flight day
  readiness better than any quiz score.
- Vague documentation. Push for a measurement or a location every time.
- If the pretest still has not run by Friday, schedule it into Unit 1.1 rather than letting it drift. The
  strand data drives what gets emphasized all year.
