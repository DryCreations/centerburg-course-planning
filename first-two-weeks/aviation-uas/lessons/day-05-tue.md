## Objective

Every student completes and can show proof of **TRUST** (The Recreational UAS Safety Test), and can state in
plain terms what makes a flight legal and safe: where you may fly, what a preflight is for, and who is
responsible on the sticks.

## Materials & Setup

TRUST portal open on the lab machines (FAA-approved test administrator), a place for students to save or
screenshot their completion certificate, the FAA UAS Facility Map on the projector from Monday, the aircraft
in hand and unpowered for the safety walk-through.

## Bell Ringer (5 min)

*You are about to fly on Friday. Name one thing that could make the answer "not today," even if the drone works
perfectly.* (Weather, airspace, a person in the way, a low battery. The point is that "the drone works" is not
the whole question.)

## Direct Instruction (15 min)

1. **What TRUST is and is not.** It is the recreational safety test: it proves you know the safety rules. It is
   not a Part 107 certificate and it does not let you fly for work. We do it because it is the honest baseline
   for putting a real aircraft in the air on Friday.
2. **The safety rules TRUST covers,** said out loud with the aircraft in hand: stay under 400 feet, keep it in
   sight, do not fly over people, give way to crewed aircraft, and know your airspace before you go. Tie each
   one back to Monday's airspace lookup.
3. **The order of the week.** Preflight and controls Wednesday, pretest Thursday, flight Friday. Nobody flies
   Friday who has not done the preflight drill Wednesday. Make that a rule, not a threat.

## Work Time (25 min)
