# Day 05 - Tue, Aug 25 (Week 2)

**Course:** Aviation UAS, **Unit:** 1.0 Orientation

## Objective

Students can identify controlled and uncontrolled airspace on a sectional chart and determine whether
authorization would be required to fly at a given location.

## Standards / Outcomes

- 7.5: airport environments
- 7.13: flight environment

## Materials & Setup

Printed sectional excerpt covering the local area (one per pair), sectional legend, B4UFLY or the FAA UAS
Facility Map on the projector, highlighters.

## Bell Ringer (5 min)

*Can you legally fly a drone right outside this building? What would you need to know to answer that?*

## Direct Instruction (20 min)

1. Airspace in plain terms first: **controlled means someone is responsible for separating traffic; uncontrolled
   means you are.** Classes B, C, D, E, G, what each is in one sentence.
2. Sectional chart basics: the magenta and blue lines, airport symbols, the numbers in the grid boxes.
3. Look up **our school** together on the FAA UAS Facility Map. Find the ceiling. Say out loud what a pilot
   would actually have to do here.

## Work Time (20 min): Lab: Airspace Reading

In pairs, using the printed sectional:

1. Identify the airspace class at five marked points.
2. For each: could a Part 107 pilot fly there without authorization? What altitude limit applies?
3. Find the nearest airport to the school and state its class and its distance.
4. One written scenario: a farmer three miles from a Class D airport wants field imagery, what does the pilot
   need to check before saying yes?

## Closure / Exit Ticket (5 min)

**Exit ticket:** *What airspace is our school in, and what is the ceiling?*
