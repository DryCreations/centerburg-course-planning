# Day 02 - Thu, Aug 20 (Week 1)

**Course:** Aviation UAS, **Unit:** 1.0 Orientation

## Objective

Students know the safety rules that govern aircraft and batteries in this room, and can take a documentation
photo that is sharp, lit, and readable.

## Materials

One aircraft and one battery staged for the safety demonstration, a labeled component for the documentation
shots, cameras and card readers out, checkout form live, printed syllabus.

## Bell Ringer

A pilot notices a small crack in a propeller five minutes before a flight that people are waiting on. What
should happen, and what would be tempting to do instead?

## Policies, Keep It Short

Ten minutes. The full procedures handout comes tomorrow.

- Grading categories and the 50% floor
- Late work, and that flight days cannot be made up
- No phones, no headphones
- AI: the FAA test is you alone with no help, so shortcuts now are gaps then

## Safety

This is the real lesson today.

1. Take the bell ringer seriously. Professional aviation exists because people chose the honest answer over
   the convenient one.
2. Battery handling, storage, and what damage looks like. Show a battery. Show swelling if you have an example.
3. Checklists get read out loud, every time.
4. Anyone may call a stop. Have the room say the word out loud once, so it is normal before there is pressure.
5. Report damage and near misses right away.

## Camera Intro and Documentation Photos

Why this belongs in an aviation course: preflight records, damage reports, and mapping are all photographs. A
soft or dark photo is a report that proves nothing.

1. Handling: strap, two hands.
2. Focus: half press, confirm, shoot.
3. Documentation standard: sharp, lit, filled frame, nothing staged.
4. Show a readable label shot next to an unreadable one.

Then rotation. Camera turns work through the six shot list. Everyone else works on the About Me Square.

Nothing gets powered on and nothing gets picked up without asking.

## Closure

Cameras in and offloaded before the exit ticket.

Exit ticket: name two things that must happen before an aircraft in this room gets powered on.
