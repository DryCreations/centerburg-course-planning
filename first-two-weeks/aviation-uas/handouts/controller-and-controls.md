# Aviation UAS: The Controller and the Controls

**What this is.** A teacher reference and a student handout for the day we introduce the aircraft controller,
before anyone flies. Our aircraft is the **DJI Mavic 3 Classic**, flown with the **DJI RC** style controller.
It has no built-in simulator, so today is hands-on-the-controller on the ground: names, buttons, and what each
stick does. Nothing takes off today.

Pull up a labeled photo of the actual controller on the projector while you walk this. Hold the real one.

---

## Part 1: The Words We Will Use All Year

Say each one, then show it with the aircraft in hand (unpowered) or with your own body.

| Word | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Throttle** | Up and down. Altitude. More throttle climbs, less descends. |
| **Yaw** | Rotating in place, the nose swinging left or right, like turning your head. |
| **Pitch** | Nose tilts forward or back, which moves the aircraft forward or backward. |
| **Roll** | Tilts left or right, which slides the aircraft left or right without turning. |
| **Hover** | Holding still in the air, in one spot, at one height. The first real skill. |
| **Home point** | The spot the aircraft remembers as "where I took off." It can return there on its own. |
| **RTH** | Return to Home. The aircraft climbs to a set height and flies back to the home point. |
| **Line of sight** | You can see the actual aircraft with your own eyes, not just on the screen. Required. |
| **Visual observer (VO)** | The person whose only job is watching the aircraft and the sky for hazards. |
| **Lost link** | The controller and aircraft stop talking. The aircraft is set to hover or RTH when this happens. |

The three that matter most today are **throttle, yaw, and pitch/roll**. Everything a beginner does is some
combination of those.

---

## Part 2: The Sticks (Mode 2, our default)

Almost every drone ships in **Mode 2**, and that is what we fly. Two sticks, each does two things.

| Stick | Push it... | The aircraft... |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Left stick** | up / down | climbs / descends (**throttle**) |
| **Left stick** | left / right | rotates left / right in place (**yaw**) |
| **Right stick** | up / down | flies forward / backward (**pitch**) |
| **Right stick** | left / right | slides left / right (**roll**) |

Two things to say out loud and repeat:

- **Center is stop.** Let go of a stick and it springs back to center, and the aircraft holds position and
  hovers. The safest thing you can ever do is take your thumbs off the sticks.
- **Small inputs.** Beginners shove the sticks. The aircraft is smooth when you nudge and jerky when you jam.

## Part 3: A Tour of the Controller

Point at each one on the projected photo and the real unit. Not every controller has every button; skip what
ours does not have.

| Control | What it does |
|---------|--------------|
| **Power button** | Press, then press-and-hold to turn the controller on and off. |
| **Sticks** | The two above. On some units they unscrew and store inside the controller. |
| **Flight-mode switch** | **Cine / Normal / Sport.** We fly in **Cine** (slowest, gentlest) or **Normal**. Never Sport with a class. |
| **Return-to-Home (RTH) button** | Press and hold: the aircraft climbs and flies back to the home point. Our "bring it back now" button. |
| **Pause / hover button** | Stops the aircraft and makes it hover in place. The panic button. Know it before you fly. |
| **Gimbal dial** | Tilts the camera up and down. Camera only, it does not move the aircraft. |
| **Shutter / record button** | Takes a photo or starts/stops video. |
| **Zoom dial or lever** | Zooms the camera in and out. |
| **Customizable / Fn buttons** | Assigned to a function; we will set and confirm these before flight. |
| **Screen or phone clamp** | Where you see what the camera sees, the battery, the height, and the distance. |
| **Antennas** | Keep them pointed toward the aircraft. Do not grip them. |

## Part 4: The Screen Reads We Care About

Even on the ground, powered on, show them where to find these. In the air, these four numbers are the job.

- **Battery percent** (aircraft, not just controller). We land well before it gets low.
- **Height** above the takeoff point.
- **Distance** from the home point.
- **Number of satellites / GPS lock.** More is better; we wait for a solid lock before flying.

---

## Part 5: What Flying Will Look Like (preview for tomorrow)

Walk this so nobody is surprised on flight day.

1. **Preflight** the aircraft against the checklist. No preflight, no flight.
2. **Power on** controller first, then aircraft. Wait for GPS lock and the home point to set.
3. **Launch** to a low hover, about head height, and just hold it. Thumbs light.
4. **Small moves:** a little yaw, a little forward and back, then back to a hover.
5. **Land** on the same spot: ease the throttle down slowly, all the way, until it settles and the motors stop.
6. **Roles rotate:** pilot, visual observer, logger. Everyone watches before everyone flies.

The one rule above all others: **anyone may call an all-stop, for any reason, and the pilot lands or hovers
immediately, no argument.**

---

## Teacher Notes

- No simulator talks to our controller, and that is fine. Ground handling, stick naming, and the pause/RTH
  buttons are exactly what a first-flight briefing should cover anyway.
- If you can, power the controller and aircraft on (props off or secured, indoors) so they can see the sticks
  move the gimbal and the screen come alive without any chance of flight.
- Drill the pause button and the RTH button by name until they can point to them without looking. Those two
  buttons are the whole safety story for a first flight.
- Hand the controller around. Everyone holds it, finds the two sticks, and shows you throttle and yaw before
  they leave. That is the exit ticket.
