## The Short Version

**Part 107** is the section of federal regulation that covers flying small drones for anything other than pure
recreation. To do that legally, you need a **Remote Pilot Certificate**. To get the certificate, you pass a
knowledge test at an FAA-approved testing center.

**That certificate is the goal of the knowledge side of this course.**

## Why It Matters

- It is a **real FAA certificate**, not a class completion badge. Employers recognize it.
- Anyone doing drone work for money or for an organization: surveying, real estate, inspection, agriculture,
  public safety, news, film, needs it.
- You can hold it at 16. It stays valid as long as you keep the currency requirement up.
- It is something concrete to put on an application while you are still in high school.

## What the Test Covers

| Area | Roughly | What it looks like |
|------|--------:|--------------------|
| Regulations | 15-25% | What Part 107 allows, requires, and prohibits; waivers |
| Airspace & requirements | 15-25% | Classes of airspace, sectional charts, where you may operate |
| Weather | 11-16% | METARs, TAFs, wind, visibility, density altitude, clouds |
| Loading & performance | 7-11% | Weight, balance, battery and payload effects |
| Operations | 35-45% | Preflight, crew, emergencies, night ops, judgment, physiology |

60 multiple-choice questions, 2 hours, **70% to pass**.

## Questions People Ask

**Do I have to take it?** No. The course stands on its own and the WebXam is the graded state exam. Part 107 is
the opportunity on top.

**What if I fail?** You can retake it after 14 days. People retake it. It is not a permanent verdict.

**Can I fly on my own after I get it?** With your own aircraft and following the rules, yes: that is what the
certificate is for. School aircraft stay under school procedures.

**Do I need it to fly in this class?** No. Student operations here run under my supervision.
