# Aviation UAS: Vocabulary and Standards to Post

Print this and put it on the wall. Post the same text in Classroom on Monday. Friday's quiz comes from the
week's word list, and students should know that from Monday morning.

## Week 1 (Aug 19 to 21)

### We Are Learning To

- Say what Part 107 is, what the test covers, and who needs one.
- Handle batteries and aircraft in this room without being told twice.
- Take a documentation photo that is sharp, lit, and readable.
- Explain why a checklist gets read out loud even on the thousandth flight.

### Words to Know

| Word | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| UAS | Unmanned Aircraft System. The aircraft plus the controller and the link between them. |
| sUAS | A UAS weighing 55 pounds or less at takeoff, including everything on board. |
| Part 107 | The FAA rule that governs flying a small drone for anything other than fun. |
| RPIC | Remote Pilot in Command. The person responsible for the flight. There is always exactly one. |
| Visual observer | The person whose only job is to keep eyes on the aircraft and the area. |
| VLOS | Visual line of sight. You can see the aircraft with your own eyes, no binoculars, no screen. |
| Preflight | The inspection done before every flight, off a written checklist. |
| Documentation photo | A photo taken as a record, not as art. Sharp, lit, filled frame, nothing staged. |

## Week 2 (Aug 24 to 28)

### We Are Learning To

- Name the four forces of flight and say what changes when one of them changes.
- Read a sectional chart well enough to say what airspace we are standing in and what the ceiling is.
- Decode a METAR and make a go or no go call that I can defend.
- Run a full preflight inspection off a checklist and document what I found.
- Plan a flight on paper: objective, location, airspace, weather, crew, hazards, emergency plan.

### Words to Know

| Word | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| Lift | The force holding the aircraft up. |
| Weight | Gravity pulling it down. Includes the battery and anything you bolted on. |
| Thrust | The force pushing it forward, from the motors. |
| Drag | The air resisting the aircraft as it moves. |
| Center of gravity | The balance point. Move it too far and the aircraft becomes hard or impossible to control. |
| Airspace | The air above us and the rules that govern who may use it. |
| Controlled airspace | Somebody is responsible for separating traffic. Classes B, C, D, and E. |
| Uncontrolled airspace | Class G. Nobody is separating traffic, which means you are. |
| Sectional chart | The aviation map. Magenta and blue lines, airport symbols, altitude numbers in the boxes. |
| Ceiling | The highest altitude you are allowed to fly at that location. |
| LAANC | The system that grants near instant authorization to fly in controlled airspace. |
| NOTAM | Notice to Air Missions. A short term warning about something that changed. |
| METAR | A coded weather report from an airport, updated hourly. |
| Visibility | How far you can see. Part 107 requires at least 3 statute miles. |
| Cloud clearance | Part 107 requires staying 500 feet below and 2,000 feet horizontally from clouds. |
| Go / no go | The decision made before the flight, using rules and judgment, not hope. |
| Airworthy | The aircraft is in a condition safe to fly. The RPIC decides this, every time. |
| Lost link | The aircraft stops hearing the controller. Every flight plan has an answer for this. |

### The Exam Connection

The WebXam for this course weights Preflight Preparation at about 15 percent, Flight Environment at about 10
percent, and Aerodynamics at about 9 percent. Week 2 is the first pass at all three. The Part 107 test covers
the same ground, so the year serves both at once.
