## Coming In

1. Assigned seat, machine awake, logged in.
2. **Bell ringer** on the board and in Classroom: 5 minutes, start immediately.
3. Bags off the desks and away from the aircraft cases.

## Phones & Headphones

Phones away, per district policy. No headphones.

## Food & Drink

Closed water bottle on the floor. Nothing near the machines, the charts, or the aircraft.

## Your Files

- Everything in your **school Google Drive**. Naming: `lastname-assignment-version.ext`.
- Flight planning documents get dated: `lastname-flightplan-2026-09-14.pdf`.
- Turned in through Google Classroom.

## Aircraft and Equipment: Ground Rules

Until we complete flight training, aircraft are handled **only when I hand them to you, for a specific purpose**.

1. Nobody powers on an aircraft without direct permission.
2. Props off or props clear: we cover this before anything spins.
3. Batteries are the most dangerous thing in this room. They are stored, charged, and transported only by me
   or with me standing there. A swollen or damaged battery is reported immediately and not moved.
4. Aircraft are carried with two hands, in their case where possible.
5. **Report any damage, malfunction, or near-miss immediately.** Reporting is expected professional behavior.
   Concealing a problem is the offense.

## Cameras and Shared Gear

Cameras, tripods, and other shared equipment are checked out on the **shared room checkout form**, the same
system every class in this room uses. Pick "Aviation UAS" as the course. Strap around your neck any time a
camera is in your hands, and return it the same period unless we arranged otherwise.

## Checklists

We use written checklists and we read them out loud. Not from memory, not skimmed. This is how the industry
works and it is how this room works.

## When We Fly (later in the year)

- Every operation has a **Remote Pilot in Command** and a **Visual Observer**. Both roles are real jobs.
- The flight area is briefed before anyone launches. Non-participants stay outside it.
- **Anyone can call "abort" or "land now."** No one is questioned in the moment for calling a stop. We debrief after.
- Weather and airspace decide whether we fly. Not the lesson plan, not how much anyone wants to.

## Getting Help

1. Check the study guide or handout. 2. Try it. 3. Ask a neighbor. 4. Ask me.
Asking questions is a pilot habit. Use it.

## Turning Work In

Google Classroom, on the assignment post, named correctly. Charts and worksheets are submitted as scans or
photos when done on paper, legible, whole page in frame.

## Leaving the Room

Sign out on the pass log. One at a time. Not during a briefing or a demo.

## End of Period

1. Exit ticket submitted.
2. Materials returned: charts flat, not folded into a wad; references back on the shelf.
3. Any equipment back in its case and logged.
4. Chairs in, station clear.
5. Log off.

## Emergencies

Fire, tornado, lockdown routes posted by the door. Battery fire is its own procedure, we cover it before any
battery is charged in this room. Take nothing, follow me, stay with the class.
