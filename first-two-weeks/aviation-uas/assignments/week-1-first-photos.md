# Week 1 Assignment 2: Documentation Photos

**Assigned:** Fri Aug 21 (Day 03) | **Due:** end of the day Fri Aug 21
**Turn in:** Google Classroom | **Category:** Labs | **Points:** 20

## What This Is

This is not a photography class, but you will take a lot of photos this year. Aircraft get documented before
and after flights, damage gets photographed, sites get surveyed, and mapping work is nothing but images. A
photo that is soft or too dark is a report that does not prove anything.

So: six photos, taken like documentation.

## What You Turn In

**6 photos**, uploaded to the Classroom assignment, named:

```
lastname-doc-01.jpg  through  lastname-doc-06.jpg
```

## The List

| # | Shot |
|---|------|
| 1 | Wide shot of the workspace, showing the whole area |
| 2 | An aircraft or piece of equipment, whole, on a clean surface |
| 3 | A close up of one component, sharp enough to see condition |
| 4 | A close up of a label, serial number, or marking that is actually readable |
| 5 | Something outside the window or the door: sky conditions today |
| 6 | Your choice, but it has to show something a person would need to see |

## Rules for Documentation Photos

- Sharp. If a detail is soft it is not evidence.
- Lit. Shadow across the subject makes it useless.
- Filled frame. Get close enough that the subject is the photo.
- Nothing staged or edited. Documentation shows the thing as it is.

## Steps

1. Check out a camera on the room checkout form.
2. Strap around your neck.
3. Shoot the list. Nothing gets powered on, and nothing gets picked up without asking.
4. Card out, into the reader, copy to your Drive folder, rename.
5. Upload the 6 photos to Classroom.
6. Check the camera back in: card cleared, battery on the charger, lens cap on.

## Notes

Photo 4 is the one people fail. Getting a serial number readable takes light, distance, and a steady hand, and
that is exactly the skill you need in a preflight report.
