Two parts. One is code, one is thinking. They are about the same thing.

## Part 1: Your Own Arcade Program (20 pts)

You finished the tutorial on the first day. Now build something small that is yours.

| # | Requirement |
|---|-------------|
| 1 | One event: on start, a button press, or on overlap |
| 2 | A sequence of at least four actions |
| 3 | At least one loop |
| 4 | Something you added that was not in the tutorial |
| 5 | An explanation of what every part does, in comments or a text box |

Requirement 5 is the one that matters. If you cannot explain what a block does, it does not count yet. That is
true whether it came from a friend, the internet, or an AI. Ask me instead, that always works.

**Turning it in:** in Arcade, share your project and copy the link. Submit the link on Classroom. You do not
need to download anything. As long as you are logged into your Chrome profile at your seat, your work stays
where you left it.

## Part 2: Puzzle Reflection (10 pts)

Think back to the lateral thinking puzzles from the first day.

| # | Requirement |
|---|-------------|
| 1 | Pick one puzzle you did not get right away |
| 2 | What did you assume that turned out to be wrong? |
| 3 | What made you finally see it? |
| 4 | Three sentences minimum, your own words |

## Words You Should Be Able to Use

| Word | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| Algorithm | A precise sequence of steps to do something |
| Event | Something that makes code start running |
| Loop | Code that repeats |
| Bug | A step that does not do what you expected |
| Debugging | Finding the bug and fixing it |

## Notes

The puzzles and the code are the same skill. In both, you get stuck because of something you assumed without
noticing. Finding that assumption is the whole job.
