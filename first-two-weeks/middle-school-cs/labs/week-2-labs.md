# Middle School CS: Week 2 Lab Set

Four labs, each written to paste straight into Classroom. Labs and Activities category.

| Lab | When | Points | Notes |
|-----|------|-------:|-------|
| 1. Parts and Jobs | Day 04 | 10 | Part A of the Week 2 package |
| 2. Exact Instructions | Day 04 or Day 05, unplugged | 10 | New, no computers |
| 3. Debug Log | Day 07 | 15 | Part D of the Week 2 package, Sphero or Arcade |
| 4. How Big Is a File | Day 06 or early finisher work | 10 | New |

## Lab 1: Parts and Jobs

**Steps**

1. Label the diagram: CPU, RAM, storage, GPU, power supply, motherboard.
2. Fill in the table. For each part, what it does and what happens if it fails.
3. Look up the machine you are sitting at. Apple menu, About This Mac. Write down the processor, the memory,
   and the storage.
4. Answer: is your machine's memory or storage bigger, and why does that make sense?
5. Three troubleshooting scenarios. For each, what would you check first?

**Turn in:** the labeled diagram, the table, your machine's specs, and the answers.

## Lab 2: Exact Instructions

**What this is about.** A computer does exactly what you said, not what you meant. This lab is the fastest way
to feel that, and it takes no computers at all.

**Steps**

1. On paper, write instructions for drawing a simple shape or picture. Do not show anyone your drawing.
2. Trade instructions with a partner. Do not talk. Follow their instructions exactly, even when you can tell
   what they meant.
3. Compare the two drawings.
4. Circle every instruction that was ambiguous. Write a better version of the three worst ones.
5. Answer: which instruction caused the biggest difference, and what was missing from it?

**Turn in:** your original instructions, your partner's drawing, your three rewrites, and the answer.

**How it is graded:** 10 points. A drawing that came out wrong is a good result. The rewrites are the part
that counts.

## Lab 3: Debug Log

We have two Spheros, so you are logging either your Sphero run or your Arcade debugging. Both count the same
because the skill is identical.

**Steps**

1. Write down the program you are starting with. On paper for Sphero, or a screenshot for Arcade.
2. Run it. Write down what actually happened.
3. Change **one thing.** Run it again. Write down what happened.
4. Keep going until it works or your time runs out.
5. Write down the final program and one sentence on what is different from where you started.

**Turn in:** the whole log, start to finish.

**How it is graded:** 15 points. A log with no failures in it is not a good log. The failures are the content.
An incomplete run with a good log scores better than a lucky run with no log.

## Lab 4: How Big Is a File

**What this is about.** Everything on a computer is stored as numbers, and different kinds of things need
wildly different amounts of room. This is where storage numbers stop being abstract.

**Steps**

1. Make four files and record the size of each.
   - A text file with one sentence in it.
   - A text file with the same sentence pasted 1,000 times.
   - A photo you take right now.
   - A ten second screen recording or video.
2. Put them in order, smallest to biggest.
3. Answer these.
   - How many copies of the one sentence file would fit in the photo?
   - Your machine has some number of gigabytes of storage. Roughly how many of those videos would fit?
   - Why does a video take so much more room than a photo? Think about what a video actually is.
4. One prediction: which would be bigger, a one minute song or a one minute video? Check it if you can.

**Turn in:** the four sizes, your ordering, and the answers.

**How it is graded:** 10 points. Show the numbers you actually got, not numbers you expected.
