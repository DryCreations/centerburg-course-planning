# Middle School CS: Year Map (DRAFT for discussion)

**Not a student document.** The planning skeleton for the 7-8 combined, year-long course: breadth every
quarter, built on equipment already in the room, feeding the high school pathway.

Aligned to the **Ohio K-12 Computer Science Standards, grades 6-8**, five strands:
CS = Computing Systems, NI = Networks & the Internet, DA = Data & Analysis ·
ATP = Algorithmic Thinking & Programming, IC = Impacts of Computing

Standards detail: `courses/middle-school-cs/standards.md`.

## Design principles

1. **Breadth every quarter**: each quarter touches at least three of the five strands.
2. **Something physical every quarter**: robots, printers, or sensors.
3. **Unplugged before plugged**: nearly every new concept starts away from the screen.
4. **Feeds the pathway**: skills recur in Design Techniques, Video & Sound, and Aviation UAS.
5. **Two-week rhythm**: most units run 2-3 weeks.

## How the two Spheros actually get used

They are a **station**, and that is fine, a station is often better than a class set, because the other 15
students are doing something else that matters.

| Model | How it runs |
|-------|-------------|
| **Rotation** | 2-3 students at the Sphero course while the rest work on Arcade or an unplugged task. Rotate every 8-10 minutes. |
| **Challenge queue** | Teams write and test their program on paper or in the app's simulator, then get one live run when their turn comes. Makes them plan instead of trial-and-error. |
| **Demo device** | Teacher-driven demonstration of a concept, then students implement the same idea in Arcade. |

The queue model is the most useful pedagogically: limited hardware forces the "write it before you run it"
habit that the whole course is built on.

## Where drones and cameras fit

**Drones:** topic only, how they work, what jobs use them, what the rules are. No middle school flight. A
demonstration flight by the UAS class, watched from outside the flight area, is a possible Q4 tie-in.

**Cameras:** documenting builds in Q2 and Q3, and the Q4 showcase. Previews Video & Sound.

## Open questions

2. **Filament budget** for the Q3 print project. Scale depends entirely on this.
3. **Repeat students**: with 7th and 8th combined, an incoming 7th grader could take this course twice. Do
   they? If so, build a Q4 alternate path (independent project track) rather than a full A/B rotation.
4. **MakeCode Arcade accounts**: Arcade works signed out, but progress saves better with a Microsoft account.
   Do students have one, or do we save projects as files to Drive?
5. **Old machine for teardown**: is there a dead desktop or laptop that can be sacrificed for the Q1 hardware
   unit? It is the single most memorable lesson in that quarter.
