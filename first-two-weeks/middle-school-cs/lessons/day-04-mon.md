## Objective

Students can name the major parts of a computer and say what each one does, and can explain the difference
between storage and memory. Everyone leaves with a spec hunt submitted.

## Standards This Week

Post these and leave them up. Ohio K to 12 Computer Science, grades 6 to 8.

- **Computing Systems, Devices.** Identify and describe the components of a computing system and how they
  interact.
- **Computing Systems, Troubleshooting.** Apply a systematic troubleshooting process to identify and fix
  problems.
- **Algorithmic Thinking and Programming, Variables.** Create and use variables, and understand data types at
  a basic level.
- **Impacts of Computing, Safety, Law and Ethics.** Explain digital footprint, privacy, intellectual property,
  and responsible use.

## Vocabulary and Standards, About Eight Minutes

Post the sheet. One physical object per word wherever you can.

**CPU, RAM, storage, GPU, motherboard, variable, conditional, troubleshooting, digital footprint.**

**The desk metaphor, done properly.** RAM is your desk. Big enough to work on, wiped clean every night.
Storage is the filing cabinet. Slower, but it is still there tomorrow. That one comparison explains every
assignment they have ever lost.

If a machine can be opened, open it. Even a dead laptop. Point at each part in real hardware. This is the
lesson students remember years later.

## Explore Block: Find Out What You Are Sitting At

Two things, in this order.

**1. Spec hunt.** Apple menu, About This Mac. Then dig further into System Report and see what else is in
there. Tell them to go poke around and find something they did not expect.

**2. Arcade tinker time.** Open your week 1 project. Find three blocks we have never used. Make one of them
do something, anything. It does not have to make the game better and it is allowed to make it worse.
