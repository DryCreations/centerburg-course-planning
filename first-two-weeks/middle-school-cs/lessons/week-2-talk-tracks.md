# Middle School CS: Week 2 Talk Tracks

Teacher facing. Seventh and eighth graders together, no assumed experience. Keep every one of these under
eight minutes and make something physical happen in each.

---

## Monday: The Desk and the Filing Cabinet

- Start with the bell ringer answers. "Where does your work go when you save it, and where does it go when you
  do not?" Most of them have lost work and none of them know why.
- RAM is your desk. Big enough to work on, wiped clean every night. Storage is the filing cabinet. Slower, but
  it is still there tomorrow. That one metaphor explains every disappeared assignment they have ever had.
- One sentence and one physical object per part. CPU does the thinking. GPU draws things. Power supply. The
  board that connects it all.
- If you can open a machine, open it. Even a dead laptop. This is the lesson students remember years later,
  and for the kids who only have a phone at home it is the first time they have seen inside a computer.

---

## Tuesday: A Box With a Name On It

- A variable is a named box that holds a value. Score lives in a box. Lives live in a box.
- Make the score variable live on the projector. Set it to 0 on start, change it by 1 on overlap, and let them
  watch the number tick up in the simulator. Do not explain it first, do it and then name it.
- A conditional decides. Say the shape out loud: if this is true, do that.
- Loop versus conditional goes on the board side by side and stays there. They mix these up for weeks and that
  is normal.
- Then break something on purpose. Put the score change inside the forever loop so it counts forever. Ask what
  is happening before you fix it. Let them figure it out. This is the most common Arcade bug there is and they
  should meet it while you are the one who caused it.

---

## Wednesday: The Screen Light Day

Deliberately in the middle of a tool heavy week, and between the two robot days.

- Digital footprint is everything you post plus everything posted about you. It is bigger than they think and
  it does not expire.
- Be honest about what is private. School accounts are not. Deleted is rarely deleted. Screenshots exist.
- The line that actually matters at this age is not hacking. It is something somebody posted about somebody
  else. Say that plainly.
- The class rule: the account is yours and the responsibility is yours. Do not use anyone else's login, and do
  not post other people's work or photos without asking.
- Let the scenario disagreements run. This age group learns this from arguing with each other, not from a list
  of rules. One gotcha lecture and they stop talking honestly for the rest of the year.
- Never use a real student example, even anonymized. They always know who it was.

---

## Thursday: Change One Thing

- Plan, run, fix. Say the rule again: write it before you run it.
- Model debugging out loud on a broken program. "It overshoots, so either the speed is too high or the
  duration is too long. I am going to change one of those, not both."
- Change one thing at a time is the single most important habit of the day and it applies at the Sphero
  station and on the screen equally.
- Two Spheros means a queue. Say why the paper plan earns the run: it is the only thing that makes two robots
  work for a whole class, and the same rule comes back in Q2 when the VEX kits are also limited.
- The debug log is the actual assessment, not the successful run. A team that never fails is either lucky or
  not writing things down.
- Watch for one partner owning the controls the whole time. Enforce the swap.

---

## Friday: Fewest Blocks

- Checkpoint first, ten questions, same format most Fridays so it never feels like an ambush.
- Then the fewest blocks challenge, run as a bracket because there are two robots. Winner is the fewest blocks
  that completes the course, not the fastest time.
- Efficiency is the concept being smuggled in and it comes back all year. Do not announce that.
- Everyone waiting a turn does the same challenge in Arcade. Same behavior, fewest blocks. It is not filler.
- Collect the "one thing you want more of" answers. This year map is deliberately flexible and those answers
  are the best sequencing information you will get.

---

## Things to Watch For This Week

- Arcade submissions with no explanation attached. Anyone who cannot explain theirs gets a redo, framed as a
  redo and not as an accusation.
- Students who stalled on the Day 01 tutorial. Variables is where a shaky start compounds. Catch them Tuesday.
- Fast finishers. Give them a second condition, a timer, or lives, rather than a new lesson.
- Remind them that Arcade projects live in their Chrome profile at their assigned seat. Nothing gets
  downloaded, and submitting means sharing the project link on Classroom.
