# Day 06 - Wed, Aug 26 (Week 2)

**Course:** Middle School CS, **Unit:** Digital Citizenship
**Strands:** Impacts of Computing, Networks & the Internet

## Objective

Students can explain what a digital footprint is, identify what is and is not private online, and describe
what this class expects of them online.

> **Deliberately a screen-light day** in the middle of a tool-heavy week. It also breaks up the two robot/code
> days on either side.

## Materials & Setup

Whiteboard, printed scenario cards, no machines needed for the first half.

## Bell Ringer (5 min)

*Name three things about you that a stranger could find online in ten minutes.*

## Direct Instruction (15 min)

1. **Digital footprint**: everything you post, and everything posted about you. It is bigger than they think
   and it does not expire.
2. **What is actually private**: a short, honest list. School accounts are not private; the district can see
   them. Deleted is rarely deleted. Screenshots exist.
3. **The line that matters at this age:** the thing that gets people hurt is usually not hacking, it is
   something someone posted about someone else.
4. This class's rule: **the account is yours, the responsibility is yours.** Do not use anyone else's login,
   and do not post other people's work or photos without asking.

## Activity (22 min): Scenario Sort

In small groups, with scenario cards (a shared password, a reposted photo, an unattributed image, a mean
comment, a phishing email, an account left logged in on a lab machine):

1. Sort each into **fine / risky / never**.
2. For each "risky" and "never": say what could go wrong and what to do instead.
3. Groups report their hardest disagreement: the disagreements are the good part.

Then, individually: **Week 2 Part C**, three sentences on one thing they will do differently.

## Closure / Exit Ticket (5 min)

**Exit ticket:** *One thing you thought was private that isn't.*

## Teacher Notes

- Let the disagreements run. This age group learns this from arguing with each other, not from a list of rules.
- Keep it non-preachy. One "gotcha" lecture and they stop talking honestly for the rest of the year.
- Do not use a real student example, ever: even anonymized, they always know who it was.
