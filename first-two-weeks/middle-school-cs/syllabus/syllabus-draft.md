# Middle School Computer Science Syllabus

**Course:** Middle School Computer Science, grades 7 and 8
**Teacher:** Mr. Mankin | **Room:** 173 | **Email:** hayden.mankin@centerburgschools.org
**Full year. Meets daily.**

## What This Class Is

Computer science is bigger than coding. This year you will program, build and drive robots, work with data,
learn how the internet moves information, design and 3D print things, and talk about what technology should
and should not be allowed to do.

Every quarter covers a range of topics instead of one long unit, so you see more of the field.

No experience needed. Most of you have never done any of this.

## What You Will Do

| Quarter | Topics |
|---------|--------|
| Q1 | How computers work, first programs in MakeCode Arcade, digital citizenship, problem solving |
| Q2 | VEX IQ robotics, algorithms, debugging, networks and the internet |
| Q3 | Data and charts, 3D design and printing, sensors |
| Q4 | A bigger build project, AI, cybersecurity, showcase |

## What We Will Cover

Roughly in this order. Nothing here is locked. If a topic lands well we spend longer on it.

| Quarter | What we are doing |
|---------|-------------------|
| Quarter 1 | How computers work, MakeCode Arcade programming, algorithms, digital citizenship, Sphero robots |
| Quarter 2 | Networks and the internet, breaking problems into parts, VEX IQ building and programming |
| Quarter 3 | Data: collecting it, storing it, charting it, and spotting when a chart is lying to you. Physical computing with sensors |
| Quarter 4 | Cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and its impacts, careers in computing, and a choice project you pick yourself |

Every quarter has programming in it, and every quarter has something you build with your hands.

## Standards

This course follows the Ohio K-12 Computer Science Standards for grades 6-8, across all five strands:
computing systems, networks and the internet, data and analysis, algorithmic thinking and programming, and
impacts of computing.

There is no state exam in this class. It is built to feed the high school courses in this same room: Design
Techniques, Video and Sound, and Aviation Unmanned Aircraft.

## Equipment

### Late Work

I do not plan to take points off for being late.

If an assignment will not take late work, the assignment says so. Read the assignment first.

I close submissions on an assignment once I finish grading what came in. If you want to turn something in
late, check that the assignment does not say no late submissions, then come ask me to reopen it. I will.

Just ask. An assignment that stays closed and never gets mentioned stays a zero, and that is the only way
being late actually hurts you here.

### Redoing Work

If an assignment did not go well, you can redo it. Come talk to me. Learning it on the second try still counts.

## Expectations

1. Bell ringer when you come in, exit ticket before you leave.
2. No phones. That is district policy. No headphones.
3. Take care of the equipment. Four other classes use this room.
4. Things breaking is normal here. Code stops working, robots drive into walls, prints fail. What you do next
   is the skill.
5. Help by explaining, not by taking over someone's mouse.

## AI Use

AI can help you, or it can let you skip the part that was the point.

Fine: asking for something to be explained a different way, help understanding an error message.

Not allowed: having AI write your code or your project. If you cannot explain how it works, you did not do the
assignment.

The test is simple: can you explain what you turned in? If not, it does not count yet. Ask me instead, that
always works.
