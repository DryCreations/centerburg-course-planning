## Before You Shoot

Three settings control how bright the image is and what it looks like. They trade against each other. Change
one and you give something back on another.

| Setting | What it controls | What it costs |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|
| Aperture, the f number | How wide the lens opens, and how much is sharp front to back | A low f number blurs the background and leaves less room for focus error |
| Shutter speed | How long the sensor is exposed | Fast freezes motion and darkens the shot. Slow blurs motion and brightens it |
| ISO | How sensitive the sensor is | Higher brightens a dark shot and adds grain |

Three modes matter today. Learn these and you can stop using Auto.

| Mode | Who decides what |
|------|-----------------|
| **Av**, aperture priority | You pick the aperture. The camera picks the shutter speed. |
| **Tv**, shutter priority | You pick the shutter speed. The camera picks the aperture. |
| **M**, manual | You set all three. Slower, and the only mode that stays consistent shot to shot. |

On some cameras Av is marked A and Tv is marked S. Same thing.

## Shot 1: Auto Baseline

Pick a subject near a window. One shot in Auto. Write down what the camera chose for aperture, shutter, and
ISO. Everything else gets compared to this.

---

## Part A: Av Mode, Depth of Field (shots 2 to 5)

Same subject, same spot, do not move it.

| Shot | What to do |
|------|-----------|
| 2 | Lowest f number your lens allows |
| 3 | f/8 |
| 4 | f/16 |
| 5 | Move the subject further from the background, back to the lowest f number |

Watch what happens to the background across shots 2, 3, and 4. Then notice that shot 5 gets you more of that
effect without touching the camera at all.

---

## Part B: Tv Mode, Motion (shots 6 to 10)

This is the fun part. Fast shutter freezes. Slow shutter smears.

| Shot | What to do |
|------|-----------|
| 6 | Water dripping from the sink at 1/1000 or faster. Get the drop hanging in the air. |
| 7 | The same drip at about 1/15. It should be a streak, not a drop. |
| 8 | Someone walking past you at 1/500, camera still. Frozen. |
| 9 | Someone walking past you at about 1/30, **panning with them.** Turn with the person and keep them in the same place in the frame. Done right, the person is sharp and the background is streaked. |
| 10 | Your choice of motion: a ball bouncing, paper tossed in the air, a spinning chair, a skateboard, a fan. Pick your own shutter speed and say why. |

Shot 9 is the hard one. Expect to try it eight or ten times. That is normal, that is what everyone does, and
the throwaways do not count against you.

Turn the lights on for the fast shutter shots. A short exposure means less light gets in, so the camera has to
open up or raise ISO to compensate. You will see it happen in the numbers.

---

## Part C: M Mode (shots 11 to 13)

| Shot | What to do |
|------|-----------|
| 11 | Set all three yourself and match your Auto baseline from shot 1 as closely as you can |
| 12 | Go to the dim corner of the room. Get a usable exposure in manual. Write down the ISO you needed. |
| 13 | One deliberate mistake. Blown out white, solid black, or hopelessly blurred. Label it so I know you meant it. |

Shot 11 is the point of the whole assignment. Anyone can turn a dial. Matching a known exposure means you
understand what the three settings are doing to each other.
