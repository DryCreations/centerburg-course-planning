## Lab 1: Shooting Modes

Full student instructions are in `../assignments/week-2-camera-modes.md`. Post that one to Classroom, it is
written to be pasted as is.

**Short version.** Thirteen frames, shot in this room. One Auto baseline, four in Av testing depth of field,
five in Tv covering a frozen water drip, a streaked water drip, a frozen walker, a panning shot, and one of
their own choosing, then three in M including a matched exposure and a deliberate mistake. Submitted with a
settings table read off the file info.

**Category:** Labs, 30 points. **Runs:** Day 06, finished Day 07 if needed.

## Lab 2: Depth of Field Pair

**What this is about.** The single most recognizable difference between a phone snapshot and a shot someone
made on purpose.

**Steps**

1. Put a person about six feet in front of a busy background.
2. Shoot them at the lowest f number your lens allows.
3. Without moving anything, shoot them again at f/16.
4. Now move the person further from the background and repeat the low f number shot.

**Turn in:** three frames and one sentence on which one you would use in a video and why.

**How it is graded:** Lab rubric. The three shots have to be the same subject in the same spot except for the
one change being tested.

## Lab 3: Sound Check

Graded as Part B of the Week 2 assignment, not separately.

**Steps.** With a partner, each person submits their own files.

1. Record the same sentence at about six inches, two feet, and across the room.
2. Record thirty seconds of room tone. Nobody talks, nobody moves.
3. Record one take deliberately too loud so you hear what clipping sounds like.
4. Offload, name the files, submit.

**Turn in:** five files named `lastname-soundcheck-6in.wav`, `-2ft.wav`, `-far.wav`, `-roomtone.wav`,
`-clipped.wav`, plus two sentences on which distance sounded best and why.

## Lab 4: Five Shot Sequence

**What this is about.** Five shots that make any simple action editable. Learn this and you can cover
anything.

**The five shots**

1. Close up of the hands.
2. Close up of the face.
3. Wide of the whole scene.
4. Over the shoulder.
5. One unusual angle.

**Steps**

1. In pairs, check out a kit. Run the whole checkout yourself.
2. Pick a simple repeated action: writing, tying a shoe, opening a locker.
3. Shoot all five, five to eight seconds each, camera braced or on a tripod.
4. Shoot in aperture priority, not Auto. You just learned it, use it.
5. Offload, name, submit. Check the gear back in.

**Turn in:** five clips and one sentence on which shot is weakest and what you would do differently.

## Lab 5: First Cut

**What this is about.** Four moves in Premiere: import, trim, order, export. Everything else this year builds
on those four.

**Steps**

1. New project from the class template. Import your own media from Drive, not from the desktop.
2. Drag clips to the timeline. Cut the head and tail off each one.
3. Put them in an order that makes sense.
4. Drop your Day 05 audio underneath.
5. Export with the class settings and submit the file, not the project.

**Turn in:** an exported piece, twenty to thirty seconds, at least four clips and one audio track.

**How it is graded:** Lab rubric. It has to play. A project file is not a deliverable.
