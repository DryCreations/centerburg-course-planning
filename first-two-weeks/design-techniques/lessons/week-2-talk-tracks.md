# Design Techniques: Week 2 Talk Tracks

Teacher facing. These are the things worth saying out loud in this course and nowhere else. Week 1 was the
same in every room, so this week is where Design Techniques starts sounding like Design Techniques.

Keep each one under ten minutes. They are talks, not units.

---

## Monday: What This Course Actually Trains You For

They need the pitch now that the novelty of week 1 has worn off.

- Interactive Media is the pathway. Design Techniques is the front door and there are more courses behind it.
- The work here is not art class work. Art answers to you. Design answers to a brief. Both are hard, in
  different directions.
- Two credentials come out of this course: the WebXam at the end of the year and Adobe Certified Professional
  in Express. Both go on a resume and both are free to them.
- The portfolio is the real deliverable of the year. Every project lands on it. In May, the portfolio is the
  proof the year happened.

Close honest: nobody is good at this in August, and everything already on the wall has something in it worth
keeping.

---

## Tuesday: Why Brands Lock Their Colors

Do not define brand. Show it.

- Put up three logos with the color stripped out. They will still name most of them.
- Consistency is cheaper than creativity. A brand kit means making the color decision once instead of two
  hundred times.
- Hex codes exist because "blue" is not a specification. Two people given "blue" make two designs. Two people
  given #1F4E79 make one.
- The adjective exercise is the actual content. "Cool" and "nice" tell a designer nothing. "Rugged," "quiet,"
  "expensive," "handmade" tell them what to do next. Model choosing badly, then well.

Say plainly that the kit gets rebuilt in Unit 1.3 once they know type and color. Nobody should freeze trying
to decide who they are on a Tuesday.

---

## Wednesday: Raster and Vector, Once, Properly

The most useful technical distinction in the course, and it takes six minutes.

- Raster is pixels. A photo is raster. Enlarge it and you are inventing information nobody captured.
- Vector is math. A shape is vector. Enlarge it and it recalculates, so it stays sharp.
- Blow up one of each to 800 percent on the projector. Do not describe it, show it.
- Then the practical rule. Photos are raster and always will be. Logos should be vector. What you export
  depends on where it is going: screen means RGB and PNG or JPG, print means CMYK and PDF.
- One sentence on why a color looks duller in print than on the monitor, and that this is why the printed
  squares on the wall do not match what they saw on screen. They already noticed. Explain it.

---

## Thursday: Simple Survives Scaling

The monogram lesson, and it applies to everything after it.

- Show six real marks and ask what they have in common. Almost all of them are two or three shapes.
- Shrink a complex student mark and a simple one to thumbnail size side by side. The complex one turns into a
  smudge. Nobody argues with that.
- The habit being taught is sketch first, generate options, then build. Building first means committing to the
  first idea, and the first idea is usually the obvious one.
- Transparent background exports. Show what happens when a JPG logo lands on a colored background. They will
  make that mistake on every project this year until they stop.

---

## Friday: How Critique Is Going to Work

Set this up now, before real critique starts in Unit 1.1.

- Today's gallery walk has one rule: say what worked. That is deliberate. Trust comes first.
- Starting next unit, critique has a format. What you see, what works, one question. No "I like it." No "it is
  bad."
- Critique is about the work on the screen, never the person who made it. Say it once and hold it all year.
- Their work is going on the wall all year. That is not a punishment and it is not a reward, it is what a
  studio looks like.

---

## Things to Watch For This Week

- The published Google Site nobody can open. Sharing defaults to restricted. Make them test the link in a
  private window while you watch.
- All black and neon brand kits. Ask what it would look like on a poster for a hardware store.
- Students who overbuild the monogram and then cannot use it anywhere.
- Anyone still saving to the desktop. Fix that habit now, it gets expensive in Q2.
