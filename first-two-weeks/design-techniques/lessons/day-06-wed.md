**Course:** Design Techniques, **Unit:** 1.0 Orientation and Portfolio Setup

## Objective

Students can control what a viewer looks at first using size, contrast, and position, and can say what raster
and vector each mean and which export belongs to which job.

## Materials

A deliberately bad flyer built ahead of time, the flyer text posted in Classroom, one raster and one vector
graphic ready to blow up on the projector, the printed squares still on the wall.

## Bell Ringer

Two posters on the board. Which one tells you the most important thing first, and how do you know?

## Direct Instruction

**Hierarchy, about ten minutes.** Three moves: size, contrast, position. Take the bad flyer and fix it live in
under five minutes using only those three. Do not add anything. Removing and resizing is the whole demo.

**Raster and vector, about six minutes.** Blow up a photo and a shape to 800 percent side by side on the
projector. Do not describe it, show it. Then the practical rule: photos are raster, logos should be vector,
screen means RGB and PNG or JPG, print means CMYK and PDF.

Point at the printed squares while you say it. They already noticed that the print does not match the screen.
Now they know why.

## Work Time

**Lab 1: Hierarchy Fix.** Same flyer text, built twice, second version changing only size, contrast, and
position. Both exported.

**Lab 2: Export Test** for anyone who finishes early. Three exports of the monogram compared at 300 percent,
plus a file size table.

Then Week 2 assignment work. Parts A and B should be finished today.

## Closure

Exit ticket: which of the three moves did the most work in your version, and why that one?
