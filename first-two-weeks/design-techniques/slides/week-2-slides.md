# DAY 04 — Express Past the Surface

---

## Two words that explain what you noticed

- **Raster:** made of pixels. Photos are raster.
- **Vector:** made of math. Logos should be vector.
- **demo:** blow up one of each to 800%. Do not describe it, show it.

---

## Why your square printed different

- **RGB:** light on a screen. What you designed in.
- **CMYK:** ink on paper. What the printer uses.
- Same design, different color space. That is the gap you saw.

---

## Four features to try today

- **Brands:** a saved set of colors and fonts
- **Layers:** order, lock, group
- **Remove background** (and where it gets the edge wrong)
- **Export:** PNG, JPG, PDF, and the transparent checkbox

---

## Your turn — feature test

- A shape or text over a background-removed photo
- Three layers, two of them grouped
- One PNG with a transparent background
- One JPG of the same thing
- One sentence: which goes to a printer, and why

# DAY 05 — Brand Kits

---

## A brand you know by color alone

- Name one. What colors?
- Brands lock their colors and fonts on purpose
- Consistency is the whole point

---

## What goes in a kit

- 3 to 5 colors (with hex codes)
- 2 fonts: one for headings, one for body
- 3 adjectives for the feel

---

## Adjectives that actually help

- Useless: "cool," "nice," "good"
- Useful: "rugged," "warm," "plainspoken," "loud," "handmade"
- Pick words that tell someone else what to do next

---

## demo + your turn — Part B

- Express → Brands → new brand → add colors, fonts, a logo slot
- See it appear inside a new document
- Build yours: 3-5 colors, 2 fonts, 3 adjectives
- Two sentences on your About page: why these

# DAY 06 — Hierarchy and Exports

---

## Hierarchy: control what they see first

- Three moves only: **size, contrast, position**
- **demo:** fix the bad flyer live in under five minutes
- Do not add anything. Remove and resize. That is the whole trick.

---

## Raster vs. vector, the practical rule

- Photos are raster → screen means RGB, export PNG or JPG
- Logos should be vector → print means CMYK, export PDF
- (point at the printed squares while you say it)
