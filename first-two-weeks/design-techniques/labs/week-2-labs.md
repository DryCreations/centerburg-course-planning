## Lab 1: Hierarchy Fix

**What this is about.** Three moves control what someone looks at first: size, contrast, and position. That is
most of graphic design. One page, built twice.

**You will need:** Adobe Express, your brand kit, the flyer text posted in Classroom.

**Steps**

1. Copy the flyer text from Classroom. Do not change the words. Every word has to appear in your version.
2. Build version one in five minutes. Set a timer. Do not fuss with it.
3. Look at your own page and write down what a stranger reads first, second, and third.
4. Build version two. Change only size, contrast, and position. No new images, no new fonts.
5. Export both as PNG.

**Turn in:** both PNGs, plus two sentences naming what a reader hits first in each version.

**How it is graded:** Lab rubric. Full credit needs both versions, the reading order written down, and a
version two where the change is obvious at a glance.

## Lab 2: Export Test

**What this is about.** Most first year mistakes are export mistakes. A blurry logo on a poster, a file too
big to email, a mark with a white box behind it. Understanding raster and vector fixes all three.

**You will need:** Adobe Express, your monogram or any shape based graphic, one of your week 1 photos.

**Steps**

1. Export your monogram three ways: PNG with a transparent background, JPG, and PDF.
2. Place all three on a canvas at about 300 percent size. Screenshot the comparison. Which one broke, and why?
3. Write down the file size of each.
4. Take a week 1 photo. Export it at full size, then export a copy at 25 percent. Enlarge the small one back
   to full size on the canvas and screenshot that too.
5. Fill in the table.

| File | Type | Raster or vector | File size | Where you would use it |
|------|------|------------------|-----------|------------------------|
| Monogram PNG | | | | |
| Monogram JPG | | | | |
| Monogram PDF | | | | |
| Photo, full size | | | | |
| Photo, 25 percent enlarged | | | | |

**Turn in:** the completed table, both screenshots, and one sentence on why a logo should not be a JPG.

**How it is graded:** Lab rubric. The answers have to match what your files actually did, not what you
assumed they would do.

## Lab 3: Type Pairing Test

**What this is about.** Two typefaces chosen on purpose beat six chosen at random. Typography is about 12
percent of the exam and it is the fastest way to make your work stop looking like a first draft.

**You will need:** Adobe Express and the headline and paragraph posted in Classroom.

**Steps**

1. Copy the headline and paragraph. Same words in all three versions.
2. Build the same simple layout three times, changing nothing but the type.
   - Version A: serif headline, sans serif body.
   - Version B: sans serif headline, serif body.
   - Version C: one family for both, separated only by weight and size.
3. Look at all three at full size, then shrink them to a quarter of the screen and look again.
4. Pick the one you would actually use. Two sentences on why, and say something about legibility rather than
   which one looks cool.
5. Add the winning pair to your brand kit.

**Turn in:** all three exports and your two sentences.

**How it is graded:** Lab rubric. The three versions have to differ only in type. If the layout moved, the
test proves nothing.

## Lab 4: Photo Cleanup

**What this is about.** Photographs get edited before they get used, and cropping is a design decision rather
than a repair.

**You will need:** three of your own week 1 photos and Adobe Express.

**Steps**

1. Pick three of your own photos. At least one should be a shot you were not happy with.
2. For each: crop it deliberately, then adjust exposure and contrast until it looks right.
3. Keep the before and after of each one.
4. Take one of the three and crop the same photo two different ways so the picture is about two different
   things.
5. One sentence per photo on what the crop changed.

**Turn in:** three before and after pairs, the two crops of one photo, and your three sentences.

**How it is graded:** Lab rubric. Cropping to a tidier rectangle is not the point. The crop has to change what
the photo is about.
