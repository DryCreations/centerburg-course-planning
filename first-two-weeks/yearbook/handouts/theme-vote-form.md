# Yearbook: Final Theme Vote

Paste into a Google Form and put it up as today's bell ringer. Yesterday the staff brainstormed about six
theme ideas; three drew votes and three did not. This vote decides between the **three finalists.** Whatever
wins shapes the cover, the section openers, the type and color, and, for some ideas, how the whole book is
organized.

Set the form to collect one response per student. Show the live results before you discuss.

---

## Form intro (paste at the top)

> This is the real one. The theme we pick shapes the whole book for the rest of the year, so vote for the one
> you would be proud to have your name on, not just the funniest. There are no wrong answers here, only the
> one the most of us can get behind.

---

## Question 1 (required, multiple choice): Which theme should the book be built around?

- **Seasons of Our Lives** — the year told through its seasons; the book is organized chronologically, fall to
  summer, so it reads like the year actually happened.
- **[Finalist 2 — fill in]** — [one line on what it means and how it would show up in the book]
- **[Finalist 3 — fill in]** — [one line on what it means and how it would show up in the book]

> Fill in finalists 2 and 3 with the other two that got votes yesterday. Keep each to one line, and where a
> theme changes how the book is organized, say so, because that is part of what students are voting on.

## Question 2 (required, short answer): In one sentence, why that one?

The reasons are as useful as the count. A theme that wins the vote but that nobody can explain is weaker than a
close second that everyone has a reason for.

## Question 3 (optional, short answer): One spread this theme would make possible that a plain book would not.

This is the question that turns a slogan into a plan, and the best answers become spreads on the ladder today.

---

## After the vote

- Show the results and read a few of the Question 2 reasons out loud.
- If it is close, the Question 2 and 3 answers break the tie better than a runoff does.
- Announce the winner as a decision, then move straight into what the book now looks like and how the ladder
  changes. The momentum from deciding is the best time to re-plan.
