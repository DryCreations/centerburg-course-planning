# Yearbook: The Page Ladder

**What this is.** The ladder is the whole book on one sheet: every page, in order, with what goes on it. We
build the first draft together on Day 05. It is a draft, not a promise. It changes all year. But nothing else,
not a beat, not a deadline, not a coverage minimum, makes sense until we can see all 96 pages at once.

---

## The Numbers

- **96 pages total.** That is **48 spreads** (a spread is two facing pages, and we plan in spreads because
  that is the unit a designer actually works in).
- Some spreads are locked by convention: cover, title page, table of contents, the index, and the closing
  pages. Those come off the top first.
- Everything else is ours to assign.

## How We Fill It

Work in this order on Day 05. Do not design anything. We are only deciding **what each spread is about**.

1. **Block out the fixed spreads first.** Cover, title/opening, table of contents, dividers, index, closing.
2. **Pull last year's book apart, spread by spread.** For each one: keep it, cut it, or change it. What was
   missing entirely? That is where new spreads come from.
3. **Assign sections to the beats** we built on Day 04. Fall/winter/spring sports, arts, clubs, academics,
   student life, events, seniors, underclass, faculty, community.
4. **Count as you go.** When the assigned spreads pass 48, something has to merge or cut. When they fall
   short, we have blank pages, and blank pages get filled with student life, which is usually the best part of
   the book anyway.

## The Ladder Sheet

Fill one row per spread. This lives as a Google Sheet on the coverage board so the whole staff can see it.

| Spread | Pages | Section | Beat / owner | What is on it | Status |
|-------:|:-----:|---------|--------------|---------------|--------|
| 1 | 1 (+ inside cover) | Opening | EIC | Title, theme statement | fixed |
| 2 | 2-3 | Opening | | Student life intro, big photo | |
| 3 | 4-5 | Table of contents | Design Lead | Built last, once order is final | fixed, later |
| ... | | | | | |
| 48 | 94-95 (+ inside back) | Closing | EIC | Colophon, thanks, final photo | fixed |

**Status** is one of: `fixed`, `assigned`, `open`, or `cut`. Most of the sheet is `open` when we finish Day 05,
and that is correct. Open spreads get owners as beats and roles firm up this week.

## Rules of Thumb While We Build

- **A spread is a subject, not a leftover.** "Sports, page 5" is not a plan. "Volleyball, the senior night
  spread" is.
- **Reference before you invent.** For every new spread idea, find the closest thing in last year's book and
  say what you would do differently. That is faster and better than starting from nothing.
- **Balance the calendar.** Fall content is easy to over-plan in August because it is what is happening now.
  Leave real room for winter and spring.
- **Every student in the building appears at least once.** The Index / Names Lead watches the ladder for who
  is missing, not just which events are missing.

## When the Theme Changes the Organization

A ladder is not always organized the same way. The theme can decide the order.

- **The default: by section.** Sports together, clubs together, academics together. Easy to plan, and what most
  books do.
- **Chronological / by season.** If the theme is time-based, like **Seasons of Our Lives**, the book reads
  fall to summer and events sit where they happened: homecoming and fall sports early, winter sports and the
  play in the middle, prom and spring sports and graduation at the end. Student life threads through all of it.
- **What to do about it:** once the theme vote lands, walk the drafted ladder and ask, spread by spread,
  "does this still go here, or does the theme move it?" Most spreads keep their content and just change
  neighbors. A seasonal book, for example, splits the sports spreads across the calendar instead of grouping
  them.

Do not rebuild the ladder from scratch when the organization changes. Re-order the spreads you already have.

## After Day 05

The ladder is never finished, but it is *usable* once every spread has a section and roughly half have an owner.
From there:

- Beat owners claim the spreads in their section.
- The coverage board and the ladder point at each other: an event on the board should map to a spread on the
  ladder, and a spread on the ladder should have coverage scheduled on the board.
- We revisit the ladder at the start of each quarter and whenever publisher deadlines land.
