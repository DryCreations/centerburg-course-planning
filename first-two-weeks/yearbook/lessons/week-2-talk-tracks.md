# Yearbook: Week 2 Talk Tracks

Teacher facing. The things worth saying out loud in this room and nowhere else. Week 1 was the same assignment
in four courses, so this is where Yearbook becomes a staff instead of a class.

---

## Monday: This Is a Staff, Not a Class

- The difference: in a class, you do the work I assign. On a staff, the book has to exist in May and the work
  is whatever gets it there.
- A beat is one slice of school life that one person owns all year. It fixes the way yearbooks usually fail,
  which is that nobody thought about the bowling team until March.
- Owning a beat means four things: know the schedule, make sure it gets photographed, get the names, and tell
  me when a deadline in your beat is coming.
- Let them argue about how the list gets split. A staff that argued over the list owns the list.
- Academics and faculty are the beats nobody volunteers for, and they often turn into the best spreads. Plan
  to assign them and say so up front.

Also today: everyone gets into eDesign, even if only for fifteen minutes. The point is that nobody meets the
software for the first time on a deadline.

---

## Tuesday: How Books Fail

The coverage lesson. The bell ringer is the lesson, so let them answer it before you say anything.

- Nobody decides to skip the fall play. Nobody was scheduled to be there. That is the whole failure mode.
- Points system, plainly: what earns points, the bonus for filling a gap, the quarter target.
- What counts as covered: twenty usable frames, names verified, submitted within 48 hours, named and filed
  right. Showing up is not coverage. Submitting is.
- Sign ups close 48 hours before the event. A conflict told in advance costs nothing. A no show costs points
  and costs the book.
- Say out loud that in class and daytime coverage counts, and nobody is stuck because they cannot get a ride
  to a Friday game. Tell me and we plan around it.

Everybody signs up for something before they leave. The first commitment is the habit that carries the year.

---

## Wednesday: Getting Off Auto

The camera talk. Keep it short and physical.

- Almost every unusable yearbook photo is one of three things: too dark, too blurry, or the background is
  louder than the person. All three are settings problems.
- Av is portraits. Low f number, the background goes soft, the face pops off a busy hallway wall.
- Tv is sports. 1/500 indoors, faster outside. A volleyball spike at 1/60 is a smear and there is no fixing it
  later.
- ISO is what saves you in the gym, and grain is what it costs. Say the rule plainly: a grainy sharp photo
  goes in the book, a clean blurry one does not.
- Panning is the trick shot and they will chase it. Let them. It is the shot that makes a student feel like a
  photographer, and the ten failed attempts are the practice.

Also Wednesday: theme. Keep asking what the idea looks like on page 87. That question kills the bad ideas
without you having to.

---

## Thursday: Captions and the Gutter

- A caption is the only place in the book where you say something the photo cannot. "Students walk in the
  hallway" wastes the space.
- Three parts: who, what is happening, and one thing the photo does not show. The third part means you have to
  actually go ask somebody. That is the job.
- Names are not negotiable. A misspelled name is permanent and it is the thing people remember about a book
  for twenty years. Check against a roster, not against your memory.
- Design rules that matter on day one in eDesign: every spread needs one dominant photo, faces stay out of the
  gutter, and people should be looking into the spread rather than off the edge.
- Check the page back in. A page left checked out is locked for everyone else, and that is how a deadline gets
  missed by one person forgetting.

---

## Friday: Deadlines Are the Course

- Announce beats and roles. Read them out. Explain the harder calls honestly, because anyone who did not get a
  first choice deserves a reason.
- The deadline board goes up today. Every publisher deadline we know, plus the internal deadlines that lead to
  each one. Point out that the first internal deadline is closer than it looks.
- The Friday status is five minutes: what you finished, what is stuck, what is due next. It counts in Staff
  Contribution and it is how we catch a problem in week one instead of week four.
- The publisher deadline is the one thing in this room that cannot be made up. Everything else in this course
  is flexible. That one is not, and saying it now is kinder than saying it in November.

---

## Things to Watch For This Week

- Anyone who signed up for a Friday away game after telling you on the survey they cannot get to evenings.
  Catch it kindly and now.
- eDesign pages left checked out. Sweep the ladder at the end of every period this week.
- Captions that all sound the same. That is the sign nobody went and asked anybody anything.
- Every student should leave Friday able to say four things: their beat, their role, one event they are
  covering, and their first deadline. Anyone who cannot, catch before they walk out.
