# Day 05 - Tue, Aug 25 (Week 2)

**Course:** Yearbook

> **Today we build the ladder.** After yesterday in eDesign and the old-book review, the staff can see what a
> spread is. Now we decide what all 96 pages are going to be. That is the whole period, so beat and role
> preferences move to tomorrow, once everyone can see the sections a beat actually covers.

## Objective

The staff produces a first-draft page ladder for the whole 96-page book: every spread with a section and a
subject, and a rough count against the 48 spreads the book holds.

## Materials & Setup

Last year's book (several copies if we have them) and one or two older books on the tables, the *Page Ladder*
handout, the ladder started as a shared Google Sheet on the coverage board (48 blank spread rows, the fixed
spreads pre-filled), the *Beats and Roles* handout from Day 04. The beat/role preference form and the
*Coverage & Points* handout are tomorrow, not today.

## Bell Ringer (5 min)

*Open last year's book. Find one spread you would cut, and one thing that happened last year that is not in the
book at all. Both, in writing.*

That is the ladder conversation in miniature: what to keep, what to cut, what is missing.

## Direct Instruction (15 min)

1. **What a ladder is.** 96 pages is 48 spreads. Walk the *Page Ladder* handout. The ladder is the whole book
   on one sheet, and nothing, not beats, not deadlines, works until we can see all of it at once.
2. **Block the fixed spreads live** on the projected sheet: cover, opening, table of contents, section
   dividers, index, closing. Those come off the top first. Count what is left.
3. **Reference, do not invent.** Hold up last year's book. For every spread we add, find the closest thing in
   an old book and say what we would do differently. Faster and better than a blank page.
4. **A spread is a subject, not a leftover.** "Sports, page 5" is not a plan. "Volleyball senior night" is.

## Work Time (25 min): Build the Ladder

The whole period, whole staff, on the shared sheet. This is enough for one day on its own, so the beat and
role preference form moves to tomorrow.

1. **Block the fixed spreads** with me on the projected sheet: cover, opening, contents, dividers, index,
   closing. Count what is left.
2. **Assign sections to the beats** from Day 04, spread by spread, using the old books as reference. A spread
   is a subject, not a leftover.
3. **Count as you go:** past 48 spreads, something merges or cuts; short of 48, the blanks become student life.
4. Do not design anything. We are only deciding what each spread is about.

Editor-in-Chief, Design Lead, and Coverage Coordinator candidates work the fixed spreads and section blocking
with me; everyone else takes a section and drafts its spreads against last year's book, then we pull it
together on the shared sheet.

## Closure / Exit Ticket (5 min)

Each beat area says, in one sentence, how many spreads it is carrying so far. That is the first gut check on
whether the book is balanced.

**Exit ticket:** *One spread on the ladder you would want to own, and why.* (I read these against tomorrow's
preference forms.)

## Teacher Notes

- The ladder does not have to be finished today. It has to be *usable*: every spread with a section, and a
  rough count against 48. Open spreads with no owner yet are correct at this stage, not a failure.
- Fall content over-plans itself in August because it is what is happening now. Push the staff to leave real
  room for winter and spring, or February has no pages left.
- The whole period on the ladder is the point today. Beat and role preferences are tomorrow, so nobody is
  rushed through either one. Use the exit tickets to see who is drawn to which section.
- Decide the per-quarter point target before tomorrow; students will ask when the points lesson runs. 12 is
  the draft.
