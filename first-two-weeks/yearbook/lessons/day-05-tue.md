# Day 05 - Tue, Aug 25 (Week 2)

**Course:** Yearbook

> **Today we build the ladder.** After yesterday in eDesign and the old-book review, the staff can see what a
> spread is. Now we decide what all 96 pages are going to be, and we start turning beat and role preferences
> into assignments.

## Objective

The staff produces a first-draft page ladder for the whole 96-page book, every spread with a section and a
subject, and every student submits beat and role preferences so assignments can start.

## Materials & Setup

Last year's book (several copies if we have them) and one or two older books on the tables, the *Page Ladder*
handout, the ladder started as a shared Google Sheet on the coverage board (48 blank spread rows, the fixed
spreads pre-filled), the *Beats and Roles* handout from Day 04, the beat/role preference form in Classroom,
the *Coverage & Points* handout ready but not the focus today.

## Bell Ringer (5 min)

*Open last year's book. Find one spread you would cut, and one thing that happened last year that is not in the
book at all. Both, in writing.*

That is the ladder conversation in miniature: what to keep, what to cut, what is missing.

## Direct Instruction (15 min)

1. **What a ladder is.** 96 pages is 48 spreads. Walk the *Page Ladder* handout. The ladder is the whole book
   on one sheet, and nothing, not beats, not deadlines, works until we can see all of it at once.
2. **Block the fixed spreads live** on the projected sheet: cover, opening, table of contents, section
   dividers, index, closing. Those come off the top first. Count what is left.
3. **Reference, do not invent.** Hold up last year's book. For every spread we add, find the closest thing in
   an old book and say what we would do differently. Faster and better than a blank page.
4. **A spread is a subject, not a leftover.** "Sports, page 5" is not a plan. "Volleyball senior night" is.

## Work Time (25 min)

Two things happen at once. Split the room or run them back to back, teacher's call.

1. **Build the ladder** (whole staff, on the shared sheet). Assign sections to the beats from Day 04, spread by
   spread, using the old books as reference. Count as you go: past 48 spreads something merges or cuts, short of
   48 the blanks become student life. Do not design anything. We are only deciding what each spread is about.
2. **Beat & role preference form** (Week 2, Part A, individually). Rank top three beats, name any role of
   interest, and say honestly what coverage they can get to. This is what I assign from Day 06 on, so it is not
   a formality.

Editor-in-Chief, Design Lead, and Coverage Coordinator candidates: work the fixed spreads and the section
blocking on the sheet with me while the rest fill in preferences, then swap.

## Closure / Exit Ticket (5 min)

Each beat area says, in one sentence, how many spreads it is carrying so far. That is the first gut check on
whether the book is balanced.

**Exit ticket:** *One spread on the ladder you would want to own, and why.*

## Teacher Notes

- The ladder does not have to be finished today. It has to be *usable*: every spread with a section, roughly
  half with an owner. Open spreads are correct at this stage, not a failure.
- Fall content over-plans itself in August because it is what is happening now. Push the staff to leave real
  room for winter and spring, or February has no pages left.
- Read the preference forms tonight against the ladder. Where a popular beat is oversubscribed and a thin one
  has no takers is exactly the assignment problem to solve Wednesday.
- The full coverage-points lesson and live board sign-up ride on top of this once roles firm up. Introduce
  points in one minute today so the preference form makes sense; do not let it eat the ladder time.
- Decide the per-quarter point target this week; students will ask. 12 is the draft.
