## Lab 1: Camera Modes for Yearbook

**What this is about.** Auto works in a bright hallway and falls apart in a gym. Almost every unusable
yearbook photo is one of three things: too dark, too blurry, or the background is louder than the person. All
three are settings problems and you can fix them today.

**Three settings, and what each one costs you**

| Setting | What it does | What it costs |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Aperture, the f number | Low number blurs the background and lets in more light | Less is in focus, so you have to nail focus on the eyes |
| Shutter speed | Fast freezes motion, slow lets in more light | Fast shots get dark, slow shots get blurry |
| ISO | Raises brightness in a dark room | Too high and the photo gets grainy |

**Two modes cover almost everything you will shoot**

- **Av**, aperture priority. You pick the f number, the camera picks the rest. Use it for portraits, club
  photos, candids in the hallway, anything not moving fast.
- **Tv**, shutter priority. You pick the shutter speed, the camera picks the rest. Use it for sports, dance,
  band, anything moving. Start at 1/500 indoors and go faster outside.

**Steps.** Shoot in this room and the hallway. Nine frames.

| Shot | Mode | What to do |
|------|------|-----------|
| 1 | Av | A person at the lowest f number your lens allows. Background should go soft. |
| 2 | Av | Same person, same spot, f/8. Compare the backgrounds. |
| 3 | Av | Move the person away from the wall, lowest f number again. Even softer background. |
| 4 | Tv | Someone walking past at 1/500. Frozen. |
| 5 | Tv | Someone walking past at 1/30, panning with them. Person sharp, background streaked. |
| 6 | Tv | Something falling or bouncing at 1/1000. |
| 7 | Av | Dim corner of the room, note what ISO the camera picked. |
| 8 | Av | Same dim corner, raise the ISO yourself, look at the grain. |
| 9 | Your choice | The best photo you can take of a person in this room right now. |

Shot 5 takes practice. Ten tries is normal.

**Turn in:** nine frames named `lastname-modes-01.jpg` through `-09.jpg`, plus a table with the mode, f number,
shutter, and ISO for each, read off the file info. Then one sentence: which mode would you use to shoot a home
volleyball game, and why?

**How it is graded**

| | Points |
|---|---:|
| Nine frames submitted and named correctly | 10 |
| Settings table complete and matching the files | 8 |
| Panning shot attempted | 3 |
| The question answered | 4 |

## Lab 2: Caption Lab

**What this is about.** A caption is the only place in the book where you get to say something the photo
cannot. Most beginner captions describe what a reader can already see, which is wasted space.

**The rule.** Every caption has three things: who, what is happening, and one thing the photo does not tell
you.

Weak: "Students walk in the hallway."

Better: "Between fourth and fifth period, Elena Ruiz and Marcus Vail argue about Friday's game, which they had
been going back and forth on since Tuesday."

**Steps**

1. Take five of your own week 1 photos.
2. Write a caption for each. Full names, spelled right, checked against a roster or the person.
3. For the third thing, you will have to actually know something. If you do not, go ask. That is the job.
4. Trade with a partner. Check their names against a roster and mark anything you cannot verify.

**Turn in:** five captions, each attached to the photo, plus the name of the person who checked yours.

**How it is graded:** 20 points, 4 per caption. A misspelled name is an automatic loss on that caption, the
same way it would cost the book in print.

## Lab 3: Photo Cull

**What this is about.** You will shoot 200 photos at an event and the spread has room for six. Choosing is a
skill and it is faster to learn on somebody else's photos.

**Steps**

1. Open the shared folder of week 1 photos.
2. Pick 30 to work from. Cut them to 8. Then cut those 8 to 3.
3. For each of the final 3, one sentence on why it survived.
4. For two you cut that were technically fine, one sentence on why they still did not make it.

**Turn in:** your final 3, your 2 rejects with reasons, and your 3 sentences.

**How it is graded:** 20 points on the Craft rubric. "It looked good" is not a reason. Say what the photo does
that the others do not.

## Lab 4: eDesign Layout Practice

Graded as Part C of the Week 2 Staff Setup Package.

**Day 04, login only.** Log in, find the ladder, open a spread, look at the template library, close it without
breaking anything. Ten minutes. The point is that nobody sees eDesign for the first time on a deadline.

**Day 07, the build.**

1. Open your practice spread.
2. Place four photos from your week 1 shoot.
3. Pick one to be the dominant photo and make it clearly bigger than the others.
4. Write a caption for each, names checked.
5. Add a headline that says something rather than labeling the page.
6. Keep faces out of the gutter.
7. Save and check the page in.

**Turn in:** the spread, checked in. Post a screenshot to Classroom so it is in the grade book.
