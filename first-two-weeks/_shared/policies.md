# Shared Policy Language (Canonical)

This is the source text for policies that appear identically in every syllabus. Edit here first, then
propagate. Yearbook overrides the grading weights and the late-work policy, those overrides are noted inline.

**Weights confirmed.** Projects 40 / Labs 20 / Assessments 30 / Readiness & Participation 10 = 100%.

---

## Grading Categories (CTE courses + Middle School CS)

| Category | Weight | What lands here |
|----------|-------:|-----------------|
| **Projects** | 40% | Unit projects, portfolio pieces, competition-track work |
| **Labs** | 20% | Guided skill work, hands-on practice, worksheets tied to a lab |
| **Assessments** | 30% | Weekly quizzes, unit exams, WebXam-style checks, credential practice |
| **Readiness & Participation** | 10% | Bell ringers and exit tickets |

**Naming note:** "Assessments" replaces "Quizzes & Exams", it covers quizzes, unit tests, and credential
practice exams without needing a new category later.

**Readiness & Participation is bell ringers and exit tickets only**: it is a work-completion score, not a
behavior score. Miss the bell ringer, miss the points; do it, get the points.

## Grade Floor

Quarter grades are not recorded below **50%**. A zero on an individual assignment is still a zero, the floor
applies to the quarter average only. The floor exists so one bad stretch is recoverable, not so work is optional.

## Late Work

Canonical wording. This is what goes in every syllabus and what gets said out loud in every course.

> I do not plan to take points off for being late.
>
> If an assignment will not accept late work, the assignment itself says so. That is the only place that rule
> lives, so read the assignment first.
>
> I close submissions on an assignment once I finish grading what came in. So if you want to turn something in
> late, check that the assignment does not say no late submissions, then reach out and ask me to reopen it. I
> will.
>
> What I need from you is the asking. An assignment that sits closed and never gets mentioned stays a zero,
> and that is the only way late work hurts you in this class.

**What this means operationally**

- No percentage penalties anywhere. No sliding scales. No one week cutoff.
- The gate is the grading pass, not the calendar. Submissions close when you finish grading that assignment.
- Anything that genuinely cannot be repeated gets **no late submissions** written on the assignment itself:
  quizzes and tests, shoot and crew days, flight days, event coverage, and publisher deadlines.
- Reopening is routine and costs nothing. Say yes.

> **Yearbook note:** the same policy applies, with more assignments carrying the no late submissions mark,
> because a game does not happen twice and the publisher does not wait. See the Yearbook syllabus.

## Academic Integrity & AI Use

Learning is about the process. AI can be a tool that helps you, or it can let you skip the part of the
assignment that was the point.

**How to think about it:** most assignments in this class ask you to *engage in a process*, planning,
drafting, shooting, revising, troubleshooting, explaining your choices. If AI does that part for you, it is
not allowed. If AI helps you get unstuck inside a process you are actually doing, it is usually fine.

- **Usually fine:** explaining a concept you are stuck on, suggesting vocabulary, checking spelling and
  grammar, generating practice questions, troubleshooting an error message.
- **Usually not allowed:** generating the deliverable itself, writing your reflection or rationale, producing
  images or footage you are supposed to create, or answering assessment questions.
- **Always:** if an assignment says AI is part of the work (some design and media units use generative tools
  on purpose), say what you used and what you did yourself.

**If you are unsure whether AI use is appropriate, ask.** Asking is never the wrong move; guessing wrong is.

Copying another student's work, submitting someone else's photos or footage as your own, or using assets you
do not have the rights to falls under the same expectation: the work you turn in is yours.
