## 1. Design Techniques: Onto the portfolio

**Documents:** `week-6/handouts/portfolio-embed.md` (student-facing, post it),
`week-5/project.md` (due Friday)
**Run it as:** demo the embed live on the projector, start to finish, in about ten minutes. Figma Share,
Get embed code, Google Sites Insert, Embed, Embed code, paste, resize, **Publish**, then open the
published site in a new tab and click through it. Then they do it, then the reflection.
**Have a fallback ready:** if Get embed code is not where you expect on the free plan, show Insert > Embed
> By URL with the share link instead.

### Slide 1: Standards today

> Create one standards slide for a high school design class titled "Today's Standards." List these with
> their FULL text, not just the numbers, in large readable type: 7.4.7 Optimize and export graphics files
> for intended use. 2.15.1 Understand the UX/UI design process (vision, journey mapping, wireframing,
> prototyping) for the targeted platform. 7.1.2 Describe the components of interactive media. 7.2.10
> Select and create visual design elements appropriate for the intended audience and use. Then a short
> line: "Today that means publishing the prototype where someone outside this room can use it, and writing
> about these four by name." Design it to stay readable on the board all period. One slide.

### Slide 2: Get the embed code

> Create one step-by-step slide for a high school design class titled "1. Get The Embed Code." Numbered
> steps in large text: "1) Check that Present opens on your HOME screen. Fix it now if it doesn't. 2)
> Share, top right. 3) Anyone with the link, can view. 4) Find Get embed code. 5) Choose PROTOTYPE, not
> the design file, so people get the clickable version. 6) Copy the whole thing. It starts with iframe."
> Add a box at the bottom: "Can't find Get embed code? Copy the normal share link and tell me. There is a
> fallback." One slide.

### Slide 3: Put it in Google Sites

> Create one step-by-step slide for a high school design class titled "2. Put It In Google Sites."
> Numbered in large text: "1) Your portfolio, Unit 1.1 page. Make the page if it isn't there. 2) Insert,
> right sidebar. 3) Embed. 4) The EMBED CODE tab, not By URL. 5) Paste, Next, Insert. 6) Drag the corners
> so it's actually big enough to use. 7) PUBLISH, top right." Then a box in very large text: "8) Open your
> published site in a NEW TAB and click through your own prototype. If it doesn't work there, it doesn't
> work." One slide.

### Slide 4: The reflection

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "3. The Reflection." Explain that this is the
> graded part and it goes underneath the embed. A list of what it contains: the app you redesigned and
> the one task, in a sentence or two; before and after screenshots with what was wrong with the original;
> FOUR DECISIONS, each written as what I did, the problem it solved, and how I know it worked; what the
> partner testing changed; what is shown but not actually built; one thing you would do next and why. Add
> a box: "'Two people tried to tap the header, so I gave it a border and a label' is a decision with
> evidence. 'I made it blue because blue is nice' is not." One slide.

### Slide 5: Write about the standards by name

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "Name The Standards. Use The Words, Not The
> Numbers." A four row table. Row 1, "the UX/UI process, vision to wireframe to prototype": walk your
> actual process, you picked a task, sketched on paper first, built, connected, tested, changed it. Row 2,
> "components of interactive media": name the interaction types you used and why you chose those four. Row
> 3, "visual design appropriate for the audience": who is it for, and what did you do because of that,
> type size, contrast, touch targets, color. Row 4, "optimize and export for intended use": what you
> exported and why, PDF to submit, image to display, link to share, and why those are three different
> answers. Add a line in large text: "'I wireframed every screen on paper before building it' beats 'I met
> 2.15.1.'" One slide.

## 2. Video & Sound: Everyone cuts their own

**Documents:** `week-6/handouts/capcut-lab.md` (student-facing, post it),
`week-6/handouts/capcut-first-timeline.md` (Wednesday, still useful)
**Run it as:** demo on the projector first, about 15 minutes, then they work. **Two day lab, Thursday and
Friday.** Level 1 is required, Levels 2 and 3 exist so nobody sits idle.
**Say it twice: this is individual.** Same group footage, separate projects, separate cuts.

### Demo order, on the projector

1. **Storyboard out first.** What was this supposed to be, what order, what you did not get
2. New Project, import only what you might use
3. Drag to timeline. Name the four areas: media pool, timeline, preview, playhead
4. Trim heads and tails. Show one clip before and after
5. Reorder two clips. Ask the room which order is better and why
6. **Title card:** text at the front, about 3 seconds, plain and readable
7. **Credits:** names and real jobs at the end
8. Export 1080p

### Slide 1: Standards today

> Create one standards slide for a high school video production class titled "Today's Standards." List
> these with their FULL text, not just the numbers, in large readable type: 7.3.7 Edit video footage.
> 7.3.2 Generate text for multi-image presentations, including title graphics. 7.7.6 Add transitions
> (dissolves, wipes, cuts), titles, special effects, and digital effects. 7.7.5 Select a linear or
> nonlinear editing system and edit the video. 7.7.8 Export video to the desired medium. Then a short
> line: "Today that means your own cut of your group's footage, with a title and credits." Design it to
> stay readable on the board all period. One slide.

### Slide 2: This one is individual

> Create one slide for a high school video production class. Big title: "Everybody Cuts Their Own." Body
> in large text: "Your group shot it together. You each cut your own version. Same clips, separate
> projects, separate exports. They will not come out the same, and that is the point." Then a short list:
> copy the group footage into your own working folder; do not all open one project; no footage of your
> own yet, get clips from a groupmate today and do the lab anyway. Add a line: "Two days. Thursday and
> Friday." One slide.

### Slide 3: Storyboard first

> Create one slide for a high school video production class titled "Before You Open CapCut." In large
> text: "Get your storyboard out. Actually out, next to you." Then four questions numbered: "1) What was
> this supposed to be, in one sentence? 2) What order were the shots supposed to go in? 3) What did you
> plan that you didn't get? 4) What did you get that you didn't plan?" Add a box: "An edit without the
> storyboard is guessing. The storyboard is a plan somebody made while thinking clearly, which is not the
> state you're in three hours into cutting." One slide.

### Slide 4: Level 1, required

> Create one slide for a high school video production class titled "Level 1: Required." A numbered
> checklist in large text: "1) Import only the clips you might use. 2) Clips on the timeline in the order
> they should be WATCHED, not the order you shot. 3) Trim the dead time off both ends of every clip, close
> every gap. 4) TITLE CARD at the front, about 3 seconds, plain and readable. 5) CREDITS at the end,
> everyone named with the job they actually did, you as editor. 6) Export 1080p to Classroom. 7) Three
> sentences: best cut, worst cut, what would fix it." Add a box: "Target length 45 to 90 seconds, title
> and credits included." One slide.

### Slide 5: Levels 2 and 3, and the rules

> Create one slide for a high school video production class titled "Done? Keep Going." Two columns. Left,
> "LEVEL 2": cut five more seconds out of the middle, anywhere; fix your ugliest cut with a cutaway; hold
> the shot that's working one second longer and the one that isn't one second shorter; fade to black at
> the end; level your audio so cuts don't jump; add a lower third naming a person or place. Right, "LEVEL
> 3": cut a SECOND version in a different order, then say which is better and why; cut it down to 20
> seconds and see what survives; cut to a beat, then watch it muted; log every usable take with timecode.
> Then a bottom bar headed "RULES": own footage only, no filters or effects or auto-anything, no music at
> Level 1, ignore every template. Add a line: "Effects are how people avoid learning to cut. Cutting is
> the whole skill." One slide.

### Board version

```

  7.3.2  generate text for multi-image presentations,
```

## 3. Aviation UAS: The circle drill

**Documents:** `week-6/handouts/circle-drill.md`, `week-6/handouts/box-pattern-flight.md` (the warm-up)
**Run it as:** preflight gate, then **every pilot flies one box first**. A sloppy box means stay on the
box. Then the circle progression. Most will reach step 3 or 4 and that is a fine day.
**Quiz is tomorrow.** Anyone waiting for a turn has the readiness check and the regulations practice set
with them.

### Slide 1: Standards today

> Create one standards slide for a high school aviation and drone class titled "Today's Standards." List
> these with their FULL text, not just the numbers, in large readable type: 7.12 Perform takeoff, flight,
> and landing operations. 2.1 Apply safety practices. 7.13 Evaluate the flight environment, including
> airspace classification and obstructions. Then a short line: "Today that means flying a circle, which
> takes two inputs at once, the whole way around." Design it to stay readable on the board. One slide.

### Slide 2: The gate

> Create one preflight slide for a high school drone class titled "No Preflight, No Flight." A checklist
> in large text: current METAR pulled and read out loud; compared against your personal minimums card; go
> or no-go call made, with the deciding number named; airspace confirmed for this site; preflight walked
> item by item, out loud; roles assigned, Pilot, Visual Observer, Logger; everyone else behind the
> spectator line. Add a box: "A no-go is a real outcome and it gets logged like any other." Then a bottom
> line: "Warm-up: every pilot flies ONE box pattern first. Sloppy box? Stay on the box." One slide.

### Slide 3: The drill

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "The Circle." Show a simple diagram: a marker on
> the ground labeled with an X, a dotted circle around it, and the pilot standing outside the circle.
> Specs in a compact list, large text: altitude 15 feet, same the whole way around; radius about 20 feet;
> NOSE POINTED THE SAME DIRECTION THE ENTIRE CIRCLE, do not yaw; slow, slower than you think; one full lap
> each way. Then a box headed "Three things judged": ROUND, the gap to the marker doesn't change; LEVEL,
> the far side is the same height as the near side; CONTINUOUS, no stopping and restarting. Add a line:
> "A slow round level circle is a pass. A fast lopsided one is not. Nobody is timing you." One slide.

### Slide 4: Orientation, the thing that gets you

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "When It Turns Around, Your Left Is Its Right." In
> large text: "Nose pointed away from you: everything matches. Nose pointed AT you: the stick that moved
> it away now moves it toward you. This is the hardest part of flying a drone and it's why we're doing
> this." Then four short rules: slow down before the far side, mistakes at walking speed are recoverable;
> make small inputs, if you have to fix it you overcorrected; pick a reference and say it out loud, "its
> nose is at the fence, so its right is my left"; when you lose it, LET GO and let it hover, look at the
> nose, then move. Add a box: "Letting go and hovering is a correct response. It is not giving up.
> Panicking and pushing a stick is what puts a drone in a tree." One slide.

### Board version

```
  2.1   apply safety practices
  7.13  evaluate the flight environment, including airspace
        classification and obstructions
  -> today: a circle. two inputs at once, the whole way around

NO PREFLIGHT, NO FLIGHT
  METAR read aloud / vs personal minimums / go-no-go with the
  deciding number / airspace confirmed / preflight aloud
  roles: PILOT / VISUAL OBSERVER / LOGGER
  everyone else behind the line.   a NO-GO is a real outcome.

  15 ft, same all the way around
  ~20 ft radius
  NOSE FIXED. do not yaw.
  slow. one lap each direction.

  judged on:  ROUND  /  LEVEL  /  CONTINUOUS
  a slow round level circle is a pass. nobody is timing you.

  slow down before the far side
  small inputs. if you're fixing it, you overcorrected.
  say it out loud: "nose at the fence, its right is my left"
  LOST IT? LET GO. let it hover. find the nose. then move.

       5 both, no landing / 6 nose-in (hard, only if 5 is easy)
```

## 4. Middle School CS: Last quiz prep, then finish

**Documents:** `week-6/handouts/bell-ringers.md` (Thursday set, teacher reference),
`week-6/handouts/wave-defender-lab.md` (Day 4: extensions)
**Run it as:** two or three of the six prep questions, whichever Wednesday showed were weakest, then the
four sentences out loud, then lab work. **Quiz is tomorrow.**

### Slide 1: Bell ringer

> Create one bell ringer slide for a middle school computer science class. Big title: "Bell Ringer: Last
> Quiz Prep." Leave a large open area where the teacher pastes two or three block-style code questions in
> monospace text. Subtitle: "The quiz is tomorrow. These are the exact shapes you will see." Small line at
> the bottom: "Not graded. Getting it wrong right now is free." One slide.

### Slide 2: The four things

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "Four Things To Know Cold." Four big
> numbered boxes, each one short: 1, "for index from 0 to N runs N+1 times. Count the zero." 2, "A loop
> inside a loop MULTIPLIES. Inside means it runs all the way through, every time." 3, "remainder = 0 means
> it divides evenly. That's how you do every Nth time." 4, "while asks a question every time around.
> forever never asks. A while needs something that makes the answer become no." Large and memorable.
> One slide.

### Slide 3: Finish the game

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "Today: Finish It." Numbered: "1)
> Does your game have a way to WIN and a way to LOSE? If not, that's today. 2) Does the score work? 3)
> Pick ONE extension and add it." Then a short list of extension ideas: a second wave that's faster; a
> power-up that gives an extra life; enemies that shoot back; a high score that carries over. Add a box:
> "If something is broken, fix that before you add anything. A working small game beats a broken big one."
> One slide.
