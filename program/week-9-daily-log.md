**Aviation: the No-Go Hunt, on real reports.** The problem it answers: weeks of good local weather have
trained them to say "it's fine, let's go." **They spend the week finding real reasons to cancel**, in
**real reports, on aviationweather.gov and tfr.faa.gov**, so they are also learning to navigate the
actual tools. The GFA map's colored dots are the search tool: **hunt in the red and purple**, and if the
lower 48 are all green, Alaska, the Pacific Northwest coast or the mountains.

**Nine reasons to cancel**, each with what it looks like and the rule it breaks: wind over their limit,
visibility under 3 miles, cloud too low (the **500 foot rule**, so the lowest layer minus 500 is their
ceiling), thunderstorms (20 miles), precipitation, fog or a small spread, freezing, a TAF that turns bad
inside the flight window, and a TFR. Monday is gusts and low cloud, Tuesday precipitation, storms and
fog, Wednesday the sneaky ones (a TAF that turns, a real TFR, and **a nice-looking report with exactly
one reason to cancel**). **Thursday they run every row on the real KCMH report** and fly only if every
row passes. **Question 10, checking every row on a day that looks fine, is the whole skill.**

**The first draft used four written practice reports telling a cold-front story.** Replaced by request,
because finding real ones on the real tools is worth more. **The written reports are kept in the teacher
notes as a backup** for a category that genuinely cannot be found anywhere, most likely thunderstorms in
mid-October, with the rule that a documented empty search is legitimate and an invented report is not.
