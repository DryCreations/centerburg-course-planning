> partner test and final fixes. FRIDAY, write-up, post to your portfolio, submit. Add a box in large text:
> "WEDNESDAY is the real deadline. Everything connected both ways. Thursday is for fixing, not building." One
> slide.

### Slide 5: Do Now, after the quiz

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school design class. Big title: "After the Quiz: Build." Numbered: "1)
> Open your Figma file. 2) Count your screens. You should have most of them. 3) Anything half built, finish
> it. 4) Check the back button is in the same corner on every screen. 5) Check every screen is actually
> reachable from somewhere." Add a box: "Ahead of that? Start your write-up now. Four decisions, each with the

## 2. Video & Sound: Quiz, then plans

**Documents:** `week-6/quiz-bank.csv` (62 questions, cut to 20), `week-5/handouts/reset-and-replan.md`
**Run it as:** quiz first, then new groups work their plans. Cleared groups shoot.

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school video class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body: "Quiz
> first, on Google Classroom. Silent, stay on the quiz tab, not open note. Everything on it came from the
> reading worksheet and the camera work. When you submit, sit quietly and get your group's plan out, people
> are still testing." Large, clean. One slide.

### Slide 2: Standards today

> Create one standards slide for a high school video class titled "Today's Standards." List these with their
> FULL text, not just the numbers, in large readable type: 7.7.2 Analyze the script and storyboard to develop
> a production schedule. 7.3.1 Apply pre-production processes. 2.10 Operate equipment according to
> manufacturer specifications. Then a short line: "Today that means the quiz, then your new group's plan and
> schedule." Design it to stay readable on the board all period. One slide.

### Slide 3: What is on the quiz

> Create one slide for a high school video class titled "What Is On It." Three columns. CAMERA: manual
> exposure, the 180 degree rule and the shutter speed for 24 and 30 fps, why autofocus is off, why white
> balance is on a preset, the five things that cannot be fixed, ISO and noise, room tone. PREPRODUCTION: scene
> headings, filmable action, script versus storyboard versus shot list, call sheets, shooting order, coverage,
> continuity, shoot blocks and time estimates. TRANSITIONS AND FRAMING: cut, dissolve, fade, wipe, rule of
> thirds, headroom, lead room, leading lines. Add a box: "If you did the worksheet, you have seen all of
> this." One slide.

### Slide 4: Do Now, after the quiz

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school video class. Big title: "After the Quiz." Body: "Get with your
> new group, quietly, and work your plan." Then a checklist: whose script you are using; how many shots and
> how many locations; which shots can be filmed in THIS ROOM; jobs assigned by name for every shoot; a
> realistic schedule, with each block estimated and then padded by half again. Add a box: "Earning it back is
> one full period run correctly. That starts now. Worked when told, stayed in the room, stayed quiet, followed
> your own schedule, equipment signed out and back, packed up five minutes early." One slide.

## 3. Aviation UAS: Read a whole area

**Documents:** `week-5/handouts/chart-symbols.md`, `week-5/handouts/sectional-reading.md`

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school drone class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body: "Open
> your chart viewer and the legend. Warm-up: find one obstruction and write down both of its numbers. Which
> one is the one that matters to you?" Large, clean. One slide.

### Slide 2: Standards today

> Create one standards slide for a high school drone class titled "Today's Standards." List these with their
> FULL text, not just the numbers, in large readable type: 7.13 Evaluate the flight environment, including
> airspace classification and obstructions. 7.9 Identify small UAS rules and operating limitations. Then a
> short line: "Today that means reading a whole area of a sectional chart, not one symbol at a time." Design
> it to stay readable on the board all period. One slide.

### Slide 3: The week

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "This Week." A day plan: MONDAY, review the symbols,
> then read a whole area. TUESDAY, plan a flight from the chart. WEDNESDAY, work time, sectionals or ODOT.
> THURSDAY, FLIGHT DAY, the box pattern, and ODOT Module 1 is due tonight. FRIDAY, QUIZ. Add a box in large
> text: "The quiz covers airspace, sectional charts, Part 107 rules, ODOT Module 1, and weather. Module 1 is
> due Thursday night so you can take it Friday." One slide.

### Slide 4: Symbol review

> Create one review slide for a high school drone class titled "Say These Without Looking." A compact
> reference: solid blue is Class B; solid magenta is Class C; dashed blue is Class D; dashed magenta is Class
> E at the surface; no ring is Class G. Stacked numbers: top is the ceiling, bottom is the floor, in hundreds
> of feet, and SFC means it goes to the ground. Obstructions: the top number is above sea level, the number in
> parentheses is above the ground and that one is yours. Airports: magenta means no tower, blue means tower.
> One slide, dense but readable.

### Slide 5: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school drone class. Big title: "Do Now: Read a Whole Area." Body: "Pick
> an area of the chart around one airport, big enough to see several things at once. Then answer, in writing,
> for that area:" Numbered: "1) What airspace classes are present, and how do you know? 2) Where does each one
> start and stop, in feet? 3) How many airports, and which have towers? 4) What is the tallest obstruction, and
> how tall AGL? 5) Pick one spot in that area and say: could I fly here, to what altitude, and what would I
> need first?" Add a box: "Question 5 is the one that matters. Everything else is how you get there." One
> slide.

### Board version

```
STANDARDS TODAY (leave on the board)
  7.13 evaluate the flight environment, including
       airspace classification and obstructions
  7.9  identify small UAS rules and operating limitations
  -> today: read a whole AREA, not one symbol at a time

THIS WEEK  Mon read an area / Tue plan a flight
           Wed work time / Thu FLY (box pattern)
           Fri QUIZ.   ODOT Module 1 DUE THU NIGHT.

WITHOUT LOOKING:
  solid BLUE = B      solid MAGENTA = C
  dashed BLUE = D     dashed MAGENTA = E at surface
  no ring = G
   70 / 25  -> top = ceiling, bottom = floor, hundreds of ft
   SFC on the bottom = goes to the ground
   1549 / (451) -> parentheses = AGL = YOURS
   magenta airport = no tower   blue = tower

READ AN AREA: which classes, where each starts and stops,
  how many airports, tallest obstruction, and then:
  COULD I FLY HERE, TO WHAT ALTITUDE, WHAT DO I NEED FIRST?
```

## 4. Middle School CS: A new game

**Documents:** `week-6/handouts/wave-defender-lab.md`, `week-6/project.md`
**Run it as:** one new block today, the projectile. Everything else they already have.

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a middle school computer science class. Big title: "As You Come In."
> Body: "Open MakeCode Arcade and start a NEW project. Name it Wave Defender. Your maze and your catcher stay
> where they are, this is something different: a game where you shoot back." Large, clean, minimal. One slide.

### Slide 2: This week

> Create one agenda slide for a middle school computer science class titled "This Week: Wave Defender." A day
> plan: MONDAY, a ship that moves and shoots. TUESDAY, waves of enemies built with loops. WEDNESDAY, hits,
> misses, and ending the game. THURSDAY, make it yours and show it. FRIDAY, quiz on loops and if statements.
> Add a box in large text: "Only ONE new block all week. Everything else is the ifs, loops, variables, and
> overlaps you already know." One slide.

### Slide 3: The game

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "What You Are Making." Show a simple
> text diagram: a row of asterisks near the top labeled "a wave, spawned with a loop", a vertical line and a
> caret below it labeled "your shot", and a small shape at the bottom labeled "your ship". Then body text: "A
> ship at the bottom. Meteors come down in formations. Shoot them and they break. Let one reach the bottom
> and you lose a life." Add a box: "You have built a maze and a catcher. In both of those, things move and
> you touch them. In this one you act on the world." One slide.

### Slide 4: Shooting

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "The One New Block." Show in a monospace
> box: "on A button pressed:" then indented "projectile from ship with vx 0 vy -100". Underneath, read it out:
> "Make a projectile starting at the ship, moving 0 sideways and -100 up." Add a box in large text: "Negative
> vy is UP. Y counts downward from the top of the screen. If your shots fall instead of rising, that is the
> sign." One slide.

### Slide 5: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a middle school computer science class. Big title: "Do Now: Steps 1 to 3."
> Body: "Follow the numbered lab on Classroom." Numbered: "1) Make a ship, add 'move ship with buttons', set
> its y to 110, and turn on 'stay in screen'. 2) Add 'on A button pressed' with a projectile going up. 3)
> Make it feel right: a small image for the shot, try different speeds, add a sound." Add a box: "Goal for
> today: a ship that moves along the bottom, and pressing A fires a shot that travels UP. Finished? Try
> different projectile speeds and pick the one that feels best." One slide.

## 5. Yearbook: The two week assignment

### Slide 1: The assignment

> Create one slide for a high school yearbook class titled "Two Weeks. Go Get Real Photos." Body: "New
> assignment, posted today, due FRIDAY OCTOBER 2." Then the requirements: at least 20 USABLE frames, from at
> least TWO different events or subjects; usable means in focus, correctly exposed, and something is actually
> happening; named and offloaded to your folder; plus your PDF check-in of whatever spread you are working.
> Add a box in large text: "Twenty frames is not twenty attempts. Shoot far more and turn in the ones that
> work." One slide.

### Slide 2: How to get a camera (leave this up)

> Create one slide for a high school yearbook class titled "Three Ways to Get a Camera." Three blocks. DURING
> CLASS: sign out, shoot, return before the bell. ALL DAY: sign it out in the morning or during our class,
> carry it through the day, and return it at the end of the day, after 9th period. AFTER SCHOOL: sign up for
> an event, take it, return it the same day. Then a rules box in large text: "Sign it out and sign it back in,
> every time. It comes back the SAME DAY. It stays with you, not in a locker or a car. Lens cap on when you
> are not shooting. Strap on when it is out of the bag. Offload before you return it. If something happens to
> it, tell me immediately." Add at the bottom: "The all-day option is what makes this work. If there is a game
> at 4:00, sign the camera out and keep it with you." One slide, designed to stay on the board.
