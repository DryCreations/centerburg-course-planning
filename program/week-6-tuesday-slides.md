## 1. Design Techniques: Peer review, then input

**Documents:** `week-6/handouts/peer-review.md` (student-facing, post it),
`week-5/handouts/interaction-labs.md` (Lab 6: Input), `week-5/project.md` (due Friday)
**Run it as:** ten minutes to get files runnable, then peer review in pairs, then the rest of the period
is build time with Lab 6 available for anyone whose task involves typing.

### Slide 1: Standards today

> Create one standards slide for a high school design class titled "Today's Standards." List these with
> their FULL text, not just the numbers, in large readable type: 2.15.1 Understand the UX/UI design
> process (vision, journey mapping, wireframing, prototyping) for the targeted platform. 7.1.2 Describe
> the components of interactive media. 7.2.10 Select and create visual design elements appropriate for the
> intended audience and use. Then a short line: "Today that means a real user test on someone else's
> prototype, and text input." Design it to stay readable on the board all period. One slide.

### Slide 2: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school design class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body:
> "Open your Figma file. Ten minutes to make it runnable: Present opens on your home screen, frames are
> named something real, every screen has a way back, share link set to Anyone with the link can view.
> Then write your task sentence at the top of your file: 'Starting on this screen, ___.' One task. One
> sentence." Large and clean. One slide.

### Slide 3: How the test runs

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "Peer Review: The Rules." Two columns. Left
> column headed "IF IT IS YOUR FILE": read your task sentence out loud, once; then stop talking; do not
> point; do not say which button; write down every place they hesitate; if they are stuck more than 30
> seconds, say stop and write down where. Right column headed "IF YOU ARE TESTING": say what you are
> thinking out loud; tap what you actually believe you should tap; be honest and specific; "it's good" is
> not feedback. Add a box across the bottom in large text: "When I ___, I expected ___, but ___." One
> slide.

### Slide 4: After the test

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "Then What." Numbered: "1) Fill out the review
> sheet for the file you tested and submit it on Google Classroom. 2) Back in your own file, go through
> every piece of feedback and write either CHANGED IT and what, or NOT CHANGING IT BECAUSE and a real
> reason. 3) Keep that list, it goes in Friday's write-up. 4) Then build." Add a box: "If two different
> testers got stuck in the same place, that is not an opinion any more. Fix it." Add a small line:
> "Typing in your app? Do Lab 6: Input." One slide.

### Board version


## 2. Video & Sound: Filming restarts, in this room

**Documents:** `week-6/handouts/filming-signup.md` (student-facing, post it),
`week-5/handouts/reset-and-replan.md`, `week-5/handouts/camera-video-settings.md`
**Run it as:** sign-ups on the board in the first five minutes, then three filming slots run back to back.
Everyone not in the current slot is working on shot lists, scripts, or logging.

### Slide 1: Standards today

> Create one standards slide for a high school video production class titled "Today's Standards." List
> these with their FULL text, not just the numbers, in large readable type: 7.7 Apply video production
> techniques. 7.7.2 Analyze the script and storyboard to develop a production schedule. 2.10 Operate
> equipment according to manufacturer specifications. 7.3.1 Apply pre-production processes. Then a short
> line: "Today that means filming in this room, on a schedule you signed up for." Design it to stay
> readable on the board all period. One slide.

### Slide 2: Sign-ups

> Create one slide for a high school video production class titled "Sign Up For A Slot." Explain: three
> cameras shoot manual video, A is the T4i, B is the T5, C is the T6. The period splits into three
> fifteen-minute slots. Write your group name in one box. Rules as a short list: one slot per group per
> day until everyone has had one; sign up for a slot you can actually be ready for; a group that is not
> ready loses the slot; trades have to be told to me before, not after; say at sign-up if your shot needs
> a quiet room. Add a small note: "The T3 is stills and second angles only. It does not do manual video."
> One slide.

### Slide 3: Ready means ready

> Create one slide for a high school video production class titled "Ready Means Ready." Subtitle: "Setup
> is your time. The clock starts when your slot starts." A checklist in large text: shot list printed or
> on a screen, in shooting order; anyone who is on camera is actually in the room; props already here;
> your corner of the room cleared; settings written down, 1/60 shutter, 30fps, manual, white balance set;
> someone holding the shot list calling the next shot. Add a box: "In a group of two, whoever is not
> holding the camera is holding the shot list. There is no job called watching." One slide.

### Slide 4: While someone else is recording

> Create one slide for a high school video production class titled "While Another Group Is Recording."
> In very large text: "SILENT." Then, smaller: "Not quieter. Silent. A microphone picks up your
> conversation from across the room." Then a short list: stay out of the frame; no walking behind the
> camera during a take; work on something, shot lists, scripts, storyboards, logging. Then a bottom
> section headed "Before you leave today": offload your footage, name files like scene2_shot3_take1, log
> what you got and what you still need, camera back five minutes before the bell. Add a line: "This is
> the period that gets groups cleared to shoot outside this room." One slide.

## 3. Aviation UAS: Coordinate bell ringer, then chart work

**Documents:** `week-6/handouts/coordinate-bell-ringers.md` (teacher reference, do not post),
`week-5/handouts/sectional-reading.md`, `week-5/handouts/airspace.md`, ODOT Module 1
**Run it as:** the bell ringer on the board as they come in, about ten minutes including going over it,
then work time. ODOT Module 1 is due Thursday night.

### Slide 1: Standards today

> Create one standards slide for a high school aviation and drone class titled "Today's Standards." List
> these with their FULL text, not just the numbers, in large readable type: 7.13 Evaluate the flight
> environment, including airspace classification and obstructions. 7.9 Identify small UAS rules and
> operating limitations. 2.1 Apply safety practices. Then a short line: "Today that means finding a set
> of coordinates on a chart and saying what airspace you are standing in, and how high it goes." Design it
> to stay readable on the board all period. One slide.

### Slide 2: Bell ringer

> Create one bell ringer slide for a high school aviation class. Big title: "Bell Ringer." Show a
> latitude and longitude in very large monospace text, with a blank space for the teacher to fill in the
> actual numbers. Then numbered: "1) Find it on SkyVector. 2) What airspace class are you in at ground
> level? 3) What is its FLOOR and its CEILING?" Then a box at the bottom: "Finished? Do every layer above
> that spot. Floor and ceiling for each one." Add a small line: "skyvector.com, paste the coordinates in
> the search box." Large and readable. One slide.

### Slide 3: How to answer it

> Create one slide for a high school aviation class titled "Saying It Right." Explain in large text that
> an airspace answer has three parts: the CLASS, the FLOOR, the CEILING. Give a worked example laid out as
> a short table: at this spot, Class G from the surface up to 700 feet, then Class E from 700 up to 4,000,
> then Class C from 4,000 up to 7,000. Then a short list headed "Things that go wrong": naming the class
> above you instead of the one you are standing in; reading 25 as 25 feet when it means 2,500; forgetting
> that Class G is a real answer; mixing up AGL and MSL, parentheses mean AGL. One slide.

### Slide 4: Work time

> Create one work-time slide for a high school aviation and drone class titled "Rest Of The Period."
> Two boxes. Box one, "SECTIONAL CHARTS": keep working the reading practice, this is a lookup skill, not
> a memory test, the chart is allowed to be in front of you. Box two, "ODOT MODULE 1": due Thursday
> night, before Friday's quiz. Add a line at the bottom: "Thursday we fly the box pattern. Friday is the
> quiz." One slide.

## 4. Middle School CS: Bell ringer, then waves

**Documents:** `week-6/handouts/bell-ringers.md` (teacher reference, do not post),
`week-6/handouts/wave-defender-lab.md` (Day 2: waves)
**Run it as:** bell ringer on the board, three to five minutes plus going over it, then the rest of the
period is lab work. No new blocks today beyond nesting loops they already have.

### Slide 1: Bell ringer

> Create one bell ringer slide for a middle school computer science class. Big title: "Bell Ringer." Show
> this as indented block-style pseudocode in large monospace text: "repeat 3 times" then indented "repeat
> 6 times" then indented further "set mySprite to sprite of kind Enemy". Below it the question: "How many
> enemy sprites does this make?" Then a box: "Finished? Change ONE number so it makes 24 instead." Do not
> show the answer. Large and readable. One slide.

### Slide 2: Today's job

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "Today: Waves." Explain the goal in
> plain language: instead of placing eighteen aliens by hand, use one loop inside another loop to place
> them all, rows and columns. Numbered steps: "1) One repeat loop for the row. 2) Put it inside another
> repeat loop for how many rows. 3) Use the loop's index to set the x and y position so they don't stack
> on top of each other. 4) Run it. Count them." Add a box: "Six blocks should make eighteen aliens. If you
> are dragging out eighteen sprites by hand, stop and ask." One slide.

### Slide 3: When it goes wrong

> Create one troubleshooting slide for a middle school computer science class titled "If It Breaks." A
> two-column table. Left column "WHAT YOU SEE", right column "WHAT IT PROBABLY IS". Rows: all the aliens
> are stacked in one spot, you are not using the index to change the position; only one row appears, the
> inner loop is not actually inside the outer one, check the indent; the game is crawling, you made way
> more sprites than you meant to, check your numbers; nothing appears, the sprite kind is wrong or the
> loop never runs. Add a line: "Before you ask, say out loud what you expected to happen and what
> happened instead." One slide.

### Board version

```
BELL RINGER
  repeat 3 times
    repeat 6 times
      set mySprite to sprite of kind Enemy

  how many enemy sprites?
  done? change ONE number so it makes 24

TODAY: WAVES
  1. a repeat loop for one ROW
  2. put it inside a repeat loop for how many ROWS
  3. use the index to set x and y so they don't stack
  4. run it, count them
  6 blocks -> 18 aliens. dragging out 18 by hand? stop, ask.

IF IT BREAKS
  all stacked in one spot -> not using the index for position
  only one row -> inner loop isn't actually inside, check indent
  crawling -> too many sprites, check your numbers
  nothing appears -> wrong sprite kind, or the loop never runs
```
