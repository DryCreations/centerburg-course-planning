## 1. Design Techniques: Wireframe every screen

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school design class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body: "Get
> out your sketches and your ONE TASK sentence from yesterday. Read the sentence again. Today you draw every
> screen that sentence needs." Large, clean. One slide.

### Slide 2: Lab: Overlay

> Create one instructional slide for a high school design class titled "Ten Minute Lab: Overlay." Body: "An
> overlay sits ON TOP of a screen instead of replacing it. Confirmations, menus, popups. In a scratch file."
> Numbered: 1) Duplicate a frame. 2) On the copy, draw a rectangle covering the whole screen, fill it black,
> set opacity to 40 percent. 3) On top of that, a smaller white rounded rectangle with text and two buttons,
> Cancel and Confirm. 4) Connect a button on the original to this frame. 5) Connect Cancel back. Add a box:
> "The dim is the whole trick. It says the screen is still there, you are just on top of it." One slide.

### Slide 3: What every wireframe needs

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "Boxes and Labels. That Is All." A checklist: a
> labeled box for every element; ONE clear primary action, bigger or bolder than everything else, and circle
> it; a deliberate reading order, number your boxes 1, 2, 3; grouping, related things close together and
> unrelated things apart; navigation and the back button in the SAME place on every screen. Add: "No color. No
> real images. No fonts. If you are picking a color you are doing the wrong step." One slide.

### Slide 4: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school design class. Big title: "Do Now: Draw Every Screen." Numbered:
> "1) One wireframe per screen, on paper. 2) Number the boxes in reading order. 3) Circle the primary action
> on each. 4) Put the back button in the same corner every time. 5) Lay them out in order and walk your own
> task through them: can you actually do the thing?" Add a box: "Then hand them to a partner without saying
> what the app is. Ask them what it does and where they would tap first. Wrong guess means the hierarchy is
> not working yet." One slide.

## 2. Video & Sound: Scene breakdown and drafting

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school video class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body: "Get out
> your idea and yesterday's format drill. Warm-up question: how many different PLACES does your video happen
> in? Write the number down." Large, clean. One slide.

### Slide 2: Today

> Create one agenda slide for a high school video class titled "Today: Scenes, Then Pages." Agenda: 1) What
> counts as a new scene, 2) Write your scene list, 3) Draft. Add a box in large text: "Your script LOCKS
> tomorrow. We start filming Thursday, so today is a real writing day." Large readable text. One slide.

### Slide 3: When you need a new scene heading

> Create one slide for a high school video class titled "When Do You Need a New Scene Heading?" Two columns.
> Left, "YES, new heading": the location changes; the time changes, morning to night; you cut somewhere else
> entirely. Right, "NO, same scene": a different camera angle; a new character walks in; the conversation
> shifts; a close up instead of a wide. Caption: "Pick ONE name for each location and reuse it exactly.
> HALLWAY, then HALL, then MAIN HALL reads as three different places to your crew and your schedule." One
> slide.

### Slide 4: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school video class. Big title: "Do Now: Scene List, Then Pages."
> Numbered: "1) List every scene heading your video needs, in order, correctly formatted. 2) Count them. Two
> or three is normal for a 2 to 3 page script. 3) Next to each, one line: what HAPPENS here. 4) Then write."
> Add a box titled "Stuck?": "Read your last line out loud and ask what physically happens next. Write that.
> Not what someone feels, what they DO." And: "Only one scene? Maybe fine, maybe you are missing a moment.
> Fifteen scenes? You are writing a feature. Cut." One slide.

## 3. Aviation UAS: Sky, temperature, and weather codes

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school drone class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body: "Pull
> today's METAR for our field. Decode the wind and the visibility out loud to the person next to you, no
> handout. Then look at the part you could not read yesterday, the letters and numbers in the middle. That is
> today." Large, clean. One slide.

### Slide 2: Sky condition

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "How Much Sky, and How High." A table, the code and
> the coverage: SKC or CLR is clear; FEW is 1 to 2 eighths; SCT is scattered, 3 to 4 eighths; BKN is broken, 5
> to 7 eighths; OVC is overcast, all 8. Then in large text: "The three digits after are the height in HUNDREDS
> of feet. OVC007 is 700 feet. SCT045 is 4,500 feet. ADD TWO ZEROS." Add a box: "BKN or OVC is a CEILING, the
> lowest layer you cannot see through. FEW and SCT are not." One slide.

### Slide 3: Temperature and dew point

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "24/13." Body: "Temperature over dew point, always
> Celsius. A minus sign shows as M, so M02/M05 is minus 2 over minus 5." Then in a box in large text: "When
> the two numbers get CLOSE, expect fog or mist. A METAR reading 18/17 is one degree apart and is telling you
> the air is nearly saturated. That is a visibility problem about to happen." One slide.

### Slide 4: Weather codes

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "The Letters in the Middle." A two column table: RA is
> rain; SN is snow; BR is mist; FG is fog; HZ is haze; TS is thunderstorm; DZ is drizzle. Then: "Intensity
> goes in front. Minus is light, plus is heavy, nothing means moderate. So -RA is light rain and +TSRA is a
> heavy thunderstorm with rain." Add a box: "VC means in the vicinity. VCTS is a thunderstorm nearby but not
> overhead. Treat that as a no-go anyway." One slide.

### Slide 5: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school drone class. Big title: "Do Now: Decode Drill 2." Body: "Pull
> three live METARs. This time decode EVERY field: station, time in Zulu and local, wind, visibility, every
> cloud layer with its real height in feet, temperature and dew point, any weather codes, and the altimeter.
> For each, write one sentence: what is the most important thing this METAR tells a drone pilot?" Add a box:
> "Finished? Find a station right now reporting a ceiling below 1,000 feet and work out what that means for
> us." And a reminder: "ODOT Module 1 due Thursday the 24th. Start it." One slide.

## 4. Middle School CS: `for index` and modulo

**Run it as:** content first, both concepts with example problems, then lab steps 6 to 9.

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a middle school computer science class. Big title: "As You Come In."
> Body: "Open Star Catcher and run it. Your ten stars should all appear in the same instant. That is correct.
> Today we learn a loop that can tell them apart." Large, clean, minimal. One slide.

### Slide 2: repeat cannot count

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "repeat Has a Problem." Body in large
> text: "A repeat loop runs your code ten times, but it has NO IDEA which time it is on. Every pass is
> identical. So if you want ten stars in ten different places, repeat cannot help you. You need a loop that
> counts." One slide.

### Slide 3: for index

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "for index: The Counting Loop." Show in a
> monospace box: "for index from 0 to 4:" then indented "set star to sprite of kind Food" and "set star
> position to x (20 + index * 30), y 0". Underneath, a trace table with columns Pass, index, and the x value:
> pass 1 index 0 gives 20; pass 2 index 1 gives 50; pass 3 index 2 gives 80; pass 4 index 3 gives 110; pass 5
> index 4 gives 140. Add a box in large text: "for index from 0 to 4 runs FIVE times. It counts 0, 1, 2, 3, 4.
> This catches everybody once." One slide.

### Slide 4: Modulo, the remainder block

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "remainder: What Is Left Over." Body: "In
> the Math category there is a block: remainder of ( ) divided by ( ). It gives you what is LEFT OVER after
> dividing." Then a small table: remainder of 7 ÷ 3 is 1, because 3 goes into 7 twice with 1 left; remainder of
> 12 ÷ 5 is 2; remainder of 10 ÷ 5 is 0, it divides evenly; remainder of 9 ÷ 3 is 0. One slide, large readable
> text.

### Slide 5: Every Nth

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "That Is How You Do Every Nth." Body in
> large text: "remainder of N ÷ 5 = 0 is true EXACTLY when N is a multiple of 5. So it is true at 0, 5, 10,
> 15, 20." Then a table: every 2nd means remainder of N ÷ 2 = 0; every 3rd means ÷ 3 = 0; every 5th means ÷ 5
> = 0; every 10th means ÷ 10 = 0. Add a box: "Common mistake: writing '= 5' instead of '= 0'. A remainder can
> never equal the number you divided by." One slide.

*(Quick problems to ask aloud: what is remainder of 14 ÷ 4? (2) For which of 1 to 10 is remainder of N ÷ 3 =
0? (3, 6, 9) What condition gives a bonus every 4th star? (remainder of score ÷ 4 = 0))*

### Slide 6: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a middle school computer science class. Big title: "Do Now: Steps 6 to 9."
> Body: "In the lab on Classroom: 1) Replace your repeat with 'for index from 0 to 4'. 2) Set the star's x to
> 20 plus index times 30, so you get an even row. 3) Set its vy to 30 plus index times 10, so each one falls
> faster than the last. 4) Add a starCount variable and an if using 'remainder of starCount ÷ 5 = 0' to make
> every fifth star a fast, different-colored one." Add a box: "Finished? Make a row that goes DOWN the side
> instead of across the top. What do you change?" One slide.

## 5. Yearbook: Rotation and work

### Slide 1: Today (the only slide, leave it up)

> Create one work-time slide for a high school yearbook class. Big title: "Today." Top, a compact reminder of
> the four shot set due Friday: 1 FROZEN at 1/500+; 2 PANNING INDOORS at about 1/60; 3 WATER FROZEN at
