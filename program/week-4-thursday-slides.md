## 1. Design Techniques: UX/UI vocab, then into Figma

**Today's documents:** `courses/design-techniques/planning/week-4/handouts/figma-intro-build.md`, `vocab.md`
**Post:** the UX/UI half of the vocab, and the build handout (it is written so absent students can catch up
alone).
**Run it as:** short vocab pass, then **build alongside them on the projector, one step at a time.** Step,
wait, look around, next step. Do not demo the whole thing and set them loose.

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school design class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body: "Open
> a browser and go to figma.com, but do not log in yet. Warm-up question to think about, we will discuss it in
> a minute: what is the last app or website that confused you, and what specifically made it hard to use?"
> Large, clean. One slide.

### Slide 2: Standards + Today

> Create one standards and agenda slide for a high school design class titled "Today: Meet Figma." List this
> standard with its full text: 2.15.1 Understand the UX/UI design process (vision, journey mapping,
> wireframing, prototyping) for the targeted platform. Then a short agenda: 1) UX vs UI, wireframes and
> prototypes, 2) Get into Figma, 3) Build a phone screen together, step by step. Add a box: "This is a LAB,
> graded on completion. You are learning the tool, not being judged on design." Large readable text. One
> slide.

### Slide 3: UX vs UI

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "UX vs UI." Two large definitions side by side.
> Left: "UX, user experience: how it FEELS to use something. Is it clear? Can I find what I need?" Right:
> "UI, user interface: the actual screens, buttons, menus, and visuals you interact with." Underneath in
> large text: "Good UI with bad UX is a beautiful screen nobody can use." One slide.

*(Turn and Talk here, 3 minutes: from the warm-up, was the app that confused you a UX problem or a UI
problem? Take three or four out loud. This also seeds next week's project, so note who names a good target.)*

### Slide 4: Wireframe and prototype

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "Two Words You Need." Two large boxes. Box 1,
> WIREFRAME: "A low-detail sketch of a screen's structure. Boxes and labels. No colors, no real images. It
> answers WHERE things go." Box 2, PROTOTYPE: "A rough, connected version you can actually click through. It
> answers DOES THIS WORK before anybody builds it for real." Caption underneath: "Today you build a screen.
> Tomorrow you make it click. That is a prototype." One slide.

### Slide 5: Signing in

> Create one instructional slide for a high school design class titled "Get Into Figma." Large numbered list
> with plenty of space: 1) Go to figma.com. 2) Click Log in. 3) Click "Continue with Google." 4) Use your
> SCHOOL account, the same one as Classroom. 5) If it offers a tour or asks what you will use Figma for, SKIP
> it. 6) Click "New design file." 7) Top left, rename the file to "Figma Intro" plus your name. Add a box at
> the bottom: "There is no save button. Figma saves as you go." One slide, large and legible from the back of
> the room.

### Slide 6: What you are looking at

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "The Four Panels." Present as a labeled list: TOP
> MIDDLE is the toolbar, frame, shapes, and text. LEFT is the layers list, everything you have made. MIDDLE is
> the canvas, infinite space. RIGHT is properties, the size, position, and color of whatever you have
> selected. Add underneath: "Zoom: Ctrl and scroll. Fit everything on screen: Shift + 1. Pan: hold space and
> drag. Undo: Ctrl + Z, use it constantly, you cannot break anything." One slide, large readable text.

### Slide 7: The five tools

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "Five Tools. That Is All." Show a two column table
> with the keyboard shortcut in bold. V: Move, select and drag things, your default. F: Frame, makes a screen,
> everything lives inside one. R: Rectangle, for bars, buttons, and image placeholders. O: Ellipse, for
> circles. T: Text, click once then type. Add two habits in a box underneath: "After you draw something, press
> V to go back to the move tool. Hold Shift while drawing to keep squares square and circles circular." One
> slide.

### Slide 8: Type your numbers, do not eyeball it

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "Stop Dragging. Start Typing." Body: "Select
> anything and look at the RIGHT panel. You will see W and H for width and height, and X and Y for position. X
> is how far from the left edge of the frame. Y is how far from the top. You can TYPE numbers into those
> boxes. That is how you match the spec exactly instead of dragging until it looks about right. This is the
> single thing that will save you the most frustration today." One slide, large text.

### Slide 9: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school design class. Big title: "Do Now: Build the Player Screen."
> Body: "Follow along with me, step by step, using the handout in Google Classroom. We are building a phone
> screen for a music player from a written spec: background, header, album art, song title, progress bar, and
> three buttons. Every number is in the handout. Match it as closely as you can." Add a box at the bottom:
> "Finished? Restyle it in your own colors, or start Part 4 and get a day ahead. Absent today? The handout has
> everything, you can catch up on your own." Large, clean. One slide.

*(When they finish the frame, point at the play button: it is bigger and it is the only bright color on a
gray screen, so your eye goes straight to it. That is hierarchy from last week, doing its job. Ninety seconds,
no lecture.)*

### Slide 2: Standards + Today

> Then a short agenda: 1) The three documents and how they differ, 2) Pick your one idea, 3) Write your script

### Slide 3: Three documents, three jobs

> Create one slide for a high school video class titled "Three Documents, Three Jobs." Show a three column
> table. Columns: Document, Answers, Looks like. Row 1: SCRIPT, what is SAID and what HAPPENS, text in
> scenes. Row 2: STORYBOARD, what each shot LOOKS like, drawings one box per shot. Row 3: SHOT LIST, every

> script is the words. The storyboard is the pictures. The shot list is the plan." One slide.

*(Turn and Talk, 3 minutes: cover the slide and quiz each other. Which one tells you what a shot LOOKS like?
Which one do you carry with you while shooting? Which one has the audio in it?)*

### Slide 4: What each one looks like

> Create one slide for a high school video class titled "What They Actually Look Like." Two halves. Top half,
> "SCRIPT, two column": a small table with headers VIDEO and AUDIO, one row reading "Close up, hands opening a
> locker" and "NARRATOR: Most people never notice the first ten minutes." Bottom half, "SHOT LIST": a small
> table with columns number, Description, Type, Location, one row reading 1, "Hands opening the locker", Close
> up, Main hallway. Caption at the bottom: "The script is in STORY order. The shot list is in SHOOTING order,
> grouped by location, because you shoot everything in the hallway while you are standing in the hallway." One
> slide.

### Slide 5: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school video class. Big title: "Do Now: Script, Then Shot List."
> Numbered body: "1) Pick your ONE idea. Can I film this at school this month? Do I know the first shot? Is

> 90 second video, grouped by LOCATION." Add a box: "Done? Thumbnail your first three shots, write your
> production schedule, and add a cutaway for each location." Large, clean. One slide.

### Slide 1: As You Come In (leave this up)

## 4. Middle School CS: Variables and the win condition

**Today's documents:** `courses/middle-school-cs/planning/week-4/handouts/bell-ringer-thu.md`,
`handouts/thu-live-build-script.md`, `project.md`
**Run it as:** bell ringer on the board, then **build the counter and the win live** while they watch, hands
off keyboards. The full five-beat script is in the handout, including deliberately breaking the win.

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a middle school computer science class. Big title: "As You Come In."
> Body: "Open your maze project and run it. Drive at a wall to check your collision still works. If your walls
> leak, fix that first, we build on top of the maze today. Then get out paper for the bell ringer." Large,
> clean, minimal. One slide.

### Slide 2: Standards + Today

> Create one agenda slide for a middle school computer science class titled "Today: Count What You Collect."
> Text: Today's skills are variables and conditionals, from the Ohio CS standards for Algorithmic Thinking and
> Programming. Agenda: 1) Bell ringer, the same kinds of questions as the quiz, 2) Watch me build the counter
> and the win, live, 3) Build yours. Add a box in large text: "Quiz TOMORROW on IF STATEMENTS and everything
> before. NOT tile maps." Large readable text. One slide.

### Slide 3: Bell Ringer

> Create one bell-ringer slide for a middle school computer science class titled "Bell Ringer: Five
> Questions." Present as a numbered list in large readable text, with a monospace box for the first one. 1)
> In a monospace box: "set score to 0, set lives to 3, change score by 7, if score > 7: change lives by -1,
> else: change score by 3." What are score and lives at the END? 2) If x = 6, is "x > 5 AND x < 6" true or
> false? What about OR? 3) If score < 20 blue, else if score < 40 yellow, else red: what color when score is
> exactly 20? Exactly 40? 4) "score % 4 == 0" is true for which numbers? 5) In an if/else, how many branches
> run on a single pass? One slide.

*(Answers: 1) score 10, lives 3, because 7 > 7 is FALSE so the else runs. 2) AND is false, OR is true. 3) 20
is yellow, 40 is red. 4) 0, 4, 8, 12. 5) Exactly one, never both, never neither.)*

### Slide 4: The four traps

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "The Four Traps." Numbered list in large
> text: 1) ">" is not ">=". When the number is exactly equal, ">" is false. 2) AND needs BOTH sides true. OR
> needs only ONE. 3) A chain stops at the FIRST true condition, it does not keep checking. 4) "%" is about the
> remainder, so "% 5 == 0" means "every 5." Add a caption in a box: "These are on the quiz tomorrow." One
> slide.

### Slide 5: The code, then Do Now

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "Do Now: Make It Winnable." Top half,
> the code in a monospace box: "let keys = 0", "on overlap Player with Food:", indented "otherSprite.destroy()",
> "keys += 1", "info.setScore(keys)", "if keys == 5: game over WIN". Bottom half, a numbered task list: 1)
> Place your items around the maze. 2) Add the counter variable. 3) Add the on-overlap that destroys the item
> and adds one. 4) Add the if that ends the game as a win. 5) TEST IT, collect everything and confirm you
> actually win. Add a box in large text: "Count your items. The number in your if has to MATCH how many you
> placed. This will be the bug in half your projects today." One slide.

*(Then Turn and Talk while they work: trade seats and play your partner's maze all the way through. Does the
win actually fire? Finished early: dead ends, new tile art, an enemy that costs a life, a second level, a
timer.)*
