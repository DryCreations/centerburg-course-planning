### Slide 2 prompt (Do Now / bell ringer, review yesterday)

> Create one "Do Now" bell-ringer slide for a high school photography class. Big title: "Do Now: RAW and White
> Balance." Numbered list students answer on paper: 1) Why does RAW give you more room to fix a photo than JPEG?
> 2) A photo shot indoors looks too orange. Which control fixes it, and which way do you push it? 3) What does
> "non-destructive editing" mean? Footer line: "Answer key is posted on Google Classroom." Clean, large text, one
> slide.

*(Classroom answer key: 1) RAW keeps all the sensor data, so white balance and exposure are fully editable. 2)
White balance / temperature, push it cooler (toward blue). 3) The original file is never changed; your edits can
always be undone.)*

### Slide 3 prompt (open in Camera Raw)

> One slide titled "Open Your RAW in Adobe Camera Raw." Text: When you open a RAW file it opens in Adobe Camera
> Raw (through Photoshop). Editing here is non-destructive: your original is never changed. Large text, one slide.

### Slide 4 prompt (edit order)

> One slide titled "Edit Top to Bottom." Ordered list: 1) White balance, 2) Exposure, 3) Highlights and shadows,
> 4) A little contrast, 5) Crop and straighten, 6) A little vibrance. Note: subtle, not extreme. One slide.

### Slide 5 prompt (white balance how-to)

> One slide titled "Fix the White Balance." Text: Use the White Balance eyedropper on something that should be
> gray or white, OR drag the Temperature slider (left is cooler and bluer, right is warmer and more orange) until
> whites look white and skin looks natural. One slide.

### Slide 6 prompt (rename files)

> One slide titled "Rename Your Files." Text: A name like IMG_4821 tells nobody anything. Rename before you
> submit: lastname-what-it-is.jpg, for example smith-daylight-edited.jpg. Right-click, choose Rename, keep the
> .jpg. One slide, large text.

### Slide 7 prompt (export JPG)

> One slide titled "Export as JPG, Not RAW." Text: RAW files are huge and won't open for everyone. Save as JPG.
> Save both: the untouched original as a JPG and your edited version as a JPG. One slide.

### Slide 8 prompt (submit directly)

> One slide titled "Submit the Files Directly." Numbered steps: 1) Open the assignment in Google Classroom. 2)
> Click Add or create, then File, and upload both JPGs. 3) Check both files are attached. 4) Click Turn in. Note:
> the actual files, not a link, not left in a folder. One slide.

### Slide 9 prompt (what to submit)

> One slide titled "What to Submit Today." Checklist: your original photo (JPG, renamed); your edited photo (JPG,
> renamed); one or two sentences on what you fixed (white balance? crop? exposure?). One slide.

## Aviation UAS (controller practice)

Use the handout `courses/aviation-uas/planning/week-3/handouts/wed-controller-practice.md` and project each
flight path. For each path, students answer four things out loud or on paper: **sticks? heading? pointing? how
to recreate?** Emphasize that **heading (where it travels) and pointing (where the camera faces) are often
different** (strafe, orbit, the "camera on the bleachers" shot).

### Slide prompt (the framing slide)

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "Read the Path, Name the Sticks." Two definitions in
> large text: HEADING = which way the drone is traveling. POINTING = which way the nose and camera are facing.
> Note: they are often NOT the same. Footer: "For each path: sticks? heading? pointing? how would you recreate
> it?" One slide.

*(Then walk the five worked paths and the three "now you try" paths from the handout: push-in, rise-and-reveal,
truck/strafe, orbit, pedestal-down, plus the spin, the follow, and the "fly down the track while pointing at the
bleachers" heading-vs-pointing problem.)*
