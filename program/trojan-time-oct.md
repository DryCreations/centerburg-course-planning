# Trojan Time: 25 Minutes, 17 Kids, No Setup

**Teacher-only.** Thursday Oct 8. Monthly, no academic obligation.

**The plan: a five-minute warm-up that needs nothing, then one real game.** Paper and pencils are the
only supplies used, and only for the backup.

---

## Minutes 0 to 5: Count To Twenty

**Zero materials. Zero explanation.** It takes thirty seconds to teach and it does more for a room than
any icebreaker question.

**The rules, exactly as you say them:**

> As a group, count out loud to twenty. **Anybody can say the next number at any time. There is no
> order.** If two people say a number at the same time, **we start over at one.**

**That is all.** No pointing, no signals, no going around the circle, no deciding an order in advance.

**What happens:** they fail at four, then at seven, then they get quiet and start listening to each
other, and somewhere around the fourth attempt they get to twenty and the room cheers. **The listening
is the point**, and it sets up the next twenty minutes.

**If they get it on the first try**, say "now with your eyes closed" and they will not.

---

## Minutes 5 to 25: Werewolf

**Seventeen is close to the ideal player count.** No materials needed if you assign roles by tapping
shoulders with everyone's eyes closed. **Expect to get through two rounds.**

**If "werewolf" is a problem for your room, rename it.** The mechanics do not change: Sleeping Village
with "troublemakers," or Spies, or whatever you like. **Nobody is out of the game permanently in a
25-minute version**, see the house rule below.

### Roles for 17

| Role | How many | What they do at night |
|---|---|---|
| **Werewolf** | **4** | Eyes open, silently agree on one person to take out |
| **Seer** | **1** | Points at one player. You nod yes or shake no: is that person a werewolf? |
| **Healer** | **1** | Points at one player. That person survives the night |
| **Villager** | **11** | Nothing at night. Everything during the day |

**Four werewolves in seventeen is the right ratio.** Fewer and the village wins too easily, more and it
is over in one round.

### How to run it, start to finish

**1. Everyone sits in a circle or at their desks. "Heads down, eyes closed."**

**2. Assign roles by tapping shoulders.** Walk the circle and tap: four taps for werewolves, one tap on
the head for the seer, two taps for the healer. **Say the pattern out loud first** so they know what
their own tap meant.

**3. Night one.** "Werewolves, open your eyes." Give them ten seconds to point at someone and agree
silently. "Werewolves, close your eyes." Then "Seer, open your eyes, point at someone." Nod or shake.
"Seer, close your eyes." Then the healer the same way.

**4. Morning.** "Everybody open your eyes." Announce who was taken out, **unless the healer saved
them**, in which case announce that nobody was taken.

**5. The day.** **Five minutes, maximum.** Anyone can accuse anyone. The accused gets thirty seconds to
defend themselves. Then a show-of-hands vote, majority decides, and that person is out.

**6. Reveal nothing** about who was what until the game ends.

**7. Repeat** until either all four werewolves are out (**village wins**) or the werewolves equal the
remaining villagers (**werewolves win**).

### The things that go wrong, and the fixes

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Somebody peeks** | Say up front: "if you peek, you are a werewolf for the rest of the game and I will not tell anyone." It stops immediately |
| **Players who are out get bored and talk** | **House rule: anyone out becomes a "ghost."** Ghosts may not speak, but you let them open their eyes at night and watch. They stay engaged and they love knowing |
| **The day phase drags** | **Hard five-minute cap, and you say it out loud at the start.** A vote has to happen whether they are ready or not |
| **One kid gets piled on every round** | Werewolves vote in silence, so this is a village problem. Say once: "you need a reason, not a feeling" |
| **It ends in four minutes** | Good, play again. The second round is always better because now they know how it works |

### Why this one and not a questionnaire

**It gets the quiet kids talking because the game needs them to.** A villager who says nothing all game
looks suspicious, so the structure does the work that "tell us a fun fact about yourself" never does.
**You will learn more about seventeen kids in two rounds of this than in a month of advisory questions.**

## Backup: Drawing Telephone

**If you would rather not moderate**, or if the group is low-energy, this needs paper and pencils and
nothing else.

1. **Groups of five or six.** Three groups for seventeen
2. Everyone **writes a short phrase** at the top of a sheet. Something concrete and absurd is best:
   "a raccoon driving a bus"
3. **Pass left.** The next person **draws** it, then **folds the paper over** so only the drawing shows
4. **Pass left.** The next person **writes what they see**, then folds over so only the words show
5. **Keep alternating** until the sheet comes back around
6. **Unfold and read them out.** This is the whole payoff, so leave five minutes for it

**The drift is the joke.** Tell them not to try to be funny; the game does that by itself.

---

## If 25 minutes becomes 15

**Run Count To Twenty, then one round of Drawing Telephone in groups of five or six.** Werewolf does not
compress below about eighteen minutes, and a rushed first round will put them off it.
