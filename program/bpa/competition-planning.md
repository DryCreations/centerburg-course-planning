# Business Professionals of America (BPA) — Competition Planning

## About BPA

Business Professionals of America is the leading CTSO (Career and Technical Student Organization) for students pursuing careers in business management, information technology, finance, accounting, office administration, and other business-related career fields. Ohio is one of BPA's active state associations. BPA National Center is headquartered in Columbus, Ohio.

Per the Ohio Department of Education and Workforce, **BPA is the CTSO aligned with the Information Technology career field** (along with Business Management and Finance).

Source: https://education.ohio.gov/Topics/Career-Tech/Program-Enhancements/CTSO

## Competition Structure

1. **Regional Competition** — Top qualifiers advance to state
2. **State Competition** — Top qualifiers advance to national
3. **National Leadership Conference (NLC)** — Held each May

Competitions range from individual to team, from objective tests to judged presentations.

### Starting from Scratch — First Year Guidance

For a new program without an established chapter:

1. **Establish the chapter first.** Submit the New Charter Application (register.bpa.org) before pursuing competition. This must be done before members can register.
2. **Contact the Ohio State Advisor early** (August/September). Get current-year deadlines, Ohio competition dates, and new-chapter guidance before the year begins.
3. **Virtual events offer the lowest barrier to national competition.** Events V01–V15 are national-only and **do not require state qualification**. Students submit their work online. Registration deadline is December 1; submission deadline is January 15. This is the most accessible path to national participation in year one.
4. **Objective/test events** (390, 391, 490, 491, etc.) are also accessible — students study the knowledge domain and take a test at the competition event. No project submission required beforehand.
5. **Run a local school competition regardless.** Even before entering state, organize an internal competition using WSAP guidelines. This builds student experience, demonstrates the program's value to administration and the BAC, and prepares students for the format of state competition.

### Competition Formats at a Glance

| Format | How It Works | Lead Time Needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Project / Pre-submission** | Work created ahead of time; submitted before competition; judged at competition (or virtually for V events) | 8–12 weeks of integrated coursework |
| **Objective Test (Open Events)** | Multiple-choice knowledge test taken at competition | 4–6 weeks of study/review |
| **Live Presentation** | Presentation prepared in advance; delivered live to judges at competition | 6–10 weeks of coursework |
| **Speech** | Extemporaneous (spontaneous topic) or Prepared (written in advance) | 4–8 weeks |

Project-based events (Video Production, Computer Modeling, Graphic Design, Podcast, Animation, etc.) are specifically designed to align with coursework. The student's BPA entry **is** their course project.

### Running a Local School Competition

For a new program, organize a school-level competition each January as a foundation step:

| Element | Details |
|---|---|
| **When** | January (4–6 weeks before estimated state date) |
| **Judges** | BAC members, other teachers, or school administrators |
| **Format** | Mirror BPA's competition format; use WSAP scoring rubrics as the judging sheet |
| **Events covered** | Run whichever events students have prepared (project display, presentations, or objective tests) |
| **Purpose** | Build student confidence; identify strong competitors; demonstrate program value; fulfill BAC engagement |
| **Logistics** | 1–2 class periods; project events need display/display time; presentation events need a room with projection; objective tests need printed or computer-based test format |
