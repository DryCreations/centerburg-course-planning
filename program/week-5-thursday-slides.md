## 1. Design Techniques: Feedback and state, then screens 2 and 3

**Documents:** `handouts/ui-patterns.md` (section 3), `handouts/interaction-labs.md` (Lab 4)

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school design class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body: "Open
> Figma. Screen one should be built. Today: screens two and three, plus the pieces that tell a person
> something happened." Large, clean. One slide.

### Slide 2: Telling the person something happened

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "Show That It Worked." Four short blocks. SELECTED
> STATE: which option is currently picked, shown filled, colored, or check-marked. A choice with no visible
> selection is a choice a person cannot verify. MODAL: a box over a dimmed screen that has to be dealt with
> before continuing. Use it to confirm something destructive, not to tell people things they did not ask.
> TOAST: a small message that appears briefly and disappears. "Saved." "Added." LOADING STATE: what shows
> while waiting, a spinner or a gray outline of the content. Add a box in large text: "Nothing happening
> reads as broken. Even in a mockup, showing you thought about it counts." One slide.

### Slide 3: Lab: Scroll

> Create one instructional slide for a high school design class titled "Ten Minute Lab: Scroll." Body: "A list
> longer than the screen that actually scrolls. In a scratch file." Numbered: 1) Make a phone frame. 2) Build
> a list that runs PAST the bottom of the frame, eight or ten rows. 3) Select the frame and turn ON "Clip
> content" in the right panel. 4) In the Prototype tab set Overflow scrolling to Vertical scrolling. 5)
> Present and drag the list up and down. Add a box: "Any list of more than about five things should scroll,
> not shrink to fit." One slide.

### Slide 4: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school design class. Big title: "Do Now: Screens Two and Three." Body:
> "Build them from your wireframes. Two things stay consistent across every screen: the BACK BUTTON in the
> same corner, and anything that repeats stays in the same place." Then a short list: is there a selected
> state anywhere a person chooses something; does anything destructive ask first; does any list start empty.
> Add a box: "Tomorrow: connect everything and your first partner test. Something has to be clickable by the
> end of tomorrow, even if it is only two screens." One slide.

### Board version (no projector)

## 2. Video & Sound: FILMING, IN THE CLASSROOM

**Bring:** cameras, charged batteries and spares, cards with space, tripods, any external mics.
**Reference:** `handouts/camera-video-settings.md`, and each group's call sheet from yesterday.
**Three shooting cameras** (T4i, T5, T6), three groups, running on the shared schedule. The T3 shoots
behind-the-scenes stills only.

### Slide 1: As You Come In (leave this up)

> Create one "As You Come In" checklist slide for a high school video class. Big title: "As You Come In: Set
> Up First." A large numbered checklist designed to stay on the board: 1) Get your call sheet out and your
> assigned camera: T4i, T5, or T6. 2) Movie mode, exposure MANUAL. 3) Resolution and frame rate, the SAME for everyone in your group. 4)
> Shutter at double the frame rate: 1/50 at 24fps, 1/60 at 30fps. 5) White balance on a PRESET, not auto. 6)
> Lens switch to MF, focus with the magnifier. 7) Audio level peaking below the red. 8) Card space and battery
> checked, spare in the bag. Add a box in large text: "Then shoot ten seconds and PLAY IT BACK before you
> shoot anything real." One slide, legible from across the room.

### Slide 2: The five unfixables

> Create one slide for a high school video class titled "Five Things You Cannot Fix Later." A list in large
> text: autofocus left on, the image pulses in and out of focus; auto exposure left on, brightness drifts
> mid-shot; auto white balance, shots do not match when you cut them; shutter too fast, motion looks stuttery;
> clipped audio, distorted crackling voices. Caption at the bottom: "All five are set before you press record.
> None of them can be repaired afterward. That is the whole reason for the checklist." One slide.

### Slide 3: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school video class. Big title: "Do Now: Shoot In Room." Numbered: "1)
> Run the setup checklist. Ten seconds, play it back. 2) Work your call sheet in order. 3) Get at least TWO
> in-room shots today. 4) Shoot more takes than you think you need, it costs nothing now and everything later.
> 5) Record ROOM TONE: 30 seconds, nobody talking, nobody moving. 6) Hand the camera off on time, another
> group is waiting." Add a box: "Room tone feels pointless right now. You will need it in the edit and you
> cannot go back for it." And: "Next week you spread out into the building. Today is the room." One slide.

### Slide 4: Before You Leave

> Create one "Exit" slide for a high school video class titled "Before You Leave." Body: "1) Offload
> everything today. Not tomorrow. 2) Name your files: LastName_Project_Shot03_Take2. Not MVI_4821, you will
> not remember what that was. 3) Update your shot log: what is done, what still needs shooting. 4) Batteries
> on the charger, gear back in the case." Add a box: "Editing software is not installed yet. Offload and
> organize anyway. When it lands you want to be cutting, not hunting." One slide.

## 3. Aviation UAS: TAFs, then your own limits

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school drone class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body: "Pull
> our field's METAR and decode it silently, all the way. Then find the TAF for the same field. It looks
> similar but it is doing something different. What do you think it is?" Large, clean. One slide.

### Slide 2: METAR vs TAF

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "METAR vs TAF." Two boxes side by side. Left, METAR:
> "What the weather IS doing, observed right now, at one moment." Right, TAF: "What the weather is EXPECTED to
> do, over a window of time." Underneath: "Same codes. A TAF uses FM to mark when something changes, so
> FM150200 means from 0200Z on the 15th, expect what follows." Add a box in large text: "The METAR tells you
> whether you can launch. The TAF tells you whether you will be able to finish." One slide.

### Slide 3: Personal minimums

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "Your Own Limits." Body: "Part 107 gives you the legal
> floor: 3 statute miles, 400 feet, cloud clearance. That is the absolute edge of what is allowed, not a
> target. Your PERSONAL MINIMUMS are the line you draw above that floor." Add in large text: "You decide them
> now, calm, at a desk. Not later, standing in a field with a drone in your hands and everybody waiting on
> you." One slide.

### Slide 4: The rule

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "The Rule That Makes It Worth Doing." Body in very
> large text: "If ANY single number is outside your limits, it is a no-go. Not a discussion. Not a vote. Not
> 'but we only need five minutes.'" Underneath: "You wrote these down when nothing was on the line. The
> version of you standing in a field with a class watching is not the one who should be renegotiating them."
> One slide.

### Slide 5: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school drone class. Big title: "Do Now: Write Your Card." Body: "Fill
> in the Personal Minimums Card from Classroom. For every line: your number, and WHY you picked it. 'Twenty
> knots' is not an answer. 'Twenty knots, because the aircraft is rated near twenty-four and I want margin' is
> an answer. Sign it." Add a box: "Your numbers must be at least as strict as Part 107, and for most of them
> they should be stricter. You are a student, flying a school aircraft, near other people." And: "Also due:
> drill 4, one METAR and its TAF together. And quiz tomorrow." One slide.

## 4. Middle School CS: `while`, for real

**Run it as:** they learned this Monday. Ask them the rule rather than reminding them of it.

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a middle school computer science class. Big title: "As You Come In."
> Body: "Open Star Catcher and play it for thirty seconds. Does it still run fast? If it is slowing down,
> something is not being destroyed. Fix that first. Then: remember Monday's while loops? Today you actually
> use one." Large, clean, minimal. One slide.

### Slide 2: The countdown

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "A Countdown, With while." Show in a
> monospace box: "set timer to 30" then "while timer > 0:" indented "pause 1000" and "change timer by -1" and
> "set score to timer" then unindented "game over WIN". Caption: "Wait one second, take one off the timer,
> check again. When the timer hits zero the condition is false and the loop stops." Add a box in large text:
> "Before you run it: does something inside that loop change the timer? If not, you just froze your game." One
> slide.

### Slide 3: The four loops

> Create one slide for a middle school computer science class titled "All Four, Side by Side." A two column
> table. "repeat 10 times": when you know exactly how many times and you do not care which pass you are on.
> "for index from 0 to 9": when you know how many times AND you need the count, for spacing, speed, or color.
> "while (condition)": when you do not know how many times, only when to stop. "forever": when it should never
> stop while the game runs. One slide, clean table.

### Slide 4: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a middle school computer science class. Big title: "Do Now: Steps 13 and 14."
> Body: "1) Add the countdown timer with a while loop. Test that the game actually ends. 2) Then pick ONE
> more:" then three lettered options: "A. DIFFICULTY RAMP: a speed variable that grows each wave. B. LEVELS: a
> 'repeat 3 times' around your wave, then a LEVEL UP splash. C. A WHILE THAT WAITS: 'while score < 10, pause
> 500' then splash HALFWAY." Add a box in large text: "Your game has to END, win or lose. A game that runs
> forever with no ending is not finished." And: "Tomorrow: finish, then we play each other's." One slide.
