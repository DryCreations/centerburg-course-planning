## 1. Design Techniques: Write it up and post it

**Due today:** everything in `project.md`.

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school design class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body: "Get
> out your teardown from Monday and open your Figma file. Today: write the before and after, post it to your
> portfolio, and submit. Everything is due at the end of the period." Large, clean. One slide.

### Slide 2: The write-up

> Create one slide for a high school design class titled "The Write-Up Is the Whole Argument." Body: "Four to
> six sentences. Not a description, an ARGUMENT. Every sentence names a decision and the problem it solved."
> Then sentence starters in a box: "The biggest problem with the original was ____, because ____." "I made
> ____ larger and ____ smaller so that ____." "I moved ____ because a person coming here to ____ needs it
> before ____." "I removed ____ entirely. It was competing with ____ and nobody came to this screen for it."
> "If I had more time I would ____." One slide.

### Slide 3: Do Now

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school design class. Big title: "Do Now: Finish and Post." A
> checklist: annotated screenshot with five findings; user journey with the breakdown point marked; paper
> wireframe with the primary action circled; Figma file with two frames connected both ways and working in
> Present; partner's name and what you changed after their test; the before and after write-up naming four
> decisions; the pairing posted to your portfolio under Unit 1.1; Figma share link on Google Classroom, set
> to Anyone with the link. One slide, clean checklist layout.

### Slide 4: Before You Leave

> Create one "Exit" slide titled "Before You Leave." Body: "Your portfolio should now show a real problem and
> your solution to it, side by side, with the reasoning written out. That pairing is the strongest single
> thing you can show anyone: not that you can use a tool, but that you can find what is wrong and fix it."
> One slide, large text.

## 3. Aviation UAS: Quiz, then weather briefs

**Quiz:** `courses/aviation-uas/planning/week-5/quiz-bank.csv`, cut to 20.
**Then:** live briefs, about 90 seconds each, on a real current METAR you hand them.

### Slide 1: As You Come In

> Create one "As You Come In" slide for a high school drone class. Big title: "As You Come In." Body: "Quiz
> first, on Google Classroom. Silent, stay on the quiz tab, not open note. Have your Personal Minimums Card
> out on the desk, you will need it after. When you submit, sit quietly, people are still testing." Large,
> clean. One slide.

### Slide 2: What is on it

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "What Is On the Quiz." Four short groups. DECODING:
> wind groups including gusts, visibility, sky condition codes and their real heights, temperature over dew
> point, altimeter, weather codes. TIME: what Zulu means, converting to local, why an observation's age
> matters. FORECAST: how a TAF differs and what FM marks. HAZARDS AND LIMITS: gusts versus steady wind, wind
> shear, thunderstorms at a distance, precipitation, the 3 statute mile minimum, and personal minimums. Add a
> box: "Remember: add two zeros for cloud height. Wind direction is FROM. The gust is the number that
> matters." One slide.

### Slide 3: Do Now (the briefs)

> Create one "Do Now" slide for a high school drone class. Big title: "After the Quiz: Your Weather Brief."
> Body: "I hand you a real, current METAR. You have about ninety seconds in front of the class." Numbered: 1)
> Read the station and time, and say how OLD this observation is. 2) Decode every field in plain English. 3)
> Name the one number closest to a limit, and say which limit. 4) Give a go or no-go call and name the
> deciding number. 5) Say what the TAF expects to change. One slide.

### Slide 4: A no-go is a perfect brief

> Create one slide for a high school drone class titled "A No-Go Is a Perfect Brief." Body in large text:
> "You are not being graded on whether you say go. You are being graded on whether the number behind your
> call is the right one. Calling a scrub, with the deciding number named, is a perfect brief." Underneath:
> "Do not go hunting for a reason to say go. That is exactly the habit this week exists to prevent." One
> slide.
