# Video & Sound Week 12: Daily Slides

**Teacher-only.** One Google Slides prompt per day. Paste each into "Help me create a presentation."
Slide 1 stays up, or post it on the board.

**Observation: Tuesday, 3rd period.** The run of show is under Tuesday.

**Every day is the same three parts:** start (slide 1 and a do-now), a short lesson, then project work
with a target, and a two-minute exit ticket. Post each exit ticket as a Classroom question.

---

## Monday

```
Create a presentation for a high school video production class called "Tuesday: Getting Good Sound." Dark charcoal background, bold yellow and white headings, large readable text, simple flat icons. Keep text short. 8 slides:

Slide 1, "Today": top section headed "What we are covering" lists in full, in smaller text: "7.8.4 Determine microphone and speaker placement." "7.8.6 Set up and operate audio-for-video recording devices." "7.8.7 Set up and operate a time code system for audio-video synchronization." "2.9.5 Develop a timeline, a communication plan, a task breakdown, costs (e.g., equipment, labor), deliverables, and responsibilities for completion." Bottom section headed "Agenda": "Do now (7.8.4)." "Mic setup review: three test clips (7.8.4, 7.8.6)." "Quick check (7.8.6, 7.8.7)." "Work on your project, mic station, progress check-ins (2.9.5)." "Exit ticket."

Slide 2, "Do Now": "Where should a clip-on mic go on the speaker? Why does closer beat louder?" Answer in Classroom.

Slide 3, "The Setup (7.8.4, 7.8.6)": five numbered steps in large text: "1. Turn on the transmitter and receiver." "2. Clip the transmitter mid-chest, a hand below the chin." "3. Plug the receiver into the T4i MIC jack." "4. Movie mode. Sound recording: Manual." "5. Set the level so talking peaks around -12." Include a simple diagram: a person with a small clip-on mic, a wireless signal to a receiver on top of a camera.

Slide 4, "Name That Problem": three large numbered boxes: "Clip 1." "Clip 2." "Clip 3." Under them: "What went wrong? How would you fix it?"

Slide 5, "The Answers": three rows: "Clip 1: good. Mic placed right, level around -12." "Clip 2: clipping. Level too high. Turn the record level down. You cannot fix this in editing." "Clip 3: mic too far away. You hear the room. Put the mic on the speaker." Include a simple audio meter graphic with a green zone and a red zone at the top.

Slide 6, "Quick Check": three numbered questions in large text, no answers: "1. Which of our cameras has a mic jack?" "2. Where should the loudest talking land on the meter?" "3. Recording sound on a phone instead? What do you do at the start so you can sync it?"

Slide 7, "Work Time (2.9.5)": "Your target: finish the changes from your feedback, then the next step on your timeline." "Mic station: I will call your group." "I will check in with every group. Have your progress open."

Slide 8, "Exit Ticket": "Name one thing you will do differently when you record your sound."
```

## Wednesday

```
Create a presentation for a high school video production class called "Wednesday: Copyright." Dark charcoal background, bold yellow and white headings, large readable text, simple flat icons. Keep text short. 9 slides:

Slide 1, "Today": top section headed "What we are covering" lists in full, in smaller text: "1.3.8 Verify compliance with computer and intellectual property laws and regulations." "2.9.5 Develop a timeline, a communication plan, a task breakdown, costs (e.g., equipment, labor), deliverables, and responsibilities for completion." Bottom section headed "Agenda": "Do now (1.3.8)." "Copyright: what you can use (1.3.8)." "Practice: yes, no, or depends (1.3.8)." "Check your own project, then work (1.3.8, 2.9.5)." "Exit ticket."

Slide 2, "Do Now": "You found the perfect song on YouTube. Can you use it in your BPA video? Why or why not?"

Slide 3, "Copyright": "The creator owns their work the moment it is made. No sign-up needed." "Covers music, video clips, photos, and art." "Giving credit is NOT the same as permission." Include a simple copyright symbol icon.

Slide 4, "What You CAN Use": "Your own work." "Public domain: no one owns it anymore." "Creative Commons: follow the license, usually give credit." "Royalty-free libraries: use it under the license." "Anything you got written permission for."

Slide 5, "Fair Use": "Limited use without permission, for things like commentary, teaching, or parody." "It is decided case by case." "For a competition, do not count on it. Use your own or licensed work."

Slide 6, "Release Forms": "Anyone who appears in your video should give written permission." "Under 18? A parent signs." Include a simple graphic of a form with a signature line.

Slide 7, "Yes, No, or Depends?": four numbered situations: "1. A beat you made yourself in GarageBand." "2. A popular song, with the artist credited at the end." "3. A Creative Commons photo, credited the way its license asks." "4. A three-second clip from a movie for a joke."

Slide 8, "The Answers": "1. Yes. You made it." "2. No. Credit is not permission." "3. Yes. You followed the license." "4. Depends. It might be fair use, but it is risky in a competition."

Slide 9, "Work Time and Exit Ticket": "Target: list everything in your project you did not make yourself, and where it came from." "Then keep working." Bottom, headed "Exit ticket": "Is everything in your project yours or licensed? If not, what will you replace?"
```
