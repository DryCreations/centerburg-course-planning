# Editing Basics + How to Submit Your Photo

**Video & Sound · Week 3 · Wednesday Sep 2**

Today you edit a RAW photo in **Adobe Camera Raw** (it opens through Photoshop when you open a RAW file), then
**rename it and submit it the right way.** Read the submitting part carefully: a lot of this is new.

## Part 1: The editing basics (Adobe Camera Raw)

Open your best RAW frame. It opens in **Camera Raw**. Work top to bottom:

1. **White balance** (fix the color). Use the **White Balance eyedropper** and click something that should be
   neutral gray or white, or drag the **Temperature** slider (left = cooler/blue, right = warmer/orange) and
   **Tint** (green/magenta) until whites look white and skin looks natural.
2. **Exposure** (fix the brightness). Drag **Exposure** so the photo isn't too dark or blown out. Watch the
   **histogram** in the corner: you want a spread, not everything jammed to one side.
3. **Highlights and shadows** (recover detail). Pull **Highlights** down to bring back bright areas (sky,
   windows); raise **Shadows** to open up dark areas.
4. **Contrast and tone** (a little punch). A small **Contrast** bump, and tweak **Whites/Blacks** if needed.
   Keep it subtle, not extreme.
5. **Crop and straighten** (fix the framing). Use the **Crop tool**: straighten a tilted horizon, and crop for a
   stronger composition (try the rule of thirds).
6. **Vibrance** (optional color pop). A little **Vibrance** boosts color gently. Don't overdo it.

When it looks good, click **Open** (into Photoshop) or **Done** to save the settings. Editing here is
**non-destructive**: your original RAW is never changed.

### Some good ideas to try

- **The rescue:** take your "wrong white balance on purpose" shot and fix it so it looks correct.
- **Warm and moody:** push the temperature warm, lower exposure a touch, deepen shadows.
- **Clean and bright:** neutral white balance, lift exposure and shadows, light and airy.
- **Black and white:** drop saturation to zero (or use a B&W profile) and lean on contrast.

## Part 2: Rename your files (do this before submitting)

Your camera names files like `IMG_4821.CR2`. That tells nobody anything. **Rename before you submit** so your
work is easy to find and grade.

**Name it like this:** `lastname-what-it-is.jpg`

- `smith-daylight-original.jpg`
- `smith-daylight-edited.jpg`

How to rename: right-click the file, choose **Rename**, type the new name. Keep the file extension (`.jpg`).

---

## Part 3: Export as JPG (not RAW)

- RAW files (`.CR2` / `.NEF` / `.ARW`) are huge and won't open for everyone. **Submit JPGs.**
- In Photoshop: **File > Save As** (or **Export**) and choose **JPG**. In Camera Raw you can also **Save Image**
  as JPG.
- Save **both**: the untouched **original** as a JPG, and your **edited** version as a JPG.

---

## Part 4: Submit the photos directly (how to turn it in)

Submit **the files themselves**, directly to the Google Classroom assignment. Not a link, not left in a folder.

1. Open the assignment in **Google Classroom**.
2. Click **Add or create > File** and **upload** your two JPGs (original + edited), OR drag them in.
3. Make sure **both** files are attached and correctly named.
4. Click **Turn in / Mark as done.**

**What you're submitting today:**

- [ ] Your **original** photo (JPG), renamed
- [ ] Your **edited** photo (JPG), renamed
- [ ] One or two sentences: what did you fix (white balance? crop? exposure?)

> If you're not sure a file uploaded, look for it listed under "Your work" on the assignment. If it's not there,
> it didn't submit. Ask if you're stuck: turning it in the right way is part of the grade.
