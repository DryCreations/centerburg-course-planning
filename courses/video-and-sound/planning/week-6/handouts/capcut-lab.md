**Video & Sound Production (145110) | Thursday Sep 24 and Friday Sep 25**

**This one is individual.** Your group shot the footage together. **Everybody cuts their own version of
it.** Same clips, different edits, and they will not come out the same, which is the point.

Two days. **Level 1 is required.** Levels 2 and 3 are there so nobody sits idle, and they are what a good

## Step 0: Get your storyboard out. Actually out.

**Before you open CapCut.** Put your storyboard and shot list next to you.

Answer these in your head, or write them down:

1. **What was this supposed to be?** In one sentence
2. **What order were the shots supposed to go in?**
3. **What did you plan that you did not get?** Every shoot has one
4. **What did you get that you did not plan?** Those are often the good ones

> **An edit without the storyboard is guessing.** The storyboard is a plan somebody made when they were
> thinking clearly, which is not the state you are in three hours into cutting.

## Level 1: Required. A cut that makes sense.

This is the whole assignment. If you only get here, you are fine.

### 1. Set up the project

- **New Project**
- **Import** only the clips you might actually use, not the whole card
- Drag your first clip to the **timeline**

### 2. Assemble

- Put your clips on the timeline **in the order they should be watched**, which is not the order you shot
- Use the storyboard order as your starting point, then change it if the footage says otherwise

### 3. Trim

- Every clip you shot has dead time on both ends. **Cut it off**
- Find the frame where the action actually starts. Start there
- Find the frame where it is over. End there
- **Close every gap.** No black between clips unless you put it there on purpose

### 4. Title card

- A **title card** at the front: the name of the piece
- Keep it on screen long enough to read twice, roughly **3 seconds**
- Plain text, high contrast, readable. No animated preset

### 5. Credits

- A **credit list** at the end. Every person in your group, with the job they actually did
- Director, camera, sound, talent, editor. Real jobs, real names
- **Your name goes on it as editor**, because this cut is yours

### 6. Export and turn in

- Export at **1080p**, into your folder
- Post it to Google Classroom
- With it, **three sentences**: your best cut and why, your worst cut and what would fix it

**Length target: 45 to 90 seconds**, title and credits included.

## Level 2: If you got there and there is time left

Pick any of these. They are the difference between "assembled" and "edited."

| | What to do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Kill your darlings** | Watch it through and cut **five more seconds** out of the middle. Anywhere | Nearly every first edit is too long. The discipline is finding what is not earning its place |
| **Fix one cut with a cutaway** | Find your ugliest cut. Put a different shot over the seam | This is what B-roll is for. It is the most-used trick in editing |
| **Hold the right shot longer** | Find the shot that is working and give it another second. Find one that is not and take one away | Timing is a choice. Every clip being the same length is a tell |
| **Fade to black** | Put a fade at the very end, into the credits | The audience needs to know it is over |
| **Match your audio** | Is one clip much louder than the next? Level them so the cut does not jump | Bad audio reads as amateur faster than bad video |
| **Lower third** | A text label naming a person or place, bottom left, on the shot where they first appear | Standard in everything from documentary to news |

## Level 3: If you are genuinely ahead

| | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Cut a second version** | Same footage, different order, different length. Then decide which is better and say why. This is the single most useful thing on this page |
| **Cut to a beat** | Bring in music and make your cuts land on it. Then watch it muted and see if the edit still works |
| **A 20 second version** | Cut your piece down to 20 seconds. Not trimmed, re-cut. What survives is what the piece is actually about |
| **Title sequence** | Build a real opening: a shot under the title rather than text on black |
| **Shot log** | Go through all your footage and log every usable take with a timecode and a note. Tedious, and it is what real assistant editors do all day |

## Today's rules

- **Your own footage.** Not downloads, not memes, not something off a phone
- **No filters, no effects, no auto-anything.** Text and a fade are allowed because they are the
  assignment. Nothing else
- **No music at Level 1.** You need to hear your own audio to know whether you got it
- **Ignore every template CapCut offers you.** None of them teach you anything and none of them exist in
  Resolve

> **Effects are how people avoid learning to cut.** Cutting is the entire skill. Everything else is
> decoration on top of it, and decoration on top of a bad edit is still a bad edit.

## Vocabulary: today's additions

You already have timeline, playhead, clip, trim, split, in and out point, rough cut, and export.

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Assembly / rough cut** | Clips in order, roughly trimmed. Where you start |
| **Fine cut** | The pass where you tighten timing and fix the cuts that do not work |
| **Title card** | Full-screen text, usually the name of the piece, at the front |
| **Lower third** | A text label in the bottom portion of frame naming a person or place |
| **Credits** | Who did what, at the end. Not optional and not a joke |
| **B-roll** | Supporting footage that is not the main action. What you cut away to |
| **Cutaway** | A shot inserted over a cut to hide it or to show something else briefly |
| **Head and tail** | The beginning and end of a clip. What you trim |
| **Handles** | Extra footage left before the in point and after the out point, so you have room to adjust later |
| **Fade** | A gradual change to or from black |
| **Dissolve** | One shot blending into the next. Different from a cut |
| **Jump cut** | Two similar shots cut together so the subject appears to jump. Usually a mistake |
| **Pacing** | How long shots are held, and how that makes the piece feel |
| **Timecode** | The hours, minutes, seconds, frames address of a specific point |
| **Sync** | Audio lined up with the picture it belongs to |
| **Levels** | How loud audio is. Matching levels across clips so nothing jumps |
| **Render / export** | Turning the timeline into a single finished video file |
