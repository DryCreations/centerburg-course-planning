# CapCut: Your First Timeline

**Video & Sound Production (145110) | Week 6, Wednesday Sep 24**

We do not have DaVinci Resolve yet. **CapCut is installed on the Macs**, and the parts of editing that
matter (the timeline, the playhead, trimming, ordering, and export) work the same way in every editor
ever made. Learn them here and Resolve will be a change of buttons, not a change of skill.

**Everybody in the group does this.** Not one person driving while two watch. Everyone pulls the footage,
everyone builds a timeline.

---

## Step 1: Get your footage onto the machine

1. Camera or card reader connected
2. Copy the clips into **your group's folder**, not the desktop, not Downloads
3. Name them so a stranger could find a shot: `scene2_shot3_take1`
4. **Eject the card properly** before pulling it out

> Footage that lives only on the card is footage that gets overwritten by the next group. Offload first,
> every time, before you touch CapCut.

---

## Step 2: Start a project

1. Open **CapCut**
2. **New Project**
3. **Import** your clips, or drag them into the media pool from your group folder
4. Drag one clip down to the **timeline** at the bottom

Look at what is on the screen now. **Four areas, in every editor that exists:**

| Area | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Media pool** | Everything you imported. Nothing here is in your edit yet |
| **Timeline** | The strip at the bottom. This *is* your edit, left to right in time |
| **Preview / program monitor** | What the playhead is currently sitting on |
| **Playhead** | The vertical line. Where you are in time. Drag it, it scrubs |

**The single idea to take away:** the media pool is what you *have*. The timeline is what you are
*making*. Nothing you import is in the edit until you put it there.

---

## Step 3: The assignment, specifically

**Build a 30 to 45 second timeline from your own footage.** Not a finished piece. A rough assembly.

Do exactly this:

1. **Put at least four clips on the timeline in an order that makes sense.** Not the order you shot them
   in, the order they should be watched in
2. **Trim the dead time off every one.** Cut the start before the action begins and the end after it
   finishes. Every clip you shot has a second or two of nothing on each end. Remove it
3. **Close the gaps** so it plays continuously with no black
4. **Find your best cut and your worst cut.** Play it through, watch for the moment it feels wrong
5. **Export it** at 1080p and put the file in your group folder

Then write **three sentences** in your group's doc and turn it in on Classroom:

> Which cut works best, and why. Which cut feels wrong, and what you think would fix it: a different
> trim point, a different clip, or a different order.

**That last part is the actual assignment.** Anybody can drag clips onto a line. Noticing that a cut is
wrong is the skill.

---

## The four moves that are the same in every editor

Learn these four and you can sit down at anything.

| Move | In CapCut | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| **Scrub** | Drag the playhead across the timeline | Find the frame you care about |
| **Trim** | Grab a clip's edge and drag it in | Remove what happens before and after the shot |
| **Split** | Playhead where you want the cut, then Split | Take a chunk out of the middle |
| **Reorder** | Drag a clip left or right | The order is the story |

**Everything else is decoration on top of these four.**

---

## Vocabulary, and it is on a future quiz

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Timeline** | The left-to-right strip that is your edit, in time order |
| **Playhead** | The vertical line marking where you are in the timeline |
| **Clip** | One piece of footage on the timeline. The same source can be on the timeline many times |
| **Media pool / bin** | The imported footage you have available, before it is in the edit |
| **Trim** | Shortening a clip by moving its start or end point, without deleting the footage itself |
| **In point / out point** | The frame a clip starts on and the frame it ends on |
| **Split** | Cutting one clip into two at the playhead |
| **Cut** | The transition from one clip to the next with nothing in between. The most common edit there is |
| **Rough cut / assembly** | The first pass. Clips in order, roughly trimmed. What you are making today |
| **Non-linear editing** | You can change any part of the edit at any time without redoing the rest. Every modern editor is this |
| **Ripple** | Closing the gap when you remove something, so everything after it slides left |
| **Export / render** | Turning the timeline back into a single video file |
| **Rendering** | The computer building that file. Long timelines take longer |
| **Aspect ratio** | The shape of the frame. 16:9 is what we shoot |
| **Resolution** | Frame size in pixels. We export 1920x1080 |
| **Frame rate** | Frames per second. We shoot 30. Your project should match it |

## Rules while you are in here

- **Your own footage.** Not downloaded clips, not something off a phone, not a meme
- **No effects, no filters, no transitions today.** Cuts only. The reason is that effects are how people
  avoid learning to cut, and cutting is the whole skill
- **No music today either.** You need to hear your own audio to know whether you got it
- CapCut will offer you templates and auto-everything. **Ignore all of it.** None of it teaches you
  anything and none of it exists in Resolve

## If you have no footage yet

You are either filming this period or you have missing work. In order:

1. **Filming** if you signed up for a slot. That comes first
2. **Missing work.** Scripts, storyboards, shot lists, the review packet, the production schedule
3. Then borrow a clip from a groupmate and do the timeline exercise anyway. The four moves are the point,
   and you need them before Resolve arrives
