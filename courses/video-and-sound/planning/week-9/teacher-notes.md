## The footage

From the published description, since **I could not open the page itself:**

- **Shot on a Blackmagic URSA Mini**, 3840×2160 at 23.976 fps. **Drone shots** on a DJI Phantom 3, same
  resolution
- **Several takes of each shot**: wides, close-ups, inserts and drone shots
- **No usable production sound.** It is a picture-only scene, which keeps the week about cutting and color
- Clips are hosted on **Vimeo**, and the page offers **a smaller download** for anyone who cannot handle 4K

**Before Tuesday:**

1. **Watch it yourself first.** I have not seen it, so check content and work out the scene's story
   before you need to answer "what order does this go in?"
2. **Download the smaller version once** and put it in a shared Drive folder. Thirty machines pulling 4K
   from Vimeo at once will not work
3. **Find out whether it is truly flat.** URSA Mini footage is often shot in a log mode, which looks
   washed out until it is corrected. **That is ideal:** correcting it on Monday is a visible, satisfying
   change. If the smaller download is already corrected, the lesson still works, but Monday's job becomes
   matching shots rather than rescuing them
4. **Credits:** Cinestudy asks for credit when edits are **posted publicly.** Class submissions are not
   public. If anyone posts theirs, the credit list on the project page goes with it

## CapCut checks, on one lab Mac, before Tuesday

**I could not confirm which color tools are free in the current version.** CapCut's own pages describe
all of its color correction tools as free, but recent third-party reviews say some advanced grading is
limited without Pro, and CapCut changes this often. **Five minutes on a lab Mac settles it:**

| Check | Where | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Adjust panel tabs have no Pro badge:** Basic, HSL, Curves, Color wheel | Select a clip, right panel | Basic plus HSL is enough. Drop whatever is locked from the handout |
| **Adjustment layers** exist | **Adjustment** in the top toolbar, or the media panel | Students grade each clip and copy-paste instead |
| **Copy and paste adjustments** between clips | Right-click a clip | Students set one clip, then match the rest by eye |
| **Preview quality** can be lowered | The player's settings | Use the smaller download, which should be enough on its own |
| **A free 1080p export** works with the adjustments on | Export | **This is the deal-breaker.** Test it once with every tool you plan to teach |

**The crown rule matters most.** A student who uses a Pro filter on Tuesday finds out at export on
Friday, and the fix is to undo it shot by shot. **Say it on Monday and again on Tuesday.**

**Filters and LUTs:** many filters are locked, so the handout leaves them out. Importing a `.cube` LUT
is reported as free. It is a fine extension for a fast student, but **not the lesson:** a LUT is someone
else's grade.

## What each day is really teaching

- **Tuesday, selects:** most students will drop the first take onto the timeline. **Make them watch every
  take** and say why they kept one over another. That is question 1
- **Wednesday, assembly:** the whole scene in order, untrimmed. It will be long and slow. **That is
  correct.** They need to see the shape before cutting it down
- **Thursday and Friday, rough cut:** cutting on action and the wide-then-close logic from Week 7. **No
  color yet.** The gray will bother them. Tell them it is supposed to
- **Monday, correction:** the order matters. **Blacks and contrast first** (the gray goes away), then
  white balance, then matching. The DT class learned the same order in Camera Raw, if anyone takes both
- **Tuesday and Wednesday, grade:** **the adjustment layer is the professional habit.** Correction lives on
  each clip, the look lives on one layer above them. That is also what makes the two exports a single
  click apart
- **Thursday and Friday:** the corrected vs. graded comparison is what question 6 asks about, and it is
  the clearest evidence of 7.2.7

## Grading, next quarter

**Out of 20, to match the edit rubric's scale.** No rubric doc yet. Ask when you want one.

| Area | Points | Full credit |
|---|---|---|
| **The cut** | 6 | Whole scene, sensible order, best takes, at least one clean cut on action |
| **Correction** | 4 | Real blacks, believable white balance, **shots from the same angle match** |
| **Grade** | 6 | **One mood**, held across every shot, skin still looks like skin |
| **Questions 5 and 6** | 4 | Names the tools, and says what the grade does to the viewer |

**The Friday rough cut is a checkpoint, not a grade.** It is how you see who is behind before color
starts. Questions 1 to 4 are completion.
