## Four rules for any text on screen

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Long enough to read it twice** | If you cannot read it out loud twice before it disappears, it is too fast |
| **Never touching the edge** | Leave a margin. Some screens crop the edges, and text jammed into a corner looks like a mistake |
| **It has to stand out from the picture** | Light text on a busy shot disappears. Add a **shadow**, an **outline**, or a **bar behind it** |
| **Two fonts, at most** | One for titles, one for everything else. A third font looks like a ransom note |

## Monday: a lower third

**First: is version 1 submitted?** If not, export it at 1080p and submit it before anything else today.

**A lower third** is the name that appears in the bottom part of the screen the first time someone
speaks. It sits in the lower third of the frame, which is where the name comes from.

1. **Text** panel, add a text box. Two lines: **a name**, and under it **who they are**
2. Move it into the **lower left**, inside the margin
3. Give it something to sit on: a **shadow**, an **outline**, or a **background** behind the text
4. Put it on screen **the first time that person speaks**, for **3 to 5 seconds**

**Documentary:** the interviewee's name and role. **Scripted scene:** the character's name, or a location
and time card, like *Hospital, 3:00 a.m.*

**1.** Who did you label, and how many seconds is it on screen? Did it pass the read-it-twice test?

## Tuesday: an animated title

**Rebuild your title card so it moves.** Select the text, then the **Animation** tab: there are **In**,
**Out**, and **Loop** animations.

- **In** is how it arrives. **Out** is how it leaves
- **Use an In and an Out. Skip Loop.** A title that never stops moving is hard to read

**2.** Which In and Out did you pick, and why do they suit **this** video?

## Wednesday: your own motion, with keyframes

**No presets today.** You already made credits scroll with keyframes. Now make one piece of text move
exactly how you want.

**The shape of good text motion is always the same: move in, hold still, move out.**

1. Pick one text element: your title, a location card, or a new one
2. **Keyframe 1:** off screen, or at 0% opacity
3. **Keyframe 2:** in position. **Keyframe 3:** same position, a few seconds later. This is the hold
4. **Keyframe 4:** off screen again, or back to 0% opacity

**The hold is the part people skip.** Text that is always moving is never readable.

**3.** What did you keyframe: position, scale, opacity, or a mix? How long is the hold?
