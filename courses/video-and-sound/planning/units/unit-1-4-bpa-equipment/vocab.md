## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **WSAP (Written Student Advancement Plan)** | The official BPA document for a specific competition event — contains deliverable specifications, scoring rubric, time limits, file format requirements, and submission instructions | Course | 2 |
| **BPA event** | A specific competitive event offered by Business Professionals of America — students submit a production or take an exam and are scored against a defined rubric | Course | 1 |
| **Virtual event** | A BPA competition event submitted online by a deadline — no qualifying round required; examples include V11 (2D Animation), V01 (Virtual Multimedia – Individual), V02 (Virtual Multimedia – Team), V15 (Virtual Interview & Portfolio) | Course | 1 |
| **Event portfolio** | A curated collection of a team's pre-production and production documentation submitted alongside the final BPA video entry | 1.1.5 | 2 |
| **Camera system** | The complete set of equipment for capturing video — camera body, lenses, memory cards, batteries, tripod, and stabilizers | 2.10.2 | 1 |
| **Mirrorless / DSLR camera** | Interchangeable-lens cameras with large sensors — used in production courses for their image quality, manual control, and diverse lens options | 2.10.2 | 1 |
| **Shotgun microphone** | A highly directional microphone mounted on a camera or boom pole — picks up sound from directly in front while rejecting sound from the sides and rear | 2.10.4 | 1 |
| **Lavalier (lav) microphone** | A small clip-on microphone worn by a subject — provides consistent close-up audio pickup; avoids handling noise | 2.10.4 | 1 |
| **Boom pole** | An extendable pole used to hold a shotgun microphone above and in front of a subject — operated by a boom operator | 2.10.4 | 1 |
| **Audio interface** | A device that converts microphone signals to a format that a computer can record — connects professional mics to a DAW | 2.10.7 | 1 |
| **NLE (Non-Linear Editor)** | Video editing software that allows access to any frame at any time — examples include Adobe Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro | 2.10.8 | 1 |
| **File naming convention** | A systematic rule for naming media files so they are organized, identifiable, and retrievable — e.g., ProjectName_Scene_Take_Date | 2.10.2 | 2 |
| **Data backup / 3-2-1 rule** | Best practice for protecting media files: 3 copies, on 2 different media types, with 1 copy offsite or in the cloud | 2.10.2 | 2 |
| **Codec** | A method of compressing and decompressing video or audio data — examples include H.264, H.265, ProRes; determines file size and quality | 2.10.8 | 2 |
| **Export settings** | The parameters used when rendering a final file — includes codec, resolution, frame rate, and audio sample rate; BPA has specific required settings | 2.10.8 | 2 |

## Bell Ringer Prompts

1. What event would you choose to compete in, and why? (Reference #430, #445, #450, #420, V11, V01)
2. A video file is labeled "video001.mp4." What is wrong with this file name from a professional standpoint? What would be better?
3. What is the 3-2-1 backup rule, and what problem does it solve?
4. What is the difference between a shotgun microphone and a lavalier? When would you use each on a shoot?
5. You just filmed a major scene and you only have one copy of the footage on the camera's memory card. What should you do before anything else?
