# Vocabulary: Pre-Production — Scripts, Storyboards & Planning

**Course:** Video and Sound (145110)
**Unit:** 1.2 — Pre-Production: Scripts, Storyboards & Planning
**Weeks:** 3–4

---

## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **Script** | A written document for a video or audio production — specifying dialogue, narration, visual directions, and timing; the blueprint for the production | 7.2.4 | 1 |
| **Two-column script format** | A script layout with video/visual instructions in the left column and audio/narration/dialogue in the right column — common for informational and news-style productions | 7.2.4 | 1 |
| **Storyboard** | A visual sequence of sketched frames representing planned camera shots in order — the visual translation of the script | 7.2.5 | 1 |
| **Shot list** | A written document listing every planned shot in a production — including shot type, camera angle, location, subject, and duration | 7.2.7 | 1 |
| **Production schedule** | A timeline that maps out when each element of the production (writing, filming, editing) will take place — includes deadlines and milestones | 7.2.9 | 1 |
| **Equipment checklist** | A document inventorying all gear needed for a shoot — cameras, lenses, lights, microphones, cables, batteries, memory cards | 7.2.10 | 1 |
| **Location scout** | The process of finding and evaluating potential shooting locations before principal photography — considering lighting, sound, access, and safety | 7.2.11 | 2 |
| **Talent release form** | A legal document signed by anyone who appears on camera giving permission to use their image in the production | 7.2.11 | 2 |
| **B-roll** | Supplemental or secondary footage used to cut away from the main interview or narrative — provides visual context | 7.3.1 | 1 |
| **A-roll** | The primary footage in a production — typically the main interview, presentation, or narrative action | 7.3.1 | 1 |
| **Logline** | A one or two sentence summary of a video production concept — describes the subject, purpose, and hook | 7.2.4 | 2 |
| **Call sheet** | A document distributed to the production crew listing the schedule, location, shot list, and crew assignments for a specific production day | 7.2.9 | 1 |
| **Production budget** | A financial plan outlining estimated costs for all elements of a production — equipment rental, talent, locations, props, post-production services | 7.2.8 | 2 |
| **Revision/feedback cycle** | The iterative process of presenting a draft of a production element (script, storyboard) to an audience, receiving structured critique, and improving the work | 7.2.5 | 3 |
| **Stakeholder feedback** | Input from the intended audience or client for a media production — used to validate concept and direction before production begins | 2.9.1 | 3 |

## Bell Ringer Prompts

1. Before you start filming, what are at least 5 things you would need to plan? Be specific.
2. What is the difference between a storyboard and a shot list? When would you use each?
3. Why is a production schedule important when working as a team? What happens if you skip it?
4. What is B-roll, and why is it important in a video production?
5. You are about to shoot a scene at a local business. What would you need to arrange before the crew arrives?
