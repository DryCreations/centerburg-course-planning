# Vocabulary: Audio Production Fundamentals

**Course:** Video and Sound (145110)
**Unit:** 1.3 — Audio Production Fundamentals
**Weeks:** 5–7

---

## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **Microphone placement** | The deliberate positioning of a microphone relative to the sound source — affects clarity, room noise, proximity effect, and overall recording quality | 7.8.1 | 2 |
| **Polar pattern** | The directional sensitivity of a microphone — how it picks up sound from different angles; common patterns are cardioid (front), omnidirectional (all around), and figure-8 | 7.8.1 | 2 |
| **Dynamic microphone** | A rugged mic type that uses electromagnetic induction — handles loud sounds well, doesn't need power; good for live recording and instruments | 7.8.1 | 1 |
| **Condenser microphone** | A sensitive mic type that requires phantom power — excellent detail capture; used for studio vocals, dialogue, and podcasting | 7.8.1 | 1 |
| **Proximity effect** | The increase in bass frequencies that occurs when a microphone is placed very close to a sound source — common in cardioid mics; can add warmth or cause muffled sound | 7.8.1 | 2 |
| **Signal chain** | The path audio travels from source to recording — microphone → preamp → audio interface → DAW; understanding the chain is essential for diagnosing problems | 7.8.2 | 2 |
| **DAW (Digital Audio Workstation)** | Software used to record, edit, mix, and produce audio — examples include GarageBand, Adobe Audition, Audacity, Logic Pro, Pro Tools | 7.8.3 | 1 |
| **Track** | A single layer of audio in a DAW — one track might contain a vocal recording; another might contain a music bed; multiple tracks are mixed together | 7.8.3 | 1 |
| **Multi-track mixing** | The process of combining and balancing multiple audio tracks — adjusting volume levels, panning, EQ, and effects to produce a unified sound | 7.8.4 | 2 |
| **EQ (Equalization)** | Adjusting the frequency balance of an audio signal — boosting or cutting bass, midrange, or treble to improve a recording or achieve a desired sound | 7.8.5 | 2 |
| **Foley** | Sound effects created and recorded in post-production to replace or enhance sounds in a production — footsteps, cloth rustling, door creaks | 7.8.6 | 1 |
| **Room tone** | The ambient background sound of a location — recorded during a shoot and used in editing to fill in audio gaps between cuts | 7.8.7 | 2 |
| **Noise reduction** | The process of removing or reducing unwanted background noise from an audio recording — done using software tools in post-production | 7.8.8 | 2 |
| **Time code** | A system of numerical labels assigned to each frame of video or audio — ensures audio tracks stay synchronized with video during editing | 2.10.5 | 2 |
| **Audio sync** | The alignment of audio and video so that sound matches the on-screen action — a clapper board or time code is used to maintain sync | 2.10.5 | 2 |
| **Levels / gain staging** | Managing the loudness of audio at each stage of the signal chain — preventing distortion (too loud) or noise (too quiet) | 7.8.2 | 2 |

## Bell Ringer Prompts

1. Listen to this 30-second audio clip. What can you tell about where it was recorded? What microphone placement techniques do you hear (or hear lacking)?
2. What is the difference between a dynamic microphone and a condenser microphone? When would you use each?
3. A student recorded dialogue, but there's a loud air conditioner hum in the background. What steps would you take to fix this?
4. What are Foley effects, and why do producers record them in post-production instead of relying on what was captured on set?
5. Why does time code matter when a camera and an external audio recorder are recording simultaneously?
