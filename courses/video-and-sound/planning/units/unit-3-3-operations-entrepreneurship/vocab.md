# Vocabulary: Operations, Entrepreneurship & Media Business

**Course:** Video and Sound (145110)
**Unit:** 3.3 — Operations, Entrepreneurship & Media Business
**Weeks:** 25–27

---

## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **Entrepreneurship** | The process of starting and operating a business by taking on financial risk in exchange for potential profit — includes identifying market opportunities, planning, and executing | 1.8.1 | 1 |
| **Production company** | A business entity that creates media content — manages clients, equipment, crew, and deliverables in exchange for payment | 1.8.3 | 1 |
| **Service contract** | A legally binding agreement between a production company and a client specifying deliverables, timeline, cost, revision policy, and ownership of the final product | 1.3.2 | 2 |
| **Scope of work** | A written description of exactly what a production company will deliver for a client — prevents misunderstandings and scope creep | 1.3.5 | 2 |
| **Scope creep** | The gradual expansion of a project beyond its original agreed-upon scope — often without additional payment; a major risk in client work | 1.3.5 | 3 |
| **Client brief** | A document provided by a client describing the purpose, audience, budget, and desired outcome of a production project | 2.9.1 | 2 |
| **Production rate / day rate** | The price charged by a production company or freelancer per day of service — a standard way to quote commercial video work | 1.8.6 | 2 |
| **Overhead** | Fixed business costs that occur regardless of revenue — equipment maintenance, software subscriptions, insurance, and facility costs | 1.8.7 | 2 |
| **Profit margin** | The amount of revenue remaining after all costs are paid — expressed as a percentage of revenue; a healthy margin sustains a business long-term | 1.8.7 | 2 |
| **Equipment maintenance** | Regular inspection, cleaning, and repair of production gear — protects the value of investment and prevents equipment failure on location | 2.10.8 | 2 |
| **Equipment insurance** | Coverage that compensates for the loss, theft, or damage of production equipment — a standard operating cost for professional productions | 1.8.8 | 2 |
| **Portfolio / reel** | A production company's primary marketing tool — shows prospective clients the range and quality of previous work | 1.1.5 | 2 |
| **Career pathway in media** | A structured sequence of roles and experiences leading to a specific position — example paths include director, DP, editor, audio engineer, producer | 1.1.4 | 1 |
| **Professional communication** | Written and verbal communication that is clear, respectful, and appropriate for a business context — emails, contracts, client presentations | 1.1.11 | 2 |
| **Self-assessment** | Regularly evaluating one's own skills, interests, and growth — a professional practice for continuous improvement | 1.1.12 | 2 |

## Bell Ringer Prompts

1. If a local business hired your production team to make a 60-second promotional video, what would you include in your service contract?
2. What is "scope creep" and why is it a financial risk for a small production company?
3. List 3 fixed costs a small video production company would need to budget for every month, regardless of how many projects they have.
4. What is the difference between a client brief and a scope of work? Which comes first?
5. Name 3 career paths in video/audio production that interest you. For each, describe one key skill you're building in this course that directly applies to that role.
