**Unit:** 1.1 — The Media Industry & Interactive Media
**Week:** 2

## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **Interactive media** | Digital content that responds to user input — allowing the user to direct the experience rather than passively receive it; examples include web video, games, apps, and VR | 7.1.1 | 1 |
| **Interactive media environment** | The platform or context in which interactive media is experienced — web browser, mobile device, gaming console, virtual reality system | 7.1.1 | 1 |
| **Interactive media genre** | A category of interactive media defined by its purpose and form — educational, gaming, simulation, social, commercial, entertainment | 7.1.5 | 1 |
| **Non-linear media** | Media where the user controls the sequence of content — web pages, interactive videos, games — as opposed to linear media (film, broadcast TV) that plays from start to finish | 7.1.2 | 2 |
| **Convergence** | The merging of previously separate media technologies and industries — e.g., smartphones combining phone, camera, video, and internet in one device | 7.1.4 | 2 |
| **Streaming** | Delivering media content over the internet in real time without requiring full download — Netflix, YouTube, Spotify | 7.1.9 | 1 |
| **Target market** | A specific, defined group of consumers or viewers a media product is designed to reach | 7.1.10 | 1 |
| **Critical review** | A structured analysis of a media product — evaluating purpose, audience, technique, effectiveness, and production quality | 7.1.6 | 3 |
| **Intellectual property (IP)** | Creative works protected by law — including video, music, images, scripts, and software code — covered by copyright, patent, or trademark | 7.1.7 | 2 |
| **Copyright** | Legal protection granted to original creative works — gives the creator exclusive rights to reproduce, distribute, and adapt the work | 1.3.8 | 2 |
| **Fair use** | A legal doctrine that allows limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes such as commentary, criticism, education, or parody | 1.3.8 | 3 |
| **Creative Commons** | A licensing system that allows creators to specify how others may use their work — useful for sourcing royalty-free music, images, and footage | 1.3.8 | 2 |
| **Royalty-free** | A type of license allowing use of media assets without paying ongoing royalties — often requires a one-time fee or is free with attribution | 1.3.8 | 2 |
| **Social/cultural implications** | The potential positive or negative effects of media on society — including representation, stereotyping, privacy, and cultural values | 7.1.8 | 3 |
| **Career pathway** | A sequence of education and work experiences that leads to a specific occupation — in media, examples include: video producer, broadcast journalist, audio engineer, animator | 1.1.4 | 1 |

---

## Key Distinctions

| Term 1 | Term 2 | Key Difference |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| Linear media | Non-linear media | Linear = fixed sequence (film, TV); Non-linear = user-controlled sequence (web, game) |
| Copyright | Fair use | Copyright = creator's exclusive right; Fair use = narrow exception for education/commentary |
| Royalty-free | Public domain | Royalty-free = licensed, may need attribution; Public domain = no restrictions at all |
| Streaming | Download | Streaming = plays in real time; Download = full file transferred first |
