# Lab: Emerging Technology Research & Hands-On Exploration

**Course:** Video and Sound (145110)
**Unit:** 3.2 — Emerging Technologies in Media
**Duration:** Weeks 23–24

---

## Objective

Research one emerging media technology at depth, present it to the class, participate in a class discussion on the IP implications of AI-generated content, and complete a hands-on experiment with an emerging production tool.

---

## Part A — Technology Research & Presentation (Week 23)

Each student or team selects ONE emerging media technology to research from the following list (or propose an approved alternative):
- AI video generation (text-to-video AI)
- Spatial audio / ambisonic recording
- Volumetric capture
- VR /360° filmmaking
- AI voice synthesis / audio deepfake
- Real-time motion capture for video production
- AI color grading / scene analysis tools

### Research Requirements

| Research Element | What to Cover |
|-----------------|--------------|
| Technology definition | What is it? How does it work conceptually? |
| Current use cases | 2–3 real-world professional applications |
| Adoption stage | Early adopter / mainstream / emerging? Why? |
| Ethical/IP implications | What questions does this technology raise about copyright, consent, or deception? |
| Impact on video/audio production | How will this change a specific job or workflow in media production? |

### Presentation Format

- 3–5 minutes (individual) or 5–7 minutes (team)
- Must include at least one visual example (screenshot, demo clip, or interactive demo if accessible)
- Conclude with 1 question for the class to discuss

---

## Part B — Class Discussion: AI-Generated Content & IP (Day 3, Week 23)

After presentations, the class holds a structured discussion on the following questions:

1. If a student uses an AI tool to generate B-roll for their production project, should they disclose this to the audience or the teacher? Why?
2. Who should own copyright on fully AI-generated content — the AI user, the AI company, or no one?
3. Where is the line between using AI as a tool (like using a camera) and replacing human creativity?
4. Should BPA competition events have a policy on AI-generated content? What would a fair policy look like?
