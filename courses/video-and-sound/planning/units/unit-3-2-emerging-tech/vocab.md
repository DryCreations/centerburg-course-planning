# Vocabulary: Emerging Technologies in Media

**Course:** Video and Sound (145110)
**Unit:** 3.2 — Emerging Technologies in Media
**Weeks:** 23–24

## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **AI-generated content** | Media — images, video, audio, or text — created or substantially modified by artificial intelligence algorithms rather than solely by human effort | 2.4.1 | 2 |
| **Generative AI** | AI systems that create new content based on training data — examples include text-to-video generators, AI image creation tools, and AI voice synthesis | 2.4.1 | 2 |
| **Deepfake** | AI-generated video or audio that realistically replaces a person's likeness or voice with someone else's — raises significant ethical and legal concerns | 2.4.1 | 3 |
| **Spatial audio** | An audio format that creates a three-dimensional sound environment — listeners perceive sound coming from specific directions; used in VR, immersive media, and ambisonic recording | 2.4.2 | 2 |
| **Ambisonic audio** | A spherical audio recording and reproduction format — designed for use with 360° video and VR; captures sound from all directions simultaneously | 2.4.2 | 2 |
| **Volumetric capture** | A technology that records a person or object from multiple angles simultaneously — producing a 3D digital representation that can be viewed from any angle; used in VR and XR | 2.4.3 | 2 |
| **Extended reality (XR)** | An umbrella term for augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and mixed reality (MR) — technologies that blend or replace the physical environment with digital content | 2.4.3 | 2 |
| **VR filmmaking** | Creating video content specifically designed to be viewed with a virtual reality headset — typically uses 360° cameras and spatial audio | 2.4.3 | 2 |
| **360° video** | Video captured in all directions simultaneously using omnidirectional cameras — viewed in VR headset or as interactive web video | 2.4.3 | 1 |
| **Ethical use of AI in media** | Principles governing responsible AI content creation — includes disclosure, consent, avoiding deception, and respecting intellectual property of training data | 1.7.13 | 3 |
| **IP in AI-generated content** | The unresolved legal question of who owns AI-generated work — the AI tool, the user who prompted it, or no one — and whether training data ownership is respected | 1.7.13 | 3 |
| **Automation bias** | The tendency to over-trust automated systems — in media, the risk of accepting AI-generated content without critical evaluation or ethical consideration | 2.4.4 | 3 |
| **Technology adoption cycle** | The pattern by which society adopts new technologies — from early adopters to late majority — applies to new media production tools | 2.4.4 | 2 |

## Bell Ringer Prompts

1. Name one emerging media technology you have personally encountered in the last year. How did it change the experience of the content?
2. If an AI tool generates a video from your text prompt, who owns the copyright to that video — you, the AI company, or no one?
3. What is a deepfake, and what are two specific harms it could cause in a news or political context?
4. What is the difference between spatial audio and standard stereo audio? Give an example of where spatial audio would improve the experience.
5. Should AI-generated content in a media production be disclosed to the audience? Why or why not?
