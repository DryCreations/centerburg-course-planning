**Weeks:** 13–18

## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **Project proposal** | A formal document that defines the scope, purpose, timeline, team roles, and requirements for a production — presented to stakeholders for approval before work begins | 2.9.1 | 2 |
| **Team roles** | Defined responsibilities within a production team — director, cinematographer (camera operator), audio engineer, editor; may overlap in small teams | 2.9.2 | 1 |
| **Production timeline** | A week-by-week or day-by-day schedule of all production tasks — pre-production, principal photography, post-production, review, and submission | 2.9.3 | 2 |
| **Production log** | A daily or session-by-session record of what was accomplished, footage captured, and issues encountered during production | 2.9.4 | 2 |
| **Principal photography** | The main video-capture phase of a production — when planned scenes, interviews, or action sequences are filmed | 7.7.5 | 1 |
| **Cutaway** | A shot that cuts away from the main action to show a related detail — used editorially to add context, cover edits, and control pacing | 7.7.6 | 2 |
| **Jump cut** | An edit between two shots of the same subject with slightly different framing or timing — creates a jarring, disorienting effect; typically unintentional unless used for stylistic effect | 7.7.7 | 2 |
| **Continuity** | The consistency of visual elements (props, lighting, costumes, actor positions) from shot to shot — maintains believability | 7.7.8 | 2 |
| **Transition** | The method used to move from one clip to the next — cut, dissolve, wipe, fade; a cut is the most common and least distracting | 7.3.7 | 1 |
| **Color grading** | Adjusting the color, contrast, and tone of video footage in post-production to achieve a desired look or consistent visual style | 7.3.8 | 2 |
| **Color correction** | Fixing technical color problems in footage (wrong white balance, overexposure) — done before creative color grading | 7.3.8 | 2 |
| **Lower third** | A graphic or text element placed in the lower portion of the screen — used for names, titles, locations | 7.3.9 | 1 |
| **Title sequence / credits** | On-screen text elements at the beginning and end of a production — identify the project, creators, and sources | 7.3.9 | 1 |
| **Sound mix** | Balancing and combining all audio tracks (dialogue, music bed, SFX) in post-production to create the final audio of the video | 7.3.10 | 2 |
| **Rough cut** | An early version of an edited video — all clips are in order but not yet fine-tuned for pacing, transitions, or audio polish | 7.3.11 | 1 |
| **Fine cut** | A near-final version of the edit — clips trimmed, transitions smooth, audio mixed, color corrected; awaiting final approval | 7.3.11 | 2 |

## Bell Ringer Prompts

1. What is the difference between a rough cut and a fine cut? At which stage would you share your video with your teacher for feedback?
2. Your team interviews someone on video, but every cut between interview clips has a jarring jump. What is this problem called, and how do you fix it?
3. What is the difference between color correction and color grading? In what order do you do them?
4. A lower third graphic is used in the video — what information does it typically display?
5. Your team has 2 weeks for principal photography. You've shot all of the planned dialogue but forgot to capture B-roll. Why does this matter in the editing phase?
