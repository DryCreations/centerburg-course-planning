## Questions

| # | Question | A | B | C | D | Answer | DOK | Standard |

| 1 | A project proposal is submitted before production begins in order to: | Avoid having to create a storyboard | Define the scope, purpose, timeline, team roles, and requirements — establishing shared expectations and getting stakeholder approval | Replace the need for a production log | Submit early to BPA | B | 2 | 2.9.1 |
| 2 | In a small 2-person video production team, which statement is most accurate about roles? | One person handles all work while the other watches | Roles are rigidly fixed — one person only films, one only edits | Roles may overlap — both students may share filming and editing responsibilities while maintaining clear primary accountabilities | Each person only works on the parts they like | C | 2 | 2.9.2 |
| 3 | A "production log" is kept during principal photography to: | Replace the pre-production script | Record daily progress, footage captured, problems encountered, and decisions made — creating an accountability trail for the team | Automatically upload footage to the editing computer | Communicate with the BPA judges | B | 2 | 2.9.4 |
| 4 | A "jump cut" in a video edit is: | A planned, stylistic quick-cut technique always intended by directors | An abrupt edit between two shots of the same subject where there is not enough change in framing or action, causing a jarring visual break | The cut from A-roll to B-roll | A wipe transition | B | 2 | 7.7.7 |
| 5 | Color correction and color grading are performed in this order during post-production: | Color grade first, then color correction | They are done simultaneously | Color correction first (fix technical problems) — then color grade (apply creative look) | Color correction only — grading is optional | C | 2 | 7.3.8 |
| 6 | A "lower third" graphic is used in video to: | Cover up an error in the shot | Display identifying text (names, titles, locations) in the lower portion of the screen | Add a blur effect to the background | Transition between scenes | B | 1 | 7.3.9 |
| 7 | The "sound mix" phase of post-production involves: | Adding all visual titles and graphics | Balancing and combining all audio tracks (dialogue, music, SFX) to create the final audio layer of the video | Recording new dialogue | Selecting the export codec | B | 1 | 7.3.10 |
| 8 | The difference between a "rough cut" and a "fine cut" is: | There is no meaningful difference — both are final | A rough cut has all clips in order but is unpolished; a fine cut is trimmed to exact timing, with smooth transitions, color, and audio — nearly final | A rough cut is used for BPA submission | A fine cut must be approved by a professional | B | 2 | 7.3.11 |
| 9 | "Continuity" in video production refers to: | Filming continuously without stopping | The consistency of visual details (lighting, props, actor position) from shot to shot — so cuts appear seamless | Using one camera the entire production | Submitting the same project for multiple BPA events | B | 2 | 7.7.8 |
| 10 | **[HOT]** A team finishes their rough cut but notices two problems: (1) All interviews have a slightly orange tint from indoor tungsten lighting, and (2) the interview footage feels flat and low-contrast. They want to solve both. What is the correct sequence and approach? | Grade the interviews first for contrast, then correct the color temperature | First apply color correction to fix the white balance/orange tint — then apply color grading to add contrast and the desired visual look | Export at maximum quality first, then fix color in a separate program | Both corrections are done in the audio mixing session | B | 3 | 7.3.8 |
