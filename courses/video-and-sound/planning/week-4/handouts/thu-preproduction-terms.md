# Thursday: The Preproduction Documents

**Video & Sound, Week 4, Thursday.** Before you shoot anything, you write three things. They are not the same
thing, and people mix them up constantly. Here is what each one actually is.

---

## The three documents

| Document | Answers | Looks like | Written by |
|----------|---------|------------|------------|
| **Script** | What is SAID and what HAPPENS | Text, in scenes | Writer |
| **Storyboard** | What each shot LOOKS like | Drawings, one box per shot | Director / storyboard artist |
| **Shot list** | Every shot we have to GET, and what it needs | A table, one row per shot | Director / AD |

**The short version:** the script is the words, the storyboard is the pictures, the shot list is the plan.

---

## 1. The Script

What is said and what happens. If nobody talks in your video, you still have a script: it just describes the
action and any narration or text on screen.

**Two kinds you will run into:**

- **Screenplay format:** scene heading, action, character name, dialogue. Used for anything with actors.
- **Two column (A/V) format:** VIDEO on the left, AUDIO on the right, lined up so you can see what is heard
  while each thing is seen. Used constantly for documentary, news, promos, and PSAs. **This is probably your
  format.**

**Two column example:**

| VIDEO | AUDIO |
|-------|-------|
| Wide shot, empty hallway, early morning | Ambient hallway sound, footsteps in the distance |
| Close up, hands opening a locker | NARRATOR: "Most people never notice the first ten minutes." |
| Over the shoulder, student reading a note | Music fades up |

**Rule:** if you cannot see it or hear it, it does not belong in a script. "She feels nervous" is not
filmable. "She checks the clock three times" is.

---

## 2. The Storyboard

One box per shot, drawn. **You do not have to be able to draw.** Stick figures and boxes are the professional
standard for a rough board. What matters is:

- **Framing:** how much of the subject is in the box
- **An arrow** for any movement, of the subject or the camera
- **A label** under each box: shot number, shot type, and a few words

Storyboards exist so everyone on set pictures the same thing. If two people picture different shots, you find
that out with a pencil, not with a camera and a lost afternoon.

---

## 3. The Shot List

The one you will actually hold while shooting. Every shot gets a row, and you check them off.

| # | Description (what we SEE) | Type | Location | Equipment | Got it |
|---|---------------------------|------|----------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | Hands opening the locker | Close up | Main hallway | Camera, tripod | |
| 2 | Student walking away down the hall | Wide | Main hallway | Camera | |
| 3 | Over the shoulder as they read the note | OTS | Main hallway | Camera, tripod | |

**The shot list is not the script in a table.** The script is in story order. The shot list is in **shooting
order**, grouped by location, because you shoot everything in one hallway while you are standing in that
hallway, no matter where those shots land in the finished video.

---

## Shot types to use

| Type | What it shows | Use it to |
|------|---------------|-----------|
| **Wide (WS)** | The whole scene and where you are | Establish the location |
| **Medium (MS)** | Waist up | Normal conversation, the workhorse shot |
| **Close up (CU)** | Face or an object filling the frame | Emotion, or an important detail |
| **Extreme close up (ECU)** | Part of a face, or a small detail | Emphasis, tension |
| **Over the shoulder (OTS)** | From behind one person, looking at another | Conversation, point of view |
| **Cutaway** | Something else in the scene | Cover an edit, add context |

## The vocabulary check

Cover the table above and answer:

1. Which document tells you what a shot **looks like**?
2. Which document do you carry with you and check off **while shooting**?
3. Why is the shot list in a different order than the script?
4. What does OTS stand for, and what does it show?
5. Your video has no dialogue at all. Do you still need a script? Why?
6. Name one thing that belongs in a script and one thing that does not.

---

## Do Now

Using your one chosen idea:

1. Write a **two column script** for the whole thing. Video left, audio right.
2. Turn it into a **shot list**: number, description of what we SEE, shot type, location, equipment.
3. Aim for **6 to 12 shots** for a 30 to 90 second video.
4. Group the shot list by **location**, not story order.

Storyboards come next.

## If You Finish Early

1. Thumbnail sketch your first three shots. Rough boxes, arrows for movement.
2. Write your production schedule: what gets filmed on which day.
3. Mark the one shot you think will be hardest, and write why.
4. Add a cutaway to your list for each location. You will want them in the edit and you will not think of it
   later.
