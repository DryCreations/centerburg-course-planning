# Splitting Audio and Layering Video

**Video & Sound Production | Week 8**

Two skills, and they turn out to be the same idea: **a timeline has more than one layer, and picture and
sound do not have to move together.**

## Part 1: Splitting audio from video

Every clip you drop on the timeline arrives as **one object with picture and sound locked together.**
The first thing you do is break that.

**Find the control that detaches or extracts the audio.** Right-click the clip and look for **Detach
audio**, or **Separate audio**. The sound drops onto its own track below the video.

**Once they are separate:**

- You can trim the picture without touching the sound
- You can trim the sound without touching the picture
- **That is all a J-cut or an L-cut is**

### The J-cut and L-cut, mechanically

```
  J-CUT              L-CUT
  PIC [ A    ][ B ]  PIC [ A ][ B    ]
  AUD [ A ][ B    ]  AUD [ A    ][ B ]
```

**Drag the audio edge past the picture edge.** Half a second is usually enough. **Adjust in tenths, not
seconds.**

**1.** Detach the audio on one clip. Then build one J-cut. **Write the timecode.**

**2.** Build one L-cut. Write the timecode.

**3.** Play both. Which one felt more natural, and where in the scene was it?

## Part 2: Layering video

The timeline has **more than one video track.** A clip on the track above **covers** the clip below it
for as long as it lasts.

**That is the whole mechanism.** Everything below is a use for it.

### A cutaway is just a clip on the track above

You already planned cutaways on your storyboard. Here is how one is actually made:

1. Leave the main clip on the bottom track, **with its audio**
2. Put the cutaway clip on **the track above**, over the moment you want covered
3. **Mute or delete the cutaway's audio.** The sound from underneath keeps running
4. Trim it to one or two seconds

**The audience hears the scene continue while they look at something else.** That is why it hides a bad
cut.

**4.** Find your worst cut. Cover it with a cutaway on the upper track. **Did it disappear?**

**5.** Why does the cutaway need its audio removed? What happens if you leave it in?

### Other things layering does

| | How |
|---|---|
| **Titles over footage** | Text on an upper track instead of on black |
| **Picture in picture** | Upper clip scaled down and positioned in a corner |
| **Split screen** | Two clips, one above the other, each cropped to half the frame |
| **Logo or watermark** | A still image on the top track for the whole piece |
| **A flash or a fade between shots** | A solid color clip on an upper track, briefly |

**6.** Try one of those five. Say which, and whether it improved the piece or just decorated it.

## Part 3: What order the tracks go in

**Top covers bottom.** If something has disappeared, it is because something above it is in the way.

**7.** Put a clip on the upper track, then move it below the main one. **What happens, and why?**

**8.** You want your title to appear over a shot, but you cannot see the title. **What is wrong?**

## Audio layers work the same way

Audio tracks **mix** rather than cover. Two audio clips playing at once produce both sounds at once.

**9.** Why does that mean you have to think about **levels** as soon as you add a second audio track?

**10.** If you add music under dialogue, which one should be louder, and by a lot or a little?

## Words

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Detach / separate audio** | Breaking a clip's sound onto its own track |
| **Track** | One layer of the timeline. Video tracks stack, audio tracks mix |
| **Upper track** | Covers whatever is below it, for as long as it lasts |
| **Cutaway** | A shot on the upper track, covering a moment in the clip below |
| **J-cut / L-cut** | Audio starting early, or running late, relative to the picture |
| **Offset** | How far apart the picture edit and the sound edit sit |
| **Picture in picture** | A smaller clip laid over a full-frame one |
| **Levels** | How loud each audio track is, relative to the others |
| **Mix** | The combined result of every audio track playing together |
