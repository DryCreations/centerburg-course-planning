## The format change, and how to frame it

Last week they were taught the **two-column A/V** format. This week is **screenplay format**, which is what
Unit 1.2 specifies and what BPA expects.

**Do not present this as a correction.** Both formats are real and professional. Frame it as: two-column is
for narrated work (documentary, promo, PSA), screenplay is for anything with characters and scenes, and the
shot list they already wrote works with either. There is a table on this at the top of the handout.

## What to insist on

**Action must be filmable.** This is the whole lesson and the thing they will fight. "She is nervous" is not
a shot. "She checks the clock three times" is.

The drill's answer key lists twelve problems in the bad scene. Do not hand it out first. Let them find eight
on their own, then reveal.

## What not to insist on

- **Exact margins.** Consistency is enough in a Doc. Do not lose a period to tab stops.
- **Perfect dialogue.** First drafts sound written. That is what the table read is for.
- **Length beyond the 2 to 3 page range.** Short and finished beats long and abandoned. Cut hard.

## Differentiation

- **Blank page paralysis:** give them the scene list first. A script is much easier once the scenes exist.
- **No dialogue in their idea:** fine. Action-only screenplays are real. They still need scene headings and
  filmable action.
- **Way over length:** ask which scene could go entirely. Never trim evenly across everything.
- **Absent:** `screenplay-format.md` has the full example page and the mistake table. Self-serve.
