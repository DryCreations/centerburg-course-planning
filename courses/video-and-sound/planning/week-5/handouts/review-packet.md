# Backup Summary

**Use this only if the links will not load.** Type `USED BACKUP` at the top of Part 1.

## Transitions

| Transition | What it is | What it says |
|------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Cut** | Two clips placed back to back, no effect | Nothing. Time continues. The vast majority of edits |
| **Dissolve** | One shot fades into the next, typically about 1 to 2 seconds | Time passed, or these two things are connected |
| **Fade to black** | The picture fades out to black | A chapter ended. Bigger than a dissolve |
| **Fade from black** | The picture fades up from black | We are beginning |
| **Wipe** | One shot pushes the other off screen | A deliberate, stylized change of place. Energetic, not subtle |
| **Match cut** | A cut between two shots that share a shape or motion | These two things are linked |
| **J and L cut** | Audio from one shot starts before or continues after the picture changes | Smooths a conversation so it does not feel chopped |

**The rule underneath all of it:** the cut is the default. Everything else is a choice you should be able to
explain. A transition the audience notices had better be one you wanted them to notice.

## Framing

| Idea | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Rule of thirds** | Divide the frame into nine with two lines each way. Put your subject on a line or a crossing point, not dead center. Eyes usually sit near the upper third line |
| **Headroom** | The space above the head. Too much and they sink in the frame, too little and it feels cramped |
| **Lead room** | Space in front of where someone is looking or moving. Without it they look boxed in by the edge |
| **Leading lines** | Lines in the scene that pull the eye toward the subject: hallways, rails, rows of lockers, a row of windows |
| **Depth** | Something in the foreground, middle, and background, so the shot does not look flat |
| **Framing within the frame** | Shooting through a doorway, a window, or between two objects to draw the eye in |
| **Balance** | How weight is distributed left to right. A subject far to one side with nothing opposite feels like it is tipping |

**And underneath that:** these are defaults, not laws. Breaking one on purpose creates an effect. Breaking one
by accident just looks wrong, and the audience cannot tell which you did unless the rest of the shot is
clearly deliberate.
