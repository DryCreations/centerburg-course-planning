# Screenplay Format

**Video & Sound, Week 5.** This is the format the industry uses, and the one BPA video production events
expect. It looks fussy at first. The rules exist so that anyone can pick up your script and know instantly
who is speaking, where they are, and what the camera sees.

**Were you absent? This page has everything.** Read it, look at the example, then write.

---

## Why not the two-column format we did last week?

Both are real. They do different jobs.

| Format | Used for | Why |
|--------|----------|-----|
| **Two column (A/V)** | Documentary, news, promos, PSAs, anything narrated | Shows exactly what is heard while each thing is seen |
| **Screenplay** | Anything with characters, scenes, and dialogue | Reads like a story, and one page is roughly one minute of screen time |

Last week's two-column work was not wasted. It is the same thinking, laid out differently, and your shot
list still works with either. **This week we write screenplay format,** because it is the industry standard
and it is what competition asks for.

---

## The five building blocks

### 1. Scene heading (also called a slugline)

Always **ALL CAPS**, always the same three parts, in this order:

```
INT. HALLWAY - MORNING
```

- **INT.** or **EXT.** Interior or exterior. Inside or outside.
- **LOCATION.** Where. Be specific and reuse the exact same name every time.
- **TIME.** DAY, NIGHT, MORNING, CONTINUOUS.

A new scene heading means the camera has moved somewhere new. **Every time the location or time changes, you
need a new one.**

```
EXT. PARKING LOT - DAY
INT. GYM - NIGHT
INT. MRS. DOYLE'S CLASSROOM - CONTINUOUS
```

### 2. Action

Plain sentences, **present tense**, describing only what we can **see and hear**.

```
Maya shoulders the door open. The hallway is empty. She checks
the clock, then checks it again.
```

**The rule that catches everyone:** if a camera cannot record it, it does not belong in action.

| Do not write | Write instead |
|--------------|---------------|
| Maya is nervous | Maya checks the clock three times |
| He remembers last summer | He stops at the photo on the wall |
| She has always hated this room | She sits in the back, farthest from the door |
| It is awkward | Nobody speaks. Someone coughs. |

### 3. Character name

**ALL CAPS**, on its own line, **centered above the dialogue.** The first time a character appears in the
action, put their name in caps too, then use normal caps after that.

### 4. Dialogue

Directly under the character name, in a narrower column than the action.

```
                    MAYA
          You were supposed to be here an hour ago.
```

### 5. Parenthetical

A short note about **how** a line is delivered, in parentheses, between the name and the line. **Use these
almost never.** If your dialogue only works with a parenthetical, rewrite the dialogue.

```
                    MAYA
              (not looking up)
          I waited.
```

## A transition, if you need one

`CUT TO:` or `FADE OUT.` at the right margin. Modern scripts use very few. **Do not put one between every
scene.** A new scene heading already means we cut.

---

## A full page, formatted

```
FADE IN:

INT. CENTERBURG HIGH HALLWAY - EARLY MORNING

Rows of lockers. The lights are half on. MAYA (16, backpack on
one shoulder) walks the length of the hall alone. She stops at
locker 214.

A folded note is wedged in the vent.

She looks both ways before she takes it.

                    MAYA
          Okay.

She unfolds it. Reads. Reads it again.

                    MAYA (CONT'D)
              (quietly)
          That's not funny.

DEV (16, already in gym clothes) rounds the corner behind her.

                    DEV
          Talking to yourself again?

Maya folds the note into her pocket in one motion.

                    MAYA
          Nope.

                    DEV
          Uh huh.

He keeps walking. Maya waits until he is gone, then pulls the
note back out.

CUT TO:

EXT. PARKING LOT - CONTINUOUS

Dev stops at the door of his car. He pulls an identical folded
note out of his own pocket.
```

## Margins, roughly

You do not need to measure. If you use a screenwriting tool it handles this. If you are in Google Docs, just
be **consistent**:

| Element | Where it sits |
|---------|---------------|
| Scene heading | Hard left |
| Action | Hard left |
| Character name | Indented well right, roughly centered |
| Parenthetical | Slightly left of the character name |
| Dialogue | Indented, but narrower than action |
| Transition | Right side |

**Courier or Courier New, 12 point.** It looks old fashioned on purpose: in Courier, one page really is
about one minute of screen time, which is how anyone can guess a film's length by weighing the script.

## Your assignment this week

**2 to 3 pages**, which is roughly **2 to 3 minutes** of screen time. That is short. It is meant to be.

1. Take the idea you picked last week
2. Break it into **scenes.** Every location or time change is a new scene heading.
3. Write the action in present tense, only what we see and hear
4. Write the dialogue, if it has any
5. Read it out loud. Every line. **Out loud.** Dialogue that looks fine on a page often sounds nothing like
   a person.

---

## The most common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Writing what a character thinks or feels | Show it as an action a camera can see |
| Camera directions everywhere ("ZOOM IN ON...") | That is the shot list's job, not the script's |
| A parenthetical on every line | Delete almost all of them, rewrite the line instead |
| Scene headings that change names (HALLWAY, then HALL, then MAIN HALL) | Pick one name and reuse it exactly |
| A new scene heading for every shot | A scene heading means a new place or time, not a new angle |
| Dialogue that explains things people already know | People do not tell each other what they both already know |
| Five pages instead of three | Cut. Short and finished beats long and abandoned. |

---

## If You Finish Early

1. **Read it out loud with a partner**, each of you taking parts. Mark every line that made you stumble.
2. Count your pages. If you are over 3, find the scene that could go entirely.
3. Go back through and delete any action line that is not something a camera could record.
4. Start mapping which of your shot list entries covers which part of the script.
