## The lookup rules are the point

The packet marks every section **GREEN** (look it up freely), **YELLOW** (try from memory, then check, then
mark what you changed), or **RED** (closed note).

**Worth saying out loud once:** looking something up is not cheating. What hurts you is writing a right
answer you could not have produced yourself and never noticing. The YELLOW instruction to **mark what you
changed** is the whole mechanism, and Part 4's "circle what you cannot get" produces each student's personal
study list for Friday.

## Part 1: Transitions

1. Two clips back to back with no effect. It is invisible, it does not interrupt, and it keeps time moving
   continuously, which is what almost every moment of a film needs.
2. That time has passed, or that two things are connected. A cut says "and then." A dissolve says "later" or
   "meanwhile."
3. One shot pushes the other off screen. It is stylized and energetic, and the audience is meant to see it,
   which is the opposite of a dissolve's softness.
4. Cut → nothing, time continues. Dissolve → time passed or these are linked. Fade to black → a chapter
   ended. Wipe → a deliberate, playful change of place.
5. Roughly 1 to 2 seconds. Longer feels slower and more reflective, shorter is nearly a cut.
6. Accept anything real from the reading: match cut, smash cut, J cut, L cut, iris, whip pan.
7. **The best answer:** a transition that draws attention pulls the audience out of the story and makes them
   think about the editing. It also signals that the editor did not trust the shots. Accept any version that
   gets to "it distracts" or "it does the shot's job for it."
8. Push for a real example. Good ones: a comedy where a goofy wipe is the joke, a montage where dissolves
   carry time passing, a hard smash cut used as a shock.

## Part 2: Framing

9. Two lines each way dividing the frame into nine. Subject on a line or an intersection rather than dead
   center. Eyes near the upper third line.
10. Space above the head. Too much: they sink, and there is dead space up top. Too little: cramped, and the
    head feels cut off.
11. Space in front of where someone looks or moves. Without it they look boxed in by the frame edge and the
    shot feels wrong even to people who cannot say why.
12. Lines that pull the eye toward the subject. In this building: a hallway's floor tiles, a row of lockers,
    stair rails, ceiling light rows, the line of windows.
13. Any two from the reading: depth, framing within the frame, balance, symmetry, negative space.
14. **Look at the sketches.** Two rough boxes with a label is enough. The label is the assessment.
15. Push for a purpose, not just "it looks cool." Dead center for symmetry and confrontation. No lead room
    deliberately to make someone feel trapped. Extreme headroom to make someone feel small.
16. Self-assessment. Any honest answer counts.

## Part 3: Apply it

**This is the graded part.** What to look for:

- **Question 17 is the diagnostic.** If a student's video is mostly cuts, they understood the reading. If
  they have a dissolve between every shot, they did not, and this is where you catch it.
- **Question 18** is where a real need for a dissolve or fade shows up, if there is one.
- The framing tables should name a specific location feature, not a generic answer. "The lockers behind her
  make a leading line" is understanding. "Use leading lines" is copying.
- **Question 19 is the best question in the packet.** Critiquing their own footage in the new vocabulary is
  the whole transfer. Anyone who says "I would reshoot it because there is no lead room and she is looking
  off the edge" has learned something this period.

## Part 4: Self-check

20. 1/50 and 1/60. The **180 degree rule.**
21. Brightness would drift mid-shot. Unfixable.
22. The camera hunts, racking focus in and out mid-shot. Unfixable.
23. Auto shifts between takes, so shots do not match when cut together.
24. Autofocus on; auto exposure on; auto white balance; shutter too fast; clipped audio.
25. All five are **set before you press record**, and none can be repaired afterward.
26. Open the aperture → raise ISO → add light. **Never** slow the shutter below the frame rate rule.
27. 30 seconds of ambient sound with nobody talking. The room will not sound the same on another day.
28. Wide → establish location. Medium → the workhorse. Close up → emotion or detail. OTS → from behind one
    person looking at another. Cutaway → cover an edit.
29. Enough angles to have options in the edit. You cannot go back, and an edit that only works one way
    usually does not work.
30. Details consistent across shots. Example: a full cup in the wide, empty in the close up.
31. INT. or EXT. → location → time.
32. You shoot everything in a location while standing in it. Moving setups is the expensive part.
33. Everything you can film in one place, in one session, without moving.
34. 30 minutes. Add half again for what you cannot predict.
35. It does not say whose, what, or which take. Better: `LastName_Project_Shot03_Take2.MOV`

### Applied

36. **Auto exposure was left on.** Not fixable. Set manual exposure before recording.
37. Motion will look **stuttery and harsh**. At 30fps it should be **1/60**. For the bright room they should
    have **closed the aperture** and **lowered ISO**, not raised the shutter.
38. Shoot **by location**: all three hallway shots, then the gym, then outside. Realistically the hallway
    block plus room tone in 45 minutes. Credit any answer that groups by location and is honest about not
    finishing everything.

## Grading

**Parts 1 to 3 are the assignment.** Completion plus a read of questions 7, 15, 17, and 19, which are where
thinking shows up rather than copying.

**Part 4 is not graded.** It is a study guide that produces a personal list of gaps. Tell them that plainly,
or they will treat it as a test and copy from their handouts, which defeats it.

## What to watch for while circulating

- Students copying the reading's wording straight into questions 7 and 15. Those two ask for their own take.
  Ask them to say it out loud instead, then write that.
- A shot list with a dissolve between every clip. Question 17 catches it, but you can catch it faster by
  looking.
- Anyone who finishes Part 3 in ten minutes. They skipped the "try, then check, then mark what changed" step.
