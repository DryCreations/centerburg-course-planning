# Review Packet: Answer Key

**Teacher-only.**

## Section 1: Camera setup

1. Brightness drifting mid-shot as light changes. Unfixable afterward.
2. 24fps → 1/50. 30fps → 1/60. 60fps → 1/125.
3. The **180 degree rule.** Doubling the frame rate gives motion blur that looks the way the eye expects.
   Faster looks stuttery, slower smears.
4. **Aperture and ISO.**
5. Open the aperture → raise ISO → add light.
6. **Noise**, which is uglier in video than in a still.
7. The camera **hunts**, racking focus in and out mid-shot. Unfixable.
8. Auto **shifts between takes**, so shots do not match when cut together.
9. **T4i, T5, T6.**
10. **Shoot ten seconds and play it back.**

## Section 2: The five unfixables

Autofocus left on (image pulses in and out of focus) · auto exposure left on (brightness drifts) · auto white
balance (shots do not match) · shutter too fast (stuttery, harsh motion) · clipped audio (distorted,
crackling).

**In common:** all five are **set before you press record**, and none can be repaired afterward.

## Section 3: Shot types

11. Wide → establish where we are. Medium → the workhorse, normal conversation. Close up → emotion or an
    important detail. OTS → from behind one person looking at another. Cutaway → cover an edit or add context.
12. Accept any sensible three, e.g. a wide to establish the space, an OTS on each person for the conversation,
    and a cutaway for editing flexibility.
13. **Coverage:** shooting a scene from enough angles to have options in the edit. More than you think,
    because you cannot go back, and an edit that only works one way usually does not work.
14. **Continuity:** keeping details consistent across shots. Example: a cup is full in the wide and empty in
    the close up, or a jacket is on in one shot and off in the next.

## Section 4: Preproduction documents

15. Script: what is said and what happens. Storyboard: what each shot looks like. Shot list: every shot to
    get, with type, location, and equipment. Call sheet: who is where, when, with what. Production schedule:
    what gets filmed on which day.
16. Because you shoot everything in one location while you are standing in it. Moving setups is the expensive
    part.
17. **INT. or EXT.** → **location** → **time.**
18. Accept any filmable rewrite. E.g. "Maya checks the clock three times." "He stops at the photo on the
    wall." "Nobody speaks. Someone coughs."
19. It stops being a writing project and becomes a **shooting document** the whole crew works from. Changes
    after lock have to be small.
20. Documentary, news, promos, PSAs, anything **narrated**, where you need to see what is heard alongside
    what is seen.

## Section 5: Scheduling

21. Everything you can film **in one place, in one session, without moving.**
22. **10 to 15 minutes**, including setup and a few takes.
23. **30 minutes.** Add half again, because of things you cannot predict: a bell, a door, people walking
    through, a battery, a shot that looked fine until playback.
24. Any three: waiting for a location to clear, moving setups, battery or card problems, people not being
    where they said, retakes, forgetting room tone and going back.
25. Any five: who is where and when, shots in shooting order, gear list, camera settings the whole group
    matches, location, date, and the before-you-leave checklist.

## Section 6: On set

26. 30 seconds of a location's ambient sound with nobody talking. Needed to fill gaps and smooth cuts in the
    edit. You cannot go back because the room will not sound the same on a different day.
27. Cards get reused, footage gets lost, and you want it backed up before anything happens to the card.
28. It does not say **whose** it is, **what** it is, or **which take.** Better:
    `LastName_Project_Shot03_Take2.MOV`
29. Because you cannot go back, and the take that looked fine often is not on playback.
30. Any two: room tone recorded, footage played back and checked, everything offloaded, gear accounted for,
    batteries on the charger.

## Section 7: Applied

31. **Auto exposure was left on.** Not fixable. They should have set manual exposure before recording.
32. Shoot **by location, all three hallway shots together**, then move to the gym, then outside. Realistically
    in 45 minutes: setup and the three hallway shots, with room tone. The gym and outside are a second session.
    Credit any answer that groups by location and is honest about not finishing.
33. Motion will look **stuttery and harsh**, because the shutter is far faster than double the frame rate. At
    30fps it should be **1/60**. To handle the bright room they should have **closed the aperture** (higher f
    number) and **lowered ISO**, not raised the shutter.
34. Options: rewrite the scene for a location they can reach, cut the scene, or shoot a stand-in location that
    reads the same on camera. Credit any answer that picks one and justifies it. **Rewriting for a reachable
    location is usually right**, since a scene that cannot be shot is not a scene.

## Section 8: Vocabulary

Accept reasonable definitions in students' own words.

## Grading

**Completion, plus a spot check.** These are review, not an assessment. Sections 7 and 2 are where you learn
the most about who actually understands it.

The most useful single answer on the packet is **31**: a student who says "auto exposure, not fixable, set
manual" has the core idea of the week.
