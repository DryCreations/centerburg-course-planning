# Setting Up the Camera for Video

**Video & Sound, Week 5.** Our cameras are **Canon Rebel** bodies: T4i, T5, T6, and T3. They are not
identical, and the menus differ slightly, so **check your specific body** rather than assuming the person
next to you has the same options.

**This is the checklist you run before every single shoot.** Get it wrong and no amount of editing fixes it.

---

## Before anything: know which body you have

The model name is printed on the front, under the lens, or on the back near the screen.

| Body | Manual video exposure | Best video it shoots |
|------|----------------------|----------------------|
| **T4i** | **Yes** | 1080p at 30, 25, or 24fps. 720p at 60fps |
| **T5** | **Yes** | 1080p at 30, 25, or 24fps. 720p at 60fps |
| **T6** | **Yes** | 1080p at 30, 25, or 24fps. 720p at 60fps |
| **T3** | **No** | 720p at 30fps, auto exposure only |

### The three manual bodies are the shooting cameras

**The T3 cannot set shutter speed, aperture, or ISO in movie mode.** It decides for itself, and it will drift
mid-shot. That means it cannot do the thing this unit is about.

**So there are three shooting cameras**, and they get shared. Everything in this handout assumes one of the
three.

**The T3 still has jobs:**

- **Behind the scenes stills** for the production package
- **A second angle** on a shot where the exposure does not have to match
- **Framing practice** while you wait for a shooting camera

> **Do not use the T3 for footage that has to cut together with manual footage.** The exposure will not
> match, and there is no way to make it match afterward.

### None of these shoot 1080p60

If you want slow motion you drop to **720p at 60fps** and accept the lower resolution. **Decide that before
you shoot, not after**, and everyone in your group shoots the same setting.

## The setup, in order

### 1. Get into movie mode

Turn the mode dial to the **movie** position (the little camera icon, usually past the green auto). On these
bodies that flips the mirror up and puts a live image on the back screen.

**The viewfinder goes black in movie mode.** That is normal. You frame on the screen.

### 2. Set it to manual

In the menu, set movie exposure to **Manual**. Auto will hunt: the brightness will drift up and down mid-shot
every time someone walks past a window, and that is unfixable in the edit.

**Manual means you set all three:** shutter speed, aperture, and ISO.

### 3. Resolution and frame rate

Menu, movie recording size.

| Pick | When |
|------|------|
| **1920x1080, 24fps** | The default for anything that should feel like a film |
| **1920x1080, 30fps** | The default for anything that should feel like video, news, or a promo |
| **1280x720, 60fps** | Only when you plan to slow it down |

**Pick one and use it for the entire project.** Mixing frame rates inside one video causes problems in the
edit that are not worth the trouble.

### 4. Shutter speed: the 180 degree rule

This is the one rule people do not know, and it is why amateur video looks wrong.

> **Set your shutter speed to about double your frame rate.**

| Frame rate | Shutter speed |
|------------|---------------|
| 24fps | **1/50** |
| 30fps | **1/60** |
| 60fps | **1/125** |

Faster than that and motion looks stuttery and harsh. Slower and it smears. **Doubling the frame rate gives
motion blur that looks the way your eye expects.**

**This means shutter speed is not how you control brightness in video.** It is locked. You control brightness
with aperture and ISO.

### 5. Aperture

**Low f number** (f/1.8, f/4): more light, blurrier background. Good for a person talking.
**High f number** (f/8, f/11): less light, more in focus. Good for a wide shot where everything matters.

The kit lens on these bodies is usually f/3.5 to f/5.6, and it **changes as you zoom**. If you set exposure
zoomed out and then zoom in, it gets darker. Set your exposure at the focal length you are actually shooting.

### 6. ISO

Raise it only as far as you have to. **100 to 800** is clean. **1600** is usable. **3200 and up** gets
visibly noisy on these bodies, and noise in video is much uglier than noise in a photo.

**Order of operations when it is too dark:** open the aperture first, then raise ISO, then add light.
**Never** fix darkness by slowing the shutter below the 180 degree rule.

### 7. White balance

**Do not leave it on auto.** Auto white balance shifts mid-shot and between takes, so your footage will not
match when you cut it together.

Set the preset that matches the light: daylight, shade, tungsten (indoor warm), or fluorescent. If the room
has mixed light, pick the dominant one and stay on it.

### 8. Focus

**Turn autofocus off.** The switch is on the side of the lens: move it from AF to **MF**.

These bodies hunt badly in video. The camera will rack focus in and out mid-shot looking for a subject, and
you cannot remove that later.

**To focus manually:**
1. Press the magnify button to zoom the live view way in on your subject's eye
2. Turn the focus ring until it is sharp
3. Zoom back out
4. **Do not move the camera or the subject after that**

### 9. Audio

The built-in microphone is poor and it picks up every handling noise, including your own fingers on the body.

**If you have an external mic**, plug it into the 3.5mm jack and set audio recording to **manual**, then set
the level so the loudest part peaks around three quarters, never into the red. **Clipped audio cannot be
fixed.**

**If you do not have one**, get the camera as close to the sound as the shot allows, and plan on recording
room tone: 30 seconds of the location with nobody talking, which you will need in the edit.

### 10. Card and battery

- **Format the card in the camera**, not on the computer, and only after you have offloaded everything
- **Check the battery** and bring the spare
- Movie mode eats battery much faster than stills. A battery that lasted a whole photo day will not last a
  whole shoot day

## The preflight checklist

Run this every time, out loud if you are in a crew:

- [ ] Movie mode, manual exposure
- [ ] Resolution and frame rate set, **same as the rest of the project**
- [ ] Shutter speed at double the frame rate
- [ ] Aperture set for the shot
- [ ] ISO as low as the light allows
- [ ] White balance set to a preset, not auto
- [ ] **Lens switched to MF**, focus set with the magnifier
- [ ] Audio level checked, peaking below the red
- [ ] Card has space, battery charged, spare in the bag
- [ ] **Shoot ten seconds and play it back before you shoot anything real**

That last one catches more problems than all the others combined.

---

## The five mistakes that cost a whole shoot

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fixable later? |
|---------|--------------------|----------------|
| Autofocus left on | The image pulses in and out of focus | **No** |
| Auto exposure left on | Brightness drifts mid-shot | **No** |
| Auto white balance | Shots do not match when cut together | Partly, badly |
| Shutter too fast | Motion looks stuttery and harsh | **No** |
| Clipped audio | Distorted, crackling voices | **No** |

**Every single one of these is set before you press record.** That is why the checklist exists.

## If You Finish Early

1. Shoot the same ten seconds at 1/50 and at 1/500 and compare how the motion looks
2. Shoot the same shot at f/5.6 and at f/16 and compare the backgrounds
3. Shoot a shot with auto white balance, walk into a different room, and watch the color shift
4. Record a person from six inches away and from across the room, and listen to both
