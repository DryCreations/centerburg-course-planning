## Requirements

| # | Requirement |
|---|-------------|
| 1 | **2 to 3 pages** in screenplay format, which is roughly 2 to 3 minutes of screen time |
| 2 | Correct **scene headings**: INT. or EXT., a specific location, and a time, all caps |
| 3 | **Action in present tense**, describing only what a camera can see and hear |
| 4 | Character names in **all caps** on their own line above dialogue, with an age and short description the first time each one appears |
| 5 | At least **two locations**, so you are practicing scene breaks |
| 6 | **Courier or Courier New, 12 point** |
| 7 | A **table read** completed with a partner, and at least three lines changed as a result |
| 8 | Your **scene list**: every scene heading in order, on its own page at the front |

**Script locks Wednesday.** After that it is a shooting document, not a writing project. Changes after lock
have to be small, because the whole crew is working from it.

## Grading

| Criteria | What earns full credit |
|----------|------------------------|
| **Format** | Scene headings, action, character names, and dialogue are all laid out correctly and consistently |
| **Filmable action** | Nothing describes a thought, a feeling, or a memory. Every line could be recorded |
| **Structure** | At least two locations, scenes in a sensible order, a clear beginning and end |
| **Dialogue** | Sounds like people talking. Nobody explains things both characters already know |
| **Revision** | Real changes made after the table read, not cosmetic ones |
| **Length** | 2 to 3 pages. Not one, not six |

## If You Finish Early

- Write a **logline**: one sentence naming who the story is about, what they want, and what is in the way
- Write the scene you deliberately left out, then decide whether the script is better with or without it
- Start marking which shot on your shot list covers which line of the script
- Do a second table read with different people and see whether the same lines stumble
