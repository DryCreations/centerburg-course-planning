# Week 5 Vocabulary: Pre-Production and Screenplay Format

## Screenplay format

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Screenplay format** | The industry-standard script layout: scene headings, action, character names, dialogue. |
| **Scene heading (slugline)** | The all-caps line naming interior or exterior, the location, and the time. `INT. HALLWAY - MORNING` |
| **INT. / EXT.** | Interior or exterior. Whether the scene is inside or outside. |
| **Action** | Present-tense description of only what we can see and hear. |
| **Dialogue** | The words a character speaks, set under their name in a narrower column. |
| **Parenthetical** | A short note on how a line is delivered. Used rarely. |
| **Transition** | An instruction like `CUT TO:` or `FADE OUT.` Modern scripts use very few. |
| **CONT'D** | Marks that the same character is continuing to speak after an action line. |
| **Courier** | The typeface screenplays use, because in it one page runs about one minute of screen time. |

## Pre-production documents

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Two column (A/V) script** | Video on the left, audio on the right. Used for documentary, news, promos, and PSAs. |
| **Storyboard** | One drawn panel per shot, showing framing and movement. |
| **Shot list** | A table of every shot to get, in shooting order, with type, location, and equipment. |
| **Call sheet** | The document telling the crew where to be, when, and with what. |
| **Production schedule** | What gets filmed on which day. |
| **Coverage** | Shooting a scene from enough angles to have options in the edit. |
| **Continuity** | Keeping details consistent across shots so the edit does not jump. |
| **Table read** | Reading the script out loud with people taking parts, to hear what works. |

## Camera setup for video

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Frame rate (fps)** | How many frames are recorded per second. 24 feels like film, 30 feels like video. |
| **180 degree rule** | Set shutter speed to about double the frame rate, so motion blur looks natural. 24fps means 1/50. |
| **Manual exposure** | You set shutter, aperture, and ISO yourself, so brightness does not drift mid-shot. |
| **Manual focus (MF)** | Focusing by hand. Required for video on our bodies, because autofocus hunts. |
| **Focus hunting** | The camera racking focus in and out mid-shot looking for a subject. Unfixable later. |
| **Clipping** | Audio recorded too loud, so the waveform is cut off and distorts. Unfixable. |
| **Room tone** | 30 seconds of a location's ambient sound with nobody talking. Needed in the edit. |
| **Shot log** | A record of which shots are done and which still need shooting. |

## Production planning

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Shoot block** | Everything that can be filmed in one place, in one session, without moving. |
| **Shooting order** | Grouping shots by location rather than by where they fall in the story. |
| **Call sheet** | The document a crew carries: who is where, when, with what, and which shots to get. |
| **Turnaround** | The time it takes to move a setup from one location to the next. |
| **Padding** | Extra time added to an estimate for the things you cannot predict. |
| **Essential versus nice to have** | Marking which shots survive if you lose a session. |

## Transitions and framing

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Cut** | Two clips placed back to back with no effect. The default, and the vast majority of edits. |
| **Dissolve** | One shot fades into the next. Signals that time passed, or that two things are connected. |
| **Fade to black** | The picture fades out. Signals that a chapter ended. |
| **Wipe** | One shot pushes the other off screen. Stylized and deliberate. |
| **Match cut** | A cut between two shots that share a shape or a motion. |
| **J cut / L cut** | Audio from one shot starts before, or continues after, the picture changes. |
| **Rule of thirds** | Dividing the frame into nine and placing the subject on a line or intersection. |
| **Headroom** | The space above a subject's head in the frame. |
| **Lead room** | Space in front of where a subject is looking or moving. |
| **Leading lines** | Lines in the scene that pull the eye toward the subject. |
| **Depth** | Having something in the foreground, middle, and background so a shot is not flat. |
| **Framing within the frame** | Shooting through a doorway or window to draw the eye in. |

## Review Questions

1. What are the three parts of a scene heading, in order?
2. When would you use a two-column A/V script instead of screenplay format?
3. Rewrite this action line so a camera could record it: "Dev is furious but trying to hide it."
4. Why does a screenplay use Courier?
5. What is the difference between coverage and continuity?
6. When do you need a new scene heading?
7. You are shooting at 30fps. What shutter speed should you use, and why that one?
8. Name three camera mistakes that cannot be fixed in editing.
9. Why do we turn autofocus off for video?
10. Why does white balance get set to a preset instead of auto?
11. What is a shoot block, and why is a shot list sorted by location instead of story order?
12. You estimate a block at 20 minutes. What do you actually schedule, and why?
13. What goes on a call sheet?
14. Why is the cut the default transition, and what does using a dissolve instead tell the audience?
15. What is lead room, and what goes wrong in a shot without it?
16. Name one framing rule and describe a shot where breaking it on purpose would be the right call.
