## The cut types

### The straight cut

Picture and sound change at the same instant.

```
  PICTURE   [ shot A ][ shot B ]
  AUDIO     [ shot A ][ shot B ]
```

**Right most of the time.** Invisible and honest. **Wrong in conversation:** cutting picture and sound
together on every line makes dialogue feel like a tennis match.

### The J-cut: sound arrives first

The next shot's **audio starts before its picture**.

```
  PICTURE   [ shot A      ][ shot B ]
  AUDIO     [ shot A ][ shot B      ]
```

You hear the new person before you see them, so by the time the picture changes you were already
expecting it. **The cut disappears.**

**Use it for:** someone beginning to speak before we cut to them, arriving somewhere new, making a cut
feel motivated instead of abrupt.

### The L-cut: sound lingers

The previous shot's **audio continues after its picture ends**.

```
  PICTURE   [ shot A ][ shot B      ]
  AUDIO     [ shot A      ][ shot B ]
```

You cut to a listener while the speaker keeps talking. **The scene continues while the picture moves.**

**Use it for:** reaction shots, showing what someone is describing, getting out of a shot that has run
long without losing the moment.

> **Both of these are called split edits**, because picture and sound are split apart instead of cutting
> at the same frame.

### Why the letters

Look at the shapes above. The audio and video blocks form a rough **J** or **L** on the timeline. That is
the whole reason for the names.

## The other cuts worth naming

| Cut | What it is | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Cutaway** | A shot of something else dropped over a seam | Hiding a bad cut, compressing time, showing what is being discussed |
| **Match cut** | Two shots sharing a shape or movement, cut so it reads as one motion | Scene transitions, a deliberate visual rhyme |
| **Jump cut** | Two shots too similar, so the subject appears to teleport | Usually a mistake. Deliberate only when it is obviously deliberate |
| **Cutting on action** | Cutting **during** a movement rather than before or after | Almost always. The motion carries the eye across the cut |

**Cutting on action is the easiest upgrade to any edit and almost nobody does it.** Beginners cut when a
shot "is done," which is exactly the moment the cut is most visible.

## Listen for these, when you get to the editor

| What you hear | What it means |
|---|---|
| The conversation feels like a tennis match | All straight cuts. Add split edits |
| A word gets clipped | You cut mid-word. Never do this. Move the edit |
| Someone's voice changes tone abruptly at a cut | Different takes, different levels. Match the levels |
| It sounds smooth but looks confusing | The offset is too long. Pull it back toward a half second |

**Offsets are tenths of a second**, not seconds. Half a second is usually enough.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Split edit** | Any cut where picture and sound do not change at the same frame |
| **J-cut** | The next shot's audio starts before its picture |
| **L-cut** | The previous shot's audio continues after its picture |
| **Straight cut** | Picture and sound change together |
| **Unlink / detach** | Separating a clip's audio from its video so they move independently |
| **Offset** | How far apart the picture edit and the sound edit are |
| **Cutaway** | A shot inserted over a cut |
| **Match cut** | A cut between shots sharing shape or movement |
| **Jump cut** | Two similar shots cut together so the subject appears to jump |
| **Cutting on action** | Cutting during a movement so it carries across the edit |
