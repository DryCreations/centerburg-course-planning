# Screening Day: Watching Each Other's Cuts

**Video & Sound Production (145110) | Tuesday**

Same footage. Different edits. **We are going to watch several and talk about why they are not the same.**

This is normal professional practice. Every edit gets screened and every editor gets notes. The skill
being graded is **giving notes someone can act on.**

---

## How the screening runs

1. A cut plays **all the way through, with no commentary.** Nobody talks, including the editor
2. **The editor says nothing first.** No "it's not done" and no explaining what you meant
3. The room gives notes
4. **Then** the editor can ask questions

> **The editor talking first ruins the data.** Once you explain the shot, nobody can tell you whether it
> read on its own, and whether it reads on its own is the only thing you need to know.

**That last row is not a joke.** Watching people solve the same problem differently is the fastest way to
get better, and noticing what you want to copy is the point of a screening.

---

## Notes that are worth something

Use the words you have now. That is why we did the vocabulary.

| Weak | Useful |
|---|---|
| "It felt weird" | "The cut at 0:14 is a **jump cut**. The two shots are too similar, so it looks like a glitch" |
| "It's too long" | "The middle section holds too long. The shot of the hallway runs about four seconds and reads in one" |
| "The sound is bad" | "The **levels** jump at 0:22. The second clip is noticeably louder than the first" |
| "I liked it" | "The **match cut** at 0:30 works because both shots move left to right. It reads as one motion" |
| "Add some B-roll" | "The cut at 0:41 would disappear under a **cutaway**. You have a detail shot of the table that would cover it" |

**Point at a timecode.** "At 0:14" is the difference between a note and an opinion.

---

## If you are the one being watched

- **Write the notes down.** You will not remember them
- **Do not defend the cut.** Nobody is attacking it. A note is information, not a verdict
- **You do not have to take every note.** You do have to decide about each one
- **If two people give the same note, it is not an opinion any more.** Fix it

## Why editors screen

Because **you go blind to your own edit.** After two hours you know what every shot is supposed to do, so
you stop seeing what it actually does. The only reliable fix is someone who has not seen it.

This is true for professionals with twenty years of experience. It is not a beginner problem, it is a
structural one, and the solution is always the same: show it to someone and shut up while they watch.
