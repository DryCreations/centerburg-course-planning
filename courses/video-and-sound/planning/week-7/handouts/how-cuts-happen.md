# How Cuts Actually Happen

**Video & Sound Production (145110) | Week 7**

So far you have made one kind of cut: picture and sound end together, and the next shot starts.

**That is one option out of many, and it is the most obvious one.** Everything you watch uses the others
constantly and you have never noticed, which is exactly the point.

---

## The straight cut

Picture and audio change at the same instant.

```
  PICTURE   [ shot A ][ shot B ]
  AUDIO     [ shot A ][ shot B ]
```

**When it is right:** most of the time. It is invisible and it is honest.

**When it is wrong:** conversations. Cutting picture and sound together on every line makes dialogue feel
like a tennis match.

---

## The J-cut: sound arrives first

The next shot's **audio starts before its picture does.**

```
  PICTURE   [ shot A      ][ shot B ]
  AUDIO     [ shot A ][ shot B      ]
```

**Why it works:** you hear the new place before you see it, so by the time the picture changes, you were
already expecting it. The cut disappears.

**Use it for:** someone starting to speak before we see them; arriving somewhere new; any time you want
the cut to feel motivated rather than abrupt.

> **This is why professional dialogue feels smooth and yours does not.** It is not the camera. It is
> this.

---

## The L-cut: sound lingers

The previous shot's **audio continues after its picture ends.**

```
  PICTURE   [ shot A ][ shot B      ]
  AUDIO     [ shot A      ][ shot B ]
```

**Why it works:** you cut away to a reaction, a detail, or a listener while the speaker keeps talking.
The scene continues while the picture moves.

**Use it for:** reaction shots, showing what someone is describing, getting out of a shot that has run
too long without losing the moment.

### How to do either one in CapCut

1. Put the clip on the timeline
2. **Detach or unlink the audio** from the video
3. Drag the audio edge earlier (J) or later (L) than the picture edge
4. Play it. **Adjust by tenths of a second**, not seconds

Half a second is usually enough. This is a small adjustment with a large effect.

---

## The cutaway

A shot of something else, dropped over a seam.

**Why it works:** the audience is looking at the new shot, so the join underneath goes unnoticed. **The
most used trick in editing**, and it is in everything you watch.

**Use it for:** hiding a jump cut, compressing time, showing what someone is talking about, covering a
mistake.

**This is why you shoot B-roll.** Not because it is pretty, but because it is what you use to solve
problems later. **The editor always wishes they had more coverage, and the editor is you.**

---

## The match cut

Two shots that share a shape, a movement, or a direction, cut together so it reads as one motion.

**Why it works:** the eye follows continuity. When the shapes line up, the cut feels intentional rather
than like an interruption.

**Use it for:** transitions between scenes, showing time passing, a deliberate visual rhyme.

---

## The jump cut

Two shots too similar to each other, so the subject appears to teleport.

**Usually a mistake.** It reads as a glitch.

**Except** when it is obviously on purpose: compressing a long process, a stylized rhythm, the way most
online video is cut now. **If it looks like an accident, it is one.**

---

## Cutting on action

Cut **during** a movement rather than before or after it.

Someone reaches for a door: cut halfway through the reach, and the movement finishes in the next shot.
**The motion carries the eye across the cut and it becomes nearly invisible.**

> **This is the single easiest upgrade to any edit, and almost nobody does it.** Most beginners cut when
> a shot "is done," which is exactly the moment the cut is most visible.

---

## Cutting on sound

- **Never cut in the middle of a word**
- A cut lands better **just before** someone speaks than on the syllable
- A beat of quiet before a line makes the line land harder

## The point of all of this

**You have been learning the tool. This is the craft.**

Knowing where the trim handle is takes an afternoon. Knowing *when* to cut is the actual job, and it is
the thing that separates someone who can operate CapCut from someone who can edit.

Every technique on this page works identically in Premiere, Resolve, Final Cut, and Avid. **None of them
are CapCut features.** They are decisions.
