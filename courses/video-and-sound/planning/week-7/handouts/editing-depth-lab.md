# From Assembly to Edit

**Video & Sound Production (145110) | Week 7**

Last week you got clips in an order with the dead time trimmed. That is an **assembly**, and every
assembly is too long, too flat, and has at least one cut that hurts to watch.

This week you fix that. **Same footage. The improvement comes entirely from decisions.**

Still individual. Your cut, your project.

---

## Monday: Pacing. Cut 20% out.

**Take your current runtime and remove a fifth of it.** 90 seconds becomes 72. No exceptions, no
negotiating.

### Why this is the assignment

Because everybody's first edit is too long, and the only way to learn what is not earning its place is
to be forced to find it. **You cannot cut 20% by trimming frames off the ends.** You have to remove
something you liked.

### Where the time actually is

| Look for | Why it goes |
|---|---|
| **The shot you are proud of that does not move the piece** | This is the first and hardest one. Being a nice shot is not a reason to be in the edit |
| **Anything the audience already knows** | Showing a thing twice because you shot it twice |
| **Dead air at the top** | Most pieces should start later than they do. Try deleting your first shot entirely and see if you miss it |
| **The long walk** | Someone crossing a room for four seconds when one second reads the same |
| **The tail after the point** | The piece is over. Stop |

### The test

Watch the short version, then the long version. **Ask whether you lost anything a viewer would notice.**
Usually you did not, which is the lesson.

**Keep both.** You are submitting the before and the after on Friday.

---

## Tuesday: Cutaways. Hide your worst cut.

Every edit has one cut that is visibly wrong. A **jump cut**, a mismatch, an awkward jolt.

### What a cutaway is

A shot of something else, dropped over the seam. The audience is looking at the new shot, so the join
underneath it goes unnoticed. **This is the most used trick in editing and it is in everything you watch.**

### Do it

1. **Find your worst cut.** Play the edit and watch for the moment you flinch
2. Find a clip of something else: a hand, a detail, a wide shot, a reaction, an object in the room
3. Put it **on the track above**, over the seam
4. Trim it so it covers the join, usually **1 to 2 seconds**
5. Play it back. **The bad cut should be gone**

### The lesson under the trick

**This is why you shoot B-roll.** Not because it is pretty, but because it is what you use to solve
problems in the edit. Every shoot from now on, get extra coverage: details, hands, wides, reactions.

> **The editor always wishes they had more coverage. The editor is you.**

---

## Wednesday: Audio. The half nobody looks at.

**Bad audio reads as amateur faster than bad video.** People will watch a soft, poorly framed shot. They
will not sit through audio that jumps in volume every three seconds.

### 1. Levels

- Play your whole edit with your eyes closed and listen for jumps
- Find the loudest clip and the quietest clip
- **Adjust each clip's volume so nothing jumps at a cut**
- Aim for dialogue to sit at a consistent level, not peaking and not buried

### 2. Room tone

**Silence is not silent.** Every room has a sound: air handling, hum, the building. When you cut from a
clip with room tone to a gap with *actual* digital silence, the audience hears a hole.

- Find a moment with no talking and steady background
- Use it to fill any gaps
- **If you have none, record 30 seconds of it next shoot.** Everybody standing still, not talking. It
  feels ridiculous and every professional crew does it

### 3. Cut on sound, not just picture

- Do not cut in the middle of a word. Ever
- A cut lands better just **before** someone starts speaking than on the syllable
- Try letting the audio from the next shot start slightly before the picture does. That is a **J-cut**,
  and it is why professional dialogue feels smooth

## Thursday: Titles and the finish

You did a title card and credits last week. Now make them look deliberate.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title card** | Readable, high contrast, on screen long enough to read twice. **No animated presets** |
| **Lower third** | Name and role, bottom left, on the shot where a person first appears. On screen 3 to 5 seconds |
| **Credits** | Everyone, with the job they actually did. You as editor |
| **Fade to black** | At the end, into the credits. The audience needs to know it is over |
| **Consistency** | Same typeface, same size, same position. Two fonts is a maximum, and one is fine |

### Then: watch it start to finish without touching anything

Hands off the keyboard. Take notes. **Then** fix.

You cannot judge pacing while you are scrubbing, and the single most common editing mistake is polishing
one section forty times without ever watching the whole thing.

| **Match cut** | Find two shots with similar shape or movement and cut between them so it feels like one motion |
| **Shot log** | Log every usable take with timecode and a note. Tedious, and it is literally an entry level job |

## Vocabulary for Friday's quiz

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Assembly** | Clips in order, roughly trimmed. The starting point |
| **Fine cut** | The pass where timing is tightened and bad cuts are fixed |
| **Pacing** | How long shots are held, and how that makes a piece feel |
| **Cutaway** | A shot inserted over a cut, often to hide it |
| **B-roll** | Supporting footage that is not the main action |
| **Coverage** | How many different shots you have of the same moment. More coverage means more options |
| **Jump cut** | Two similar shots cut together so the subject appears to jump |
| **Match cut** | A cut between two shots that share shape or movement, so it reads as continuous |
| **J-cut** | The next shot's audio starts before its picture does |
| **L-cut** | The previous shot's audio continues after its picture ends |
| **Room tone** | The natural background sound of a location, recorded on purpose |
| **Levels** | How loud audio is. Matching levels stops jumps at cuts |
| **Lower third** | A text label in the bottom of frame naming a person or place |
| **Fade** | A gradual change to or from black |
| **Dissolve** | One shot blending into the next |
| **Timecode** | The hours, minutes, seconds, frames address of a point in footage |
| **Handles** | Extra footage beyond the in and out points, kept so you can adjust later |
| **Rough cut / final cut** | The draft, and the version you deliver |

- Your own footage
- **No filters, no effects, no auto-anything.** Titles, a fade, and volume adjustment are the
  assignment. Nothing else
- Music only at the extension level, and only after Level 1 is done
- Ignore every template CapCut offers you

> **Effects are how people avoid learning to cut.** A week from now the only thing that will have
> improved your work is decisions.
