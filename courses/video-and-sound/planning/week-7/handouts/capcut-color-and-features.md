# Making It Look Intentional

**Video & Sound Production (145110) | Wednesday**

Two shots of the same scene, recorded four minutes apart, often do not match. One is bluer. One is
darker. The audience cannot say why, but they feel that something is off.

**Today is about fixing that**, and about the parts of CapCut you have not touched yet.

---

## Part 1: Color, in the right order

**There is a correct order and doing it backwards wastes your time.**

| | Do this | Why it is first |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Exposure / brightness** | Get the overall brightness right | Everything else depends on it |
| **2. Contrast** | Set how far apart the darks and lights are | Flat footage looks like nothing until this is set |
| **3. White balance / temperature** | Make white things look white | This is what fixes the orange or blue cast |
| **4. Saturation** | How intense the colors are | Last, and use less than you want to |

### Correction versus grading

| | |
|---|---|
| **Color correction** | Making it look **right**. Shots match each other, white is white, nothing is blown out |
| **Color grading** | Making it look **like something**. A warm nostalgic feel, a cold clinical feel, a specific mood |

**Correct first, then grade.** Grading a shot that is not corrected means you are fighting the original
problem with a second problem.

### The assignment

1. Find **two shots that should match but do not.** Same scene, different look
2. Correct them until they match. Exposure, then contrast, then white balance
3. **Toggle it on and off.** If you cannot tell the difference, go further. If it looks fake, go back
4. **Then** pick one look and apply it consistently, if you want a grade at all

### The rule that will save you

> **If you can tell a shot has been graded, you went too far.**

The exception is a look that is obviously deliberate: a flashback, a dream, a stylized sequence. Even
then, the rest of the piece has to be neutral for the stylized part to read as stylized.

## Part 2: Audio, briefly

Not the focus today, and two things are worth thirty seconds each because they cost you points on any
piece you make.

**Levels.** Play your edit with your eyes closed and listen for jumps. Adjust each clip's volume so
nothing jolts at a cut. That is the whole fix.

**Room tone.** Silence is not silent. Cutting from a clip with background sound to true digital silence
sounds like a hole. If you have a moment of steady background with no talking, use it to fill gaps. If
you have none, **record 30 seconds next shoot**: everybody standing still, not talking. It feels
ridiculous and every professional crew does it.

We will do audio properly in its own unit later. **Today is picture.**

## Part 3: Other things worth knowing about

Explore these. **None are required today.**

| Feature | What it is actually for |
|---|---|
| **Speed** | Slow motion for emphasis, speed-up to compress a boring stretch. **Slow motion only looks good if you shot at a high frame rate** |
| **Freeze frame** | Holding one frame. Useful for an ending or to name someone |
| **Keyframes** | Making a setting change over time: volume fading, a slow zoom, brightness ramping. **This is the most transferable thing on this list**, it works the same in every editor |
| **Masking** | Showing only part of a frame. Used for split screens and for hiding something |
| **Stabilization** | Smoothing shaky footage. It crops in, so it costs resolution |
| **Crop and reframe** | Turning a wide shot into a tighter one in post. A real option when you did not get the close-up |
| **Split screen** | Two shots at once. Rarely the right answer, occasionally exactly right |

**Try at least two, and be able to say why each made the piece better.** A feature used because it
exists is the same mistake as a filter.

**Keyframes are the one to prioritize.** They work identically in every editor, they are how every
professional animation of any setting is done, and they are the most transferable thing on this page.
Start with something simple: a volume fade, or a slow push in on a still shot.

---

## Still banned

No filters, no auto-enhance, no templates, no effects packs.

**Color correction is not an effect.** It is making the footage look like what your eye saw. That is the
distinction, and it is worth understanding rather than memorizing.
