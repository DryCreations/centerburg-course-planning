# Week 7 Teacher Notes: Video & Sound

**Post-production depth, in CapCut. Quiz Friday closes pre-production and editing basics.**

## Pacing

| Day | Focus | The deliverable |
|-----|-------|-----------------|
| Mon | **Pacing.** Cut 20% out | A shorter version, kept alongside the long one |
| Tue | **Cutaways.** Hide the worst cut | One invisible cut where an ugly one was |
| Wed | **Audio.** Levels and room tone | No volume jumps at cuts |
| Thu | Titles, lower thirds, fade, then watch it whole | A finished piece |
| Fri | **Quiz**, then export and submit | 34 question bank, cut to 20 |

## Why this instead of the audio unit

Unit 1.3 (Audio) is next on the map and it is the highest-weight untouched strand, but they have footage
in hand right now and momentum on the software. **Post-production depth was the call.** Audio still gets
its day here (Wednesday), which is a soft on-ramp to 1.3 whenever it lands.

**Flag for later: Unit 1.3 Audio is still owed**, and it is ~16% of the WebXam. It should not slip past
Q2.

## Monday is the one that teaches

**The 20% cut is non-negotiable and that is the entire pedagogy.** If it is optional, nobody does it, and
"my edit is too long" is a thing every one of them will believe about someone else's work and never their
own.

Expect pushback. The answer:

> **"You cannot cut a fifth by trimming frames. You have to remove something you liked. That is the
> assignment."**

Then have them watch both versions back to back. The realization that nothing was lost is the moment the
lesson lands, and it only happens if you make them keep both files.

## Tuesday is the highest-value trick in the course

A cutaway is cheap to teach, works immediately, and visibly fixes a problem they can see. **It is also
the argument for B-roll that no amount of telling them to shoot B-roll ever wins.**

Say the line: **the editor always wishes they had more coverage, and the editor is you.**

## Wednesday: expect this to be the weakest day

Audio is invisible and students do not believe it matters until they hear a cut with a volume jump.

**Do the demo.** Play a clip with mismatched levels, then the fixed version, on the room speakers. Thirty
seconds and it is settled.

**Room tone:** most of them will not have any. That is fine and it is the point. Assign it for the next
shoot: 30 seconds of everyone standing still, not talking. It feels ridiculous and every professional crew
does it.

## Still individual

Same rule as last week. Separate projects, separate exports. **Watch for the group that quietly
consolidates back to one project.**

## The rules stay banned

No filters, no effects, no auto-anything, no templates. Titles, a fade, and volume adjustment are the
assignment.

The reason, stated plainly: **effects are how people avoid learning to cut.** A student who spends the
week on transitions has learned nothing and their piece will be worse.

## Friday quiz note: two 180 degree rules

The bank contains both the **shutter speed** rule (double the frame rate) and the **camera placement**
rule (the imaginary line). Unrelated concepts, same name, and students conflate them constantly.

**Both are on the bank on purpose.** If your cut of 20 includes both, say so before the quiz so it is a
test of understanding rather than of guessing which one you meant.

## Submissions

**Both cuts are due**: the Thursday assembly and the final. The comparison is the evidence that the week
worked, and it is worth two minutes of watching a few side by side.
