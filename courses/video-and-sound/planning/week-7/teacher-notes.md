# Week 7 Teacher Notes: Video & Sound

**Post-production depth, in CapCut. Quiz Friday closes pre-production and editing basics.**

## Why this instead of the audio unit

Unit 1.3 (Audio) is next on the map and it is the highest-weight untouched strand, but they have footage
in hand right now and momentum on the software. **Post-production depth was the call.** Audio still gets
its day here (Wednesday), which is a soft on-ramp to 1.3 whenever it lands.

**Flag for later: Unit 1.3 Audio is still owed**, and it is ~16% of the WebXam. It should not slip past
Q2.

## Monday is the one that teaches

**The 20% cut is non-negotiable and that is the entire pedagogy.** If it is optional, nobody does it, and
"my edit is too long" is a thing every one of them will believe about someone else's work and never their
own.

Expect pushback. The answer:

> **"You cannot cut a fifth by trimming frames. You have to remove something you liked. That is the
> assignment."**

Then have them watch both versions back to back. The realization that nothing was lost is the moment the
lesson lands, and it only happens if you make them keep both files.

## Tuesday: the screening

**Same footage, different edits, watched side by side.** This is the single most efficient teaching
device available this week, because the variation makes every choice visible.

Run it strictly:

1. The cut plays **all the way through with no commentary.** Nobody talks, including the editor
2. **The editor says nothing first.** No "it's not done"
3. Room gives notes. **Then** the editor can ask questions

> **The editor talking first ruins the data.** Once they explain the shot, nobody can tell them whether
> it read on its own, and that is the only thing worth knowing.

**Make them use the vocabulary.** "It felt weird" gets sent back; "the cut at 0:14 is a jump cut" is the
standard. This is also live rehearsal for Friday's quiz, which is why the vocab day and the screening
day sit next to each other.

**Timing:** you will not get through everyone. Four or five cuts is a full period done properly, and
doing five well beats rushing twelve. Rotate who gets screened across future weeks.

The cutaway lesson moves into Wednesday's handout and into the notes students give each other, which is
a better place for it: **"you have a detail shot that would cover that cut" lands harder from a peer
than from me.**

## Wednesday: color, and expect audio to be the weakest half

Audio is invisible and students do not believe it matters until they hear a cut with a volume jump.

**Do the demo.** Play a clip with mismatched levels, then the fixed version, on the room speakers. Thirty
seconds and it is settled.

**Room tone:** most of them will not have any. That is fine and it is the point. Assign it for the next
shoot: 30 seconds of everyone standing still, not talking. It feels ridiculous and every professional crew
does it.

## Wednesday: color order matters

**Exposure, then contrast, then white balance, then saturation.** Doing it out of order means fighting
your own earlier adjustment, and students will skip straight to saturation because it is the most
visible slider.

The rule to give them: **if you can tell a shot has been graded, you went too far.**

The distinction worth drawing, because it is the reason color is allowed while filters are banned:
**color correction is making the footage look like what your eye saw. A filter is a look applied without
a decision.** One is craft, the other is avoidance.

**The assignment is concrete:** find two shots that should match and do not, and make them match. Not
"explore color." Exploring produces purple footage.

## Thursday: vocab, then the next project

Two halves.

**First, vocab review.** `vocab.md` is the study guide and it is comprehensive through this week. Run it
as **retrieval, not reading**: cover the column, say it, check, mark misses, study only the misses. Put
the five most-confused pairs on the board, since that is where the quiz will actually catch them.

**Then, start the next project properly.** They have now felt what bad coverage costs in the edit, which
is the only moment pre-production is ever persuasive. Have them start a packet: concept, a one-sentence
goal, and a shot list that includes B-roll on purpose this time.

> **Say it directly: every problem you hit this week was created two weeks ago, on the shoot.**

That sentence is worth more now than it would have been before they edited.

## Still individual

Same rule as last week. Separate projects, separate exports. **Watch for the group that quietly
consolidates back to one project.**

## The rules stay banned

No filters, no effects, no auto-anything, no templates. Titles, a fade, and volume adjustment are the
assignment.

The reason, stated plainly: **effects are how people avoid learning to cut.** A student who spends the
week on transitions has learned nothing and their piece will be worse.

## Friday quiz note: two 180 degree rules

The bank contains both the **shutter speed** rule (double the frame rate) and the **camera placement**
rule (the imaginary line). Unrelated concepts, same name, and students conflate them constantly.

**Both are on the bank on purpose.** If your cut of 20 includes both, say so before the quiz so it is a
test of understanding rather than of guessing which one you meant.

## Submissions

**Both cuts are due**: the Thursday assembly and the final. The comparison is the evidence that the week
worked, and it is worth two minutes of watching a few side by side.
