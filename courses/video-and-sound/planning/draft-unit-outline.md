## Design Principles

- Quarter structure aligned to BPA Competition Season Calendar and WebXam testing window
- BPA primary event: **#430 Video Production Team** (summative project Q2–Q3)
- Every competency in the standards appears at least once
- At least 1 major project per quarter; BPA project spans Q2–Q3
- EOC test weights guide time allocation — Strand 7 (~67%) dominates; 7.3/7.7/7.8 each at ~16%
- Team-based events — students form production teams of 2–3 early in Q1

### Daily Class Structure

Every class period follows the same rhythm:

| Phase | Time | Details |
|-------|------|---------|
| **Bell Ringer** | First 5–7 min | One question from the current unit's `quiz.md` (projected on the board). Students respond on paper or digitally. Teacher cold-calls 2–3 students; class discusses briefly. Mon–Thu rotate DOK 1–2 vocab/recall; DOK 3–4 scenario questions after at least one day of instruction on the topic. |
| **Instruction / Demo** | 10–15 min *(as needed)* | Mini-lesson, live demo, or clarifying review. Skipped or shortened on heavy work days. |
| **Work Time** | 25–35 min | Students work on the current **lab** or **project**. There is always an active lab or project — no dead time. Students who finish early move to the extension task in the lab or project document. |
| **Wrap-Up** | Last 5 min | Teacher circulates for quick status checks; students update progress log or answer one exit question. |
| **End-of-Week Quiz** | Friday *(last 15–20 min)* | 10–20 MCQ from the unit's `quiz.md`. 70% DOK 1–2 (vocab/recall), 30% DOK 3–4 (analysis/application). Modeled after WebXam format. |

> **Always a project:** Students always have a multi-week project in progress. When a lab is finished early, the next action is always to work on the current project. Extension tasks inside labs also point forward to the next unit.

### Per-Unit Resources

Each unit has a dedicated folder under `planning/units/` containing:

| File | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| `vocab.md` | 15–25 vocabulary terms with definitions, standard codes, and DOK levels |
| `quiz.md` | 10–20 MCQ end-of-week quiz; includes answer key, DOK rating, and standard reference per question |
| `lab.md` | 1–3 day skill exercise with step-by-step instructions and a **standards-demonstrated checklist** (this is the rubric) |
| `project.md` | Multi-week summative work with a **Class Prompt** and an optional **BPA Challenge Prompt** |

## Quarter 1: Foundations — Media Landscape, Pre-Production & Audio (Weeks 1–9)

### Unit 1.0: WebXam Pretest & Course Orientation (Week 1)

**Focus:** Establish baseline. Students take the WebXam pretest under test-like conditions.

**Activities:**
- Day 1–2: Course overview, syllabus, lab/equipment orientation, safety
- Day 3: **WebXam Pretest** — full-length practice under timed conditions
- Day 4–5: Score analysis — teacher reviews class-wide results to identify weak strands; students review their own results and set personal learning goals

> **Why pretest?** The pretest provides a data-driven starting point. If the class scores low on 7.8 Audio (16% of test) but strong on 7.1 Interactive Media (9%), pacing can be adjusted. Individual results help students understand where to focus.

### Unit 1.1: The Media Industry & Interactive Media (Week 2)

**Focus:** Media landscape, career paths, interactive media foundations. BPA introduction.

**Competencies Covered:**
- 7.1.1 Types and uses of interactive media environments
- 7.1.2 Components of interactive media
- 7.1.3 Major characteristics of interactive media presentations
- 7.1.4 Historical developments and future trends
- 7.1.5 Major interactive media genres

- 7.1.7 Intellectual property rights in interactive media

**Activities:**

- Students form production teams (2–3 per team)

### Unit 1.2: Pre-Production — Scripts, Storyboards & Planning (Weeks 3–4)

**Focus:** Pre-production workflow. Every production starts here.

**Competencies Covered:**
- 7.2.4 Uses and needs for site maps, scripts, storyboards, flowcharts
- 7.2.5 Preliminary sketches — placement of images and text
- 7.2.7 Select colors based on color theory and psychology
- 7.2.8 Describe music, video, and special effects to be used
- 7.2.9 Provide sample layout to stakeholders for review
- 7.2.10 Select and create visual design elements for audience and use
- 7.2.11 Client personas and narratives for intended outcomes
- 7.3.1 Select media elements (sound, video, graphics, text, animation)

**Major Project — "Pre-Production Package":**
For a short (60-second) concept video, create: script, shot list, storyboard, equipment list, and production schedule. Present to class for stakeholder feedback. This exercise rehearses the process students will use for their BPA project.

---

### Unit 1.3: Audio Production Fundamentals (Weeks 5–7)

**Focus:** Dedicated audio unit — reflects 15.96% EOC weight on 7.8.

**Competencies Covered:**
- 7.8.1 Evaluate performance needs and technical resources
- 7.8.2 Sound requirements based on script analysis
- 7.8.3 Design score appropriate to production and post-production needs
- 7.8.4 Determine microphone and speaker placement
- 7.8.5 Select and incorporate Foley mechanical and electrical sound effects
- 7.8.6 Set up and operate audio-for-video recording devices
- 7.8.7 Set up and operate time code system for audio-video synchronization
- 7.8.8 Perform audio mixing
- 7.8.9 Operate a sound mixing board during production
- 2.10.5 Prepare and operate equipment per project design specs
- 2.10.6 Monitor equipment operation and troubleshoot issues
- 2.10.9 Routine maintenance per manufacturer specs

**Tools:** **Audacity** (primary DAW for all in-lab recording, editing, and multi-track mixing exercises in this unit); Rode/Zoom audio recorders for field/VO capture. DaVinci Resolve is introduced for audio-for-video sync but Audacity remains the standalone audio tool through end of Q1.

**Major Project — "Audio Production Demo Reel":**
Record and produce a 2–3 minute audio piece (podcast segment, soundscape, or radio-style spot) using Audacity for all in-lab work. Must demonstrate: proper mic placement, Foley effects or sound design, multi-track mixing, and time code sync. Teams submit a finished mixed audio file with production notes.

> **BPA Option — #450 Podcast Production Team:** This audio demo reel is the foundation for the competition entry. Students using #450 should structure their audio piece toward podcast format (using the WSAP topic) rather than an open-ended soundscape. The core technical requirements — mic placement, Foley, mixing, time code — are the same for both. Teams form by Week 8–9. See [bpa-events.md](bpa-events.md).

### Unit 1.4: BPA Showcase, Event Selection & Equipment Orientation (Weeks 8–9)

**Focus:** BPA event showcase and team formation (Week 8). Equipment orientation and finalization (Week 9).

**Competencies Covered:**
- 2.10.2 Processor, memory, storage, power, environmental requirements
- 2.10.4 Software application requirements
- 2.10.7 Backup, restore, test, archive, manage data
- 2.10.8 Prepare equipment for storage/decommissioning

**Activities:**

*Week 8 — BPA Event Showcase:*
- Screen and analyze actual BPA competition entries: video productions, broadcast news segments, podcasts — what separates a strong entry from an average one?
- Review event options as a class: #430 Video Production Team (primary), #445 Broadcast News, #450 Podcast, #420 Digital Media (individual/smaller scope), virtual events (V11, V01)
- Teams finalize event selection and confirm team rosters (2–3 per team for #430, #445, #450)
- Students who prefer to work individually note #420 Digital Media as their path

*Week 9 — Equipment Orientation & Finalization:*
- Equipment orientation: camera systems (DSLR, mirrorless), audio gear (boom, lavalier, Rode/Zoom recorders), editing workstations; introduce **OBS Studio** as the screen recording and format-demonstration tool (used throughout the year for tutorial capture and format-comparison lessons)
- Data management workshop: file naming conventions, backup strategy, project archiving
- Teams download and review their selected event's WSAP guidelines
- Virtual event students (V11, V01) must register by December 1 — confirm intent by end of this week

## Quarter 2: Video Production & BPA Project (Weeks 10–18)

### Unit 2.1: Camera & Lighting (Weeks 10–12)

**Focus:** Video capture skills — camera operation, composition, lighting. Core of 7.7.

**Competencies Covered:**
- 7.7.1 Equipment and production needs (drone, DSLR, mirrorless, compact, 360)
- 7.7.2 Analyze script/storyboard to develop production schedule
- 7.7.3 Set up audio, lighting, scenery for the shoot
- 7.7.4 Select video recording format and shoot video
- 7.3.3 Incorporate graphics (digital, hand-drawn, photographic)
- 7.3.5 Prepare and integrate photographic images and special effects
- 7.3.6 Incorporate video footage

**Major Project — "Shot Composition Exercise":**
Shoot a series of 10+ shots demonstrating key composition techniques (rule of thirds, leading lines, depth of field), 3 different lighting setups, and at least 2 camera types. Present with technical annotations.

> **BPA Note:** This project produces foundational footage feeding directly into the BPA production entry in Unit 2.2. Shots, B-roll, and lighting setups captured here may be incorporated into the competition submission. All teams should treat this shoot as the first phase of their BPA production pipeline.

---

### Unit 2.2: BPA Project — Proposal & Production (Weeks 13–18)

**Focus:** Full production cycle for BPA competition entry. This IS the major course project.

**Competencies Covered:**
- 2.9.1 Scope and purpose of branding
- 2.9.2 Scope and purpose of the project
- 2.9.3 Target audience, client needs, expected outcomes, objectives, budget
- 2.9.4 Conceptual model and design brief
- 2.9.5 Timeline, communication plan, task breakdown, costs, deliverables
- 2.9.6 Present comprehensive proposal to stakeholders
- 7.7.5 Select editing system (linear/nonlinear) and edit video
- 7.7.6 Add transitions, titles, special effects, digital effects
- 7.7.7 Add soundtrack, narration, voiceover
- 7.7.8 Export video to desired medium
- 7.3.2 Generate text for presentations (title graphics, charts)
- 7.3.4 Incorporate computer animation
- 7.3.7 Edit video footage
- 7.3.8 Record/acquire soundtracks (narrative, voiceover, sound effects, music)
- 7.3.9 Integrate sound with visuals
- 7.3.10 Produce, test, debug, archive final product
- 7.3.11 Apply accessibility guidelines

**BPA Project Workflow (6 weeks):**
1. **Week 13:** Project proposal — script, storyboard, shot list, team roles, schedule, budget estimate. Present to class.
2. **Weeks 14–15:** Principal photography — shoot footage; capture audio
3. **Weeks 16–17:** Post-production — edit, add effects/transitions/titles, sound mix, color grade
4. **Week 18:** First cut screening — peer review using WSAP rubric; revision notes

For most teams: **#430 Video Production Team** (3–5 min video on assigned topic)
Alternative: **#445 Broadcast News** or **#450 Podcast Production** depending on team choice

> *⚑ December 1 — Virtual Events registration deadline (V11 2D Animation)*
> *By end of Q2: first cut complete*

### Unit 4.2: Capstone Production & Showreel + Remediation & Retake (Weeks 30–37)

**Focus:** Large summative project completed during the 30-day retake window. All students produce a capstone film and professional showreel; students who need to retake also receive embedded remediation.

**Competencies Covered (applied/reinforced):**
- All 7.7 Video competencies (revisited)
- All 7.8 Audio competencies (revisited)
- All 7.3 Production competencies (revisited)
- All 2.9 Project Proposal competencies (revisited)
- 1.1.4 Professional organizations, networking
- 1.1.5 Self-promotion, portfolio development
- 1.2.2 Deliver formal and informal presentations

**Major Project — "Capstone Production & Professional Showreel" (Summative):**
Two linked deliverables:
1. **Capstone Production** — Student-chosen production: documentary short, music video, podcast series episode, animated short, or news feature. Must demonstrate full workflow from proposal through delivery. Different format than their BPA entry to show range.
2. **Professional Showreel** — 60–90 second highlight reel of best work from the year. Includes title card, music bed, credits. Portfolio-ready for sharing with potential employers or post-secondary programs.

Students present both pieces at an end-of-year screening for class and/or BAC panel.

**Weekly Structure (Weeks 30–37):**

| Day | All Students | Retake Students (additional) |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Capstone production work | — |
| Tue | Capstone production work | 30 min targeted WebXam review (weak areas from initial test) |
| Wed | Capstone production work | — |
| Thu | Peer critique / check-in | 30 min targeted WebXam review |
| Fri | Capstone production work | — |

**Week-by-Week Milestones:**
- **Week 30:** Project proposal — concept, treatment, storyboard, shot list, schedule
- **Week 31:** Pre-production complete; begin principal photography/recording
- **Week 32:** Production — shooting/recording continues
- **Week 33:** Post-production begins — rough cut assembly, audio mix
- **Week 34:** Fine cut, color correction, sound design. **NLC May 6–10** — state qualifiers travel to Nashville
- **Week 35:** Final export of capstone. Begin showreel assembly. NLC debrief.
- **Week 36:** Showreel editing and polish. **⚑ WebXam Retake — available 30 days after initial test (by May 23)**
- **Week 37:** End-of-year screening — present capstone + showreel to class/BAC. Course evaluation. Career reflection.

> **Retake policy note:** WebXam requires a minimum 30-day gap between attempts. Students who test April 23 are eligible to retake May 23. Students who test earlier can retake sooner. Schedule makeups promptly to preserve the full retake window before school ends May 27.
>
> **Differentiation:** Students who passed the WebXam on the first attempt spend full periods on production and may take on stretch goals (behind-the-scenes featurette, director's commentary track). Students who need to retake dedicate ~30 minutes twice per week to targeted review on their weakest blueprint areas (7.3/7.7/7.8 production concepts, 2.9 project proposals, 2.10 equipment), using the remaining time on the capstone.
