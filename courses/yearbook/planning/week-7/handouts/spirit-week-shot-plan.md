# Spirit Week: What To Actually Shoot

**Yearbook | Week 7**

Spirit week is the easiest coverage of the year and most staffs still blow it, because everybody shoots
the same thing: their friends, posing, in one hallway, at one time of day.

**Here is how not to do that.**

---

## The rule that fixes most of it

**Three frames of every subject: wide, medium, close.**

| | What it gets |
|---|---|
| **Wide** | Where this is happening. The hallway, the gym, the crowd |
| **Medium** | Who is doing it. Waist up, in context |
| **Close** | The detail. The hands, the face, the thing they made |

A page needs all three. **A page of twelve medium shots is a page nobody wants to look at**, and it is
what you get when everyone shoots from wherever they happen to be standing.

---

## Shoot the moments, not the poses

A posed photo tells you people were there. **A candid tells you what it was like.**

| Instead of | Shoot |
|---|---|
| Four friends lined up smiling | The moment right after something funny happened |
| A person in their costume, standing still | The person putting the costume on, or fixing it |
| The winning team holding a trophy | The face of someone watching the last play |
| The decorated hallway, empty | Someone decorating it at 7am |

**Get there early.** Setup, practice, and getting ready are consistently better than the event, and
almost nobody shoots them.

---

## Daily plan

| Day | Go get |
|-----|--------|
| **Monday** | Hallways between classes. Wide of the whole hall, then work in closer. At least one teacher participating |
| **Tuesday** | **People who are not already in the book.** Take the list. Find them |
| **Wednesday** | Something being *done*: a game, a contest, a class doing the theme. Motion, not portraits |
| **Thursday** | Setup and practice for homecoming. The before |
| **Friday** | The events. Arrive early, stay late. **The crowd is as much a photo as the field is** |

---

## Settings, quickly

| Situation | Start at |
|---|---|
| **Hallway, indoors** | Higher ISO than feels comfortable. A slightly grainy sharp photo beats a clean blurry one |
| **Anything moving** | **1/500 or faster.** This is the difference between a photo and a smear |
| **Outdoors, daylight** | 1/500, low ISO. Easy conditions, no excuses |
| **Gym** | The hardest light in the building. High ISO, fast shutter, get close |
| **Night game** | Get as close as you are allowed. Distance plus dark equals nothing usable |

**Shoot far more than you need.** Twenty usable frames might be two hundred taken. That is normal and it
is what the card is for.

---

## Before you upload

Cull honestly. **Usable means:**

- In focus, on the thing that should be in focus
- Correctly exposed. Not a black shape, not a white blob
- **Something is happening**, or someone is doing something
- You can tell where you are
- Nobody is mid-blink or caught in an unflattering half-expression they would hate

> **Turning in twenty mediocre frames is worse than turning in twenty good ones out of two hundred
> taken.** The selection is part of the job.

---

## Checkout, one more time

- **Sign it out and sign it back in.** Every time
- **Same day return.** After 9th period is fine, the next morning is not
- Camera stays with you. Not a locker, not a car, not a friend
- Lens cap on when not shooting, strap on when out of the bag
- **Offload before you return it**
- Damage reported immediately is a problem. Damage found later is a much bigger one

**Cameras are limited and this is the busiest week.** Sign up for your day now. Friday will be full.
