# Yearbook Page Checklist — the "module" to paste on every page

**Yearbook · Week 3 · use in eDesign**

Drop the checklist below into a **text box off to the side of your page/spread** (outside the trim / on the
pasteboard so it never prints). It's a reminder of what a page *can* include while you lay it out — **not a
formula.** The required items are the basics every page needs; the rest is your call based on what your page is.
The point this week: **build boxes and styles now as placeholders**, so later you just drop in photos and text.

> **First decide: page or spread?** A **page** is one side; a **spread** is two facing pages that read as one.
> Either way, aim for **cohesion** — the elements look like they belong together. If a spread is **split in half**
> (two different topics), each half should still feel connected to the other through the design.

---

## THE MODULE — paste this into a text box on your page/spread

Copy everything between the lines. Check off what applies. **REQUIRED** items are on almost every page;
**OPTIONAL** items depend on what your page is — use them if they fit.

```
------ PAGE CHECKLIST (do not print) ------
REQUIRED
[ ] A clear FOCAL POINT - one thing the eye lands on first
    (often a big photo, but it could be a team photo edge-to-edge, or your call)
[ ] PHOTOS placed with intent (sizes/spots chosen on purpose, not random)
[ ] A CAPTION near every photo (room for who + what's happening)
[ ] A HEADLINE (the title of the page/spread)
[ ] FOLIO at the bottom (page number + section)
[ ] A DESIGN CREDIT - small "Page/Spread design by Your Name" (sign your work)
[ ] Cohesion: the page/spread reads as ONE design
[ ] Aligned to the COLUMNS; nothing important crosses the GUTTER

OPTIONAL - use if it fits your page
[ ] PHOTO CREDIT on candids ("Photo by Name")
[ ] A STORY (body copy) + a BYLINE ("Story by Name") - only if your page has a story
[ ] A QUOTE / pull-quote
[ ] A subhead / secondary headline

AS YOU BUILD
[ ] Placeholder text in every box (don't leave boxes empty)
[ ] Each text box set to a fitting PRESET - ask if you're unsure which
[ ] Shapes/photo frames are square or rectangular UNLESS a shape adds something
-------------------------------------------
```

> **Not every page looks the same.** Some sports pages have one big action shot; others lead with a **team photo
> that runs edge-to-edge**; some are more up to you. There's no single right layout — make deliberate choices and
> keep the page cohesive.

> **A note on shapes.** Fun shapes are tempting, but **only use a shape (circle, oval, star, etc.) if it actually
> adds something** to the design. Most of the time a random shape looks jarring, breaks the clean column grid, or
> forces a photo to crop in a weird way (heads and edges cut off in a circle). **Default to rectangles for photos
> and frames** — reach for another shape only when you can say *why* it makes the page better.

> **Stories are optional (and new).** We didn't write stories last year, so **not every page has one.** If your
> page has a story, add a body-copy box and a byline; if it doesn't, skip them. Ask if you're not sure whether
> your page should have a story.

> **Sign your work.** Every page/spread gets a small **"Page/Spread design by [your name]"** credit in caption
> styling. Take ownership of what you made.

## Read this first — placeholders & templates (the part people get stuck on)

- A **placeholder** is a box you build **now** and fill in **later**. An empty photo frame is a placeholder for a
  photo. A text box with dummy text is a placeholder for real words.
- **Put filler text in every text box** so you can see it and size it — type junk like
  `Xxxxxxx Xxxxxxx Xxxxxxx` or real dummy text. **Make the box big enough to hold real content** (a caption box
  should be long enough to fit a real name and sentence).
- You are **not** typing real names or the real story yet. That comes when photos and info arrive.
- Building and styling the boxes now = a **template**. Later you just **replace the placeholder**, and the size,
  font, and position are already right.

---

## What each item is (so you know what a preset is for)

Use these definitions to pick the right preset — and **if you're unsure which preset fits, ask.**

| Element | Required? | What it is | Placeholder to drop in |
|---------|-----------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Focal point** | Required | The one thing the eye lands on first — often a big photo, sometimes a team photo edge-to-edge, sometimes your call | An empty frame you've made clearly dominant |
| **Photos** | Required | The images that make the page; sizes and spots chosen on purpose | Empty frames, placed with intent |
| **Caption** | Required (per photo) | A short line that says **who** is in a photo and **what's happening** | `Xxxx Xxxx does xxxx during xxxx.` (long enough for a real name) |
| **Headline** | Required | The title of the page/spread; the biggest text | `HEADLINE HERE` |
| **Design credit** | Required | Who designed the page/spread — **your ownership line**, in caption styling | `Page design by Firstname Lastname` |
| **Folio** | Required | Page number + section at the bottom | `## Section` |
| **Photo credit** | Optional (common on candids) | Who **took** the photo | `Photo by Firstname Lastname` |
| **Byline** | Optional (only if there's a story) | Who **wrote** the story | `Story by Firstname Lastname` |
| **Body copy** | Optional (only if there's a story) | The story text | A block of dummy/filler text sized to the real copy |
| **Quote / pull-quote** | Optional | A highlighted quote used big as a design element | `"Placeholder quote goes here." - Name` |
| **Subhead** | Optional | A secondary headline under the main one | `Secondary headline` |

> **Caption vs. byline vs. photo credit** — these get mixed up: a **caption** describes what's in a photo; a
> **byline** names who **wrote** a story; a **photo credit** names who **took** the photo. Pick the preset that
> matches what the text *is*.

## Formatting boxes as templates in eDesign

As you build each box, **give it a preset** instead of typing in a default box:

- Select the text box, then use the **preset dropdown in the top-left** — it lists our set-up presets (**byline**,
  **caption**, and others). Pick the one that matches what the text *is* (use the definitions above).
- **Not sure which preset fits? Ask.** Better to ask than to guess — there's no penalty for asking, and it's how
  you learn what each preset is for.
- The presets already carry the right **fonts** for the book's theme — that's the point of using them.
- Once a box has a fitting preset, you can **copy it to the next page** and reuse it — that's the template.

## Teacher note

Run this live in eDesign today: create a box of a few types and show the **preset dropdown in the top-left** —
click it and pick a preset. Teach the **difference between a caption, a byline, and a photo credit** so they can
recognize which preset to reach for, and tell them plainly: **if you're unsure which preset is appropriate,
ask.**

Frame it as **choices, not a formula:**
- **Page vs. spread** — have each student say which they're doing, and push **cohesion** (a split spread still
  reads as one design).
- **The focal point isn't always a big hero photo** — some sports pages lead with a **team photo edge-to-edge**,
  others are more open; deliberate choice matters more than a fixed layout.
- **Stories are optional and new** — most didn't do stories last year, so body copy + byline only appear where a
  page actually has a story. As program lead you want **some** real stories in the book, so encourage a few
  students to take one on, but don't require it on every page.
- **Ownership** — everyone signs their page with a small **"design by"** credit.

Empty photo boxes are expected — the win today is a **cohesive, preset-styled, placeholder layout** that fits
what the page actually is, and that they've signed.
