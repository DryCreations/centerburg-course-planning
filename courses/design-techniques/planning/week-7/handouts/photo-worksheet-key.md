# Photo Worksheet: Key and Teaching Notes

**Design Techniques | Unit 2.1** | **Teacher only**

---

## Running it

| Day | Shape |
|-----|-------|
| **Wed** | BPA mention (10 min), shutter priority on the board (15 min), then shoot Challenges 1 to 3 |
| **Thu** | Aperture priority on the board (15 min), then Challenges 4 to 6 |
| **Fri** | Short period. Offload, rename, upload, final questions |

**The worksheet is digital**, make-a-copy on Classroom. **No printing.**

**Cameras stay in the room**, shared, signed out per period. All four bodies shoot RAW, including the T3,
since this is stills rather than video.

**While waiting for a camera, students answer the written questions.** There are twelve and they need no
equipment. Say that up front or you will have people standing around.

## Day 1 answers

**1.1** A faster shutter freezes motion, because the subject has less time to move while the sensor is
exposed.

**1.2** Open the aperture wider (smaller f-number), or raise the ISO. Accept "add light" as a third.

**Challenge 1.** Indoors on a kit lens, `1/500` and `1/1000` will usually be too dark away from a window.
**Say so in advance and frame it as the lesson**, not a failure: the trade is the content.

**Challenge 2.** Motion blur is the subject moving while the shutter is open. Camera shake is the whole
frame smearing because the photographer moved. **Below about `1/60` handheld, everything smears.**

**Challenge 3, the panning shot.** This is the one that was too fast last time.

> **A walking person needs `1/30`, `1/15`, or `1/8`.** `1/60` and faster will not blur a background
> behind someone walking.

Turning from the waist works because it rotates around a single stable axis. Moving arms introduces
vertical wobble that shows up immediately at these speeds.

**Expect roughly one keeper in fifteen.** Say that number out loud. Students think a low hit rate means
they are bad at it.

**Day 1 written**

1. Most frozen to most blurred: `1/1000`, `1/500`, `1/60`, `1/15`, `1"`
2. Any reasonable answer. Look for blur communicating **speed or energy** that a frozen frame loses
3. `1/1000` indoors: far too dark. `1/30`: the players smear and it is unusable for sports
4. The camera sets the **aperture**. It has to, because something must compensate for the exposure you
   changed

---

## Day 2 answers

**2.1** `f/3.5` lets in more light. **This is the one everybody gets wrong**, because the numbers run
backwards. Say it three times.

**2.2** Wide aperture (small f-number), to throw the cluttered background out of focus so the subject
separates from it.

**Challenge 4.** **The hidden variable is distance.** A wide aperture with the background two feet
behind the subject barely blurs. Students will conclude the aperture "did not work." Walk the room and
move them, or their subject, until there is real distance behind it.

**Challenge 5.** At `f/22` indoors the camera will pick something like `1/15` or slower and the photos
will shake. **That is the point of the question**, and it sets up question 7.

**Challenge 6.** Neither is "better." Look for an answer that says **it depends on what the photo is
about**: the subject, or the place.

**Day 2 written**

5. Shallowest to deepest: `f/3.5`, `f/5.6`, `f/8`, `f/16`, `f/22`
6. The **shutter speed**
7. Narrow aperture means little light, so the camera chose a slow shutter and the photo has camera shake.
   Fixes: open the aperture, raise ISO, brace the camera, or add light
8. **Both trade light against something else.** You cannot change one setting in isolation, because
   exposure has to stay balanced. That is the exposure triangle, with ISO as the third corner

---

## Day 3 answers

**9.** Expect "the panning shot." Correct.

**10.** RAW keeps everything the sensor recorded, so **white balance, exposure and color can be changed
after the fact** with no quality loss. A JPEG has already thrown that information away.

**11.** **Priority** means you set that one setting and the camera makes everything else work around it.
You have prioritized it.

**12.** Grade on whether the vocabulary is used correctly, not on whether the photo is good.

---

## White balance is deliberately not mentioned

**7.9.5 covers ISO, shutter, aperture and white balance together.** We are doing three of the four.

**Do not bring up white balance this week.** Shoot RAW, leave whatever the camera does, and fix it next
week in the editor, where the change is visible and instantaneous. That is a far better lesson than a
menu setting they will not understand the purpose of yet.

**7.9.5 is marked partial**, completing next week alongside 7.9.6 (editing photographs). The worksheet
says so, so the alignment is honest.

---

## What to grade

**Not photo quality.** Grade the explanations: 1.1, 1.2, the three Challenge tables, and questions 1
through 12.

Say this to students: **a blurry photo with a correct explanation of why it blurred is worth more than a
lucky sharp one with nothing behind it.**

---

## The ranges are ranges on purpose

Every challenge gives **three or four values to try**, not one to use.

A student handed `1/500` learns to obey a number. A student handed `1/250, 1/500, 1/1000` **finds the
edge themselves**, and the edge is the actual content of the lesson.

---

## Known correction

The Yearbook Week 5 shutter handout lists **`1/60` for panning a runner**. That is too fast for a walking
subject and is why the panning shots came out with sharp backgrounds.

**This worksheet uses `1/30`, `1/15`, `1/8`.** The Yearbook handout has already been shared with
students, so it has not been edited. Worth correcting verbally in Yearbook, or reposting that handout
with a fix.
