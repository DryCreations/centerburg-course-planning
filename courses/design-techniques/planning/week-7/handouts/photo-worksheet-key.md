**Digital, make-a-copy. No printing.** Cameras stay in the room, shared, per period.

**Check every body is on RAW and ISO on Auto before first period.** A body left on JPEG costs that
student the week and you will not find out until Monday.

---

## Day 1

**1.** A faster shutter freezes motion: the subject has less time to move while the sensor is exposed.

**2.** Open the aperture wider (smaller f-number), or raise ISO. Accept "add light."

**Challenge 1.** Indoors on a kit lens, `1/500` and `1/1000` are usually too dark away from a window.
**Say so in advance and frame it as the lesson.** The trade is the content.

**3.** Usually `1/500` or `1/1000`. **4.** Usually `1/1000` indoors.

**Challenge 2.** **5.** Around `1/15` to `1/8`. **6.** Motion blur is the subject moving while the
shutter is open. Camera shake is the whole frame smearing because the photographer moved. Below `1/60`
handheld, everything smears.

**Challenge 3, panning.** **7.** Usually `1/30` or `1/15`.

> **This is the one that was too fast last time.** A walking person needs `1/30`, `1/15`, `1/8`. `1/60`
> and faster will not blur a background behind a walker.

Turning from the waist rotates around one stable axis. Arms add vertical wobble that shows immediately
at these speeds.

**Say the one-in-fifteen ratio out loud.** Students read a low hit rate as being bad at it.

**8.** `1/1000`, `1/500`, `1/60`, `1/15`, `1"`

**9.** Look for blur communicating **speed or energy** that a frozen frame loses.

**10.** The **aperture**. Something must compensate for the exposure you changed.

---

## Day 2

**11.** `f/3.5`. **This is the one everybody gets wrong**, because the numbers run backwards. Say it
three times.

**12.** Wide aperture, small f-number, to throw the cluttered background out of focus.

**Challenge 4.** **13.** **Distance.** A wide aperture with the background two feet behind the subject
barely blurs, and students will conclude the aperture "did not work." Walk the room and move them or
their subject.

**Challenge 5.** **14.** At `f/22` indoors the camera picks something like `1/15` or slower and the
photos shake. **That is the point**, and it sets up question 19.

**Challenge 6.** **15 to 17.** Neither is better. Look for **it depends what the photo is about**: the
subject, or the place.

**18.** `f/3.5`, `f/5.6`, `f/8`, `f/16`, `f/22`

**19.** Narrow aperture means little light, so the camera chose a slow shutter and you have camera
shake. Fixes: open the aperture, raise ISO, brace it, add light.

**20.** **Both trade light against something else.** You cannot change one setting alone, because
exposure has to stay balanced. That is the exposure triangle, with ISO as the third corner.

---

## Day 3

**24.** Expect "panning." Correct.

**25.** RAW keeps everything the sensor recorded, so **white balance, exposure and color can change
after the fact** with no quality loss. A JPEG already threw that away.

**26.** You set that one setting and the camera works around it. You have **prioritized** it.

**27.** Grade on correct vocabulary use, not photo quality.

---

## White balance is deliberately absent

**7.9.5 covers ISO, shutter, aperture and white balance together.** We are doing three of four.

**Do not raise white balance this week.** Shoot RAW, fix it next week in the editor where the change is
visible and instant. Far better than a menu setting with no visible purpose yet.

**7.9.5 is marked partial** in the worksheet and outline, completing next week with 7.9.6.

---

## What to grade

**Not photo quality.** Grade 1, 2, 6, 9, 10, 12, 13, 19, 20, and 24 to 27.

Say it to students: **a blurry photo with a correct explanation beats a lucky sharp one with nothing
behind it.**

---

## Ranges are ranges on purpose

Every challenge gives three or four values to try, not one to use. A student handed `1/500` learns to
obey a number. A student handed the range **finds the edge themselves**, and the edge is the lesson.
