## Objective

Explore typography tools in Adobe Express. Practice spacing, font pairing, and font categories. Then update your personal brand kit with final font and color choices.

---

## A Note on Terminology

Adobe Express uses simplified labels for type controls:
- **Letter spacing** (similar to kerning/tracking in professional software)
- **Line spacing** (similar to leading)
- **Paragraph spacing**

These labels are different from the industry terms you will encounter in your vocabulary. When we discuss "kerning," "tracking," or "leading" — those are the professional terms for what these controls accomplish. You should know both: the standard term and what it's called in Express.

> **Font size in Express is measured in points (pt).** That is the standard for print. You may also see points referenced in WebXam questions.

## Part A — Font Categories

### Step 1: Identify and Test Font Types

1. In Adobe Express, open a new canvas.
2. Type the same short sentence (e.g., "Design is communication.") in each of these font categories:
   - **Serif** (e.g., Georgia, Merriweather)
   - **Sans-serif** (e.g., Montserrat, Open Sans)
   - **Display / Decorative** (something bold or stylized)
   - **Monospace** (if available)

3. For each, write 1 word describing the feeling or mood it creates.

---

## Part B — Spacing Experiments

### Step 2: Letter Spacing

4. Type the word "DESIGN" in a large sans-serif heading font.
5. Use the **Letter spacing** slider in Express: try three settings (tight, default, wide).
6. Write one observation: when would wide letter spacing look good? When would tight look good?

### Step 3: Line Spacing

7. Type a 3–4 line paragraph of text.
8. Use the **Line spacing** slider: try tight (around 1.0), normal, and loose (around 1.8).
9. Which version is easiest to read? Write 1 sentence explaining why.

---

## Part C — Font Pairing

### Step 4: Test 3 Pairs

10. Create a small layout with a heading, subheading, and a line of body text. Test each pair:
    - **Pair 1:** Serif heading + sans-serif body
    - **Pair 2:** Sans-serif heading + sans-serif body (same family)
    - **Pair 3:** Display heading + clean sans-serif body

11. For each pair: write one sentence — *"This pairing feels [adjective] and would work for [type of brand or context]."*

## Part D — Update Your Brand Kit

### Step 5: Finalize Your Personal Brand Kit

Now that you know more about color and type, update your brand kit in Adobe Express (**Brand → Brand Kit**):

- **Colors:** Confirm or update the 2–3 colors you want for your portfolio website. Use hex codes from color.adobe.com if needed.
- **Fonts:** Choose the pair you want (one heading, one body) from your testing above.
- **Logo:** Make sure your monogram from Unit 1.0 is uploaded. You will redesign it in the project if you want — or keep it.

Take a screenshot of your updated brand kit and submit it.
