## Student Overview

### What Is This Unit About?

This is the main production unit of Q2 — you are actually making stuff. You will design a promotional piece and a set of social media posts using your brand and your plan from Unit 2.2. This is also the unit where students who want to compete in BPA can seriously pursue that goal.

### What You'll Be Able to Do

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

- Design a primary graphic (poster, flyer, or digital piece) with your brand applied
- Create social media posts sized for at least 2 platforms
- Give and receive feedback on design work
- Export your work in the right format

### Choose Your Direction

This unit has a project, and you have choices about what you produce:
- **Option A — BPA #410 / V12:** Follow the BPA event prompt. This is the recommended path if you want to compete. Work with the teacher on which event fits your work.
- **Option B — Personal portfolio:** Make a promotional piece for yourself, your creative work, or a made-up project you care about.
- **Option C — Class client:** Design for a real or fictional organization. Teacher may have optional community client opportunities.

> If you are unsure, pick something that interests you. Better work comes from subjects you care about.

### Common Issues & Responses

- **All 4 pieces look identical:** Campaign consistency ≠ exact copy. Each piece should be adapted for its format and platform, not just resized.
- **Contrast ratio not checked:** Require a screenshot from a contrast checker (WebAIM or built-in Express accessibility) in the production log.
- **Peer critique is too vague:** Require that each feedback item names a specific standard or design principle.

### Differentiation Notes

- **Students who need more support:** Provide a platform specs reference card (Instagram, Facebook, and LinkedIn standard sizes).
- **Students who finish early:** Extension in project.md — create a "campaign brief one-pager" summarizing the campaign for a client presentation.

### Assessment Notes

- Project uses the 1–4 look-for rubric. Walk the room particularly during Weeks 17–18. Key checkpoints: brand kit applied across all 4 pieces; alt text documented; production log with file names.
