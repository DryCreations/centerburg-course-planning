# Lab 1: Photo Editing — Composition, Crop & White Balance

**Course:** Design Techniques (145095)
**Unit:** 2.1 — Photography: Capture & Editing
**Duration:** Week 10, Days 1–4

---

## Objective

Practice examining and improving photos using the editing tools in Adobe Express. Focus on composition techniques (rule of thirds, leading lines, cropping) and white balance/tone adjustments.

> **No camera required for this lab.** Your teacher will provide a set of photos to work with.

---

## A Note on Photography in This Class

In this unit we focus on the skills that apply to *any* photo — whether you took it yourself or found one. Great designers know how to look at an image and improve it. We will cover:
- Composition (how the image is framed)
- Cropping (changing the frame after the fact)
- White balance and tone (correcting color casts and exposure)
- Editing in Adobe Express

> **Promotional Photography as BPA:** If you are interested in BPA's Promotional Photography event, that requires a DSLR camera. You can use a phone camera and transfer photos, or borrow equipment. Your teacher can share details. But we will learn all the underlying concepts in class — you do not need a DSLR for the class lab work.

## Part B — Crop and Compose in Adobe Express

### Step 2: Open and Edit

1. Open Adobe Express. Upload 3 photos from the teacher-provided set.
2. For each photo:
   - Use the **Crop** tool to reframe the image in a way that improves composition.
   - Aim for at least one image that demonstrates the **rule of thirds** after cropping.
   - Aim for at least one image that demonstrates **leading lines**.

## Part C — White Balance & Tone

### Step 3: Correct the Color

3. In Adobe Express, open the photo adjustment tools for one of your images.
4. Explore:
   - **Warmth / Temperature** — makes the image warmer (more orange/yellow) or cooler (more blue)
   - **Brightness / Exposure** — overall lightness
   - **Contrast** — difference between light and dark areas
   - **Saturation** — intensity of color

5. For each adjustment, write what happened when you moved the slider:

| Adjustment | What I did | What changed in the image |
|------------|------------|--------------------------|
| Warmth | | |
| Brightness | | |
| Contrast | | |
| Saturation | | |

> **Rule first, break it later:** White balance "correct" means the image looks like it would to the human eye in that lighting. Sometimes we break this on purpose for mood — but we learn the rules first.
