# Quiz: Rollout, Handoff & Professional Publishing

---

## Questions

| # | Question | A | B | C | D | Answer |
|---|----------|---|---|---|---|--------|
| 1 | A design "rollout" refers to: | Printing physical copies | The scheduled, phased release of a brand or campaign to its intended audience | Sending files to the client via email | A type of layout grid | B |
| 2 | A professional "handoff package" to a client should include: | The designer's contact info only | Final exports (PNG, PDF), source files, asset library, brand guidelines, and training documentation | A receipt for payment | A link to the designer's Instagram | B |
| 3 | An "asset library" in a client handoff means: | A list of stock photos available to purchase | An organized collection of all project files: logo variants, color codes, licensed fonts, and photography — delivered in a structured folder | The designer's personal file archive | The project brief from the start of the project | B |
| 4 | For a commercial print job, the minimum resolution for images is: | 72 PPI | 150 PPI | 300 DPI | 96 PPI | C |
| 5 | Print-ready PDF files require: | RGB color mode and no bleed | CMYK color mode, 300 DPI resolution, bleed area, and trim marks | JPEG format only | Transparency set to 50% | B |
| 6 | "Training documentation" in a client handoff is: | The designer's invoice | Written instructions explaining how the client can update or maintain the delivered design using their own tools | A copy of the project brief | The designer's contract | B |
| 7 | A "deployment checklist" is used to: | Track hours worked on a project | Verify that all requirements — technical and creative — are met before publishing or delivering a design | Schedule client meetings | Send reminder emails | B |
| 8 | A "soft launch" differs from a full rollout in that: | It costs more money | It targets a limited audience before the full public launch — allowing final testing and adjustments | It involves only print pieces | It skips all review steps | B |
| 9 | **[HOT]** A designer finishes a brand identity project for a client. She delivers a PDF export and says "done!" The client wants to use the logo on a T-shirt, update the website, and post on Instagram — but has no idea how. What did she forget to include? | A QR code | The handoff package: source files, asset library (all logo formats, color codes, font names), brand guidelines, and brief training notes on how to use each asset | The final invoice | A second revision | B |
| 10 | "Usage rights" in a handoff context refers to: | The designer's personal copyright over the brand | The specific terms under which fonts, images, or other assets may legally be used — commercial, web, or print — which must be documented and transferred | The client's right to request refunds | File naming conventions | B |
| 11 | **[HOT]** A designer uses a free Google font in her brand design. The client wants to put the logo on merchandise sold at events. Is there an issue? | Yes — no fonts may be used commercially | No — all Google Fonts are licensed under open licenses permitting commercial use, including merchandise; but the designer should still document this in the asset library | Yes — Google Fonts expire after 30 days | No — fonts don't require licensing documentation | B |
| 12 | When delivering a published digital deliverable to a client at project completion, a designer should document: | Only the date it was published | The published URL, the platform used, any custom domain configuration, and instructions for the client on how to manage future updates | Only the web link | The font sizes used | B |
| 13 | **[HOT]** A designer creates a rollout timeline for a new brand launch. She plans: Week 1 = update website, Week 2 = post social media announcement, Week 3 = print posters, Week 4 = email newsletter. What launch strategy is this called, and what is its benefit? | A hard launch — all at once for maximum impact | A phased rollout — spreading the brand reveal across channels over time, building audience awareness and engagement progressively | A soft launch — only reaching a subset of the audience | An A/B test — comparing which channel works better | B |
