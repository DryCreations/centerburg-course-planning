## Design it yourself (2.9.5)

**Your logo and the main pieces of your project have to be your design.** A template with your name
typed in is not your design, and neither is a traced or changed version of someone else's logo.

You have the **full Adobe suite.** If you want this to turn out well, use it:

- **Illustrator:** logos and anything that has to stay sharp at any size. Try the **Pen tool**, the
  **Shape Builder**, and **Image Trace** on a sketch you drew yourself
- **Photoshop:** editing your photos, and putting your logo on a shirt or sign for a mockup
- **InDesign:** layouts with several pages, like a brochure or menu
- **Adobe Express:** quick drafts and social posts

We will go deeper into these in later quarters. Working ahead now is encouraged.

## What you have the right to use (1.3.8)

- **Your own photos, drawings, and designs.** Always the best choice
- **Fonts:** Adobe Fonts (in Creative Cloud) or Google Fonts. Both allow use in logos and projects
- **Public domain:** work no one owns anymore
- **Creative Commons:** follow the license. Most ask you to give credit
- **Free-license photo sites,** like Unsplash or Pexels, for backgrounds or extras only. Save the link
- **Anything you have written permission to use.** Photographed a person? Get a **model release**

**Check your event guidelines first.** Many BPA events require your own photos and graphics, and some
limit AI-generated images.

## Never

- Google Images or Pinterest
- Another company's logo, or artwork you traced
- Something credited but not licensed. **Credit is not permission**
