## Part 3: Write about your poster

Post your poster in **Design Techniques > Unit 1.1**, then write a short description underneath it. Use the
sentence starters below, the same ones from last week's bell ringer, now pointed at your own poster.

1. The **first thing I see** (the focal point) is ______, because I made it stand out by ______.
2. This design uses **size** to create hierarchy: the headline that says "______" is the biggest, so a viewer
   reads it first, then ______, then ______.
3. This design uses **color** to create hierarchy: the ______ color stands out against the ______ background,
   which pulls the eye to ______.
4. This design uses **font weight or different fonts**: the bold ______ font on the headline makes "______"
   pop, while the ______ font is used for the smaller details.
5. This design uses **alignment**: the elements line up along ______.
6. The **reading order** is: first ______, then ______, then ______.

Turn **two to four** of those into a paragraph. Do not just list the sentences.

### This is what a finished one looks like

> The first thing you see on this poster is the headline "FALL TALENT SHOW," because I made it by far the
> largest element and set it in a heavy display font. The date and location sit underneath at about half that
> size, so the reading order is headline, then details, then the ticket fine print at the bottom. I used one
> orange accent against a dark navy background so the headline separates instantly, and I lined every element
> up to the same left edge to keep it feeling organized.

That is four sentences, and it names **five** decisions: size, weight, reading order, color contrast, and
alignment. That is the standard. "I made a poster about the talent show and I think it looks good" is not.

## Part 4: The real names for what's on a website

Every site is built from the same handful of parts. Point at your own site and name each one:

| What you might call it | The real name | What it does |
|-------------------------|---------------|--------------|
| "The bar at the top" | **Navigation menu (nav)** | Links that let a visitor move between pages |
| "The big section at the top of the home page" | **Header / hero section** | The first thing a visitor sees |
| "A page" | **Page** | One section of the site with its own content and address |
| "The stuff at the bottom" | **Footer** | Contact info, links, or credits repeated on every page |
| "The picture at the top of a page" | **Banner / hero image** | A large image that sets the tone for that page |
| "One entry in the menu" | **Navigation link / tab** | A single clickable item that goes to one page |
| "The menu that opens when you hover" | **Dropdown / submenu** | A nested group of links under one menu item |

## Why this matters

This is your first look at **interactive media components** (standard 7.1.2) and a preview of the UX/UI work
starting tomorrow: the navigation, the pages, and the order they are arranged in are exactly the kind of
structure you will be planning on purpose.
