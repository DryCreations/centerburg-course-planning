# Week 5 Project: The Redesign Project

**Course:** Design Techniques | **Assigned:** Mon Sep 14 | **Due:** Fri Sep 18
**Category:** Projects | **Tool:** paper, then Figma
**Late:** accepted, ask to reopen

Find a real screen that does not work. Prove exactly what is wrong with it. Redesign it so it works.

**You are redesigning ONE screen, phone sized.** Not a whole app. One screen done well beats five done
vaguely.

---

## Choosing your target

Your screen has to pass all three:

1. You can **screenshot** it
2. It is **school appropriate**
3. Something about it **actually bothers you**, and you can say what in one sentence

Get your target approved Monday before you mark it up.

**Stuck?** A food ordering app, a school or district page, an athletics schedule, a streaming service's
browse screen, a store checkout, a game's settings menu, a ticket or airline page, a library catalog, a
recipe site buried in ads.

## The write-up

Four to six sentences. Not a description, an argument. Each sentence should name a decision and the problem
it solved.

**Sentence starters:**

- "The biggest problem with the original was ________, because ________."
- "I made ________ larger and ________ smaller so that ________."
- "I moved ________ because a person coming here to ________ needs it before ________."
- "I removed ________ entirely. It was competing with ________ and nobody came to this screen for it."
- "If I had more time I would ________."

---

## What makes this good, not just done

- Your complaints are **specific and named**. "It is ugly" is not a finding. "The primary action is the same
  size and color as four other buttons, so nothing wins" is a finding.
- Your redesign **fixes what you complained about.** If your teardown is about hierarchy and your redesign has
  the same flat hierarchy, you did not solve it.
- You **removed** things. Most broken screens are broken because everything competes. Deleting is a design
  decision.
- **A stranger can use it.** That is what the partner test is for.
