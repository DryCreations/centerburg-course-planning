# The App Redesign Project (Weeks 5 and 6)

**Course:** Design Techniques | **Assigned:** Mon Sep 14 | **Due:** Fri Sep 25
**Category:** Projects | **Tool:** paper, then Figma
**Late:** accepted, ask to reopen

**Two weeks, one prototype.** You will work on the same app the whole time, adding a little more each day.

---

## What you are making

**A small, working prototype of 3 to 4 screens** that a person can actually click through, plus the paper
thinking behind it.

**Scope is deliberately tiny.** You are not designing a whole app. You are designing **one task** a person
does in an app, done well. "Order one coffee." "Sign up for a club." "Check tomorrow's lunch." That is the
whole product.

---

## Pick your project

**Option A: Fix something that frustrates you.** An app or website you actually use where something is
genuinely annoying. Screenshot the bad screen, prove what is wrong, redesign it. This is the best option if
you already have something in mind.

**Option B: Pick from this list.** All of these are small on purpose:

| Idea | The one task |
|------|--------------|
| **School lunch menu** | See what is for lunch tomorrow |

| **Athletics schedule** | Find when the next home game is |

**Either way, write your one task down on day one.** Every screen you build has to serve it.

## Requirements for the final prototype

| # | Requirement |
|---|-------------|
| 1 | **3 to 4 phone frames**, all named |
| 2 | A clear **home or starting screen** |
| 3 | One **primary action** per screen, obviously the most prominent thing on it |
| 4 | **At least 4 different interaction types** from the list below |
| 5 | Every screen reachable, and a way **back** from every screen |
| 6 | Consistent **navigation**: things that repeat sit in the same place on every screen |
| 7 | A **paper wireframe** for every screen, made before you built it |
| 8 | A **partner test**: someone completes your task without you explaining anything |
| 9 | A **write-up** naming at least four decisions and what each one solves |

## The interaction types

Last week you did one: a button that goes to another screen. Here are the rest. **Use at least four
different ones.**

| # | Interaction | What it means | Taught |
|---|-------------|---------------|--------|
| 1 | **Navigate** | Tap something, go to another screen | Done |
| 2 | **Back** | Return to where you came from | Mon wk 5 |
| 3 | **Overlay** | Something appears on top: a confirmation, a menu, a popup | Tue wk 5 |
| 4 | **State change** | The same screen, one thing different: a toggle flips, a heart fills, a button says "Added" | Wed wk 5 |
| 5 | **Scroll** | A list longer than the screen that actually scrolls | Thu wk 5 |
| 6 | **Selection** | Pick one of several options and see which is picked | Mon wk 6 |
| 7 | **Input** | A field that looks like you typed in it | Tue wk 6 |
| 8 | **Transition** | The animation between screens, chosen on purpose | Wed wk 6 |

**You do not need all eight.** Four, done well, is the requirement. There is a short lab for each one.

## What makes this good, not just done

- **The task is small and it works.** A person can do the one thing, start to finish, with no explanation.
- **One thing wins on every screen.** If everything is the same size, nothing is the primary action.
- **It is consistent.** The back button is in the same corner every time. Things that repeat do not move.
- **You can say why.** Every decision should have a reason that is about the person using it, not about what
  looked cool.
