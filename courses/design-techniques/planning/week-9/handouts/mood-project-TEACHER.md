# Three Moods: Sourcing the Photos

**Teacher-only.** Student sheet is `mood-project.md`.

---

## Why this instead of the repair clinic

The repair clinic needed four RAW files each broken exactly **one** way: underexposed but recoverable,
one clear color cast with a known-white object in frame, flat with no clipping, one color off and the rest
correct. **A free gallery is not going to hand you that set.** Those are photos somebody failed to take
well, and galleries post their good ones. Building it meant shooting all four yourself.

**This assignment does not care what the files look like**, as long as they are decent RAW photos with
some range in them. That makes it a download instead of a shoot, and it puts the lesson on the decision
rather than the repair, which is the harder skill anyway.

**The diagnostic muscle is not lost.** It is Tuesday's `tone-lab.md` and Wednesday's `two-photos.md`,
where they push every slider to both ends and find clipping by causing it.

## Where to get the photos

**[lapseoftheshutter.com free RAW landscapes](https://www.lapseoftheshutter.com/free-raw-landscape-images-for-retouching/)**
is the best starting point. No email and no signup, and they are landscapes, which is the ideal subject
for this assignment: a landscape has sky, shadow and a horizon, so all three mood decisions have
something to act on.

**[signatureedits.com/free-raw-photos](https://www.signatureedits.com/free-raw-photos/)** has far more
variety, including portraits, and is mostly **Canon CR2**, which is the same format the Rebels shoot, so
the sliders behave identically to their own files. Free for any use.

**[raw.pixls.us](https://raw.pixls.us/)** if you want files from a specific camera body. Technical and
plain, but reliable.

**I could not open any of these pages.** Outbound file fetching is blocked from the environment that
built this, so everything above comes from search results and descriptions, not from looking. **Budget
ten minutes to check them yourself**, and keep in mind that a very recent camera's RAW may not open in an
older Camera Raw. A CR2 from a Rebel-era body is the safe bet.

## What to pick

**Six to eight files is plenty**, and students pick one. You want range inside each photo:

- **Sky and ground in the same frame.** Something bright and something dark, so Highlights and Shadows
  both have work to do
- **Real color somewhere.** A photo that is already grey gives Temperature nothing to push against
- **Not already graded.** Some free files have been edited and exported back out. If it opens looking
  finished, skip it: the student has nowhere to go
- **Mixed subjects if you can.** A landscape, a building, one with a person in it. The person makes
  question 4 land, because skin resists a cold grade in a way a barn does not

**Avoid:** anything with a face you cannot identify the rights on, sunsets (already so warm that
Temperature does almost nothing visible), and night shots (no highlight range).

## Setup

1. **Read-only class folder** on the shared drive, named `Mood-Photos`
2. **Tell them to copy before editing.** Someone will edit in place
3. Confirm **one** file opens in Camera Raw on a student machine before you post the assignment

## What you are actually grading

**Questions 5, 6 and 7.** The exports are evidence; the defense is the assignment.

| Q | What you are listening for |
|---|---------------------------|
| **2** | A reason tied to **this photo**. "It has a big sky so bright and open works" beats "I like warm" |
| **3** | Three named sliders and a direction. Vague answers mean they dragged until it looked nice, which is fine as practice and not fine as an answer |
| **4** | The honest one. Skin fights cold grades, green foliage fights heavy crushes, overcast skies fight high contrast |
| **5** | **The audience is the whole question.** Any mood is right for somebody. If they cannot name who, they have not made a decision |
| **6** | **Either answer earns full credit.** You are grading the defense. The students who say "yes, honest is better" and mean it are often the sharper ones |
| **7** | That the photo decides how much a slider matters. A photo with no shadows does not care about the Shadows slider |

## The thing to say out loud at least once

**A mood that fights the photo is not a failed edit, it is a finding.** Several of them will discover
that their photo refuses to go cold or refuses to go faded. That is question 4, and it is worth more than
a clean result.
