## Where to get them

**[signatureedits.com/free-raw-photos](https://www.signatureedits.com/free-raw-photos/)** is the best
match. It is a gallery of RAW files uploaded by photographers for free use, personal and commercial,
**mostly Canon CR2**, which is the same format your students are already shooting on the Rebels. No
account needed as far as the coverage of it goes, though I could not open the page to confirm that
myself.

Backups if that one disappoints:

- **[raw.pixls.us](https://raw.pixls.us/)** organizes samples by camera body, so you can pull files from
  a Rebel specifically. Smaller and more technical, less pretty
- **[fileexamples.com/category/photography](https://www.fileexamples.com/category/photography)** for
  plain CR2, NEF and DNG samples when you just need a file that opens

**Check one file opens in your actual editor before you commit to a set.** If the raw dialog on these
machines is an older Camera Raw, a very recent camera's RAW may not be supported. A CR2 from a Rebel-era
body is the safe bet, and it is also what the students shot, so the sliders behave the same.

## What to pick, and nothing more

**Do not pick four nice photos.** Pick four with the specific problems, and if the free gallery does not
have them, **make them.** Shoot four yourself in five minutes with the Rebel:

| File | What it needs | How to shoot it on purpose |
|------|---------------|----------------------------|
| **1. Too dark** | Underexposed by two or three stops, but **not** black. Detail still recoverable in the shadows | Meter correctly, then dial exposure compensation down three stops |
| **2. Wrong white balance** | A strong cast, and **something in frame you know is white or grey** | Shoot indoors under the fluorescents with white balance set to Daylight. Put a sheet of paper in the frame |
| **3. Flat and lifeless** | Correctly exposed, no real blacks, no real whites. Everything mushed into the middle | Shoot on an overcast day, or through a window, with a low-contrast subject. Avoid anything with a bright highlight |
| **4. One color wrong** | Correct overall, with one obviously off color | A green cast on skin under gym lighting works. So does one oversaturated red object |

**If you shoot them yourself, shoot RAW**, and keep them under control: the point is that each file has
**one** problem. A photo with three problems makes question 1, 3, 5 or 7 unanswerable, because the
student cannot tell which thing they are naming.

## Setup

1. Put the four in a **read-only class folder** on the shared drive, named `Repair-Clinic`
2. Name them `Fix1_TooDark`, `Fix2_WhiteBalance`, `Fix3_Flat`, `Fix4_ColorCast`. **The names give away
   the diagnosis**, so if you want questions 1, 3, 5 and 7 to be real, name them `Fix1` through `Fix4`
   and nothing else
3. **Tell them to copy before editing.** Someone will edit in place and someone else will open it after

## Why this one and not the photo essay

The unit's own artifact is a 6-8 image annotated edit suite, which is a Q2-sized deliverable. Week 9 is
the last week of the quarter and there is a quiz plus BPA selection landing in it. **This is the same
competencies at a quarter of the size**, and it has one advantage the essay does not: everyone works
identical files, so a student with four weak photos is not graded on their photos.

## Answer sketches

| Q | What you are listening for |
|---|---------------------------|
| **1** | Only in parts, and the way to tell is that the bright areas are fine. A globally dark photo needs Exposure; a partly dark one needs Shadows |
| **2** | Shadows, because Exposure would blow out the parts that were already correct |
| **3** | Name the object, not the direction alone. "The paper is blue" beats "it looks cold" |
| **4** | Any Kelvin they can defend. **Ask what the eyedropper did when they clicked something that was not grey** |
| **5** | No true black and no true white. Everything is mid-grey. Contrast, or Whites and Blacks |
| **6** | Three is the limit on purpose. Contrast, Whites, Blacks is the clean answer. Clarity instead of Contrast is defensible |
| **7** | Name it. If they cannot name it, that is the lesson of question 9 |
| **8** | Global Saturation would have pulled the colors that were already correct. HSL touches one |
| **9** | **Usually file 3.** Nothing is wrong enough to point at, which is exactly why flat photos stay flat |
