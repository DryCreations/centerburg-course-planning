## Step 2: Ten minutes. Get all three looking good.

**Everything from Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday. No questions for this part.**

**Work in this order on each photo:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Temperature**, and Tint if it needs it |
| **2** | **Contrast**, then **Exposure** |
| **3** | **Tonal details:** Highlights, Shadows, Whites, Blacks |
| **4** | **Vibrance, Saturation, Clarity, Dehaze** |
| **5** | Final tweaks. **Get it looking nice** |

**You need a decent base before the color work makes any sense.** A photo that is still too dark will
not be saved by changing its greens.

## Step 4: Find out which slider owns what

**Pick any photo. Pick one color in it. Drag that color's Saturation to −100.**

**2.** What went grey, and **what did you expect to go grey that did not?**

> **Most things in a photo are not one color.** A sky is usually blue **and** aqua. Grass is green **and**
> yellow. Skin is orange **and** red. **One slider rarely owns a whole object**, so you will often need
> two of them to do one job.

---

## Step 5: Make one color win, on each photo

**For each of the three photos, pick one color and make it the first thing the eye lands on.**

Three ways, and you can use all of them:

- **Pull the other colors' Saturation down.** Subtracting beats adding
- **Shift the Hue** of anything competing with it, out of its way
- **Change its Luminance** so it sits forward or sits back

**3.** On your favorite of the three: which color did you make win, and **what three moves did you
make?**

**4.** Which of the three photos needed **the most** color work? Why that one?

**5.** Name one thing you set in the first ten minutes that you **went back and changed** after doing the
color work. Why did it need changing?
