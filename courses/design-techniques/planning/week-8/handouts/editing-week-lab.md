## About clipping: a correction from yesterday

**Yesterday the rule was "avoid red and blue." That was too strict.**

**A clipped area is a solid block of one color.** No detail, no texture, nothing to tell apart. The
warnings tell you where that is happening. They do **not** tell you that you made a mistake.

**Most good photos have at least one thing that is solid black.** A photo with no true black anywhere
looks washed out, foggy and weak. **That true black is what makes an image feel anchored.**

### So here is the actual rule

| | |
|---|---|
| **What you want** | Push until the warning **just barely appears**, then stop. A few pixels. Not a spreading patch |
| **What you do not want** | A **large area** clipped, or clipping **on the subject.** A blown-out face is a mistake. A blown-out light bulb is not |
| **Who decides** | **You do. Aesthetics come first.** The warning is information you are choosing with, not a rule you are obeying |

**The question to ask is never "is anything clipped."** It is **"is anything I care about clipped."**

# Wednesday: The whole process, plus four new sliders

**Start with a new photo** from the set. You are taking this one all the way through, in order.

## The order, and why it is this order

**Coarse before fine. Global before local. Color last.**

| | Step | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Temperature, Tint** | Fix the color cast. A cast makes every decision after it a guess |
| **2** | **Exposure** | Overall brightness. Get the midtones roughly right |
| **3** | **Contrast** | Coarse separation of dark from light |
| **4** | **Whites, then Blacks** | **Set the two ends.** This is where your black point gets made |
| **5** | **Highlights, then Shadows** | Recover what is sitting inside those ends |
| **6** | **Dehaze** | Cuts through atmospheric haze. **Strong and global**, so it goes before the fine work |
| **7** | **Clarity** | Midtone contrast. Texture and edges |
| **8** | **Vibrance, then Saturation** | Color intensity. **Last, because everything above changes color** |

**Each step leaves less for the next one to do.** If you skip to Clarity first, you will do it twice.

### Where to find the new ones

**Clarity** is in the Basic panel, below Saturation, in a group sometimes labeled **Presence.**

**Dehaze** is in the Basic panel in newer versions of Camera Raw. **In older versions it is in the FX
panel**, the one with the **fx** icon in the row of tabs. **If you cannot find Dehaze at all, your version
does not have it.** Say so on your sheet and skip step 6: Contrast and Blacks do part of the same job.

## Work through it

**1.** Which photo did you pick, and why that one?

**2.** Do steps 1 through 5. **Write down your six numbers.** Temperature, Exposure, Contrast, Whites,
Blacks, and whichever of Highlights or Shadows you used.

**3.** At step 4, you set your black point. **Did you take Blacks far enough to just trigger the blue
warning?** Where in the photo did it appear first, and did you keep it?

## Dehaze

**4.** Push **Dehaze** up hard, then down hard. **What is it doing?** Look at the distance, not the
foreground.

**5.** Dehaze also changes something other than contrast. **What?**

**6.** Set it where you want it. What did you land on?

## Clarity

**7.** Push **Clarity** up hard. **What is it doing to edges and texture?** Where in your photo is it most
obvious?

**8.** Pull Clarity down hard. **Describe the look in one word.**

**9.** Now try Clarity at +100 on **the portrait** in the set. **Describe exactly what it does to skin.**
This is why people hate over-clarified photos.

**10.** Go back to your photo. Set Clarity somewhere you would keep. What is the number?

## Vibrance and Saturation

**11. Saturation** to +100 on your photo. **Which colors broke first**, and what does "broke" look like?

**12.** Saturation to −100. What is the photo now?

**13. Vibrance** to +100 instead. **What is different?** Look at the dull colors and the already-bright
ones separately.

**14.** In one sentence: **when do you reach for Vibrance instead of Saturation?**

**15.** Set both somewhere you would keep. Write both numbers.

## Finish it

**16.** Go back through the clipping rule above. **Is anything you care about clipped?** If yes, fix it. If
no, say what is clipped that you decided to leave, and why.

# Thursday: Color, one at a time, and mood

In Camera Raw, find the **HSL / Color** panel, below Basic. Three tabs: **Hue, Saturation,
Luminance**, each with a row of color sliders.

**18.** Pick one color that actually appears in your photo. Move its **Hue** slider. What happens?

**19.** Move that color's **Saturation** down to −100. Everything else keeps its color. **Why is this
more useful than the global Saturation slider you used yesterday?**

**20.** Move its **Luminance**. What is this changing that Saturation did not?

**21.** Make a deliberate choice: pick **one** color and make it the thing the eye goes to first, by
adjusting the others down. **Say what you did and why it works.**

## Color theory, applied

**22.** Look at your photo. Are the main colors **complementary** (opposite on the wheel),
**analogous** (next to each other), or basically **monochromatic**?

**23.** Using HSL, push your photo toward one of those three on purpose. **Which did you pick, and what
did it do to the feel of the image?**

## Mood is the last decision

**Correct is a measurement. Mood is a decision.** Everything up to now was getting the photo honest.
Grading is choosing what it says.

| The decision | The slider | One way | The other way |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Where the shadows sit** | Shadows, Blacks | **Lifted:** soft, open, modern | **Crushed:** heavy, serious, cinematic |
| **How far apart dark and light are** | Contrast | **High:** hard, loud, energetic | **Low:** calm, faded, nostalgic |
| **Which way the color leans** | Temperature | **Warm:** inviting, late afternoon | **Cool:** lonely, clinical, early morning |

**24.** Take your finished photo and make it feel **heavier**, using only those sliders. Which did you
move, and which direction?

**25.** Undo that, and make it feel **lighter and more open.** Same question.

**26.** Which of the two is more honest about what the scene actually looked like? **Does that make it the
better photo?** Answer both parts.

## Words

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Dehaze** | Cuts through atmospheric haze in the distance. Also deepens color |
| **Clarity** | Midtone contrast. Affects texture and edges rather than the whole image |
| **Saturation** | Intensity of all colors, equally |
| **Vibrance** | Intensity of the duller colors, leaving already-strong ones mostly alone |
| **Black point** | The darkest point in the image. Most photos want one real one |
| **HSL** | Hue, Saturation, Luminance. Controls colors one at a time |
| **Hue** | Which color it is |
| **Luminance** | How bright that one color is |
| **Grading** | Choosing the mood. The last step, after the photo is honest |
