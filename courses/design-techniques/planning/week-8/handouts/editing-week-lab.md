# Tuesday: The histogram, exposure, and tone

**Finish yesterday first.** If your four white balance files are not turned in, that is the first ten
minutes. Then come back here.

Work on **one photo** for all of Tuesday.

## Part 1: The graph in the corner

**Top right of Camera Raw there is a graph. That is the histogram, and it is the only honest opinion in
the room.**

- **Left is dark. Right is bright.** Nothing else.
- **Height is how much of the photo** is that bright. A tall spike means a lot of pixels at that
  brightness
- **Piled against the left wall** means black with nothing in it. **Piled against the right wall** means
  white with nothing in it

**1.** Look at your photo's histogram. **Where is the data piled up:** left, right, or the middle? What
does that tell you before you touch anything?

**2.** Find the **two small triangles in the top corners of the histogram.** Click both. Those are the
clipping warnings: **blue overlay means shadow detail is gone, red means highlight detail is gone.**
Leave them on all period. **Is anything red or blue on your photo right now?**

## Part 2: The five regions, left to right

**The histogram is split into five zones, and there is one slider for each.** In order, dark to bright:

| Region | Slider | What it owns |
|---|---|---|
| Far left | **Blacks** | The darkest point. Where black begins |
| Left | **Shadows** | The dark areas, without touching black |
| Middle | **Exposure** | The midtones, and the overall brightness |
| Right | **Highlights** | The bright areas, without touching white |
| Far right | **Whites** | The brightest point. Where white begins |

**In most versions of Camera Raw you can hover over a region of the histogram and it names the slider
that controls it.** If yours does not, the order above does not change.

**3. Exposure.** Drag it down, then up. **Watch the histogram, not the photo.** What does the whole graph
do?

**4.** Push Exposure up until **red appears** on the photo. Now bring it back down. **Is the detail
back?** What does that tell you about pushing too far?

**5. Contrast.** Push it both ways, watching the histogram. **Does the graph spread out or bunch up?**
Say which direction does which.

**6. Blacks.** Push it both ways. **Which end of the histogram moved, and which end did not?**

**7. Whites.** Same question. Then: **how is Whites different from Highlights?**

**8. Shadows.** Pull it up. **What came out of the dark areas**, and does it look clean or noisy?

**9. Highlights.** Pull it down. **What came back that you could not see before?**

**15.** Toggle your edits off and on. If you cannot see a difference, go further. If it looks fake, back

# Wednesday: Mood, and the color sliders

## Part 1: Correct versus chosen

**Yesterday you made a photo correct. Correct is a measurement. Mood is a decision.**

Three decisions carry most of it, and you already have all three sliders:

| The decision | The slider | One way | The other way |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Where the shadows sit** | Shadows, Blacks | **Lifted:** soft, open, modern | **Crushed:** heavy, serious, cinematic |
| **How far apart dark and light are** | Contrast | **High:** hard, loud, energetic | **Low:** calm, faded, nostalgic |
| **Which way the color leans** | Temperature | **Warm:** inviting, late afternoon | **Cool:** lonely, clinical, early morning |

**16.** Take the photo you fixed yesterday. **Without touching Exposure**, make it feel **heavier.**
Which sliders did you move, and which direction?

**17.** Undo that. Make the same photo feel **lighter and more open.** Same question.

**18.** Which of your two versions is more honest about what the room actually looked like? **And does
that make it the better photo?** Answer both parts.

## Part 2: Clarity, vibrance, saturation

**19. Clarity.** Push it up hard, then down hard. What is it doing to the edges and texture?

**20.** Too much Clarity does something specific and ugly to faces and skies. Try it. **Describe it.**

## Words

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Histogram** | The graph top right. Left is dark, right is bright, height is how much |
| **Clipping** | Pushing bright to pure white or dark to pure black, where detail is gone for good |
| **Exposure** | Overall brightness, working from the midtones out |
| **Contrast** | How far apart the darks and the lights are |
| **Blacks / Whites** | The absolute darkest and brightest points in the image |
| **Shadows / Highlights** | The dark and bright areas, adjusted without touching black or white |
| **Clarity** | Local contrast. Affects texture and edges rather than the whole image |
| **Saturation** | Intensity of all colors, equally |
| **Vibrance** | Intensity of the duller colors, leaving already-strong ones mostly alone |
| **HSL** | Hue, Saturation, Luminance. Controls colors one at a time |
| **Hue** | Which color it is |
| **Luminance** | How bright that one color is |
| **Mood** | The feeling a photo gives off. A choice you make, not a thing you measure |
