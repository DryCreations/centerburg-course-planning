# Put It On Your Portfolio

**Course:** Design Techniques (145095) | **Week 6, Thursday Sep 24**

Your prototype is not done when it works. It is done when someone who is not in this class can find it,
open it, and understand what you did and why.

That is what the portfolio is for. **Today: embed the live prototype, then write the reflection.**

---

## Part 1: Get the embed code out of Figma

An embed means the prototype runs **inside your page.** A visitor clicks through your app without
leaving your portfolio and without needing a Figma account.

1. Open your file in Figma
2. Make sure **Present** opens on your **home screen**, not a scratch frame. Fix it now if it does not
3. Click **Share** in the top right
4. Set access to **Anyone with the link**, and **can view**
5. In the Share dialog, look for **Get embed code** (it may be under a `< >` icon or a tab labeled
   **Embed**)
6. Choose the **Prototype** option, not the design file, so visitors get the clickable version
7. **Copy** the code. It is a long block starting with `<iframe`

> **If you cannot find Get embed code**, copy the normal share link instead and tell me. Some things are
> in different places depending on the plan, and there is a fallback below.

## Part 2: Put it in Google Sites

1. Open your portfolio site and go to your **Unit 1.1** page (make it if it does not exist)
2. Right sidebar, **Insert**
3. Click **Embed**
4. Choose the **Embed code** tab, not "By URL"
5. Paste the `<iframe...>` block, click **Next**, then **Insert**
6. **Drag the corners** so it is big enough to actually use. A phone prototype in a tiny box is useless
7. Click **Publish**, top right
8. **Open your published site in a new tab and click through your own prototype.** If it does not work
   there, it does not work

### If the embed will not cooperate

In order, try:

1. **Insert > Embed > By URL** with your prototype share link instead of the code
2. A plain **text link** on the page that says "Open the prototype," pointing at the share link
3. **Screenshots of every screen**, in order, with a caption under each one

**Option 3 is a real fallback, not a failure.** But do not skip straight to it. Try the embed first.

## Part 3: The reflection

Underneath the embed, write it up. **This is the graded part**, and it is what a person reading your
portfolio actually reads.

### Required: four decisions

For each of four decisions you made, write:

> **What I did. The problem it solved. How I know it worked.**

That last part is where the partner tests come in. "Two people tried to tap the header image, so I gave it
a border and a label" is a decision with evidence. "I made it blue because blue is nice" is not a
decision.

### Also include

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **The app and the task** | Which app you redesigned and the one task your prototype supports. One or two sentences |
| **Before and after** | A screenshot of the original next to yours. Say what was wrong with the original |
| **Four decisions** | As above. This is the core |
| **What testing changed** | What your partners got stuck on, and what you changed because of it |
| **What is shown but not built** | The tab bar, icons, settings gear. Be honest about what is a picture |
| **What you would do next** | One thing, with the reason |

## Write about the standards, by name

**You are in a career-tech program and this is the part most people skip.** A portfolio that names the
competencies is a portfolio you can hand to an employer, a judge at a BPA event, or a college.

Work these into the reflection. **Use the words, not the numbers.** Write about what you actually did.

| Standard | What to write about |
|---|---|
| **2.15.1** Understand the UX/UI design process: vision, journey mapping, wireframing, prototyping for the targeted platform | Walk the actual process: you picked a task, sketched on paper first, built screens, connected them, tested with a person, changed it. Name the stage each thing happened in |
| **7.1.2** Describe the components of interactive media | Name the interaction types you used and what each one is for. Navigation, back, overlay, state change, scroll, selection, input, transition. Say why you chose the four you chose |
| **7.2.10** Select and create visual design elements appropriate for the intended audience and use | Who is this for, and what did you do because of that? Type size, contrast, touch target size, color. "Appropriate for the audience" is a claim you have to back |
| **7.4.7** Optimize and export graphics files for intended use | What you exported, in what format, and why. PDF to submit, image to display, prototype link to share. Say why those are three different answers |

**A sentence that names the work beats a sentence that names the number.** "I wireframed every screen on
paper before building it, so the structure was settled before I worried about color" is worth more than
"I met 2.15.1."
