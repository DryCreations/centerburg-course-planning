# What Feedback Is

the rewrite is the grade.**

---

## The standard, in one line

> **Feedback names something specific on the screen, says what you expected, and says what would fix it.**

If a reply could have been written **without opening the link**, it is not feedback.

That is the test. Apply it to your own reply before you post it. "Looks good," "nice colors," "maybe add
more," and "I like the layout" all pass that test, which means they all fail.

---

## Sorted

We are going to put real replies from yesterday on the board, with names off. Here is the sorting you
will see.

### Does not count

| Reply | Why |
|---|---|
| "Looks good!" | Names nothing. Could be written without opening it |
| "I like the colors" | A preference, not an observation. Nothing to act on |
| "Maybe add more stuff" | More what? Where? For what reason? |
| "It's confusing" | Names a feeling, not a screen, and gives no fix |
| "Add a back button" | Closer, but only if there is actually not one. Check first |
| Three sentences of nothing | Length is not the requirement. Substance is |

### Counts

| Reply | Why |
|---|---|
| "When I tapped the three lines in the top left, I expected a menu, but it went back to the home screen. I thought it was a menu because that is where menus usually sit. An actual menu icon, or the word Filter if that is what it does, would have told me." | Names the element, names the expectation, names what broke, proposes a fix |
| "The green Order button won my eye first on the home screen, which I think is right because ordering is the main thing the app does. Nothing competes with it. On the second screen nothing wins, because the three buttons are all the same gray at the same size." | Hierarchy, on both screens, with the reason |
| "I tried to tap the photo at the top of the profile screen to change it, and nothing happened. If it is not supposed to be tappable, it looks tappable, because it has a circle border like a button." | Something that looks clickable and is not. Specific and actionable |

**Notice what the good ones have in common.** Every one of them names a *thing on a screen*. Not the
project, not the person, not the vibe.

---

## Why this matters more than the grade

You are going to spend your working life having your work looked at. **The skill being graded here is
the reviewing, not the receiving.**

Reviewing well is how you learn to see your own work. The reason you cannot find the problems in your own
prototype is that you know what everything is supposed to do. Practicing on someone else's is how you
build the eye that catches it in yours.

And on the other side: a person who gives real feedback is a person people bring work to. A person who
says "looks good" gets asked once.

**Yesterday a lot of you spent a full period producing nothing usable, for yourself or for the person
whose link you replied to.** That is the part worth fixing.

## Do it again, now

Go back to the Classroom Question. Open the **two links you replied to** (or pick two now if you did not
reply at all).

**Rewrite your reply.** Post it as a new reply, or edit the old one. It needs, at minimum, **three
sentences** covering all four:

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. What looks clickable** | Name the actual elements. "The green button," not "the buttons" |
| **2. What should be clickable but isn't** | What you tried to tap that did nothing, or expected and could not find |
| **3. Hierarchy** | What wins your eye first on the main screen? Should it? If not, what should, and what would make it win? |
| **4. Clarity** | One thing that was confusing, and what specifically would make it clearer |

Use the shape. It does the work for you:

> **When I** [did a thing], **I expected** [something], **but** [what happened]. **It would be clearer if**
> [what would fix it].

## Before you post: check it yourself

- [ ] Could this have been written without opening the link? **If yes, rewrite it**
- [ ] Does it name at least two specific things on specific screens?
- [ ] Does it say what you expected, not just what you felt?
- [ ] Does it propose something, even if you are not sure it is right?
- [ ] Three or more sentences of substance, not three sentences of padding

## Then talk to them, then finalize

Same as yesterday, and this part still counts:

1. **Go find the person.** They demo the one task and tell you what it was supposed to do
2. **Did anything change?** Something confusing that only made sense after the demo **is still a
   problem.** A real user gets no demo. Say so in your reply
3. **Edit your reply one more time** with what the conversation changed. That final version is what
   gets graded

## And everybody still changes something

You have feedback from at least two people. **Make at least one real change to your own prototype from
it, today.**

"Mine is perfect" is not an option. If nobody got stuck, there is still a screen where the hierarchy
could be stronger, a label that could be clearer, or something that looks tappable and is not.
