# Peer Review: Run Someone Else's Prototype

**Course:** Design Techniques (145095) | **Week 6, Tuesday Sep 22**

This is the partner test. You hand your prototype to someone who did not build it, give them one
sentence, and then say nothing else until they are done.

---

## Before You Start

Your file needs to be runnable. Five minutes, by yourself:

- [ ] Present mode opens on your **home screen**, not on a scratch frame
- [ ] Your frames are named something real (`Home`, `Search`, `Confirm`), not `Frame 12`
- [ ] Every screen has a way back
- [ ] Share link set to **Anyone with the link, can view**

Then write your **task sentence** at the top of your file, on a sticky note or a text layer:

> "Starting on this screen, ___________."

One task. One sentence. No steps. Examples:

- "Starting on this screen, order a medium coffee for pickup."
- "Starting on this screen, find the score of last night's game."
- "Starting on this screen, add a friend's birthday to your calendar."

---

## The Rules of the Test

**For the person testing (the builder):**

1. Read the task sentence out loud. Once.
2. Then **stop talking.** Do not point. Do not say "you have to tap the blue one."
3. Write down where they hesitate. Hesitation is the data, not the complaint.
4. If they get truly stuck for more than 30 seconds, say "go ahead and stop," and write down where.

**For the person running it (the tester):**

1. **Say what you are thinking out loud.** "I think this is the button." "I don't know what this does."
2. Tap what you actually believe you should tap, not what you think the builder wants.
3. It is not your job to be nice. It is your job to be honest and specific.
4. "It's good" is not feedback. Neither is "it's bad."

## Feedback That Is Actually Usable

Use this shape. It takes the opinion out of it.

> **When I** [did a thing], **I expected** [something], **but** [what happened].

- Bad: "the menu is confusing."
- Good: "When I tapped the three lines, I expected a menu, but it went to the home screen."

- Bad: "I like the colors."
- Good: "When I looked at the screen, I expected the green button to be the main action, and it was."

---

## After the Test: You Are the Builder Again

You are not required to make every change someone suggests. You **are** required to decide about each one.

For each piece of feedback, write one of:

- **Changed it.** What you changed.
- **Not changing it, because.** Your reason. A real one.

Keep this list. It goes in Friday's write-up, and it is part of your grade.

> **Watch for this:** if two different testers got stuck in the same place, that is not an opinion any
> more. That is a problem with the screen. Fix it.

---

## If You Finish Early

- Trade with a second person. Two tests is better data than one.
- Do **Lab 6: Input** if your task involves typing.
- Start the write-up. Four decisions, each with the problem it solved.
