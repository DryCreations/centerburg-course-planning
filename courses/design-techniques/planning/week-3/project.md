# Week 3 Project — Hierarchy Poster

**Course:** Design Techniques | **Assigned:** Mon Aug 31 | **Due:** Fri Sep 4
**Category:** Projects | **Tool:** Adobe Express | **Late:** accepted, ask to reopen

Design a **poster** that a stranger can read across a room, where the information is organized into a clear
reading order. This is our first strict test of **visual hierarchy**.

---

## Pick a topic (choose one)

1. **An upcoming event** — a real school event (game, concert, club fair, spirit week, fundraiser).
2. **A club you like** — a recruitment/announcement poster for a real club or team.
3. **Something else** — your own idea, **approved by the teacher first** (a cause, a product, a band, an event
   you invent). It still must have real information to organize.

---

## Requirements (all required)

| # | Requirement |
|---|-------------|
| 1 | Poster canvas: **portrait, 18 × 24 in** (Express "Poster" preset is fine) |
| 2 | **Three clear levels of information:** ① a headline (what/who), ② the key details (date, time, place), ③ the fine print (cost, contact, small notes) |
| 3 | **One dominant element** the eye lands on first — the headline or the image, not both fighting |
| 4 | A real **size jump** between the three levels (no "everything the same size") |
| 5 | **Type pairing:** one heading font + one body font, from different families that contrast |
| 6 | At least **one image**, placed and cropped intentionally (from last year's yearbook, your own emailed-in photo, or a licensed/branded graphic) |
| 7 | **Alignment:** elements line up to shared edges or a center line (no random placement) |
| 8 | **Whitespace:** the poster is not crammed; the message can breathe |
| 9 | Exported as a **PDF** (print) **and** a PNG/JPG (screen), correct color mode noted |
| 10 | Posted to your portfolio site |

---

## Hierarchy rubric (the strict part)

| Criterion | Full | Partial | Not yet |
|-----------|------|---------|---------|
| **Reading order** | A stranger names ①→②→③ correctly and instantly | Order is mostly clear | Everything competes; no clear first |
| **Dominant element** | One clear focal point | Two things compete | No focal point |
| **Size/contrast** | Strong, deliberate jumps | Some contrast | Flat, uniform |
| **Alignment** | Everything aligns to a system | Mostly aligned | Scattered |
| **Type pairing** | Two contrasting families used well | Acceptable pair | One font, or clashing |
| **Whitespace & craft** | Balanced, uncrowded, clean export | A little tight | Crammed or messy export |

---

## Turn in

- [ ] Paper sketch (photo of it) showing the three levels
- [ ] Final poster: PDF **and** PNG/JPG
- [ ] One sentence: what should a viewer see **first**, and how did you make that happen?
- [ ] Poster placed on the portfolio site

## Extension (finished early)

Make a **second version** that changes the hierarchy strategy — e.g., lead with the image instead of the
headline, or flip the color contrast — and write one sentence comparing which reads faster and why.
