# Hierarchy Toolbox — the moves that control what a viewer sees first

**Design Techniques · Week 3 · Tuesday Sep 1**

Hierarchy = deciding the **order** someone reads your poster: what they see **1st**, **2nd**, and **3rd**. You
already have the tools — this is how to *use* them on purpose. Keep this open while you sketch and build.

## The six tools

| Tool | What it does | Do this | Avoid |
|------|--------------|---------|-------|
| **Size** | Biggest thing is seen first | Make the headline clearly the largest; fine print the smallest | Everything close to the same size |
| **Weight (bold/thin)** | Heavy type pulls the eye | Bold the one thing that matters most on each level | Bolding everything (= nothing stands out) |
| **Contrast** | Difference makes things pop | Dark on light, big next to small, one bright color on calm ones | Low-contrast text on a busy photo |
| **Color** | A strong/warm color grabs attention | One accent color for the most important thing | Five loud colors fighting |
| **Alignment** | Lined-up edges feel organized and guide the eye | Pick left / center and line things to it; use a few shared edges | Everything floating at random |
| **Spacing (whitespace + proximity)** | Space groups things and gives the eye a rest | Put related items close; leave room around the headline | Cramming every corner full |

## The one rule of the week

**Three levels, and a real jump between them.**

1. **Level 1 — the headline / focal point:** what or who. The single biggest, boldest thing.
2. **Level 2 — the key details:** date, time, place. Clearly smaller than the headline.
3. **Level 3 — the fine print:** cost, contact, small notes. Smallest.

If levels 1, 2, and 3 look almost the same size, you don't have hierarchy yet.

## The squint test

Squint at your poster (or look at the thumbnail). **The first thing you can still read is your focal point.**
If two things fight to be first, make one bigger, bolder, or a stronger color — and shrink the other.

## Type pairing (quick version)

Use **one heading font + one body font** that clearly contrast (for example, a bold display or slab for the
headline, a clean sans-serif for the details). Two is plenty. Same font for everything reads flat; three or
more reads messy.

## Today's move

1. Pick your **topic** (event, club, or approved idea).
2. On paper, block out the **three levels** — draw the headline biggest, details medium, fine print smallest.
3. Mark your **one focal point** with a star. That is what a stranger should see first.
4. Note which **tools** you'll use to make it win (size? a bold weight? an accent color?).

You don't touch Express until your paper sketch has one clear #1.
