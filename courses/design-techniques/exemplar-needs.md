# Exemplar Needs — Design Techniques

This document outlines every assignment that needs a teacher-made exemplar, what to build, and enough context to create something clearly different from what students will produce. Priority ratings guide where to start.

---

## Strategy: Use One Consistent Exemplar Brand

Rather than making a new subject for each exemplar, build **one fictional brand** and grow it across the course the same way students grow theirs. This models the full-year arc and makes it easy to reference earlier work when demonstrating new concepts.

### Suggested Subject: **Ridgeline Supply Co.**
A fictional small-batch outdoor gear and apparel brand. Sells hiking packs, camp cookware, and branded apparel. Target market: 25–40 year old weekend hikers and car campers. Personality: rugged but clean, approachable, honest.

**Why this works for differentiation:**
- Students tend to pick: personal brand, a favorite character, a restaurant, a sports team, or a music artist
- Ridgeline is a small business / product brand — none of those categories
- You can invent all the copy, no need to cite sources
- It scales naturally: logo → style guide → website → campaign → handoff → capstone

**Alternate if Ridgeline feels too niche:** Use a fictional local bakery (`Cornerstone Bakery & Coffee`) or a fictional community theater (`Meridian Stage`). Same principle — pick something students aren't likely to choose.

## Detailed Exemplar Specs

### 🔴 Unit 1.3 Project — Brand Style Guide
**Assignment summary:** Multi-page Adobe Express document defining the visual system for a chosen brand. Sections: Cover, Brand Identity (personality + audience), Logo/Mark, Color Palette (hex + usage), Typography (fonts, sizes, spacing), Usage Rules (do/don't).

**What to build:**
- **Brand:** Ridgeline Supply Co.
- **Cover page:** Name + tagline + logo on brand background
- **Identity page:** 4–5 adjectives (rugged, honest, clean, outdoorsy, approachable) + target audience statement
- **Logo:** A simple wordmark or badge with an icon (mountain ridge, compass rose, or similar geometric shape) — keep it clean so students can see how a mark scales
- **Color palette:** 3–4 colors. Suggested: deep forest green (#2D5016), warm tan (#C9A96E), off-white (#F5F0E8), charcoal (#2B2B2B). Include hex codes, color name, and a one-sentence usage note per color.
- **Typography:** A sturdy serif heading font (e.g., Playfair Display or similar) + clean sans-serif body (e.g., Source Sans Pro or similar). Show H1, H2, body, and caption sizes in points. Include letter spacing and line spacing values.
- **Usage rules:** Show 2 DO examples (logo on correct backgrounds, correct font pairing) and 2 DON'T examples (logo stretched, wrong color on logo, unreadable text combo).

**Key differentiators that prevent copying:** Ridgeline's color palette (earthy outdoor tones) and rugged-but-clean personality is far from personal brand, superhero, or restaurant styles. The mark should be your own original creation, not a downloaded icon.

### 🔴 Unit 1.4 Project — Brand Website
**Assignment summary:** Published Google Sites website with real content applying the brand style guide. Minimum: brand kit applied, 3 content sections, 2+ navigation links, wireframe drawn first, published link.

**What to build:**
- **Site for:** Ridgeline Supply Co. — treat it as their company website
- **Wireframe:** Draw (paper or digital) before building. Must show: header/nav, hero, 2–3 content blocks, footer. Label each section.
- **Pages/sections:**
  - Home/Hero: Brand tagline + short mission statement + product photo placeholder
  - About: 1–2 short paragraphs on the brand story (you're inventing this — keep it plausible)
  - Products or Services: 3 product callouts with names and 1-sentence descriptions
- **Navigation:** At minimum: Home, About, Gear (or Shop) — 3 links
- **Content requirements:** Write real copy — no lorem ipsum. Invent believable product names and descriptions.
- **SEO elements:** Add a page title, 2 keywords, and alt text on at least one image. Document them in the submission notes.

**Key differentiators:** A real company website for a fictional outdoor brand is structurally different from a personal portfolio or a fan wiki page. The copy you write is invented — students can't copy it because it won't match their brand's voice or audience.

### 🔴 Unit 2.3 Project — Promotional Campaign + Social Media Set
**Assignment summary:** 1 primary promotional piece (poster/flyer/digital banner) + 3 social media posts for 3 different platforms at correct specs. Brand kit applied throughout, CTA in every piece, alt text documented, color contrast checked, peer critique completed.

**What to build:**
- **Campaign for:** Ridgeline Supply Co. — a seasonal sale or product launch. Suggested: "Fall Pack Drop — New for Autumn 2025"
- **Primary piece:** A digital poster (at a standard print or web size — e.g., 18×24 or 1200×628) announcing the product launch. Include: product name, tagline, CTA ("Shop Now at ridgelinesupply.com"), logo, and a lifestyle image or AI-generated background. Apply brand kit.
- **Social posts (3 platforms, 3 correct sizes):**
  - Instagram Feed post: 1080×1080 — product spotlight, same campaign visual language
  - Instagram Story or TikTok vertical: 1080×1920 — "New drop this Friday" with CTA
  - Facebook/LinkedIn banner: 1200×630 — clean version, more informational copy
- **Alt text:** Document one alt text description per image (written in your notes/submission, not just on the file)
- **Contrast check:** Run at least the body text on the primary piece through WebAIM contrast checker. Document the ratio.
- **Production log:** File names, format, and dimensions for all 4 pieces in a table.

**Key differentiators:** A product launch campaign for an outdoor gear brand is a distinct brief from what students are likely to run (personal brand, character promo, school event, restaurant). The specific campaign hook ("Fall Pack Drop") and product copy is yours to invent — students can't lift it.

### 🟡 Unit 1.2 Lab 2 — Brand Color & Graphics Sampler
**Assignment summary:** 4-page Adobe Express document. Page 1: color palette with hex/RGB and color psychology annotation. Page 2: two harmony examples + contrast check. Page 3: two composition samples (rule of thirds, symmetric balance). Page 4: format comparison table (PNG/JPEG/PDF).

**What to build:**
- **Brand:** Ridgeline Supply Co. (same palette you'll use in 1.3)
- **Page 1:** Show 4 swatches (deep green, warm tan, off-white, charcoal). Include hex + RGB values. Write a 1-sentence psychology rationale for each color (e.g., "Green communicates trust, nature, and sustainability — appropriate for an outdoor brand aimed at environmentally conscious buyers.")
- **Page 2:** Show complementary harmony (green + warm red-orange) and analogous harmony (green + teal + olive). Then show a HIGH-contrast text example (white text on deep green — document ratio) and a LOW-contrast text example (tan text on off-white — fail). Show the WebAIM tool screenshot or ratio number.
- **Page 3:** Two 1080×1080 compositions: one using rule of thirds (product image or shape off-center), one using symmetric balance (centered badge/logo layout). Label each with the principle name.
- **Page 4:** Table comparing PNG, JPEG, PDF. Fill in: use case, file size tradeoff, and when you'd choose each. Include a screenshot of the Express export settings panel.

**Key differentiators:** The specific color palette (outdoor/earthy) and the brand-based psychology rationale is distinct from students' personal brand color choices. Page 3 compositions using abstract shapes or a product image rather than a personal photo cannot be lifted.

### 🟡 Unit 2.2 Lab 2 — Project Proposal & Marketing Brief
**Assignment summary:** 4 documents: (1) Project brief with 8 sections, (2) detailed buyer persona, (3) marketing brief with 3 channels, (4) 4-week/3-platform content calendar with 12+ entries.

**What to build:**
- **Project:** Ridgeline Supply Co. Fall Pack Drop campaign (same campaign as the 2.3 project)
- **Project brief sections:** (1) Problem: "Ridgeline needs to launch its new Trail 30L daypack to outdoor enthusiasts before the fall hiking season"; (2) Target audience: 28–40 year olds, weekend hikers; (3) Deliverables: poster, 3 social posts, email header; (4) Milestones: rough draft by Week 2, critique Week 3, final Week 4; (5) Constraints: no photography budget, all imagery must be stock or AI-generated; (6) Success criteria: 200+ reach on Instagram, 50 link clicks; (7) Scope note: campaign assets only, no website rebuild; (8) Version history: v1.0 initial draft
- **Buyer persona:** Name: "Tyler Marsh," age 32, trail runner and weekend backpacker, earns $60K, frustrated by gear brands that feel too corporate, follows outdoor YouTube creators, buys 2–3 gear items per year, quote: "I just want gear that actually works without paying an influencer tax."
- **Marketing brief:** Goal = drive 200 clicks to the product page. UVP = small-batch quality, no retail markup. Channels: Instagram (visual storytelling, CTA to bio link), email (existing subscriber list, "First Look" exclusive), TikTok (30-sec gear review format)
- **Content calendar:** 4 weeks, 3 platforms, 12+ entries. Mix of countdown posts, product close-ups, behind-the-scenes "how it's made," and a launch day post.

**Key differentiators:** Tyler Marsh is a distinct persona from any student's personal audience. The specific product (Trail 30L daypack), the channels chosen, and the copy in the calendar are all yours to invent. Students designing for personal brands or fictional characters will have completely different personas and campaign goals.

### 🟡 Unit 3.1 Project — Refined Campaign Package
**Assignment summary:** Updated version of the Unit 2.3 campaign with: design audit completed, peer usability test results incorporated, 3+ tracked revisions in a revision history, WCAG contrast passing, 1 annotation per piece.

**What to build:** Take your Unit 2.3 exemplar campaign and revise it. This is intentionally a refinement, not a rebuild — changes should be visible and documented.

- **Design audit table:** Review all 4 pieces against: color contrast (WCAG AA), alt text present, CTA visible, brand kit consistent, no typos. Mark pass/fail per item.
- **Simulated peer test:** Write a 3–5 sentence mock user response: "I liked the colors but the CTA on the poster was hard to find — it blended into the background. The social posts felt consistent but the Instagram story felt cluttered." Make this realistic.
- **3+ tracked revisions (show the "before" state):**
  1. Moved the CTA from bottom-center to upper-right on the primary poster — more visible on mobile
  2. Increased body text from 12pt to 14pt on the Facebook post — failed readability audit
  3. Removed one decorative image from the Instagram story — reduced visual clutter based on peer feedback
- **Annotation page:** One per piece, 1–2 sentences each. State: what the key design decision was, and why you made it.

**Key differentiators:** The specific audit findings, revision decisions, and annotations are tied to your exemplar campaign artwork. Students can't copy the annotations without copying the reasoning, which requires actually doing the work.

### 🟡 Unit 3.3 Lab 2 — Design Studio Business Package
**Assignment summary:** 4 documents: (1) 1-page business plan with 8 sections + QR code, (2) rate sheet with 5 services × 2 tiers, (3) 8-section client contract template, (4) 1-page marketing leave-behind.

**What to build:**
- **Studio name:** "Ridge Creative" (the fictional in-house studio for Ridgeline, or a standalone studio name)
- **Business plan (1 page):** Services: brand identity, social media graphics, print collateral. Target clients: small outdoor/lifestyle businesses. Pricing model: flat project fees. Competitive advantage: specialization in outdoor/adventure brands. Startup costs: ~$800 (software subscriptions, portfolio hosting). Revenue goal: $18K in year 1. 3-month plan: months 1–2 build portfolio + network, month 3 pitch 3 clients. QR code linking to your portfolio website.
- **Rate sheet:** 5 services: (1) Logo design, (2) Brand style guide, (3) Social media post (single), (4) Social media pack (monthly), (5) Print collateral (single piece). Standard and Rush pricing for each. Add a sentence explaining rate basis (hourly estimate × complexity factor).
- **Client contract template:** Write all 8 sections: scope, deliverables, revision limit (2 rounds included), timeline, payment (50% upfront, 50% on delivery), IP (client owns final files; designer retains process work), late fee (1.5%/month), kill fee (25% of total if project cancelled after work begins). Personalize the language — don't copy from a generic template.
- **Leave-behind:** 1-page Express design using your brand kit. Studio name, tagline, 3 services listed with 1-line descriptions, contact info (invent an email and phone), and a QR code to the portfolio. Export as PDF and test the QR code works.

**Key differentiators:** The studio name, services list, pricing rationale, and all contract language are yours. Students designing their own freelance business will have different services and can't use your rate calculations as their own.

### 🟡 Unit 4.1 Lab 2 — Client Delivery Package
**Assignment summary:** Organized file structure + 3-page brand guidelines PDF (written for a non-designer) + 1-page plain-language training notes + deployment checklist + 4-week rollout timeline.

**What to build:**
- **Client:** Ridgeline Supply Co. (you are handing the brand back to them)
- **File structure:** Show a screenshot or text outline of your folder structure: `/01-logos`, `/02-colors-fonts`, `/03-production-files`, `/04-exports`, `/05-guidelines`. List 3–5 example file names per folder using the naming convention (e.g., `ridgeline-logo-primary-color.png`, `ridgeline-style-guide-v2.pdf`).
- **Brand guidelines PDF (3 pages):**
  - Page 1: Logo suite (all variants shown), clearspace note, what NOT to do with the logo (2 examples)
  - Page 2: Color palette (swatches + hex codes + "use this color for..."), typography (font names, sizes, where to use each)
  - Page 3: Usage examples — a "DO" layout and a "DON'T" layout labeled, tone of voice note
  - Write all text assuming the reader has never heard of "kerning" or "hex codes" — define everything in plain language
- **Training notes (1 page):** How to: (1) update the website copy, (2) create a new social post using the brand kit, (3) request additional design files from the designer. Write each as numbered steps, tested against: could a person who has never used these tools before follow this?
- **Deployment checklist:** Binary ✓/✗ items only. Example items: "Website published and live link working," "Logo files exported in PNG and SVG," "Brand kit shared with client login," "All social templates tested on mobile," "Brand guidelines PDF exported and reviewed."
- **4-week rollout timeline:** Week 1 = soft launch to email list + 2 social posts; Week 2 = full public Instagram launch + paid boost; Week 3 = partner outpost (retailer or local trail club); Week 4 = follow-up + analytics review.

**Key differentiators:** Your file naming convention, your training note language, and your rollout timeline are tied to Ridgeline's specific context. Students can't use your training notes because their client's tools and workflows are different. The checklist items you write reflect your project's deliverables specifically.

### 🔴 Unit 4.3 Project — Brand Identity Capstone
**Assignment summary:** 7 deliverables — logo suite (6 variants), brand style guide (6 sections), collateral set (2 pieces), animated social ad (5–15 sec), published website, marketing strategy brief, process book.

**What to build:** The Ridgeline exemplar should be a complete, polished brand identity. This is the most important exemplar to build — it sets the quality bar students aim for in their final project.

**Deliverable-by-deliverable guidance:**

1. **Logo suite (6 variants):**
   - Primary: horizontal badge with wordmark and ridge icon
   - Horizontal: wordmark only, no badge
   - Icon/mark: ridge icon alone, for app icons or small spaces
   - Full-color: on white background
   - One-color: all in brand green
   - Reversed: white mark on dark background
   - Export all 6 as PNGs with transparent backgrounds. Name clearly: `ridgeline-logo-primary-fullcolor.png`, etc.

2. **Brand style guide (6 sections):**
   - Cover, Logo rules, Color palette (hex/RGB/CMYK for all 4 colors), Typography (all type roles: H1/H2/body/caption with sizes + spacing), Imagery style (outdoor photography guidance — warm tones, natural light, real people), Tone of voice (3 "do say / don't say" examples)

3. **Collateral set (pick 2):**
   - Suggested: business card (3.5×2 in) and a product hang tag template (3×4 in folded card)
   - Both should apply brand kit and show how the identity works in small-format print contexts

4. **Animated social ad (5–15 sec):**
   - A short animation for Instagram Reels or Stories: brand logo animates in, product name appears, tagline + CTA fades in. Keep it clean — 3–4 timed elements is enough.
   - Export as MP4 or GIF

5. **Published website (final version):**
   - Update the Unit 1.4 site with all final brand elements applied
   - Run a WCAG contrast audit. Document passing ratios for at least body text and heading text.
   - SEO: confirm page title, meta keywords, and alt text are in place. Note them in submission.

6. **Marketing strategy brief (1–2 pages):**
   - Audience: Tyler Marsh persona (from Unit 2.2)
   - UVP: "Small-batch gear built for real trails — no retail markup."
   - 3 channels with key message + CTA per channel
   - 3-month content plan outline (can be a simplified version of the Unit 2.2 calendar)
   - 2 KPIs (e.g., 500 Instagram followers in 3 months, 10% email open rate)

7. **Process book:**
   - Brief → Research (mood board, competitor examples) → Ideation (3 logo sketches shown) → Iterations (before/after comparison of at least 2 decisions) → Final deliverables → Reflection (what worked, what you'd do differently, what you learned)
   - Show the thinking. The exemplar process book should feel honest — include a sketch that didn't work and explain why it was rejected.

**Key differentiators:** Every element of Ridgeline's identity is invented by you. The logo, color palette, type choices, copy, and strategic decisions are yours and cannot be directly copied. A student making a personal brand or a superhero character will have completely different visual DNA, copy, audience, and creative decisions.

## Low Priority Exemplars

These are simpler to create or less critical. Build them if time allows, after the high/medium priority items are done.

### 🟢 Unit 1.0 Lab 2 — Portfolio & Brand Kit Setup
**What to build:** A short screen recording or screenshot walkthrough showing: a live Express portfolio website with placeholder pages, a configured brand kit (swatches labeled with hex codes, 2 fonts selected, logo uploaded), and a simple monogram.

**Purpose:** Students mostly need to see that the technical setup works and what "configured" looks like. A screenshot annotated with arrows ("this is the brand kit screen," "this is the published link") is enough.

**Key differentiator:** Use Ridgeline colors and monogram (e.g., "RS" initials). Nothing personal — students are building their own.

### 🟢 Unit 1.1 Lab 2 — Infographic
**Assignment summary:** 1-page infographic in Adobe Express on one of 4 topic options: (A) interactive media career landscape, (B) how digital marketing works, (C) a specific interactive media genre, (D) IP and copyright in digital media.

**What to build:** Topic D (IP and Copyright) is a good choice — it's not a popular student pick and it's genuinely useful content to model. Include: 4–6 key facts, a clear visual hierarchy, 1 image from Express library, brand kit applied, IP source note. Keep it to one page.

**Key differentiator:** Choose Topic D or Topic B. Students tend toward Topic A or C (careers/genre). Your infographic's specific facts, layout choices, and label hierarchy are yours. Don't use a brand students are likely to build.

### 🟢 Unit 1.3 Lab 1 — Typography Practice
**Assignment summary:** Exploration of font categories, spacing experiments, and font pairing. Final step is updating the personal brand kit.

**What to build:** A simple 1-page document showing 3 font pair options tested, with a 1-sentence "works for ___" note on each. Include screenshots of letter spacing and line spacing at tight/normal/loose settings. End with a screenshot of the configured brand kit.

**Purpose:** Students need to see what "annotating a font choice" looks like — not just picking a font, but naming the category and writing a rationale. The pairing document is the main model.

### 🟢 Unit 2.1 Lab 2 — Personal Photo Edit + Portfolio Choice
**Assignment summary:** Edit 3–5 personal photos. For each: crop to improve composition (name the principle), adjust tone (white balance/brightness), reflect on what was hard.

**What to build:** Use 3 of your own photos or openly licensed outdoor/hiking photos from Unsplash. For each: show before and after (side by side or separate files). Fill in the edit documentation table. Write a 4-sentence reflection.

**Topic guidance:** Use landscape or product photos, not portraits of people. Students will mostly use photos of themselves, friends, or school events — your outdoor/product photos are clearly different.

### 🟢 Unit 3.2 Lab 2 — AI-Assisted Design Piece
**Assignment summary:** Design piece mixing AI-generated content (Adobe Firefly or equivalent) with original work. Must include: documentation of what Firefly generated, a disclosure note on the piece, and a 4–6 sentence ethics reflection.

**What to build:**
- **Piece:** A Ridgeline social post or banner using an AI-generated outdoor background image (e.g., "misty mountain trail at dawn, cinematic, natural light") combined with your original typography, logo, and layout decisions.
- **Documentation table:** Show the prompt used, what Firefly returned, and whether you used/modified/rejected it.
- **Disclosure note:** Add subtly to the piece: "Background: Adobe Firefly (text-to-image). All typography, layout, and brand elements by [teacher name]."
- **Ethics reflection:** Be honest — address authorship (who "made" the background?), one observation about AI visual bias or aesthetic patterns you noticed, and a statement about commercial use appropriateness.

**Key differentiator:** The specific prompt, the visual result, and the reflection are yours. Students will generate completely different images for their own subjects. The ethics reflection is personal — it cannot be copied in a meaningful way.

## Notes for Building Exemplars

- **Show your process, not just the outcome.** Where possible, share a "before" state or an early draft. Students learn more from seeing the work evolve than from seeing a perfect finished product.
- **Include deliberate imperfections at lower grades.** Consider making a "developing" version (score of 2) and a "proficient" version (score of 3) of the style guide or campaign. This is more work but powerfully communicates rubric distinctions.
- **Name your exemplar files consistently:** `exemplar-[unit]-[type]-[level].ext` (e.g., `exemplar-1-3-style-guide-proficient.pdf`)
- **Store exemplars in a separate shared folder** not accessible via the student-facing portfolio link. The goal is: show during instruction, not leave open for unlimited reference while students work.
