# Extra Credit Project Ideas — Design Techniques

**Course:** Design Techniques (145095)

---

## How Extra Credit Works

If you finish an assignment early or have extra class time, pick any project from this list. Extra credit projects are **optional and ungraded for quality** — you earn credit for genuine effort and completion, not perfection.

**To submit:** Share your completed work with your teacher the same way you submit regular assignments (Google Classroom link, Express share link, or Drive folder). Write one sentence explaining which extra credit project you completed.

**Rule:** Extra credit cannot substitute for a missing assignment. Complete your regular work first.

---

## Built-In Project Ideas

These projects can be completed entirely inside Adobe Express using skills you already have. No additional tools needed.

---

### Design Skills

**Redesign a Real Menu or Flyer**
Find a local restaurant or community event flyer that could use a design upgrade (with permission, or use a public example). Redesign it in Adobe Express applying your brand principles: better hierarchy, cleaner typography, improved color use. Write a 3-sentence critique of the original and explain your redesign choices.

**Create a Brand Style Guide for Something You Love**
Pick a band, sports team, game, or any subject that interests you. Design a mini brand style guide in Adobe Express: logo concept or wordmark, color palette with hex codes, 2-font pairing, and one usage example. This can become a portfolio piece.

**Design a 3-Post Social Media Series**
Create a series of 3 connected social media posts (Instagram feed size: 1080×1080) for any subject — a cause, event, local business, or personal interest. All 3 must share a consistent visual language. Include a 2-sentence caption for each post.

**Typography Poster**
Choose a short quote, lyric, or phrase (school-appropriate). Design a typographic poster in Adobe Express where the type design IS the design — minimal or no imagery. Experiment with font weight, scale, and layout to make the typography carry the visual message.

**Icon Set**
Design a set of 5–8 original icons for a theme of your choice (sports, weather, food, tech, etc.) using shapes and text in Adobe Express. All icons must share a consistent visual style (same line weight, same corner radius, same color palette).

**Packaging Mockup**
Design a label or packaging graphic for a fictional product — a snack, a beverage, a candle, a clothing tag. Include: product name, a tagline, at least one visual element, and any required labeling info you choose to invent. Aim for shelf-ready polish.

**Event Poster Series**
Design 2 versions of a poster for the same fictional event — one aimed at teenagers and one aimed at adults. Same event, same information, different design language for each audience. Write 2–3 sentences comparing your choices.

### Business & Marketing

**Personal Resume + LinkedIn Banner**
Design a clean, professional one-page resume layout in Adobe Express for yourself (or a fictional professional persona). Also design a matching LinkedIn profile banner (1584×396 px). Apply consistent typography and a restrained color palette.

**Client Pitch Deck Slide**
Design a single-slide pitch deck cover for a fictional product or service — the kind you'd open with in a presentation to a potential investor or client. Include: company name, tagline, one bold visual, and a presenter credit line.

**Promotional Email Header**
Design an email header graphic (600×200 px is a common standard) for a promotional campaign. Include: brand name, campaign headline, and one CTA phrase. Keep it clean — email clients crop and scale images.

**Content Calendar Graphic**
Design a one-month visual content calendar for a fictional brand's social media. Use a table or grid layout in Express. Show: week, platform, content type, and a color-coded category system. This is a real deliverable a marketing coordinator would produce.

### Photography & Photo Editing

**Before/After Edit Showcase**
Take or use a photo that has something to fix — lighting, composition, color balance. Edit it in Adobe Express (or Photoshop if available). Create a side-by-side before/after layout with a 2-sentence annotation explaining what you changed and why.

**Photo Essay (5 Images, 1 Theme)**
Photograph or curate 5 images around a single theme (light, texture, symmetry, color, motion, etc.). Arrange them in a multi-panel Express layout with a brief title and a 1-sentence caption per image. No two images may use the same composition technique.

**Product Photography Flat Lay**
Arrange 5–8 objects around a theme (school supplies, snack foods, art supplies, etc.) and photograph them as a flat lay. Edit for color and exposure. No people — just objects and a clean background. Submit the raw photo and the edited version.

### Web & UX

**Wireframe a Better App**
Choose an app you use regularly and think of one feature that frustrates you or one feature that's missing. Draw a wireframe (paper or digital) for a redesigned screen or new feature. Annotate it: what does each element do, and why is it placed where it is?

**Accessibility Audit**
Visit 3 public websites and run a basic accessibility check on each: test color contrast (using WebAIM Contrast Checker), look for alt text on images, and check whether you can navigate using only the keyboard. Write a one-paragraph report on what you found.

---

### Business & Entrepreneurship

**One-Page Business Plan**
Invent a micro-business concept (design studio, photography service, custom sticker shop, anything creative). Write a 1-page business plan: what you offer, who you serve, how you charge, and what your startup costs would be.

**Freelance Invoice Design**
Design a professional invoice template in Adobe Express or Google Docs. Include: your fictional studio name and logo, client name/address fields, line-item table, total, payment terms, and contact info. It should look like something a real designer would send.

---

### AI & Emerging Tech

**AI Prompt Engineering Lab**
Pick one subject (a landscape, a product, a character, an abstract concept). Run 5 text-to-image experiments in Adobe Express using increasingly specific prompts. Document each prompt, what you got, and what you changed. Write a conclusion: what makes a good prompt?

**AI Ethics Case Study**
Research one real-world case where AI-generated art caused a controversy or legal dispute (there are several well-documented ones). Write a 1-page summary: what happened, who was affected, what was decided or settled, and what you think the right outcome should have been. Cite your source.

**Prompt vs. No Prompt Comparison**
Design the same piece twice: once using only Adobe Express's AI tools (no manual design choices beyond the prompt), and once designing it entirely yourself without any AI. Compare them side-by-side. Which do you prefer? Which took more skill? Which would you submit to BPA?
