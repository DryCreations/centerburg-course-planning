# Your Digital Footprint

**Middle School CS, Week 6.** Three days, mostly away from screens.

---

# Day 1: What a footprint is

## The idea

Your **digital footprint** is everything about you that exists online. All of it. Together.

Two kinds:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Active** | Things **you** put there. Posts, comments, photos, videos, messages, usernames, a school project |
| **Passive** | Things collected **about you** without you doing anything on purpose. What you searched, what you watched, how long you looked at something, where you were, what device you used |

**Most people only think about the active half.** The passive half is usually bigger.

## Sort these

Active or passive? Write A or P.

| | Thing | A or P |
|---|-------|--------|
| 1 | A video you posted | |
| 2 | How long you watched someone else's video | |
| 3 | A comment you left | |
| 4 | The fact that you opened an app at 11:40 PM | |
| 5 | Your username | |
| 6 | Which videos you scrolled past without stopping | |
| 7 | A photo a friend posted of you | |
| 8 | The town your phone says you are in | |
| 9 | A school assignment you turned in online | |
| 10 | The kind of phone you have | |

**Then discuss:** number 7 is interesting. **Who put that in your footprint?** Is it active or passive for
*you*?

## The three questions

For anything you are about to put online:

1. **Who can see this?** Not who do you *think* can see it. Who actually can
2. **How long does it last?** Longer than you expect, almost always
3. **Would I be fine with this in five years?** Not today. Five years

## Write

> Think of something you have posted or sent. Without telling me what it was: **who could see it, how long
> will it exist, and would you be fine with it in five years?** Three or four sentences.

# Day 2: Who collects it, and why

## Nothing free is free

If you are not paying for something, **something else is paying for it.** Usually advertising, and what
advertisers pay for is the ability to reach the right person.

**That means you are what is being sold**, not to you but about you.

## What gets collected

| What | Why anyone wants it |
|------|--------------------|
| What you watch and for how long | To show you more of it, and to keep you there longer |
| What you search | To know what you want before you ask |
| Where you are | To sell local advertising, and to know where you go regularly |
| Who you talk to | To find people like you, and to reach your friends |
| What you scroll past | To learn what you do not like, which is just as useful |
| What time you are awake | To know when to reach you |

## The part people miss

**Any one of these is boring. All of them together are not.**

Nobody cares that you watched a video about bikes. But watched a video about bikes, searched for a repair
shop, stayed on the page for four minutes, and your phone says you are near a park most afternoons: now
something knows a fair amount about you, and you never told it anything.

**That is what "profile" means.** It is built out of small, boring pieces.

## Discuss

1. **You get a free app. What is the company getting?**
2. **Why would a company want to know what you scrolled PAST?**
3. Some recommendations feel like the app is reading your mind. **Is it, or is it something else?**
4. **Is any of this illegal?** Should any of it be?
5. **What is a fair trade?** Is there an amount of data you would happily give for something you want?

## Write

> **Pick one app or site you use.** What do you think it knows about you? List at least five things. Then:
> which of those did you tell it on purpose?

# Day 3: Your footprint, on purpose

## It is not all bad

A digital footprint is not a threat to avoid. It is a **thing you can shape.**

| Working against you | Working for you |
|--------------------|-----------------|
| Something you posted angry at 1 AM | A project you are proud of, findable |
| An account name you would not say out loud | A username that is actually yours |
| Arguments with strangers | Something you made, built, or wrote |
| Photos you did not choose to be in | A record of what you are good at |

**People will look.** Colleges, employers, coaches, scholarship committees, and anyone you meet. They already
do.

**The question is not whether you have a footprint. It is whether you chose it.**

## The audit

**Do not share your answers unless you want to.** This one is yours.

1. **Search your own name.** What comes up? Anything surprising?
2. **List every account you have.** All of them, including ones you forgot
3. Of those, **which do you actually use?** An account you abandoned is still out there
4. **Look at your usernames.** Would you put any of them on a job application?
5. **Check one privacy setting** on one app. Did it say what you expected?

## What good looks like, in practice

- **A username you would say out loud** in front of a teacher and a coach
- **One thing findable that you made**, not just things you reacted to
- **Nothing posted while angry.** Write it, do not send it, look tomorrow
- **You know what your settings say**, rather than assuming
- **Old accounts closed** rather than abandoned

## Final write

> **What do you want somebody to find when they look you up in five years?** Be specific. Not "good things,"
> but what, exactly.
>
> Then: **name one thing you are going to change this week.** One. Something you can actually do.

---

## The four sentences worth keeping

1. **Your footprint is bigger than what you posted.**
2. **Small boring pieces add up into something that is not boring.**
3. **If it is free, you are what is being sold.**
4. **You cannot avoid having a footprint. You can choose what is in it.**
