# Ohio K–12 Computer Science Standards — Grades 6–8

**Course:** Middle School Computer Science (grades 7–8 combined, year long)
**Source:** Ohio Department of Education and Workforce, *Ohio's Learning Standards for Computer Science*
(adopted 2018), grade band 6–8.

> ⚠ **Verification note.** The strand and topic structure below is accurate to the Ohio standards document, and
> the descriptions are written in plain language for planning use. **The official numbered standard statements
> (e.g. `CS.D.6-8.a`) should be copied verbatim from the ODE document before this file is used for anything
> official** — a course of study submission, a district curriculum map, or a grant application. For day-to-day
> unit planning, the topic-level alignment here is enough.
>
> Get the document: search "Ohio's Learning Standards for Computer Science" on education.ohio.gov, or ask the
> district curriculum office for the copy they already have.

## The Five Strands

| Code | Strand | What it covers |
|------|--------|----------------|
| **CS** | Computing Systems | Devices, hardware and software, troubleshooting |
| **NI** | Networks and the Internet | Networking, the internet, cybersecurity |
| **DA** | Data and Analysis | Data collection and storage, visualization and transformation, inference and models |
| **ATP** | Algorithmic Thinking and Programming | Algorithms, variables and data representation, control structures, modularity, program development |
| **IC** | Impacts of Computing | Culture, social interactions, safety/law/ethics |

Each strand divides into **topics**, and each topic has grade-band statements. The tables below list the
topics and what a 6–8 student is expected to be able to do, then map them to this course.

---

## CS — Computing Systems

| Topic | Grade 6–8 expectation (plain language) | Where this course covers it |
|-------|----------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Devices** | Identify and describe the components of a computing system and how they interact | Q1 hardware unit — Parts and Jobs lab, machine teardown |
| **Hardware & Software** | Explain how hardware and software work together to execute a program; recommend improvements | Q1 hardware unit; Q3 physical computing with VEX IQ sensors |
| **Troubleshooting** | Apply a systematic troubleshooting process to identify and fix problems | Q1 troubleshooting scenarios; every debugging activity all year |

## NI — Networks and the Internet

| Topic | Grade 6–8 expectation | Where this course covers it |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Networking** | Explain how data is sent through networks in packets; describe protocols and addressing at a basic level | Q2 networks unit (unplugged packet simulation) |
| **The Internet** | Describe the physical and logical structure of the internet | Q2 networks unit |
| **Cybersecurity** | Explain common threats and protective measures; describe how encryption protects data | Q4 cybersecurity unit; Q1 digital citizenship |

## DA — Data and Analysis

| Topic | Grade 6–8 expectation | Where this course covers it |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Data Collection & Storage** | Collect data, and explain how data is represented and stored digitally | Q3 data unit |
| **Visualization & Communication** | Represent data in multiple ways; select a representation that communicates accurately | Q3 charting work |
| **Inference & Models** | Draw conclusions from data and recognize when a representation misleads | Q3 "how charts lie"; Q4 AI unit |

## ATP — Algorithmic Thinking and Programming

| Topic | Grade 6–8 expectation | Where this course covers it |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Algorithms** | Design and refine algorithms; compare two algorithms that solve the same problem | Q1 orientation (Exact Instructions, Sphero planning); Q2 algorithms unit |
| **Variables & Data Representation** | Create and use variables; understand data types at a basic level | Q1 MakeCode Arcade (score, lives); Q2 VEX IQ programming |
| **Control Structures** | Use sequence, selection (conditionals), and iteration (loops) in programs | Q1 Arcade; Q2 VEX IQ; Q4 choice project |
| **Modularity** | Decompose a problem into parts; use and create reusable pieces (functions) | Q2 decomposition unit; Q2 VEX IQ builds |
| **Program Development** | Plan, test, debug, document, and iterate on a program; collaborate on development | Every debug log, all year — the through-line of the course |

## IC — Impacts of Computing

| Topic | Grade 6–8 expectation | Where this course covers it |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Culture** | Describe how computing affects daily life, careers, and society; consider accessibility and bias in design | Q1 orientation; Q4 AI unit; Q4 careers |
| **Social Interactions** | Describe how computing enables collaboration and how online interaction differs from in-person | Q1 digital citizenship |
| **Safety, Law & Ethics** | Explain digital footprint, privacy, intellectual property, and responsible use | Q1 digital citizenship; Q4 AI ethics; the course AI policy |

| Impacts of Computing | ✅ | | ✅ | ✅ |

Every quarter hits at least three strands, which was the design requirement. **Data and Analysis is the
thinnest** — it does not appear until Q3. If that becomes a problem, the easiest fix is folding a small data
activity into the Q2 robotics unit (logging run times and charting improvement across attempts), which is
authentic rather than bolted on.
