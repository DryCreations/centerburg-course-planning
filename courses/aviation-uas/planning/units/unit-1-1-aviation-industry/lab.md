# Lab: Sectional Chart & Airspace Identification

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 1.1 — The Aviation Industry & Career Landscape
**Duration:** 2 days | **Week(s):** Week 2 (Days 3–4)
**Tools Needed:** FAA sectional chart (printed or digital via skyvector.com — no account required), Google Docs

---

## Objective

By the end of this lab, students will be able to:

- Identify all seven airspace classes (A–G) on a VFR sectional chart using color, symbology, and labels
- Locate TFRs, special use airspace, and Class B/C/D airspace rings on a provided chart excerpt
- Determine whether a hypothetical sUAS flight location requires LAANC authorization or is in uncontrolled airspace

---

## Background / Setup

> The FAA sectional chart is the primary navigational chart for visual flight rules operations. As a Part 107 operator, you will read sectional charts to identify what airspace you are flying in before every flight. The Part 107 knowledge test includes multiple questions that require interpreting a sectional chart. Today you learn the basics.

## Instructions

### Day 3 — Learn the Chart

**Step 1 — Reference the Airspace Classes**

In your Google Doc, create a reference table:

| Airspace Class | Color/Symbology on Chart | ATC Required? | Typical Altitude Range | sUAS Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class A | — | Yes (IFR only) | 18,000 ft MSL – FL600 | Never relevant for sUAS |
| Class B | Solid blue rings | Yes — clearance required | SFC to ~10,000 ft MSL | LAANC authorization required |
| Class C | Solid magenta rings | Yes — two-way radio | SFC to ~4,000 ft AGL | LAANC authorization required |
| Class D | Dashed blue ring | Yes — two-way radio | SFC to ~2,500 ft AGL | LAANC authorization required; check altitude grid |
| Class E | Various | Yes (controlled) | 1,200 ft AGL / SFC in some areas | May require LAANC if at surface |
| Class G | No shading | None | Below Class E floors | Generally free to fly below 400 ft AGL |

**Step 2 — Locate Examples on Your Chart**

Using the provided chart excerpt (teacher will distribute), find and mark:
1. One Class B airport — circle it in blue
2. One Class C airport — circle it in orange
3. One Class D airport — circle it in green
4. An area of Class G airspace — shade it lightly in yellow
5. One TFR or Special Use Airspace (SUA) notation — mark it with an "X"

---

### Day 4 — "Can You Fly Here?" Scenarios

For each scenario below, determine: (1) What airspace is the flight location in? (2) Is LAANC authorization required? (3) What step must the pilot take before flying?

**Scenario A:** A student wants to fly at 200 ft AGL over a farm field 12 miles from the nearest airport. The sectional shows the area is Class G airspace.

**Scenario B:** A pilot wants to photograph a construction site that is directly under a Class D airport's airspace floor at 300 ft AGL. The airport control tower is open.

**Scenario C:** A wedding photographer wants to film at a venue that sits at the edge of a Class B airport's outer ring. She would be flying at 100 ft AGL.

**Scenario D:** A TFR has been issued over the county fairgrounds for an airshow this weekend. A student wants to fly 2 miles from the TFR boundary for a school project.

For each scenario, write 3–4 sentences: airspace class identified, LAANC required (yes/no/why), and the step the pilot must take.
