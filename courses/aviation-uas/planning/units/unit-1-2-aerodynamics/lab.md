# Lab: Forces of Flight Analysis

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 1.2 — Aerodynamics & Physics of Flight
**Duration:** 2 days | **Week(s):** Weeks 3–4 (Days 3–4 of Week 3)
**Tools Needed:** Google Docs or Google Slides, diagram reference images (teacher-provided or hand-drawn), calculator

---

## Objective

By the end of this lab, students will be able to:

- Diagram all four forces of flight on a multirotor drone at three flight states (hover, climb, descend)
- Explain how changes in rotor speed affect lift, drag, and the resulting flight behavior
- Calculate a simplified weight and balance scenario for the DJI Mavic 3

---

## Background / Setup

> The DJI Mavic 3 uses four rotors to generate all of its lift, thrust control, and directional movement. Unlike a fixed-wing aircraft where lift and thrust come from separate surfaces, a multirotor uses differential rotor speed to control pitch, roll, yaw, and altitude. Understanding the forces lets you predict how the aircraft will behave — and what can go wrong.

> **DJI Mavic 3 reference specs (use these for Part 3):**
> - Aircraft weight (no payload): 895 g (1.97 lbs)
> - Maximum takeoff weight: 895 g aircraft + payload; do not exceed structural limits
> - Maximum payload: ~0.5 kg (1.1 lbs) with gimbal
> - Battery: 68.7 Wh LiPo — weighs approximately 176 g (0.39 lbs) — included in aircraft weight above

## Instructions

### Day 3 — Four Forces Diagrams

**Part 1 — Draw the Four Forces**

For each of the three flight states below, create a labeled force diagram (sketch on paper or in Google Slides/Docs using shapes). For each state, add arrows showing the *relative size* of lift vs. weight and thrust vs. drag (longer arrow = larger force):

1. **Hover** (constant altitude, no horizontal movement)
2. **Climb** (increasing altitude at constant horizontal position)
3. **Forward flight** (constant altitude, flying forward)

Label each arrow: Lift (L), Weight (W), Thrust (T), Drag (D). For hover: note that "thrust" in multirotor context refers to the net upward force from all four rotors combined.

**Part 2 — Axis Identification**

On a top-view diagram of the Mavic 3, label:
- The longitudinal axis (nose to tail)
- The lateral axis (left motor arm to right motor arm)
- The vertical axis (top to bottom through center)
- Which axis is involved in: pitch, roll, yaw

**Part 3 — Torque Cancellation**

Label the four rotors of a quadcopter in a diagram as either CW (clockwise) or CCW (counter-clockwise). Explain in 2–3 sentences: why do adjacent motors spin in opposite directions? What would happen if all four spun the same direction?

### Day 4 — Weight, Balance & Density Altitude

**Part 4 — Weight and Balance Scenario**

A DJI Mavic 3 operator plans to attach a payload to the front of the aircraft. The aircraft weighs 895 g. The approved payload is the DJI RC 2 controller plus a front-mounted sensor pod.

You are given: the aircraft CG is 12 cm from the front mounting point at zero payload. Each 100 g of front payload shifts the CG forward 1.2 cm.

Answer:
1. Is the total takeoff weight within the 55 lb sUAS limit? (Show your calculation.)
2. If the sensor pod weighs 250 g, how far forward does the CG shift?
3. What is the practical effect of the CG shifting forward? What should the pilot do before flying?

**Part 5 — Density Altitude Analysis**

Today's conditions at the flight site:
- Temperature: 92°F (33°C)
- Elevation: 1,100 ft MSL
- Humidity: 85%

Answer (in 3–4 sentences): How does high density altitude affect the Mavic 3's rotor performance? What specific pre-flight check should the pilot run? Would you recommend shortening the flight duration today? Why or why not?
