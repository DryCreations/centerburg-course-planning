## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **Lift** | The aerodynamic force acting perpendicular to the relative wind that supports an aircraft in flight; generated by the wing/rotor blades | 7.4.2 | 1 |
| **Drag** | The aerodynamic force opposing the motion of an aircraft through the air; always acts parallel and opposite to the direction of motion | 7.4.2 | 1 |
| **Thrust** | The forward force produced by the aircraft's power system (motors/propellers on a multirotor) that overcomes drag | 7.4.2 | 1 |
| **Weight** | The force due to gravity acting on the total mass of the aircraft (airframe, batteries, payload, attachments); always acts downward | 7.4.2 | 1 |
| **Bernoulli's Principle** | As the speed of a fluid (air) increases, its pressure decreases; explains why a curved airfoil generates lift (faster air over the top = lower pressure = upward lift force) | 7.4.3 | 2 |
| **Newton's Third Law** | For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction; explains multirotor lift — rotor blades push air downward, and equal force pushes the aircraft upward | 7.4.3 | 2 |
| **Airfoil** | A shape (such as a wing, rotor blade, or propeller) designed to produce lift when air flows over it; typically curved on top and flatter on the bottom for a fixed-wing aircraft | 7.4.4 | 1 |
| **Angle of Attack (AoA)** | The angle between the chord line of an airfoil and the relative wind; increasing AoA increases lift up to the critical AoA (stall angle) | 7.4.4 | 2 |
| **Chord Line** | An imaginary straight line drawn from the leading edge to the trailing edge of an airfoil | 7.4.4 | 1 |
| **Relative Wind** | The direction of the airflow with respect to the airfoil; it is always opposite to the direction of motion of the airfoil through the air | 7.4.4 | 2 |
| **Longitudinal Axis (Roll)** | The axis running from nose to tail of an aircraft; rotation around this axis is roll | 7.4.2 | 1 |
| **Lateral Axis (Pitch)** | The axis running from wingtip to wingtip (or left to right on a multirotor); rotation around this axis is pitch | 7.4.2 | 1 |
| **Vertical Axis (Yaw)** | The axis running from top to bottom; rotation around this axis is yaw | 7.4.2 | 1 |
| **Center of Gravity (CG)** | The point at which the entire weight of an aircraft is considered to act; affects stability and handling; must remain within approved limits for safe flight | 7.4.9 | 2 |
| **Load Factor** | The ratio of the lift generated by an aircraft to its actual gross weight; expressed in "G" units; a level turn increases load factor | 7.4.16 | 2 |
| **Multi-rotor Aircraft** | An unmanned aircraft that uses multiple rotors (typically 4, 6, or 8) spinning in opposing directions to generate lift and control; the DJI Mavic 3 is a quadrotor | 7.4.10 | 1 |
| **Torque Reaction** | On multi-rotor aircraft, adjacent rotors spin in opposite directions to cancel torque and prevent uncontrolled yaw rotation | 7.4.10 | 2 |
| **Weight and Balance** | The calculation to confirm that the aircraft's total weight is within limits AND that the center of gravity is within the approved envelope; both must be verified before flight | 7.4.9 | 2 |
| **Performance** | The capabilities and limitations of an aircraft under specific conditions — including maximum takeoff weight, ceiling, range, and how atmospheric conditions affect these values | 7.4.5 | 2 |
| **Density Altitude** | Pressure altitude corrected for non-standard temperature; high density altitude (hot, humid, high elevation) reduces lift and engine performance | 7.4.11 | 2 |

## Key Distinctions

| Term A | Term B | Key Difference |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Lift | Thrust | Lift acts perpendicular to relative wind (holds aircraft up); thrust acts forward (overcomes drag) |
| Pitch | Yaw | Pitch is nose-up/nose-down rotation (lateral axis); yaw is left/right rotation (vertical axis) |
| Angle of Attack | Pitch angle | AoA is the angle between the chord line and the *relative wind*; pitch angle is the angle relative to the *horizon* — these can differ significantly |
| Weight and Balance | Weight limit | Weight limit is the maximum total mass; weight and balance also requires checking CG position within the approved envelope |

## Bell Ringer Prompts

1. A DJI Mavic 3 is climbing steeply. Identify which of the four forces of flight is greater than the opposing force in that moment.
2. Explain Bernoulli's Principle and how it applies to the lift generated by a propeller blade.
3. A pilot adds a heavy camera payload to the front-mounting bracket of a drone. What effect does this have on the center of gravity, and why does it matter?
4. Explain why multirotor aircraft have rotors that spin in opposite directions. Which law of physics explains this design choice?
5. Density altitude is high on a hot summer day. How does this affect the drone's performance? What should the pilot check before flying?

## Study Tips

- The four forces (lift, drag, thrust, weight) are always opposing pairs: lift ↔ weight; thrust ↔ drag. In level flight at constant speed, all four are balanced.
- The three axes (roll/pitch/yaw) are tested frequently. Use your body: roll = lean side to side, pitch = bow forward/backward, yaw = spin on your feet.
- Aerodynamics is 9.38% of the WebXam — expect 3–4 questions. Scenario questions will describe a flight situation and ask you to predict the effect on forces or performance.
