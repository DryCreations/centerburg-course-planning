# Quiz: Emergency Procedures, Human Factors & ADM

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 3.3 — Emergency Operations, Human Factors & Decision-Making
**Format:** 20 MCQ | End of Week 27 | ~20 min
**Standards:** 7.8, 7.14
**FAA Study Guide Reference:** Chapters 5, 9, 10

> Questions are styled after the FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot knowledge test.

---

## Questions

### 1.
The DECIDE model is best described as:

A. A 3-step checklist for pre-flight preparation
B. A 6-step aeronautical decision-making framework: Detect → Estimate → Choose → Identify → Do → Evaluate
C. A pilot fitness self-assessment tool used before flight
D. A post-accident reporting sequence required by FAA

> **Answer: B** — DECIDE is a 6-step ADM framework (7.8.5, Ch. 10)

---

### 2.
A pilot says, "I've flown this site 50 times — nothing bad has ever happened." This statement most closely reflects the hazardous attitude of:

A. Anti-authority
B. Resignation
C. Invulnerability
D. Impulsivity

> **Answer: C** — "It won't happen to me" = Invulnerability (7.8.2, Ch. 10)

---

### 3.
The antidote for the "Macho" hazardous attitude is:

A. "Follow the rules — they are usually right."
B. "Not so fast — think first."
C. "Taking chances is foolish."
D. "I'm not helpless — I can make a difference."

> **Answer: C** — Macho antidote: "Taking chances is foolish." (7.8.2, Ch. 10)

---

### 4.
The "E" in the PAVE checklist stands for:

A. Equipment
B. enVironment
C. Emergency
D. Expectation

> **Answer: B** — PAVE: Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External pressures (7.8.4, Ch. 10)

---

### 5. [HOT]
Before a flight, a pilot completes IMSAFE and notes they took an antihistamine for seasonal allergies 4 hours ago. They feel fine. Should they fly?

A. Yes — feeling fine means it is safe to fly; self-reported wellness is the FAA standard
B. Yes — over-the-counter medications are not regulated under Part 107
C. No — antihistamines can cause drowsiness, impaired reaction time, and reduced situational awareness; the medication factor in IMSAFE triggers a no-fly decision
D. Yes — the 4-hour interval means the medication is no longer active

> **Answer: C** — Medication impairment is not dose/timing dependent in FAA guidance; pilots should not fly while taking any medication that could impair function (7.8.7, Ch. 9)

---

### 6.
Under §107.9, a remote PIC must report an accident to the FAA within 10 days if the accident resulted in:

A. Any hard landing or near-miss event
B. Serious injury, loss of consciousness, or property damage exceeding $500 (not including the aircraft)
C. Any crash, regardless of damage amount
D. Damage to the UAS exceeding $500 in repair costs

> **Answer: B** — §107.9 threshold: serious injury OR property damage >$500 (excluding the aircraft) (7.14.16, Ch. 5)

---

### 7. [HOT]
During a waypoint mission, your aircraft loses control link. It begins the RTH sequence, heading toward a crowd of people at a nearby baseball field. You regain partial link. What is the correct action?

A. Allow RTH to complete — the aircraft is programmed to return safely
B. Override RTH immediately and manually guide the aircraft to a safe landing zone away from the crowd
C. Transmit on 121.5 MHz to warn the crowd
D. Notify local authorities and allow the aircraft to complete its programmed RTH

> **Answer: B** — Under §107.21, the remote PIC may deviate from any rule in an inflight emergency; override RTH to avoid flying over people (7.14.5, Ch. 5)

---

### 8.
Part 107 §107.27 states that a remote pilot may not fly within how many hours of consuming alcohol?

A. 4 hours
B. 6 hours
C. 8 hours
D. 12 hours

> **Answer: C** — §107.27: no alcohol within 8 hours of acting as remote PIC; BAC must be below 0.04% (7.8.8, Ch. 9)

---

### 9.
Hyperventilation, which can impair pilot performance, is caused by:

A. High altitude reducing oxygen intake
B. Rapid, deep breathing that reduces carbon dioxide in the blood
C. Cold temperatures slowing metabolic function
D. Dehydration reducing blood volume and oxygen delivery

> **Answer: B** — Hyperventilation from rapid breathing reduces CO2; causes dizziness and impaired thinking (Ch. 9)

---

### 10.
A pilot is under pressure from a client to fly in marginal weather conditions (visibility 2.5 SM, winds 22 knots). The pilot says, "What's the use — the client won't understand if I cancel and I'll lose the contract anyway." This is an example of which hazardous attitude?

A. Impulsivity
B. Anti-authority
C. Macho
D. Resignation

> **Answer: D** — "What's the use" = Resignation; antidote: "I'm not helpless — I can make a difference." (7.8.2, Ch. 10)

---

### 11. [HOT]
Using the DECIDE model, a pilot flying near power lines detects the aircraft drifting toward a conductor. The pilot estimates the aircraft is 15 seconds from contact. Which step comes next?

A. Evaluate — wait to see if the drift resolves on its own
B. Choose — decide the desired outcome (aircraft safely clear of the power line)
C. Do — immediately take corrective action without further evaluation
D. Identify — pause to list all possible responses before acting

> **Answer: B** — After Detect and Estimate, the next DECIDE step is Choose (desired outcome); identifying and doing follow (7.8.5)

---

### 12.
A pilot who refuses to follow ATC instructions because "those rules don't apply to drones" is exhibiting which hazardous attitude?

A. Invulnerability
B. Anti-authority
C. Macho
D. Impulsivity

> **Answer: B** — Resentment of rules and authority = Anti-authority; antidote: "Follow the rules — they are usually right." (7.8.2)

---

### 13.
The "P" for Pilot in the PAVE checklist asks the pilot to evaluate:

A. Only the aircraft's airworthiness and registration status
B. Physical condition, recency of experience, IMSAFE self-assessment, and readiness to fly
C. Whether the planned route has published waypoints
D. The licensing requirements for the planned mission type

> **Answer: B** — Pilot factor: personal readiness, IMSAFE, experience currency (7.8.4)

---

### 14.
Fatigue is particularly dangerous for UAS pilots because:

A. Fatigued pilots fly more aggressively and take more risks
B. Fatigue impairs judgment and reaction time at the same time the pilot may feel subjectively confident — a condition called "cognitive tunneling"
C. Fatigue makes it harder to maintain communication with ATC
D. Fatigued pilots cannot complete required FAA paperwork correctly

> **Answer: B** — Fatigue reduces performance while the fatigued person often feels capable; judgment and reaction time degraded (7.8.7, Ch. 9)

---

### 15.
Under Part 107 §107.21, during an inflight emergency the remote PIC may:

A. Exceed the 400 ft altitude limit only if flying in Class G airspace
B. Deviate from any Part 107 rule to the extent necessary to respond to the emergency
C. Exceed the altitude limit but not the speed limit
D. Fly beyond VLOS only if the visual observer confirms safety

> **Answer: B** — §107.21 grants emergency deviation authority from any Part 107 rule as needed (7.14.1, Ch. 5)

---

### 16. [HOT]
A pilot completes a flight and lands safely after a minor battery failure warning. The aircraft experienced momentary altitude loss of approximately 15 ft but landed without damage. No one was injured. Must the pilot file an FAA accident report?

A. Yes — any equipment malfunction requires a report within 10 days
B. No — no serious injury occurred and no property damage exceeded $500 (excluding the aircraft)
C. Yes — any altitude deviation during flight constitutes a reportable incident
D. It depends — only if the client also witnessed the event

> **Answer: B** — §107.9 threshold not met: no serious injury, no significant property damage (7.14.16, Ch. 5)

---

### 17.
The "External Pressures" factor in PAVE refers to:

A. Barometric pressure changes affecting aircraft performance
B. Non-technical influences that push the pilot toward flying: schedule pressure, client expectations, cost concerns, desire to appear competent
C. Wind gusts exceeding the aircraft's rated performance envelope
D. Regulatory pressure from ATC to complete the flight on schedule

> **Answer: B** — External pressures are human factors that can override sound ADM; identifying them is a defense against poor decisions (7.8.4)

---

### 18.
Which of the following best describes the difference between CRM and SRM?

A. CRM applies to multi-crew aircraft; SRM (Single-Pilot Resource Management) applies the same resource-management principles to solo operations
B. CRM requires radio communication; SRM does not
C. CRM is used before flight; SRM is used during flight
D. CRM applies to Part 107 operations; SRM applies only to manned aviation

> **Answer: A** — SRM is the application of CRM principles to solo pilot operations (7.8.9, Ch. 6)

---

### 19.
Stress that degrades pilot performance can come from all of the following EXCEPT:

A. Unexpected aircraft systems failure
B. A client calling with positive feedback between flights
C. Time pressure to complete a mission before weather deteriorates
D. Emotional distress from a personal situation outside of work

> **Answer: B** — Positive feedback does not create performance-degrading stress; all others are recognized stressors (7.8.3, Ch. 9)

---

### 20. [HOT]
A pilot is planning a flight and notes during PAVE: the aircraft has an unresolved warning about magnetic interference (likely compass issue); the client has $2,000 invested in this shoot and is on-site waiting. The pilot decides to fly anyway. Which two factors from PAVE did the pilot fail to properly assess?

A. Pilot and enVironment
B. Aircraft and External Pressures
C. enVironment and External Pressures
D. Pilot and Aircraft

> **Answer: B** — Unresolved aircraft warning (Aircraft factor) + client financial pressure (External Pressures factor) were both present and both ignored (7.8.4)
