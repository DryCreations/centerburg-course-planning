## Objectives

By the end of this lab, students will:

## Instructions

### Day 26-1 — Emergency Scenarios: DECIDE Model Application

Read each scenario. Using the DECIDE model, work through all 6 steps in writing.

> **DECIDE:** Detect → Estimate → Choose → Identify → Do → Evaluate

---

**Scenario A: GPS Loss / ATTI Mode**

You are flying a site inspection at 120 ft AGL over a fenced industrial facility. Suddenly, the DJI app alerts you: "GPS signal weak — switching to ATTI mode." Wind is from the west at 10 knots. The aircraft begins drifting east toward a line of mature oak trees.

Apply DECIDE:
1. **Detect:** What has changed that requires action?
2. **Estimate:** How significant is this change? What is the timeline before the aircraft reaches the tree line?
3. **Choose:** What is your desired outcome?
4. **Identify:** What actions could you take? (List at least 3)
5. **Do:** Which action do you select? Why?
6. **Evaluate:** After you act, how will you confirm the situation is resolved?

---

**Scenario B: Control Link Failure**

You are flying a mapping grid at 200 ft AGL over a cornfield. The controller loses the link to the aircraft. You observe the Mavic 3 executing its preprogrammed RTH sequence, ascending toward 300 ft (your preset RTH altitude). However, the RTH path takes the aircraft directly over a public road where cars are passing.

Apply DECIDE:
1. **Detect:** What is the emergency?
2. **Estimate:** Is the RTH sequence a safe response given the current RTH route over the road?
3. **Choose:** What outcome do you want?
4. **Identify:** What actions are available? (Consider: regain link, cancel RTH, manual override, etc.)
5. **Do:** What do you decide? Justify.
6. **Evaluate:** After the aircraft lands safely, what procedural change would you make before the next flight?

---

**Scenario C: Battery Critical**

You are 0.4 miles from your launch point at 100 ft AGL when the aircraft signals "Battery Critical — Landing in 60 seconds." Directly below the aircraft is a private pond. An alternate landing zone exists 200 ft away in an open field, but it requires flying over the pond.

Apply DECIDE:
1. **Detect:** What is the emergency?
2. **Estimate:** Can you safely cover 200 ft in 60 seconds in current wind conditions?
3. **Choose:** Land in the pond immediately, or fly 200 ft to the field?
4. **Identify:** What are the tradeoffs? (damage to aircraft vs. landing in private water vs. time constraints)
5. **Do:** What do you decide? Why?
6. **Evaluate:** Does this situation require an FAA accident report under §107.9? Why or why not?

### Day 27-2 — Human Factors: IMSAFE, Hazardous Attitudes & PAVE

**Part A: IMSAFE Self-Assessment**

Complete this checklist honestly for a hypothetical "tomorrow morning flight" scenario. For any "yes" answer, explain why it would affect flight safety.

| Factor | Question | Y/N | If Yes — how does it affect safety? |
|--------|----------|-----|--------------------------------------|
| Illness | Am I sick or do I feel unwell? | | |
| Medication | Am I taking any medication that could impair judgment, reaction time, or focus? | | |
| Stress | Am I under unusual mental or emotional stress that might reduce my focus? | | |
| Alcohol | Have I consumed alcohol in the past 8 hours? Is my BAC below 0.04%? | | |
| Fatigue | Am I well-rested? Did I sleep at least 7 hours last night? | | |
| Emotion | Am I experiencing significant emotional distress (argument, bad news, anxiety)? | | |

> If any factor is "yes," the correct answer is: **do not fly today.** Make a note of which factors most often affect your readiness. Self-awareness is a professional skill.

---

**Part B: Hazardous Attitude Identification**

For each statement below, identify the hazardous attitude and write the FAA-prescribed antidote:

| Statement | Hazardous Attitude | Antidote |
|-----------|-------------------|---------|
| "I don't need to check the TFR — I've flown here dozens of times." | | |
| "The client needs this footage NOW. I'll launch even though the wind is 28 knots." | | |
| "I've been flying drones for 5 years. I never crash." | | |
| "There might be a helicopter inbound but I'll finish my waypoint mission — I almost have the data." | | |
| "There's an ATC restriction here but those rules are for manned aircraft, not drones." | | |

> **Answer Key for Teacher:**
> - "I've flown here before" → Invulnerability → "It could happen to me."
> - "Client needs it NOW" → Impulsivity → "Not so fast — think first."
> - "I never crash" → Macho or Invulnerability → "Taking chances is foolish." / "It could happen to me."
> - "Almost have the data" → Resignation (accepting the situation) → "I'm not helpless — I can make a difference." *Or* Impulsivity.
> - "Rules are for manned aircraft" → Anti-Authority → "Follow the rules — they are usually right."

**Part C: PAVE Checklist — Mission Pre-Assessment**

Apply PAVE to this mission:

*You are planning to fly a 20-minute real estate photography mission tomorrow morning at 7 AM. Weather forecast shows a cold front arriving by 10 AM — conditions at 7 AM: winds 8 knots, visibility 7 SM, ceiling 3,500 ft broken. Temperature 38°F. Site is in Class G airspace, no NOTAMs. The client is very eager and has asked you three times if the flight is still on.*

| PAVE Factor | Questions to Consider | Your Assessment |
|-------------|----------------------|----------------|
| **Pilot** | Am I current? Rested? IMSAFE check? | |
| **Aircraft** | Is the aircraft in airworthy condition? Cold temps affect battery? | |
| **enVironment** | Is the airspace clear? Weather acceptable? Any obstacles? | |
| **External Pressures** | Is the client pressure influencing my decision? What would I decide if the client was not pushing? | |

After completing PAVE: write a one-sentence go/no-go decision and justify it using specific data from the scenario.
