# Project: Emergency Response Brief

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 3.3 — Emergency Operations, Human Factors & Decision-Making
**Duration:** Weeks 26–27 deliverable
**FAA Study Guide Reference:** Chapters 5, 9, 10

---

## Overview

Students develop and deliver a 3–5 minute verbal Emergency Response Brief to the class, presenting their written analysis of one assigned emergency scenario. This mirrors the type of crew briefing a commercial operator would conduct before flying in challenging conditions.

The brief includes a written analysis document (submitted) and a live verbal brief to classmates.

---

## Scenario Assignments

Students receive one of three scenarios (assigned or self-selected). All scenarios involve real-world multi-factor risk.

---

**Scenario 1: GPS Loss Over Populated Area**
You are photographing a community event at the local park — a large public gathering, approximately 300 people. At 80 ft AGL, the GPS signal drops. The aircraft enters ATTI mode. Wind is from the southwest at 12 knots, pushing the aircraft toward the crowd.

**Scenario 2: Control Link Failure Near Airport**
You are conducting an inspection flight of a cell tower 0.8 miles east of Knox County Airport. LAANC authorization is active for 200 ft. At 190 ft AGL, the control link is lost. Your pre-set RTH heading would take the aircraft toward the airport's traffic pattern area.

**Scenario 3: Battery Critical + Weather Deterioration**
You are on a 15-minute inspection flight at a remote rural site when both battery critical AND a fast-moving cloud layer move in (visibility dropping from 7 SM to 2 SM within minutes). You have 60 seconds of battery remaining. No safe landing zone is immediately below you — the field is saturated mud.

---

## Written Analysis Document

For your assigned scenario, submit a 1–2 page written analysis covering:

### Section 1: Emergency Description
- Identify all risk factors present in the scenario
- Classify each risk as: operational (aircraft/systems), environmental, human factors, or regulatory

### Section 2: DECIDE Model Application
Work through all 6 steps for your specific scenario:
1. Detect
2. Estimate
3. Choose
4. Identify
5. Do
6. Evaluate

### Section 3: PAVE Risk Assessment
Complete the 4-factor PAVE checklist for the scenario mission before the emergency occurs. Were any risks identifiable before launch?

### Section 4: Hazardous Attitudes Present
Identify any hazardous attitudes that could have contributed to launching despite pre-existing risks. For each attitude identified, state the antidote.

### Section 5: Regulatory Analysis
- Does this incident require an FAA accident report under §107.9? Why or why not?
- What rule or authority (§107.21) permits deviation from regulations in an inflight emergency?
- What documentation should be completed after the flight?

### Section 6: Procedure Change
If you were the UAS operator for this business, what one operational procedure change would you implement to reduce the likelihood of this emergency occurring again?

---

## Verbal Brief (3–5 Minutes)

Deliver your emergency brief as if you are the chief pilot briefing a new crew before their first flight at this site. Your brief should include:
- Overview of the emergency that occurred (or nearly occurred)
- What risk factors were present before launch (PAVE)
- What the pilot should have done differently
- The corrected procedure your operation will follow going forward

Classmates serve as the "crew" — they may ask one question after each brief.

## Rubric

| Category | Excellent (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Beginning (1) |
|----------|--------------|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Risk Identification** | All risk factors named and classified correctly | Most risks named; 1 classification error | Key risks present; 2–3 omissions | Major risks missing |
| **DECIDE Model** | All 6 steps complete, specific, and logically sequenced | 5–6 steps; reasoning present | 3–4 steps; reasoning vague | Fewer than 3 steps |
| **PAVE Checklist** | All 4 factors assessed with scenario-specific detail | 3–4 factors; 1 factor generic | 2 factors assessed | Not completed |
| **Hazardous Attitudes** | Correct attitude identified with appropriate antidote and scenario evidence | Attitude correct; antidote absent or generic | Attitude misidentified | Not completed |
| **Regulatory Analysis** | §107.9 threshold correctly applied; §107.21 cited; reporting requirement accurate | Mostly correct; 1 regulatory error | §107.9 mentioned but incorrectly applied | No regulatory analysis |
| **Verbal Brief** | Confident, clear, specific; hits all required elements; handles Q&A | Mostly clear; 1 element missing | Reads from notes; vague; 2+ elements missing | Not delivered or under 2 minutes |
| **Written Format** | Professional document; no major errors; all sections present | Minor errors; all sections present | 1–2 sections missing | Significant sections missing |

**Total: 28 points**
