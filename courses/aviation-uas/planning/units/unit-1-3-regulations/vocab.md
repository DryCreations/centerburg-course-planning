# Vocabulary: Small UAS Regulations & Operations

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 1.3 — Small UAS Regulations & Operations

> Use these terms in bell ringer responses, lab write-ups, and project reflections. Terms may appear on the weekly quiz and the WebXam.

---

## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **14 CFR Part 107** | Title 14 of the Code of Federal Regulations, Part 107 — the FAA rule governing small UAS operations in the United States; covers certification, operating rules, and limitations | 7.9.1 | 1 |
| **Remote Pilot Certificate** | FAA credential required to fly a small UAS for non-recreational purposes; earned by passing the FAA Aeronautical Knowledge Test at an approved testing center | 7.9.1 | 1 |
| **FAA Aeronautical Knowledge Test** | The written exam (taken at a PSI testing center) required to obtain a Remote Pilot Certificate; tests knowledge of regulations, airspace, weather, and operations | 7.9.1 | 1 |
| **sUAS Registration** | Requirement to register any sUAS weighing between 0.55 and 55 pounds with the FAA; must display registration number on the aircraft | 7.9.2 | 1 |
| **Operating Limitations** | The specific rules that govern sUAS flight under Part 107 — e.g., 400 ft AGL altitude limit, daylight-only operations, 100 mph max speed, visual line of sight required | 7.9.4 | 2 |
| **Waiver** | FAA authorization that allows a remote pilot to deviate from one or more standard Part 107 operating limitations (e.g., fly at night, fly over people) for a specific operation | 7.9.5 | 2 |
| **Certificate of Waiver or Authorization (COA)** | A formal document granting public UAS operators (government entities) authorization to fly under specific conditions | 7.9.5 | 1 |
| **Crew Resource Management (CRM)** | A set of principles and techniques for coordinating crew members to optimize safety and performance; emphasizes communication, situational awareness, and shared decision-making | 7.9.6 | 2 |
| **Single-Pilot Resource Management (SRM)** | The application of CRM principles to solo operations; the pilot manages all information and resources without a crew | 7.9.6 | 2 |
| **Visual Observer (VO)** | A person stationed near the remote pilot who maintains visual contact with the UAS and communicates its position, attitude, and hazards; no certificate required | 7.9.7 | 1 |
| **Airspace Authorization** | Permission from ATC (or via LAANC) to operate a UAS in controlled airspace (Classes B, C, D, and some E); required when operating below an altitude grid in controlled airspace | 7.9.3 | 2 |
| **LAANC** | Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability; the FAA system that provides near-real-time sUAS authorization in controlled airspace | 7.9.3 | 1 |
| **B4UFLY** | FAA mobile app that provides awareness of airspace restrictions, TFRs, and special use airspace for a given location; a pre-flight planning tool (not an authorization system) | 7.9.4 | 1 |
| **FAA DroneZone** | FAA web portal for registering UAS, obtaining waivers, and checking authorization status; also the portal through which LAANC authorizations can be reviewed | 7.9.2 | 1 |
| **Reckless Operation** | Flying a UAS in a careless manner that endangers the life or property of others; prohibited under Part 107; can result in certificate revocation and civil penalties | 7.9.3 | 1 |
| **Yield Right-of-Way** | Part 107 requirement that sUAS must yield right-of-way to all crewed aircraft at all times | 7.9.3 | 1 |
| **Daylight Operations** | Part 107 requires operations only during official sunrise to official sunset (civil twilight with anti-collision lights is also allowed) unless a waiver is obtained | 7.9.4 | 1 |
| **Over People Restriction** | Under standard Part 107, flying over moving vehicles or people not directly involved in the operation is prohibited without a Category waiver | 7.9.4 | 2 |
| **Incident Reporting** | Requirement to report any UAS accident that causes serious injury or property damage exceeding $500 to the FAA within 10 calendar days | 7.9.1 | 2 |
| **Recurrency** | Part 107 remote pilot certificates must be renewed every 24 calendar months via a recurrent knowledge test or online course | 7.9.1 | 1 |

## Key Distinctions

| Term A | Term B | Key Difference |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Waiver | LAANC Authorization | Waiver = FAA manually approves deviation from a Part 107 rule; LAANC = automatic/near-real-time authorization to fly in controlled airspace within approved altitude grids (not a rule deviation) |
| Visual Observer | Remote Pilot in Command | VO maintains visual contact and communicates; RPIC is the licensed operator with final authority — can be the same person only if operating alone |
| B4UFLY | LAANC/DroneZone | B4UFLY is awareness only (shows restrictions); LAANC/DroneZone is where you actually get authorization to fly |

## Bell Ringer Prompts

1. List five Part 107 operating limitations. For each, explain why the rule exists.
2. A student wants to fly at night for a music video shoot. What does Part 107 require? What is the process to do this legally?
3. What is the difference between Crew Resource Management and Single-Pilot Resource Management? Give an example of each in a UAS context.
4. A drone pilot sees a news helicopter approaching their airspace. What must the drone pilot do under Part 107, and why?
5. True or false: A visual observer can substitute for the remote pilot's visual line-of-sight requirement, allowing the RPIC to look down at a screen during flight. Explain your answer.

## Study Tips

- Part 107 operating limitations are the most tested regulatory content on both the WebXam and the FAA knowledge test — memorize the key numbers: 400 ft AGL, 100 mph, ≤55 lbs, daylight (civil twilight with lights)
- "Can you fly here?" scenario questions will give you a location and ask you to identify what airspace class it is and what authorization is needed — practice with actual airspace charts
- The difference between Waiver and LAANC authorization is a common point of confusion — test yourself until you can explain the difference in one sentence
