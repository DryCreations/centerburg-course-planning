# Lab: "Can You Fly Here?" — Regulatory Scenario Exercises

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 1.3 — Small UAS Regulations & Operations
**Duration:** 2 days | **Week(s):** Week 6 (Days 3–4)
**Tools Needed:** Google Docs, B4UFLY (web or app — no account required), FAA DroneZone (faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/uas_within_the_nsa — no account required for basic lookup), sectional chart excerpt (teacher-provided)

---

## Objective

By the end of this lab, students will be able to:

- Determine whether a proposed sUAS flight is legal under Part 107 without a waiver
- Identify what type of authorization (LAANC, waiver, or none) is required for a given flight
- Explain which Part 107 rule applies in each scenario and why

---

## Background / Setup

> Before every commercial UAS flight, a Part 107 pilot asks the same mental checklist: *Is this flight legal? Does it require authorization? Am I allowed to do this without a waiver?* This lab simulates that pre-flight decision process. Some scenarios are straightforward; others involve rule interactions you have to think through carefully. These types of scenarios appear frequently on the FAA knowledge test and the WebXam.

---

## Instructions

### Day 6-3 — Regulatory Scenario Analysis

For each of the eight scenarios below, answer the following in your Google Doc:
1. **What Part 107 rule is most relevant?** (cite the specific limitation)
2. **Is the flight legal under standard Part 107?** (Yes / No / Needs authorization)
3. **If authorization is required, what type and how would you get it?**
4. **What would you tell the client or requestor?**

---

**Scenario 1:** A local newspaper wants you to photograph a city council meeting from the air at 7:45 PM in October. Sunset is at 7:22 PM that evening.

**Scenario 2:** An insurance adjuster asks you to fly over a neighborhood to document hail damage. Some houses have people in their yards.

**Scenario 3:** A construction firm wants you to survey a job site located 0.3 miles from a small regional airport. The site is inside the airport's Class D airspace at 200 ft AGL.

**Scenario 4:** A client wants aerial video of their warehouse, which sits directly under the approach path to a Class C airport. The warehouse is at 150 ft AGL and the LAANC grid shows 0 ft authorized at that location.

**Scenario 5:** A student wants to fly recreationally in a park on Saturday morning at 8:00 AM. The park is in Class G airspace, and the drone weighs 0.4 lbs.

**Scenario 6:** A music video director wants a drone shot at 3:00 AM to get light trails on a highway. The location is Class G airspace and far from any airport.

**Scenario 7:** An agricultural client wants you to inspect crops at a farm. The total flight will cover about 200 acres. You will stay below 400 ft AGL and fly only during daylight hours in Class G airspace. No people or vehicles are near the flight area.

**Scenario 8:** A research university wants you to operate a drone Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) using a chase vehicle and chase pilot for a 15-mile corridor survey.

---

### Day 6-4 — Class Role-Play: Regulator vs. Operator

Work in pairs. One student plays the FAA inspector; one plays the drone operator. The inspector asks:
- "Show me your authorization documentation for this flight."
- "Which Part 107 rule applies to your operation today?"
- "Your client asked you to fly over the crowd at a local festival. What did you tell them?"

Rotate roles after 10 minutes. After the role-play, each student writes a 3–4 sentence reflection: What is the hardest regulatory question you had to answer? What would you look up before a real commercial flight?
