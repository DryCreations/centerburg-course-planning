# Lab: WebXam Pretest & Score Analysis

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 1.0 — WebXam Pretest & Course Orientation
**Duration:** 2 days | **Weeks:** Week 1 (Days 3–4)
**Tools Needed:** Ohio WebXam portal (teacher-administered), Google Docs

---

## Objective

By the end of this lab, students will be able to:

- Complete a full-length WebXam practice assessment under timed, test-like conditions
- Analyze their own strand-by-strand results to identify areas of strength and areas needing the most growth

---

## Background / Setup

> The WebXam covers 15 outcomes spread across Strand 1 (Business), Strand 2 (Safety), and Strand 7 (Aviation and Aeronautics). Your score today is not graded — it is a starting point. The goal is to see what you already know and where to focus your energy this year. Many students score very low on the first attempt, especially on meteorology, GIS, and preflight topics. That is expected. This is a **diagnostic** — not a graded test.

### Day 3 — Pretest

**Step 1 — Test environment setup**

- Put away all notes, phones, and devices except the iMac you are using for the test

- Work independently; no collaboration

### Day 4 — Score Analysis & Goal Setting

**Step 4 — Highlight strength and growth areas**

In your Google Doc, complete the following:

1. **Strength:** Which 2–3 outcomes did you score highest on? Why do you think you already know this content?
2. **Growth area:** Which 2–3 outcomes did you score lowest on? What would you need to learn to improve in these areas?
3. **EOC weight:** Using the EOC Weight Summary table from the course outline, which of your low-scoring areas has the highest EOC weight? Put a star next to your most important growth area.

**Step 5 — Set learning goals**

Write 3 personal learning goals for the year using this format:
> *"By [quarter], I want to be able to [specific skill or knowledge] in the area of [outcome name]."*

Example:
> *"By Q3, I want to be able to read a METAR report and make a go/no-go flight decision based on weather conditions."*

**Step 6 — Share with teacher**

Submit your Google Doc to Google Classroom. Title it: `[LastName] — Unit 1.0 Pretest Analysis`
