# Vocabulary: BPA Presentation & Research

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **WSAP (Workplace Skills Assessment Program)** | BPA's standardized scoring rubric system used by judges to evaluate competition entries; defines what earns points at local, state, and national levels | 1.2.2 | 1 |
| **Thesis Statement** | A concise, arguable claim that states the central position of a presentation; judges evaluate clarity and support of the thesis | 1.2.5 | 2 |
| **Annotated Bibliography** | A list of sources with a brief description and evaluation of each source's relevance and credibility; required for BPA research presentations | 1.2.1 | 2 |
| **Primary Source** | Original, firsthand information (FAA regulations, government reports, original research studies); higher credibility in aviation research | 1.2.1 | 2 |
| **Secondary Source** | Analysis, summary, or interpretation of primary sources (news articles, textbooks); useful context but lower credibility than primary | 1.2.1 | 2 |
| **Storyboard** | A visual plan showing the sequence of slides or media in a presentation; maps content flow before production begins | 1.2.12 | 1 |
| **Hook** | The opening statement or moment of a presentation designed to capture audience attention immediately | 1.2.2 | 2 |
| **Call to Action** | The closing request to the audience — a specific action you want them to take based on your presentation | 1.2.5 | 2 |
| **Data Visualization** | A graphical representation of data (chart, graph, infographic, map) that communicates trends or comparisons more clearly than raw numbers | 1.2.12 | 2 |
| **Stakeholder** | Any person or group with an interest in or affected by the topic being presented; identifying stakeholders strengthens presentation relevance | 1.2.13 | 2 |
| **Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER)** | A structured argument format: state a claim, provide evidence from sources, explain how the evidence supports the claim | 1.2.1 | 3 |
| **Verbal Filler** | Words that add no meaning but fill pauses (um, uh, like, you know); reducing fillers improves presentation credibility | 1.2.3 | 1 |
| **Nonverbal Communication** | Body language, eye contact, gestures, posture, and movement; judges score these in BPA presentations | 1.2.3 | 2 |
| **Business Plan** | A formal document describing a business concept, market analysis, operational plan, financial projections, and management structure | 1.4.1 | 2 |
| **Market Analysis** | Research on the size, trends, competition, and target customers for a specific business or service | 1.6.1 | 2 |
