## Questions

### 1.
The maximum altitude for sUAS operations under Part 107 without a waiver is:

A. 400 ft MSL
B. 400 ft AGL, or within 400 ft of a structure
C. 500 ft AGL in uncontrolled airspace; 400 ft in controlled airspace
D. 1,000 ft AGL when operating in Class G airspace

> **Answer: B** — §107.51: 400 ft AGL or within 400 ft of a structure (7.11.3)

### 2. [HOT]
METAR KOXD 181655Z 26008KT 10SM SCT035 BKN070 18/12 A2998

Decode this METAR. Is a Part 107 flight authorized based on visibility and cloud clearance rules?

A. No — visibility 10 SM does not meet the 3 SM minimum
B. Yes — 10 SM visibility, scattered at 3,500 ft and broken at 7,000 ft; 500 ft below BKN = 6,500 ft; 2,000 ft horizontal from clouds; a standard 400 ft AGL flight clearly meets all §107.51 requirements
C. No — broken clouds require an IFR waiver for drone flights
D. Cannot determine — METAR does not contain wind information

> **Answer: B** — All §107.51 conditions met: visibility >3 SM, clouds far above 400 ft AGL, no weather hazards (7.6.1, 7.11.3)

### 3.
A sUAS operating commercially in Class B airspace:

A. May operate freely below 400 ft AGL — Part 107 automatically authorizes Class B
B. Must obtain prior ATC authorization; LAANC is not available in Class B
C. Must obtain ATC authorization; LAANC may be available in some Class B grids
D. Is prohibited entirely from Class B airspace regardless of authorization

> **Answer: C** — Class B requires authorization; LAANC is available in some Class B grids (7.11.8)

### 4.
The difference between a SIGMET and an AIRMET is:

A. SIGMETs cover a larger geographic area; AIRMETs cover only Class G airspace
B. SIGMETs are issued for severe or extreme hazards affecting all aircraft; AIRMETs are issued for moderate hazards primarily affecting small aircraft
C. AIRMETs are issued by ATC towers; SIGMETs are issued by the National Weather Service
D. There is no operational difference — both are equally hazardous for sUAS operations

> **Answer: B** — SIGMETs = severe/extreme (all aircraft); AIRMETs = moderate (light aircraft emphasis) (7.6.3, 7.6.4)

### 5. [HOT]
A pilot plans to fly at 2,500 ft MSL on a day when temperature is 95°F and dew point is 80°F. Pressure altitude at the site is approximately 2,600 ft. Density altitude is significantly higher than pressure altitude. What operational effect should the pilot anticipate?

A. Increased battery life — cooler, denser air requires less motor energy
B. Reduced lift efficiency — motors must spin faster to generate the same lift in low-density air; expect shorter battery life and potentially slower climb rates
C. No effect — DJI Mavic 3 performance is rated at sea level conditions and compensates automatically
D. Improved GPS accuracy due to reduced atmospheric interference at higher density altitude

> **Answer: B** — High density altitude reduces performance; hot + humid conditions compound the effect (7.4.16, Ch. 3b, 8)

### 6.
Under Part 107, sUAS operations at civil twilight are:

A. Prohibited at all times
B. Permitted without restriction — daylight rules apply throughout twilight
C. Permitted only with anti-collision lighting visible for 3 statute miles
D. Permitted only with an ATC waiver for night operations

> **Answer: C** — §107.29: civil twilight operations require anti-collision lighting visible 3 SM (7.11.3)

### 7.
A Prohibited area differs from a Restricted area in that:

A. Prohibited areas require ATC clearance; Restricted areas do not
B. Flight within a Prohibited area is absolutely prohibited at all times; Restricted areas may be entered when inactive
C. Prohibited areas apply only to manned aircraft; sUAS are exempt
D. Restricted areas are depicted on aeronautical charts; Prohibited areas are not

> **Answer: B** — Prohibited = permanently no entry; Restricted = check for active status (7.11.8)

### 8. [HOT]
An AIRMET Sierra has been issued for the route of a planned commercial photography flight. AIRMET Sierra indicates:

A. Moderate turbulence and strong surface winds
B. Moderate icing
C. IFR conditions — ceiling below 1,000 ft AGL and/or visibility below 3 SM
D. Severe thunderstorms with hail

> **Answer: C** — AIRMET Sierra = IFR/mountain obscuration (7.6.4)

### 9.
The Remote ID rule requires sUAS to broadcast:

A. The pilot's name and license number only
B. The UAS identity, real-time location and altitude, operator location, and velocity
C. Only the aircraft's FAA registration number
D. Broadcasting is only required when operating within 5 miles of an airport

> **Answer: B** — Remote ID broadcasts aircraft ID, location, altitude, velocity, and operator location in real time (7.9.1)

### 10.
Load factor increases during:

A. Straight and level flight at constant airspeed
B. Banked turns — the greater the bank angle, the higher the load factor and effective weight
C. Descents at idle power
D. GPS-mode hover in calm winds

> **Answer: B** — Load factor in a 60° bank = 2G; this doubles effective weight (7.4.8, Ch. 4)

### 11. [HOT]
A pilot notices the aircraft yawing to the right unexpectedly during a forward flight. After landing, the pilot finds that the front-left motor is slightly slower than the others. Under Part 107, can the pilot re-launch without correcting this issue?

A. Yes — slight asymmetric thrust is within normal operational tolerance
B. No — any malfunction affecting flight control must be corrected before flight; the aircraft is not airworthy
C. Yes — as long as the pilot uses GPS mode to compensate
D. Yes — but only for a short, low-altitude flight to verify the issue

> **Answer: B** — An aircraft is not airworthy if it has a known deficiency affecting flight control (7.11.14, 7.11.2, Ch. 12)

### 12.
The "E" factor in PAVE stands for "enVironment" and asks the pilot to assess:

A. Engine status and all electronic systems
B. All environmental conditions: airspace, weather, terrain, obstacles, and lighting conditions
C. Emergency procedures and contingency planning
D. External battery condition and charge status

> **Answer: B** — PAVE enVironment: all factors in the operating environment (7.8.4, Ch. 10)

### 13.
Under §107.9, the pilot must report to the FAA within 10 days if the accident results in:

A. Damage to the UAS aircraft exceeding $100 in repair costs
B. Any crash landing regardless of damage amount
C. Serious injury, loss of consciousness, or property damage exceeding $500 (not including the UAS)
D. Loss of the UAS due to flyaway

> **Answer: C** — §107.9 threshold: serious injury OR >$500 property damage (excluding the UAS) (7.14.16, Ch. 5)

### 14.
The phonetic pronunciation of the call sign "N724AG" is:

A. "November Seven Two Four Alpha Golf"
B. "N-Seven-Twenty-Four-A-G"
C. "Niner-Seven-Two-Four-Alpha-Golf"
D. "November Seven Hundred Twenty-Four Alpha Golf"

> **Answer: A** — Each character is pronounced individually; N = November in phonetic alphabet (7.7.1, Ch. 7)

### 15. [HOT]
A UAS operator is hired to photograph a new cell tower construction site. The tower is 400 ft AGL. The client wants aerial photos from 50 ft above the top of the tower (450 ft AGL). Can the pilot legally fly at 450 ft AGL?

A. No — 450 ft exceeds the absolute 400 ft AGL ceiling under all circumstances
B. Yes — when operating within 400 ft of a structure, the pilot may fly up to 400 ft above the top of that structure (850 ft AGL)
C. Yes — but only with a Class D airspace waiver
D. No — the FAA prohibits commercial photography of cell towers under Part 107

> **Answer: B** — §107.51 exception: within 400 ft of a structure, operations up to 400 ft above the structure are permitted (7.11.3)

### 16.
Class D airspace typically extends from the surface to approximately:

A. 1,200 ft MSL
B. 2,500 ft AGL
C. 4,000 ft AGL
D. 10,000 ft MSL

> **Answer: B** — Class D typically extends from the surface to approximately 2,500 ft AGL (the specific altitude is charted) (7.11.8, Ch. 2)

### 17.
Hyperventilation can be corrected by:

A. Increasing altitude to improve oxygen availability
B. Deliberately slowing the rate of breathing
C. Activating RTH and allowing the aircraft to land autonomously
D. Increasing physical activity to balance CO2 levels

> **Answer: B** — Hyperventilation is caused by excessive CO2 reduction; slow breathing restores balance (Ch. 9)

### 18. [HOT]
A pilot is experiencing Impulsivity (a hazardous attitude) after a client repeatedly calls asking for an update on imagery that was delayed due to earlier weather. What is the correct antidote response?

A. "I can do it — I'm an experienced pilot."
B. "Not so fast — think first."
C. "It won't happen to me."
D. "Follow the rules — they are usually right."

> **Answer: B** — Impulsivity antidote: "Not so fast — think first." (7.8.2, Ch. 10)

### 19.
The Chart Supplement U.S. provides which type of information NOT found on sectional charts?

A. Airspace class boundaries
B. Airport runway dimensions, lighting, services, and hours of operation
C. VFR chart depiction of terrain elevation
D. Special Use Airspace boundaries

> **Answer: B** — Chart Supplement contains operational details (hours, services, frequencies) not shown on sectional charts (Ch. 11)

### 20.
Under Part 107, a remote pilot must hold a current Remote Pilot Certificate. "Current" means:

A. Renewed annually by completing a new written exam
B. Valid for 24 months, with recurrent training completed online via FAA Safety Team (FAASTeam) before the 24-month expiration
C. Valid for 5 years from the date of initial issuance
D. Valid indefinitely once issued — no renewal required

> **Answer: B** — Remote Pilot Certificate requires recurrent training (ALC-677) every 24 months (7.9.2, Ch. 1)

### 21.
"Frontal overlap" in aerial survey photogrammetry refers to:

A. The percentage of the sensor that is in use during each image capture
B. The percentage of the area that consecutive images share along the flight path
C. The angle of camera tilt relative to nadir
D. The percentage of GPS accuracy lost due to atmospheric effects

> **Answer: B** — Frontal overlap: percentage of shared area between sequential images along the flight path (7.10.5)

### 22. [HOT]
A pilot is flying at 300 ft AGL in Class G airspace. A manned aircraft (Cessna) approaches from behind at approximately 400 ft AGL on a similar heading. The Part 107 pilot should:

A. Maintain altitude — the Cessna is 100 ft above and will pass without issue
B. Immediately descend and/or maneuver to increase separation; sUAS must give way to ALL manned aircraft
C. Transmit on 121.5 MHz to warn the Cessna
D. Activate RTH — the aircraft will navigate to a safe position automatically

> **Answer: B** — Part 107 §107.37: sUAS must yield right-of-way to all other aircraft at all times (7.13.1)

### 23.
Part 107 §107.27 prohibits operating a sUAS:

A. Within 12 hours of consuming any amount of alcohol
B. With a blood alcohol concentration of 0.04% or greater, or within 8 hours of consuming alcohol
C. After consuming any prescription medication regardless of type
D. Only after an accident report has been filed with the FAA

> **Answer: B** — §107.27: BAC < 0.04%; no alcohol within 8 hours (7.8.8, Ch. 9)

### 24.
A "Temporary Flight Restriction" (TFR) is an airspace restriction established for all of the following EXCEPT:

A. Presidential movement (VIP TFR)
B. Active wildfires where firefighting aircraft are operating
C. Major sporting events and air shows
D. The scheduled closing time of a Class D control tower at night

> **Answer: D** — Tower closure does not create a TFR; airspace class changes, not a TFR (7.11.8)

### 25.
The primary purpose of performing a compass calibration on a DJI Mavic 3 before flight is to:

A. Confirm FAA registration is current before each flight
B. Align the aircraft's magnetic compass with local magnetic conditions to ensure accurate heading and GPS-based navigation
C. Reset the RTH altitude to the factory default
D. Verify that all motors are spinning in the correct direction

> **Answer: B** — Compass calibration aligns the aircraft's magnetic reference with local field conditions; interference causes navigation errors (7.11.14, Ch. 12)
