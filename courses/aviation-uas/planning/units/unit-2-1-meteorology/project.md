# Project: Flight Weather Briefing Package

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 2.1 — Meteorology for UAS Operations
**Duration:** Weeks 11–12 (with lab running concurrently Week 10–11)
**Deliverable:** Written weather briefing + oral brief to class
**FAA Study Guide Reference:** Chapter 3a (Weather Sources), Chapter 3b (Weather Effects on Performance)

---

## Class Prompt

You have been hired by a client in central Ohio to conduct a commercial UAS operation. Your job is to produce a complete, professional **pre-flight weather briefing package** — the same documentation a licensed Part 107 pilot would prepare before any paid flight.

You will choose **one of the three scenarios below** and build your briefing around it. Your briefing must draw on real or realistic weather data and demonstrate that you can make a documented, justified go/no-go decision.

---

## Scenario Options (choose one)

**Scenario A — Agricultural Survey (Knox County, mid-summer)**
A farmer near Centerburg, Ohio has contracted you to fly a 200-acre soybean field for crop health imaging. The planned flight date is a weekday in mid-July. The field is in Class G airspace, 4 miles from a small regional airport. Flight window: 8:00–11:00 AM.

**Scenario B — Construction Progress Inspection (Columbus suburb, mid-autumn)**
A construction management firm needs aerial photos of a new subdivision progress. The site is 1.8 miles from Rickenbacker International Airport (KLCK) inside Class D airspace. Flight window: a weekday afternoon in early October.

**Scenario C — Real Estate Photography (rural Ohio, early spring)**
A real estate agency wants exterior aerial photos of a large property in rural Knox County. The client wants the shoot done on a Saturday morning in March. The property is in Class G airspace. Flight window: 9:00 AM–noon.

---

## Deliverables

Your weather briefing package includes two components: a written document and a 3–5 minute oral brief to the class.

### Part 1: Written Weather Briefing Package (Google Doc)

**Section 1 — Mission Summary (1 paragraph)**
- Client name (fictional), date, location, flight window
- Aircraft: DJI Mavic 3
- Purpose: what imagery is needed

**Section 2 — Airspace Check**
- Airspace class at the flight location
- Authorization required? (LAANC / none / manual authorization)
- Any NOTAMs or TFRs in effect (check tfr.faa.gov and notams.faa.gov)
- Screenshot or printout of your airspace check (B4UFLY or FAA DroneZone)

**Section 3 — Weather Data (with source citations)**
- **METAR** (current or realistic): full decode of all 12 elements
- **TAF** (forecast): valid period, forecast conditions, any TEMPO/FM/BECMG groups
- **AIRMET/SIGMET check**: any active advisories for Ohio? State advisory type and significance
- **Winds aloft (optional)**: winds at 3,000 ft MSL over Ohio from aviationweather.gov
- **Density altitude estimate**: using temperature and elevation, estimate whether density altitude will affect aircraft performance

**Section 4 — Go/No-Go Decision**

Complete this table:

| Factor | Part 107 / Safety Requirement | Forecast Conditions | Go / No-Go |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visibility | ≥ 3 statute miles | | |
| Cloud clearance | 500 ft below / 2,000 ft horizontal | | |
| Wind speed | Below DJI Mavic 3 max (20 kts) | | |
| Precipitation | None | | |
| Thunderstorm proximity | > 5 miles | | |
| AIRMET/SIGMET | None active | | |
| TFR/NOTAM | None in effect | | |
| **Overall Decision** | | | **GO / NO-GO** |

If NO-GO: describe what conditions would need to change and how you would communicate the delay to your client.
If GO: describe any risk mitigation actions you will take (e.g., fly before a front arrives, reduce flight time due to gusts).

**Section 5 — Weather Effects on Aircraft Performance**
Address at least two of the following in 1–2 paragraphs:
- How will temperature/humidity affect battery life or motor efficiency?
- Is density altitude a concern? Why or why not?
- What weather-related risk factor is most significant for your scenario? How does it affect your mission plan?

---

### Part 2: Oral Brief (3–5 Minutes, Week 12)

Deliver your briefing to the class as if you are speaking to your client and crew before the flight. Cover:
1. Scenario summary (30 seconds)
2. Airspace status and any authorizations needed
3. Weather summary — present your METAR/TAF findings clearly
4. Your go/no-go decision and the key factor that drove it
5. One risk mitigation action you would take

> **Do not read from your document.** Speak to your audience. Use your document as a reference, not a script. Eye contact and clear communication are scored.

---

## Rubric

| Component | Full Credit (4) | Partial Credit (2) | No Credit (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **METAR Decode** | All 12 elements correctly identified and explained with weather significance | Some elements correct; minor errors in interpretation | Incomplete or missing |
| **TAF Interpretation** | Valid periods, change groups (TEMPO/FM/BECMG) correctly explained; forecast timeline clear | Partially decoded; timeline unclear | Missing or incorrect |
| **AIRMET/SIGMET Check** | Correct advisory type identified; significance explained in flight context | Advisory noted but significance not explained | Not checked or missing |
| **Go/No-Go Table** | All 7 factors addressed with actual data; decision documented and justified | Most factors addressed; decision made but weakly justified | Incomplete; decision missing |
| **Performance Analysis** | Two weather effects analyzed with specific drone implications (battery, density altitude, etc.) | One factor analyzed; vague or generic | Missing |
| **Oral Brief** | 3–5 min; logical sequence; key data communicated clearly; not read verbatim; eye contact | Under/over time; some data unclear; mostly read from doc | Missing or completely read |

**Total: 24 points**
