# Vocabulary: Meteorology for UAS Operations

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 2.1 — Meteorology for UAS Operations
**Weeks:** 10–12

> Use these terms in bell ringer responses, lab write-ups, and project reflections. Terms may appear on the weekly quiz and the WebXam.

---

## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **METAR** | Aviation Routine Weather Report — a standardized hourly observation of surface weather conditions at an airport (wind, visibility, sky cover, temperature, dew point, altimeter setting); primary weather resource for flight planning | 7.11.4 | 1 |
| **TAF** | Terminal Aerodrome Forecast — a point forecast of weather expected at an airport over the next 24–30 hours; uses the same coded format as a METAR | 7.11.4 | 1 |
| **Area Forecast (FA)** | A general forecast of clouds, weather, and visibility for a large geographic area; provides context beyond the terminal forecast | 7.11.4 | 1 |
| **SIGMET** | Significant Meteorological Information — issued for hazardous weather affecting all aircraft: turbulence, icing, volcanic ash, or tropical cyclones | 7.11.5 | 2 |
| **AIRMET** | Airman's Meteorological Information — advisory for moderate conditions potentially hazardous to light aircraft: AIRMET Sierra (IFR/mtn obscuration), Tango (turbulence), Zulu (icing) | 7.11.5 | 2 |
| **Wind Shear** | A sudden change in wind speed or direction over a short distance; dangerous to aircraft in climb and approach phases; caused by frontal boundaries, convection, or terrain effects | 7.6.13 | 2 |
| **Microburst** | An intense, localized downdraft beneath a convective storm cell that spreads outward on impact with the ground; extremely dangerous — aircraft can lose lift suddenly on approach | 7.6.12 | 2 |
| **Turbulence** | Irregular motion of an aircraft caused by erratic air currents; classified by intensity (light, moderate, severe, extreme); caused by thermal activity, fronts, wind shear, terrain, or jet stream | 7.6.11 | 1 |
| **Mechanical Turbulence** | Turbulence created when wind flows over uneven terrain or structures (buildings, trees, hills); increases near ground in urban environments — especially relevant for low-altitude UAS flights | 7.6.11 | 2 |
| **Air Mass** | A large body of air with relatively uniform temperature and moisture throughout; classified by source region (Arctic, Polar, Tropical) and surface type (continental, maritime) | 7.6.9 | 1 |
| **Front** | The boundary between two air masses of different temperature and moisture; types include cold front, warm front, stationary front, and occluded front; frontal passages bring significant weather changes | 7.6.9 | 1 |
| **Dew Point** | The temperature at which air becomes saturated and water vapor begins to condense into fog or clouds; small spread between temperature and dew point (less than 5°F) indicates potential fog | 7.6.3 | 2 |
| **Density Altitude** | Pressure altitude corrected for non-standard temperature; at high density altitude (high elevation, hot weather, high humidity), aircraft performance degrades — motors work harder for less lift | 7.6.3 | 2 |
| **Visibility** | The distance at which a pilot can see and identify prominent objects; Part 107 requires minimum 3 statute miles ground-based visibility for sUAS operations | 7.11.3 | 1 |
| **VFR** | Visual Flight Rules — rules governing flight conducted by visual reference to the ground and other aircraft; sUAS operations are conducted under VFR conditions | 7.11.3 | 1 |
| **Crosswind** | Wind blowing perpendicular to the direction of flight or takeoff/landing; requires the pilot to compensate to maintain the intended flight path | 7.6.13 | 1 |
| **Tailwind** | Wind blowing from behind the aircraft in the direction of flight; increases ground speed but reduces control effectiveness during landing; Part 107 operations should avoid strong tailwinds on approach | 7.6.13 | 1 |
| **Mountain Wave** | A series of wave-like air motions downwind of a mountain or ridge; can extend to high altitudes; associated with turbulence and strong downdrafts even far from the terrain feature | 7.6.13 | 2 |
| **Thunderstorm** | A violent convective storm with lightning, heavy precipitation, gusty winds, and potentially severe turbulence and hail; all UAS operations must cease and aircraft be secured at a minimum of 5 miles away | 7.6.12 | 2 |
| **Go/No-Go Decision** | A systematic evaluation of weather, aircraft condition, airspace, and mission parameters before each flight to determine whether the operation is safe and legal to proceed | 7.11.5 | 3 |

## Key Distinctions

| Term A | Term B | Key Difference |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| METAR | TAF | METAR is a current observation (what the weather IS now); TAF is a forecast (what it is expected to BE over the next 24–30 hours) |
| SIGMET | AIRMET | SIGMETs are for severe/extreme conditions affecting all aircraft; AIRMETs are for moderate conditions affecting smaller, lighter aircraft |
| Turbulence | Wind Shear | Turbulence is irregular atmospheric motion (up/down/sideways eddies); wind shear is a sudden change in wind speed or direction across a boundary |
| Crosswind | Tailwind | Crosswind is perpendicular to flight path (affects lateral control); tailwind is from behind (increases ground speed, reduces landing margin) |

## Bell Ringer Prompts

1. You pull up the METAR for your flight location and the visibility is listed as 2 miles. Is this a legal and safe condition for a Part 107 commercial UAS operation? What is the minimum requirement?
2. What is the difference between a METAR and a TAF? Which one would you check to know current conditions? Which to plan a flight tomorrow morning?
3. Explain why a warm, humid summer day at high elevation can actually be MORE challenging for a drone than a cold winter day at the same location.
4. You see a SIGMET issued for severe turbulence in your area. What do you do? Can you legally fly?
5. Your weather brief shows temperature and dew point are only 2°F apart. What weather phenomenon should you anticipate, and how does it affect your flight decision?

## Study Tips

- Learn the METAR decode sequence: station, time, wind, visibility, weather, sky condition, temperature/dew point, altimeter — practice decoding a real METAR every bell ringer
- SIGMET vs AIRMET: think "Significant = Severe, Airman = Advisory"
- Density altitude shows up on both the Part 107 exam and the WebXam — know that heat + humidity + elevation = degraded performance
