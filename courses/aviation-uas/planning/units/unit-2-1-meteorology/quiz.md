# Quiz: Meteorology for UAS Operations

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 2.1 — Meteorology for UAS Operations
**Format:** 15 MCQ | End of Week 12 | ~15 min
**Standards:** 7.6.3, 7.6.6, 7.6.9, 7.6.11, 7.6.12, 7.6.13, 7.11.3, 7.11.4, 7.11.5

## Questions

### 1.
You are planning a commercial UAS photo shoot and check the METAR for your flight location. The visibility is reported as 2SM. Under Part 107, your next action should be:

A. Proceed with the flight — 2SM is sufficient for low-altitude UAS operations
B. Cancel or postpone the flight — Part 107 requires a minimum ground-based visibility of 3 statute miles
C. Reduce your maximum altitude to 50 feet and proceed
D. Request a waiver from the FAA for reduced visibility operations

> **Answer: B** — Part 107 requires minimum 3 SM ground-based visibility (Standard 7.11.3)

---

### 2.
A TAF differs from a METAR primarily in that a TAF:

A. Is issued only during severe weather events and includes emergency diversion information
B. Is a current observation of conditions at an airport
C. Is a forecast of expected weather at an airport over the next 24–30 hours
D. Is produced by the National Weather Service for agricultural and marine users

> **Answer: C** — TAF is a forecast; METAR is a current observation (Standard 7.11.4)

---

### 3.
While reviewing weather products before a drone mapping flight in rural Ohio, you see an AIRMET Tango issued for your route. This means:

A. Icing conditions exist above 10,000 feet along the route
B. Moderate turbulence is expected for aircraft along the route
C. IFR conditions due to mountain obscuration are forecast
D. A tornado watch is in effect for the area

> **Answer: B** — AIRMET Tango = moderate turbulence (Standard 7.11.5)

---

### 4.
You notice the METAR shows temperature 68°F and dew point 65°F (3°F spread). What weather phenomenon should concern you most?

A. Severe turbulence near the surface
B. Rapid formation of radiation fog — possible reduced visibility at or near your flight time
C. Ice accumulation on the aircraft's propellers
D. Microbursts associated with nearby convective cells

> **Answer: B** — Small temperature-dew point spread (less than 5°F) indicates fog risk (Standard 7.6.3)

---

### 5.
Density altitude is BEST described as:

A. The altitude shown on a GPS receiver during normal operations
B. Pressure altitude corrected for non-standard temperature; high density altitude degrades aircraft performance
C. The altitude above mean sea level at which a drone's GPS system operates most accurately
D. A calculation used only when flying above 3,000 feet MSL

> **Answer: B** — Density altitude definition and effect on performance (Standard 7.6.3)

---

### 6.
You are conducting agricultural drone mapping on a hot August afternoon (temperature 95°F, high humidity). Compared to a cool fall morning, your DJI Mavic 3 will most likely:

A. Fly longer on a battery charge due to reduced motor load
B. Experience reduced lift efficiency — motors must work harder to generate the same thrust
C. Perform identically — drone performance is not affected by temperature
D. Operate more quietly due to reduced air density

> **Answer: B** — High density altitude (heat + humidity) degrades multi-rotor performance (Standard 7.6.3)

---

### 7.
A microburst is most dangerous to aircraft because it:

A. Creates sustained headwind that causes aircraft to overshoot runways
B. Produces an intense downdraft that spreads outward on impact with the ground, causing sudden loss of lift
C. Generates widespread severe turbulence above 10,000 feet for hours after dissipation
D. Reduces visibility to zero miles across large geographic areas

> **Answer: B** — Microburst = localized intense downdraft; sudden lift loss (Standard 7.6.12)

---

### 8.
Which AIRMET type would be issued for mountain obscuration conditions?

A. AIRMET Tango
B. AIRMET Zulu
C. AIRMET Sierra
D. SIGMET Alpha

> **Answer: C** — AIRMET Sierra = IFR conditions and mountain obscuration (Standard 7.11.5)

---

### 9.
You are flying a drone near an urban area with several three-story buildings in the proximity of your flight path. The wind is 15 knots at your altitude. The greatest weather-related concern near the buildings is:

A. Crosswind effects on forward flight speed
B. Mechanical turbulence created by wind flowing over and around the building structures
C. Reduced GPS satellite availability due to building interference
D. Lightning attraction from metal structures during humid conditions

> **Answer: B** — Buildings create mechanical turbulence in their wake (Standard 7.6.11)

---

### 10.
Matching — connect each weather resource to its primary use:

| Resource | Use |
|----------|-----|
| A. METAR | 1. Forecast weather at an airport over 24–30 hours |
| B. TAF | 2. Advisory for conditions hazardous to light aircraft |
| C. SIGMET | 3. Current observed weather conditions at an airport |
| D. AIRMET | 4. Warning of severe weather hazardous to all aircraft |

> **Answers: A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2** (Standard 7.11.4, 7.11.5)

---

### 11.
A weather briefer tells you a cold front is expected to pass through your flight area by 1400 local time. Before 1400, you would expect:

A. Improving visibility and decreasing winds as the cold air mass moves in
B. Possible gusty, shifting winds and a line of convective activity associated with the frontal boundary
C. Stabilizing conditions and lower turbulence as the warm air retreats
D. No significant change — frontal boundaries rarely affect low-altitude UAS operations

> **Answer: B** — Cold front passage brings gusty winds and convective weather (Standard 7.6.9)

---

### 12.
A wind shear event is most likely to affect a UAS pilot during:

A. A long cruise leg at 400 feet AGL in calm conditions
B. Climb to altitude from the departure point or descent and landing approach
C. Hovering at a fixed altitude while monitoring a construction site
D. Flying in designated Class G airspace away from any weather

> **Answer: B** — Wind shear is most dangerous during climb and approach phases (Standard 7.6.13)

---

### 13.
True or False: A SIGMET is issued for less severe weather conditions than an AIRMET, and poses a lower threat to flight safety.

> **Answer: FALSE** — SIGMETs are for severe/extreme conditions affecting all aircraft; AIRMETs are for moderate conditions affecting lighter aircraft (Standard 7.11.5)

---

### 14.
What minimum required action should a remote pilot take when a thunderstorm is visible in the area?

A. Continue the mission at reduced altitude to stay below the storm
B. Land immediately and secure the aircraft; cease operations — thunderstorms produce severe turbulence, lightning, and microbursts that make UAS operations unsafe
C. Move the aircraft to the opposite side of the flight site from the storm and continue
D. Reduce altitude to 50 feet and monitor wind direction; land if winds exceed 25 knots

> **Answer: B** — Thunderstorms require immediate cessation of UAS operations and aircraft securing (Standard 7.6.12)

---

### 15.
A Go/No-Go decision before a UAS flight is BEST described as:

A. The pilot's instinct about whether the weather looks acceptable from the launch site
B. A rapid visual check of the sky to confirm no clouds are present directly overhead
C. A systematic evaluation of weather products, aircraft condition, airspace, and mission parameters to determine if the operation is safe and legal
D. A verbal agreement among crew members that each person is ready to begin the mission

> **Answer: C** — Go/No-Go is a systematic pre-flight decision process (Standard 7.11.5)
