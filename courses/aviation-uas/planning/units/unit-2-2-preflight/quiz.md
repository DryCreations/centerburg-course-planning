## Questions

### 1.
Under Part 107, the minimum ground-based visibility required for sUAS operations without a waiver is:

A. 1 statute mile
B. 3 statute miles
C. 5 nautical miles
D. 500 feet

> **Answer: B** — Part 107 §107.51(c) requires minimum 3 SM visibility (7.11.3)

---

### 2.
You plan to fly at 200 ft AGL within Class D airspace around a non-towered airport. What authorization method would you most likely use?

A. No authorization needed — Class D airspace is uncontrolled
B. LAANC or manual waiver — Class D requires authorization regardless of tower operational status
C. LAANC only — manual waivers cannot be used in Class D
D. Call the airport manager for verbal permission; no FAA authorization is required

> **Answer: B** — Class D requires ATC authorization; use LAANC if available or manual waiver (7.9.4, 7.11.8)

### 3. [HOT]
A remote pilot is planning a flight at a site that B4UFLY shows as a LAANC grid with 100 ft authorized altitude. The client needs video at 250 ft AGL. The pilot should:

A. Fly at 250 ft — LAANC grids are guidelines, not hard limits
B. Request a manual FAA waiver for the additional altitude; LAANC only covers up to the grid ceiling
C. Fly at 100 ft and tell the client it is the best available footage
D. Use a taller drone so the camera is physically at 250 ft while the aircraft is at 100 ft

> **Answer: B** — LAANC covers only up to the authorized altitude ceiling; additional altitude requires a manual waiver (7.11.8)

---

### 4.
NOTAMs are issued for all of the following EXCEPT:

A. Temporarily inoperable runway lighting
B. Parachute jump operations
C. Permanent changes to instrument approach procedures
D. Temporary hazards such as kite flying or rocket launches

> **Answer: C** — Permanent changes are published in official charts and publications, not NOTAMs; NOTAMs are for time-critical, temporary, or recently-changed information (7.11.6)

---

### 5.
Before a commercial sUAS flight, a remote pilot checks tfr.faa.gov and finds an active TFR centered on a major league baseball stadium 3 miles from the planned flight area. The TFR radius is 3 nautical miles. The planned flight is 3.5 statute miles from the stadium. Should the pilot fly?

A. Yes — 3.5 statute miles exceeds the 3 NM TFR radius (3.45 statute miles)
B. No — any nearby TFR automatically prohibits all UAS operations within 10 miles
C. It depends — the pilot should calculate whether 3.5 SM puts them outside the 3 NM radius, considering that 3 NM = 3.45 SM
D. Yes — TFRs for sporting events apply only to crewed aircraft, not sUAS

> **Answer: C** — The pilot must correctly convert: 3 NM = approximately 3.45 SM; at 3.5 SM the site is marginally outside the TFR but the pilot should verify exact coordinates and err on the side of caution (7.11.8)

### 6.
The primary purpose of performing a weight and balance check before flight is to:

A. Confirm the aircraft is registered with the FAA
B. Ensure total weight does not exceed manufacturer limits and CG stays within the allowable range for safe flight
C. Verify battery charge is adequate for the mission duration
D. Comply with Remote ID broadcasting requirements

> **Answer: B** — W&B check ensures safe loading and proper CG (7.4.9)

### 7. [HOT]
A remote pilot is flying a DJI Mavic 3 at a rural farm on a hot July afternoon. Temperature is 97°F, elevation is 1,050 ft MSL, and humidity is very high. Compared to the same aircraft on a cool October morning, the pilot should expect:

A. Longer battery flight time due to reduced aerodynamic drag in thin air
B. Reduced motor efficiency and shorter battery life — motors must work harder to generate lift in high density altitude conditions
C. Improved GPS lock due to warmer satellite signal propagation
D. No significant change in performance — DJI Mavic 3 performance is independent of weather

> **Answer: B** — High density altitude (hot + humid + elevated) degrades multi-rotor performance (7.11.11, Ch. 8)

### 8.
During a preflight inspection, a pilot notices that one of the DJI Mavic 3's propellers has a small nick near the tip. The correct action is to:

A. Inspect the nick carefully — if it is less than 1 cm, proceed with flight
B. Ground the aircraft until the propeller is replaced; any damage to a propeller is grounds for not flying
C. Apply tape over the nick and proceed; tape repairs are acceptable under Part 107
D. Fly carefully at reduced altitude and speed to minimize the effect of the nick

> **Answer: B** — Any propeller damage fails the airworthiness check; do not fly (7.11.14, 7.11.2)

### 9.
The Return-to-Home (RTH) altitude should be set to:

A. The aircraft's maximum certified altitude ceiling (usually 500 m)
B. An altitude higher than the tallest obstacle in the return flight path, but as low as practical
C. Exactly 100 ft AGL — the FAA-required default RTH altitude
D. The pilot's comfort level — there is no standard guidance on RTH altitude

> **Answer: B** — RTH must clear all obstacles in the return path; setting it too low risks obstacle collision; too high wastes battery (7.11.13)

### 10.
Class B airspace is best described as:

A. Uncontrolled airspace surrounding small general aviation airports
B. Controlled airspace from the surface to 10,000 ft MSL surrounding the nation's busiest airports, typically shaped like an upside-down wedding cake
C. Controlled airspace extending from 700 ft AGL to 18,000 ft MSL across most of the continental US
D. Special use airspace reserved for military training operations

> **Answer: B** — Class B surrounds busiest airports; requires ATC clearance (7.1.9, Ch. 2)

### 11. [HOT]
A non-towered airport has published UNICOM frequency 122.8. You plan to fly 0.4 miles from this airport at 150 ft AGL with LAANC authorization. Regarding radio communications, you should:

A. Tune to 122.8 and make standard position broadcasts — you are now operating as a manned aircraft pilot
B. Avoid using any radio — Part 107 remote pilots are prohibited from transmitting on aviation frequencies
C. Monitor 122.8 to be aware of manned aircraft traffic in the area; you may transmit only in an emergency
D. Ignore radio communications — LAANC authorization eliminates the need for radio awareness

> **Answer: C** — Remote pilots should monitor CTAF near airports for situational awareness; transmit only in emergency; LAANC does not replace radio awareness (7.7.7, Ch. 7)

### 12.
During a preflight check, GPS status shows only 4 satellites locked. The recommended minimum for GPS-dependent flight modes is:

A. 4 satellites — this is the minimum triangulation number required by FAA
B. 8 or more satellites — fewer satellites reduce positioning accuracy and reliability; the aircraft may revert to ATTI mode
C. 2 satellites — all modern GPS requires only 2 to function
D. GPS satellite count is not relevant for recreational drone flights under 400 ft

> **Answer: B** — DJI Mavic 3 needs 8+ satellites for reliable GPS mode; below this, positioning degrades (7.11.14)

### 13. [HOT]
You are planning a mission over a forested area. Tree canopy height is approximately 80 ft. One cellular tower 0.5 miles away stands at 320 ft AGL. What should you set as your RTH altitude?

A. 80 ft — the height of the trees is the relevant obstacle in the flight area
B. 320 ft or higher — the cellular tower is in the return flight path and must be cleared
C. 400 ft — the FAA's 400 ft limit is the appropriate default RTH altitude
D. 0 ft — set RTH to land in place to avoid any potential obstacle collision

> **Answer: B** — RTH altitude must clear the highest obstacle in the return path, which includes obstacles outside the immediate flight area (7.11.13)

### 14.
The Chart Supplement U.S. provides which of the following types of airport information?

A. Real-time weather observations and forecasts
B. Runway dimensions, frequencies, services available, operating hours, and notes about the airport
C. Sectional chart imagery of the airport's control zone
D. LAANC authorization grids for the airport's airspace

> **Answer: B** — Chart Supplement provides operational data for each airport (Ch. 11)

### 15.
A pilot's center of gravity (CG) shifts significantly forward due to a heavy camera mounted on the nose of the aircraft. This will most likely:

A. Have no effect — CG shifts only matter in crewed aircraft
B. Cause the aircraft to pitch forward; the flight controller must apply continuous nose-up correction, reducing battery life and control effectiveness
C. Improve stability by providing a more consistent reference point for the autopilot
D. Automatically be corrected by the Remote ID broadcast system

> **Answer: B** — Forward CG causes pitch-forward tendency; autopilot compensates but reduces efficiency (7.4.9, Ch. 4)

### 16.
Under Part 107, a visual observer (VO) is:

A. Required on every commercial UAS flight; the remote PIC cannot fly solo
B. Optional — the remote PIC may use a VO but is not required to do so; the VO assists with VLOS and situational awareness
C. Prohibited — only the remote PIC is legally allowed to observe the aircraft
D. Required only when flying in Class B airspace

> **Answer: B** — VOs are optional assistants to the remote PIC; not required unless the remote PIC needs help maintaining VLOS (7.9.7)

### 17. [HOT]
You complete your preflight check and find the battery shows 67% charge. Your planned mission typically uses 40% battery. Is it safe to depart?

A. Yes — 67% is more than the 40% needed plus some margin
B. No — FAA requires minimum 85% battery charge before any commercial UAS flight
C. Yes, but only if you set the low battery warning and return-to-home level appropriately; consider mission abort conditions
D. It depends on the airspace class — battery minimums are regulated differently per airspace class

> **Answer: C** — No specific FAA regulation mandates a starting battery percentage; however, the pilot must plan for the mission's full power consumption plus a safety margin; setting appropriate RTH/low battery warnings is critical (7.11.14)

### 18.
A flight plan should include at minimum:

A. The aircraft's FAA registration number, the pilot's driver's license information, and insurance documentation
B. Planned route or area, maximum altitude, emergency landing zones, and authorization documentation
C. Only the GPS coordinates of the takeoff point — the FAA does not require additional flight planning for sUAS
D. Weather briefing, fuel load, and instrument approach procedures

> **Answer: B** — A proper UAS flight plan documents route/area, altitude, emergency contingencies, and authorization (7.11.7)

### 19.
Alert areas are depicted on aeronautical charts to indicate:

A. Areas where flight is completely prohibited for all aircraft
B. Areas with high volumes of pilot training or unusual aerial activity; pilots should exercise caution but no specific authorization is required
C. Areas where military operations require all aircraft to maintain 2,000 ft separation
D. Emergency medical airlift corridors where drone operations are permanently restricted

> **Answer: B** — Alert areas ("A" followed by number) indicate areas of concentrated training/activity; caution required but no authorization (7.11.8, Ch. 2)

### 20. [HOT]
A pilot intends to fly at a site that shows a Restricted area on the sectional chart (R-4401). The pilot should:

A. Avoid the area completely — Restricted areas permanently prohibit all aircraft including sUAS
B. Contact the using/controlling agency to determine whether the Restricted area is active and whether entry is permitted
C. Enter at the nearest edge of the area only and depart immediately
D. Fly only below 400 ft AGL — sUAS are exempt from Restricted area rules at low altitude

> **Answer: B** — Restricted areas may be active or released to FAA depending on schedule; always contact controlling agency to determine current status (7.11.8, Ch. 2)
