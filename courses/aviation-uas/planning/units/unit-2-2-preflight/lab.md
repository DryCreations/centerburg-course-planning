# Lab: Complete Pre-Flight Package — Site Survey, Airspace Check & Inspection

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**Unit:** 2.2 — Preflight Preparation & Flight Planning
**Duration:** 3 days | **Weeks:** 13–14 (Days 1–3)
**Tools Needed:** Google Docs, B4UFLY (web at b4ufly.faa.gov), FAA DroneZone (faadronezone.faa.gov), tfr.faa.gov, DJI Mavic 3 (ground inspection only — no flight), teacher-provided sectional chart excerpt
**FAA Study Guide Reference:** Chapter 2 (Airspace), Chapter 4 (Loading), Chapter 8 (Performance), Chapter 11 (Airport Ops), Chapter 12 (Maintenance/Preflight Inspection)

---

## Objectives

By the end of this lab, students will be able to:
- Interpret a sectional chart to identify airspace class, altitude limits, and special use areas
- Use B4UFLY and FAA DroneZone to check airspace authorization before a flight
- Check NOTAMs and TFRs as part of a preflight workflow
- Perform a physical airworthiness inspection on a DJI Mavic 3
- Calculate density altitude and its effect on aircraft performance
- Complete a before-takeoff checklist and set RTH altitude

---

## Background / Setup

> The FAA Part 107 exam dedicates its highest proportion of questions to preflight preparation (FAA Study Guide chapters 2, 8, 11, 12). A professional remote pilot always goes through the same systematic sequence before every flight — the difference between an experienced pilot and a beginner is that the experienced pilot has made this checklist automatic. This lab builds that habit step by step.

## Instructions

### Day 13-1 — Sectional Chart Interpretation + Airspace Check

**Part A: Sectional Chart Reading**

The teacher will provide a printed excerpt of the Columbus, OH VFR sectional chart. Using the excerpt, answer the following questions in your Google Doc:

1. Locate Knox County Airport (or the nearest airport to Centerburg, OH on the chart). What is:
   - The airport's elevation (ft MSL)?
   - The traffic pattern altitude (assume standard: +1,000 ft)?
   - The communication frequency listed?
   - Is this a towered or non-towered airport?

2. Identify the airspace class at the following distances from Knox County Airport:
   - Directly over the airport at 500 ft AGL
   - 3 miles south of the airport at 800 ft AGL
   - 8 miles north at 1,200 ft AGL

3. Locate any special use airspace within 30 miles of Centerburg. For each one:
   - What type (MOA, Restricted, etc.)?
   - What altitude range is affected?
   - Who is the controlling agency?

4. Identify two obstacle symbols within 15 miles of Centerburg. What type of obstacle is each? What is the maximum elevation? How would you verify their current status?

**Part B: Digital Airspace Check (B4UFLY + DroneZone)**

Navigate to [b4ufly.faa.gov](https://b4ufly.faa.gov) in a browser.

1. Enter the address or coordinates of the school or an approved local flight site. Record:
   - What airspace class is at this location?
   - Is LAANC available? If so, what altitude is authorized via LAANC?
   - Are there any active TFRs within 5 miles?

2. Navigate to [tfr.faa.gov](https://tfr.faa.gov) and check for TFRs in Ohio. List any active TFRs and their stated reason (stadium, VIP movement, disaster, wildfire, etc.)

3. Check [notams.faa.gov](https://notams.faa.gov) for NOTAMs within 5 nautical miles of a nearby airport. List any NOTAMs you find and explain in plain language what each one means for a drone operator.

Work in groups of 2–3. Each group will inspect one DJI Mavic 3 aircraft from the fleet using the preflight checklist below. **No aircraft will leave the ground today.** Record all findings in your Google Doc.

| Item | Check | Finding / Notes |
|---|---|---|

### Day 14-3 — Performance Calculations + Mission Planning

**Part A: Density Altitude Calculation**

Use the formula or chart method to estimate density altitude for two flight scenarios. For each, state whether performance will be **better than, the same as, or worse than** standard day performance:

*Standard day: 59°F (15°C), sea level, dry air*

| Scenario | Temp | Elevation | Humidity | Estimate Density Altitude | Performance Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: Rural Ohio, July | 92°F | 1,100 ft MSL | High | | |
| B: Rural Ohio, December | 28°F | 1,100 ft MSL | Low | | |
| C: Colorado flight site | 75°F | 7,200 ft MSL | Low | | |

After completing the table, answer: For Scenario A, if your DJI Mavic 3 typically flies 31 minutes per charge, what would you expect and why?

**Part B: Flight Planning — Complete Mission Package**

You are planning a commercial agricultural survey flight over a 150-acre field in Knox County, Ohio, 2 miles southwest of the Knox County Airport. The flight date is a Tuesday in early June. Flight window: 8:00–11:00 AM.

Produce the following in your Google Doc:

1. **Airspace verification:** What class? Does this location require LAANC or waiver? What LAANC altitude is authorized?
2. **NOTAM/TFR check:** Were any active? (Use today's real data and simulate a future date if needed.)
3. **Weather brief summary:** Using the weather lab from Unit 2.1 as a guide, describe what weather data you would pull. List the specific products (METAR, TAF, AIRMET) and the conditions that would make this a go or no-go.
4. **Performance note:** Given a June morning in Ohio (estimate: 72°F, 1,100 ft elevation, moderate humidity), will density altitude be a concern? How does this affect your battery plan?
5. **RTH altitude setting:** The flight site has a tree line at approximately 60 ft and a farm silo at 90 ft at the edge of the property. What would you set as your RTH altitude? Why?
