# Meteorology Quiz Review: Google Slides Prompts

**Teacher-only.** Paste **Part 1** into Google Slides' "Help me create a presentation." Then paste
**Part 2** the same way and move its slides to the end. Two parts because the generator may stop short
on a long deck.

**How to run it:** on each **question slide**, read it aloud, give 20 seconds, then take answers from the
group (hands, or "point to the one you'd pick"). Ask **why** before you reveal. The next slide explains it.

The answer lists on the question slides are **the answers our class actually chose**, with how many
picked each. No names.

## Part 1 (slides 1 to 11)

```
Create a presentation for a high school drone class called "Meteorology Quiz Review." Dark navy background, bold pink and light blue headings, large readable text, simple flat icons or diagrams. Keep text short. 11 slides:

Slide 1, "Today": top section headed "What we are covering" lists in full: "7.6.3 Explain the effects of temperature on weather." "7.6.9 Describe weather system formation, including air masses and fronts." "7.6.12 Describe the types and impact of thunderstorms, tornados, microbursts and hurricanes." "7.11.11 Describe atmospheric conditions affecting performance." "7.11.4 Describe weather products and resources required for preflight planning, current and forecast weather for departure, enroute, and arrival phases of flight." Bottom section headed "Agenda": "Go over the most missed questions." "How to review." "Friday: optional retake, averaged with your first score."

Slide 2, "How We Did": large text "Class average: 67%." Then: "We will go question by question. Answer out loud. Explain why."

Slide 3, question slide, title "Question 1": "How does high density altitude affect a multirotor drone?" Then headed "What our class picked": "It increases lift because air molecules are tightly packed (5)." "It reduces propeller efficiency and lift because air is thinner (2)." "It causes motors to draw less current during a hover (1)." Bottom: "Which is right? Why?"

Slide 4, explanation slide, title "Hot, High, Humid = Thin Air": "Answer: it REDUCES lift. Thin air gives the propellers less to push on." Then three short lines: "Hot: warm air spreads out." "High: less air above pressing down." "Humid: water vapor replaces heavier air." Then: "Result: motors work harder, the battery drains faster." Include a simple side-by-side graphic of a box of tightly packed dots labeled "cold, low, dry: thick air" and a box of spread-out dots labeled "hot, high, humid: thin air."

Slide 5, question slide, title "Question 2": "Why does high humidity make air LESS dense than dry air at the same temperature?" Then headed "What our class picked": "Water vapor adds heavy weight that crushes air molecules down (3)." "Moist air increases overall atmospheric pressure instantly (3)." "Water vapor molecules are lighter than dry air molecules (2)." Bottom: "Which is right? Why?"

Slide 6, explanation slide, title "Water Vapor Is Light": "Answer: water vapor molecules are LIGHTER than the nitrogen and oxygen they replace." Then a simple comparison graphic with three labeled circles: "Water vapor: 18." "Nitrogen: 28." "Oxygen: 32." Caption: "More water vapor in the air means lighter, thinner air, so less lift."

Slide 7, question slide, title "Question 3": "What weather usually comes with a high pressure system, also called an anticyclone?" Then headed "What our class picked": "Clear skies and stable flyable air (3)." "Rapidly shifting, unpredictable windshear (3)." "Heavy rainfall and severe thunderstorms (2)." Bottom: "Which is right? Why?"

Slide 8, explanation slide, title "Highs and Lows": two columns. Left, headed "HIGH pressure (anticyclone)": "Air sinks. Clouds dry up. Clear and stable. Good flying." Right, headed "LOW pressure": "Air rises. Clouds build. Storms." Include a simple graphic: downward arrows under an H on the left, upward arrows and a cloud above an L on the right.

Slide 9, question slide, title "Question 4": "What hazard is a column of fast-falling air that hits the ground and blasts out in all directions?" Then headed "What our class picked": "Microburst (5)." "Anticyclone (3)." Bottom: "Which is right? Why might someone mix these up?"

Slide 10, explanation slide, title "Microburst vs Anticyclone": "Answer: microburst. A small, violent downdraft from a storm, over in minutes." "Anticyclone: a huge, gentle high pressure system that brings clear weather." "Both involve sinking air, but one is a storm hazard and one is fair weather." Include a simple graphic of a cloud with a thick arrow going straight down and curving outward in both directions along the ground.

Slide 11, question slide, title "Question 5": "What is the minimum distance to stay from an active thunderstorm?" Then headed "What our class picked": "20 miles (3)." "5 miles (3)." "10 miles (2)." Bottom: "Which is right? Why so far?"
```

## Part 2 (slides 12 to 22)

```
Continue the same "Meteorology Quiz Review" presentation for a high school drone class, same style: dark navy background, bold pink and light blue headings, large text, simple flat graphics. 11 slides:

Slide 1, explanation slide, title "Stay 20 Miles Away": "Answer: 20 miles." "Storms push gusts, hail, and lightning far beyond the cloud you can see." "The mature stage is the most dangerous: air rushing up and down side by side." Include a simple three-step graphic of a thunderstorm's life: "Cumulus: building, air rising." "Mature: most dangerous, up and down drafts." "Dissipating: fading, air sinking."

Slide 2, question slide, title "Question 6": "What does P6SM mean in a weather forecast?" Then headed "What our class picked": "Precipitation within 6 statute miles (5)." "More than 6 statute miles of visibility (3)." Bottom: "Which is right? How can you tell?"

Slide 3, explanation slide, title "Decode, Don't Guess": "Answer: visibility is MORE than 6 statute miles." "P means plus, more than. SM means statute miles." "You will see it in a TAF, the forecast. Current reports (METARs) list visibility up to 10SM." Include a forecast line in a monospace font with P6SM highlighted: "TAF KCMH 1418/1524 27010KT P6SM SCT050."

Slide 4, question slide, title "Question 7": "What does SKC mean in an aviation weather report?" Then headed "What our class picked": "Sky clear (4)." "Standard knots crosswind (3)." "Surface knot current (1)." Bottom: "Which is right?"

Slide 5, explanation slide, title "Sky Codes": a simple table: "SKC: sky clear." "FEW: a few clouds." "SCT: scattered." "BKN: broken, a ceiling." "OVC: overcast, a ceiling." Then: "Heights add two zeros: BKN015 means broken clouds at 1,500 feet." Include a simple graphic of a sky with clouds filling more of it from left to right.

Slide 6, question slide, title "Question 8": "The temperature and dew point are within 3 degrees. What does that tell you?" Then headed "What our class picked": "The air is near saturation, with a high risk of fog (5)." "The air is extremely dry and stable (3)." Bottom: "Which is right? Why?"

Slide 7, explanation slide, title "Close Spread = Fog": "Answer: the air is nearly full of moisture, so fog or low clouds can form." "Dew point is the temperature where moisture condenses." "Example: 12/11 means fog risk. 24/05 means dry air." Include a simple graphic of two thermometers close together with fog below them.

Slide 8, question slide, title "Question 9": "What kind of turbulence comes from wind breaking up around buildings and trees?" Then headed "What our class picked": "Mechanical turbulence (5)." "Convective turbulence (2)." "Frontal turbulence (1)." Bottom: "Which is right? What makes the other two?"

Slide 9, explanation slide, title "Three Kinds of Turbulence": "Mechanical: wind tumbling over buildings and trees." "Convective: sun-heated air rising unevenly, like over a parking lot." "Frontal: where two air masses meet." Include a simple graphic of wind arrows swirling behind a building.

Slide 10, title "How to Review Before Friday": five short lines in large text: "Play the Gimkit review on Classroom until you can answer without guessing." "Quiz yourself. Rereading notes feels like studying but is not." "Make flashcards for the codes: SKC, P6SM, FM, TEMPO, BKN, OVC, BR, VCTS." "Explain each big idea out loud to someone: thin air, highs and lows, storms." "On the quiz, cross out the wrong answers first."

Slide 11, title "Friday: Optional Retake": "Your retake score is averaged with your first score." "Get 100% and you earn back half the points you missed." "Not required. If you are happy with your score, keep working on BPA."
```
