## Results

**8 students. Class average 13.4 out of 20 (67%).** Scores: 20, 17, 16, 13, 12, 10, 10, 9.

| Q | Topic | Right | Most common wrong answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | High density altitude and lift | **2 of 8** | "Increases lift, molecules tightly packed" (5) |
| 19 | Why humid air is less dense | **2 of 8** | Split: "heavy water vapor" (3), "higher pressure" (3) |
| 2 | High pressure weather | **3 of 8** | "Windshear" (3), "severe thunderstorms" (2) |
| 15 | Distance from a thunderstorm | **3 of 8** | 5 miles (3), 10 miles (2) |
| 18 | P6SM | **3 of 8** | "Precipitation within 6 miles" (5) |
| 9 | SKC | **4 of 8** | "Standard Knots Crosswind" (3) |
| 3, 6, 11, 16, 17 | Spread and fog, air masses, windshear, microburst, mechanical turbulence | 5 of 8 each | Microburst confused with anticyclone (3) |

**Everyone got:** surface heating (Q5), warm fronts (Q8), the tailwind return trip (Q12).

### Three patterns

1. **Thin air is backwards in their heads (Q4, Q19).** Most think hot and humid air is thicker. It is
   thinner, which means less lift
2. **High pressure = clear and stable, low = storms (Q2, Q16).** Several mixed up "anticyclone" (a high)
   with "microburst" (a downdraft)
3. **They guess abbreviations from the letters (Q9, Q18).** "P6SM" became "precipitation." Decode,
   do not guess

### Two problems in the quiz's own key

- **Q18 says P6SM appears in a METAR.** It is a **TAF** code. U.S. METARs top out at **10SM**
- **Q18's correct answer says "unlimited."** P6SM means **more than 6 statute miles**, not unlimited

**Neither is a reason to change grades**, but fix both if you reuse the quiz. The review bank and the
slides use the correct versions.

| Minutes | What |
|---|---|
| **0 to 2** | Slide 1, standards and agenda. Slide 2: results, no names |
| **2 to 10** | **Three big ideas**, one slide each, two to three minutes apiece. Ask before you tell: "Hot day or cold day, which air is thicker?" |
| **10 to 13** | **Quick check:** five questions on one slide. Hands or whiteboards, everyone answers before you reveal |

> the nitrogen and oxygen it replaces." Then a box: "Thicker air is better for flying: cold, low, dry."
> One slide.

### Slide 4: Big idea 2, weather systems

> Create one slide titled "Highs, Lows, and Storms." Two columns. Left, headed "HIGH pressure

> air. Clouds and storms." Below, headed "Storms": "Mature stage is the most dangerous: updrafts and
> downdrafts side by side." "Microburst: a column of air slamming DOWN and spreading out. Not the same
> as an anticyclone." "Stay 20 miles from a thunderstorm." One slide.

### Slide 5: Big idea 3, decode, do not guess
