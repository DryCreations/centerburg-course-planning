## Why four reports that tell one story

**They practiced decoding in Week 5 and learned fronts and air masses last week, but never together.**
Read in order, these four reports show a cold front arrive, pass over and leave: warm humid southerly air
Monday, a thunderstorm and falling pressure Tuesday, a wind shift with cold dry gusty air Wednesday, and
calm high pressure Thursday. **The decoding stops being an exercise and becomes reading a story.**

**The practice reports are written, not real.** The sheet says so up front. Question 15 is the only
real-world call, and it is the one that decides Thursday's flight.

## Answer key

| Q | Answer |
|---|---|
| **1** | From 190 degrees at 11 knots, no gust. 10 statute miles. **Ceiling 25,000 ft** (BKN250; SCT045 is not a ceiling). 24 and 19, a **5 degree spread** |
| **2** | **Warm and moist air from the Gulf.** Southerly wind, warm, high dew point |
| **3** | **From 8 p.m. Monday:** southwest 16 gusting 26, 5 miles, light rain showers, ceiling 3,000. **From 2 p.m. Tuesday:** northwest 14 gusting 24, over 6 miles, scattered at 4,000, so no ceiling |
| **4** | **A cold front.** The wind shift to northwest, the line of showers and the gusts. Expect a short rough spell Tuesday, then cooler, gusty and clearing |
| **5** | **Go** against typical limits: 11 knots with no gust, 10 miles, no low ceiling. Accept any deciding number that is correct against **their own** limits; the wind is the closest to most limits |
| **6** | 220 at 17 gusting 28, 4 miles, **light thunderstorm with rain**, ceiling 1,800, overcast 3,500, 21 and 20, 29.79 |
| **7** | **No-go. The thunderstorm decides it on its own:** a thunderstorm reported at the station is inside 20 miles by definition. The gust of 28 also fails most limits. **Visibility 4 passes**, since Part 107 needs 3 |
| **8** | **The air is nearly saturated.** Rain, low cloud, and possibly mist or fog |
| **9** | **Lower.** Falling pressure means a **low** is arriving or overhead, and lows mean weather |
| **10** | Any three: wind shifted **southwest to northwest**; temperature fell **21 to 12**; dew point fell **20 to 3**; pressure rose **29.79 to 30.06**; the sky **cleared** |
| **11** | **Most likely no-go on the gust of 26.** Clear skies behind a cold front are often gusty, which is last week's "clears to cool and gusty." Part 107 sets no wind limit, so **their own written limit decides**: accept go only if their limit is above 26 and they say so |
| **12** | **Cold, dry air from Canada.** The dew point crash is the giveaway |
| **13** | **High pressure: sinking air kills clouds**, and the wind is light. Last week's "a high means a flyable day" |
| **14** | **Better.** Cold, dry air is dense, so the props have more to push against. Monday's warm humid air was thinner, which is higher density altitude |
| **15** | The real METAR. **Grade the reasoning, not the weather** |

**Question 11 is the one worth discussing out loud.** The sky is perfect and it is still a likely no-go,
which is exactly the trap a clear day sets.
