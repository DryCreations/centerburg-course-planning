# Week 9 Teacher Notes: Aviation UAS

**Teacher-only.** Student docs are `outline.md` and `handouts/no-go-hunt.md`.

**The point of the week:** good local weather has trained them to say "it's fine, let's go." **They spend
the week hunting real reports for reasons to cancel**, on the real tools, so the habit becomes running
the list rather than glancing at the sky.

## Monday: tour the map first, on the projector

**Five minutes, before they touch it.** aviationweather.gov, then the **GFA** observation map:

- **The dot colors:** green VFR, blue MVFR, **red IFR, purple LIFR.** Red and purple are where to hunt
- **Click a dot** to get the METAR. Zoom in to see more stations
- **The data page** takes a station ID and returns the METAR and TAF as text

**The site changes its layout often**, and the GFA observation page is marked experimental. **Click
through it yourself before class.** If something moved, the map and the text lookup both still exist.

## What they will actually find in mid-October

| Category | Where it usually is |
|---|---|
| **Gusts** | Plains, mountain passes, the Great Lakes behind any front |
| **Low cloud and poor visibility** | Pacific Northwest coast, Gulf coast mornings, **Alaska nearly always** |
| **Precipitation** | Pacific Northwest, wherever a front is. **Snow:** Alaska and the high Rockies |
| **Thunderstorms** | Florida and the Gulf coast in the afternoon. **Some days there will be none** |
| **Small spread** | Coastal stations in the morning, anywhere it rained overnight |
| **TFRs** | **Always some.** Sporting events, VIP movement, wildfires, space launches |

**Thunderstorms are the one most likely to be missing.** If a student genuinely cannot find one anywhere,
they write **where they looked** and use a backup report from the bottom of this page. **Looking
carefully and finding none is a legitimate result.** Inventing one is not.

## The rules they lean on, stated exactly

- **Visibility:** Part 107 requires **3 statute miles** from the control station
- **Clouds:** Part 107 requires **500 feet below** and **2,000 feet horizontally** from clouds. So the
  lowest layer minus 500 is the most altitude they have. **A layer at 500 or lower means no flight.** The
  sheet says "lowest layer," not "ceiling," on purpose: a FEW or SCT layer is still a cloud
- **Thunderstorms:** the standard guidance is **20 miles.** `VCTS` (vicinity) is a no-go
- **Wind:** Part 107 sets **no wind limit.** Their own written limit from last week decides
- **TFRs:** **no flight inside one**, regardless of weather

## What a correct answer looks like

| Q | Correct if |
|---|---|
| **1** | The gust group is named and it exceeds **their own** limit |
| **2** | They name the deciding number: a layer height or a visibility. **Both, if both fail** |
| **3** | **Lowest layer minus 500.** At 500 or lower, "cannot fly" |
| **4** | **Water and electronics**, and visibility is not the reason. Precipitation alone cancels |
| **5** | **20 miles**, and they name TS or VCTS |
| **6** | **Fog or low cloud is likely to form**, so a clear sky now can close in during the flight |
| **7** | They find the FM or TEMPO group and convert its Zulu time to a local landing time. **Ohio is UTC minus 4 right now** |
| **8** | A real TFR, its reason, and **no, not inside it** |
| **9** | A mostly-green report with **exactly one** failing group, correctly named. **The best answers here are small spreads and gusts on otherwise clear days** |
| **10** | **Every row** gets a line. A sheet with only the obvious rows filled in has missed the point |
| **11** | A real check, even if the answer is "nothing near us" |
| **12** | A verdict **and** the number. "Go, every row passed" is a complete answer |

**Question 10 is the week.** On a nice day it is tempting to fill in three rows and write "go."

## Backup reports, only if a category truly cannot be found

**Written for the lesson, not real.** Use only when a student has documented a real search and come up
empty. Each is labeled with what it shows.

| Shows | Report |
|---|---|
| **A cold front arriving** (TAF) | `KCMH 121120Z 1212/1318 19010KT P6SM SCT045 BKN250 FM130000 21016G26KT 5SM -SHRA BKN030 FM131800 31014G24KT P6SM SCT040` |
| **Thunderstorm, gusts, 1 degree spread** | `KCMH 131351Z 22017G28KT 4SM -TSRA BKN018 OVC035 21/20 A2979` |
| **Clear sky, still a no-go on the gust** | `KCMH 141351Z 31016G26KT 10SM SCT035 12/03 A3006` |
| **Calm high pressure, a real go** | `KCMH 151351Z 34005KT 10SM CLR 09/01 A3024` |
| **Fog, cloud at 200 ft** (from Week 5) | `KTZR 150253Z VRB04KT 1/2SM FG OVC002 16/16 A2992` |
| **Freezing drizzle** | `KCMH 161351Z 03008KT 3SM -FZDZ BR OVC006 M01/M02 A3018` |

**The freezing drizzle report fails three ways:** precipitation, freezing, and a layer at 600 feet that
leaves only 100 feet of legal altitude. Good one for question 9 discussion as a contrast: nothing about it
looks like a nice day.
