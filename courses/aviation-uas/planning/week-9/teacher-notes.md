# Week 9 Teacher Notes: Aviation UAS

**Teacher-only.** Student doc: `outline.md`. **Your mission worksheet is not in the repo yet.** Add it
when you can, and the standards in the outline can be matched to it.

**The assignment:** four missions, each with a client, a date, a time, and a latitude and longitude.
Students research airspace, TFRs, METARs and TAFs, decide whether the flight is allowed or what
restrictions apply, and draft an email to the client. Monday to Wednesday, Thursday too if it rains.

## The tools

- **aviationweather.gov:** the GFA map (click a dot for a METAR) and the data page (station ID for METAR
  and TAF)
- **tfr.faa.gov:** every current TFR
- **An airspace app or chart** (Aloft, B4UFLY, or the sectional): airspace class and LAANC availability
  at a coordinate

## Things to watch for

- **The TAF window.** A TAF covers about 24 to 30 hours. A mission on Wednesday researched on Monday
  has no TAF yet. The right answer is "check again closer to the date," not a guess
- **Zulu time.** METARs and TAFs are in UTC. **Ohio is UTC minus 4 right now**
- **Night.** If a mission time is after sunset, Part 107 allows it, but the drone needs anti-collision
  lights visible for 3 miles
- **Latitude and longitude.** Students should paste the coordinates into the tool rather than guessing
  the nearest town. Check that they found the right spot
- **Controlled airspace** is not a no. It means LAANC authorization first, often with a lower altitude
  limit at that grid square

## The rules they lean on

- **400 ft** above ground
- **3 statute miles** visibility
- **500 ft below** and **2,000 ft horizontally** from clouds
- **Thunderstorms:** 20 miles is the standard guidance
- **TFRs:** no flight inside one
- **Wind:** no Part 107 limit. Their own written limit decides

## A good client email

- States the decision in the first line: yes, no, or yes with conditions
- Gives the reason in plain words a client would understand
- Names what would have to change, or when to check again
- Short and polite. No METAR codes pasted in
