# FAA Remote Pilot Study Guide — Course Crosswalk

**Course:** Unmanned Aircraft (177024)
**FAA Document:** FAA-G-8082-22, *Remote Pilot – Small Unmanned Aircraft Systems Study Guide* (August 2016)
**Purpose:** This document maps every chapter of the FAA study guide to the course unit(s) where it is covered, notes the Ohio DOT module parallel, and identifies the WebXam standards addressed. Use this document when building or updating unit materials to ensure complete test coverage.

> The FAA Part 107 knowledge test and the Ohio WebXam share approximately 80% of content. Drilling Part 107 prep simultaneously sharpens WebXam readiness. Students who pass the WebXam will have most of the content foundation needed for the $175 Part 107 knowledge test at a PSI testing center.

## Chapter-by-Chapter Coverage Detail

### Chapter 1: Applicable Regulations

**FAA Test Coverage:** Must know all of 14 CFR Part 107 and AC 107-2.

**Key Topics:**
- Remote Pilot Certificate requirements (§107.61, §107.65)
- Registration requirements (§107.13)
- Operating rules: altitude (400 ft AGL), speed (100 mph), daylight, VLOS, no careless/reckless operation
- Operating limitations: altitude ceiling, visibility (3 SM min), cloud clearance
- Waiver process (§107.200)
- Crew roles: Remote PIC, person manipulating controls, visual observer
- Accident reporting (§107.9)
- Drug/alcohol prohibition (§107.27)
- Remote ID requirement

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 1.3](units/unit-1-3-regulations/) — Weeks 5–7
- vocab.md: Part 107 terminology, operating rules, waiver types
- lab.md: "Can You Fly Here?" scenario analysis (8 scenarios)
- project.md: Part 107 compliance analysis
- quiz.md: 15 MCQ styled after FAA knowledge test

**Review/Reinforcement:** [Unit 4.2](units/unit-4-2-part107-prep/) — Week 31–32
- Full-length practice exam integrating all Chapter 1 content
- Targeted remediation for regulations questions

### Chapter 2: Airspace Classification, Operating Requirements, and Flight Restrictions

**FAA Test Coverage:** Airspace classes A–G, special use airspace, other airspace areas, TFRs, NOTAMs, VFR chart symbols.

**Key Topics:**
- Class A (18,000 ft+, IFR only), Class B (busiest airports, wedding cake), Class C (radar approach), Class D (tower), Class E (controlled remainder), Class G (uncontrolled)
- Special Use Airspace: Prohibited, Restricted, Warning, MOA, Alert, CFA
- Other areas: TFR, NSA, MTR, ADIZ, FRZ, wildlife areas
- NOTAMs: types, sources, how to check (tfr.faa.gov)
- VFR sectional chart reading: airspace depictions, symbols, frequencies

**Primary Coverage:**
- [Unit 1.1](units/unit-1-1-aviation-industry/) — Airspace classes introduced (vocab table) | Week 2
- [Unit 1.3](units/unit-1-3-regulations/) — Airspace authorization requirements for sUAS | Weeks 5–7
- [Unit 2.2](units/unit-2-2-preflight/) — LAANC, TFRs, NOTAMs in preflight workflow | Weeks 13–15

**Sectional Chart Reading** is critical for the Part 107 test and appears in Unit 1.3 lab and Unit 2.2. Students practice decoding airspace symbols from FAA VFR sectional chart excerpts.

### Chapter 3a: Aviation Weather Sources

**FAA Test Coverage:** METAR decode, TAF, PIREP, SIGMET, AIRMET, winds aloft forecast (FB), area forecast (FA).

**Key Topics:**
- METAR: all 12 elements (station, time, modifier, wind, visibility, weather, sky, temp/dew point, altimeter, Zulu, remarks)
- TAF: valid periods, TEMPO/BECMG/FM groups
- SIGMET vs. AIRMET (Tango/Zulu/Sierra)
- Winds aloft forecast (FB): direction/speed at altitude
- Convective SIGMET (WST)
- aviationweather.gov as the primary resource

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 2.1](units/unit-2-1-meteorology/) — Weeks 10–12
- vocab.md: METAR, TAF, SIGMET, AIRMET, and all weather products ✓ complete
- quiz.md: 15 MCQ with METAR decode questions ✓ complete

### Chapter 3b: Effects of Weather on sUAS Performance

**FAA Test Coverage:** Density altitude, atmospheric stability, temperature inversions, wind shear, microbursts, thunderstorm life cycle, clouds, icing, structural icing, mountain wave, fronts.

**Key Topics:**
- Density altitude = pressure altitude corrected for temperature; hot + humid + high = less lift
- Temperature inversion: stable air layer traps haze/fog
- Low-level wind shear: silent danger, fronts and thunderstorms
- Thunderstorm life cycle: cumulus → mature → dissipating
- Cumulonimbus clouds: 3,000 fpm updrafts/downdrafts
- Structural icing: frost on propellers = reduced lift
- Mountain wave turbulence

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 2.1](units/unit-2-1-meteorology/) — Weeks 10–12
- Density altitude, wind shear, microbursts, thunderstorms in vocab.md ✓ complete
- Performance implications integrated in quiz.md questions ✓ complete

### Chapter 4: Small Unmanned Aircraft Loading

**FAA Test Coverage:** Weight and balance, center of gravity (CG), load factors, stability.

**Key Topics:**
- W&B: total weight vs. maximum gross takeoff weight
- Center of gravity (CG): forward vs. aft CG effects on stability
- Load factor: G-forces; load factor increases in turns
- Effect of CG shift on control response
- Manufacturers' limits must be followed
- Density altitude effects on payload capacity

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 1.2](units/unit-1-2-aerodynamics/) — Weeks 3–4
- Weight and balance, CG, load factors integrated in aerodynamics unit

**Secondary:** [Unit 2.2](units/unit-2-2-preflight/) — Weeks 13–15
- Preflight checklist includes W&B verification
- Performance calculations using manufacturer data

### Chapter 5: Emergency Procedures

**FAA Test Coverage:** In-flight emergency actions, required reporting (§107.9), crew briefings, battery fires.

**Key Topics:**
- Inflight emergency authority: may deviate from regulations if necessary for safety
- Report to FAA upon request (§107.9) — not all emergencies require pro-active reporting
- Exception: always report if deviation from Part 107 occurs
- Battery fire in flight: climb above 400 ft AGL to locate safe landing area
- Crew briefing requirements before flight: all VOs and non-certificated controllers must be briefed on emergency procedures

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 3.3](units/unit-3-3-emergency/) — Weeks 26–27
- Emergency Procedures are the primary focus of this unit
- Scenarios: GPS loss, control link failure, battery failure, fly-away, weather deterioration

**Preview:** [Unit 1.4](units/unit-1-4-safety-bpa/) — Week 9
- Battery LiPo safety protocol introduced during hardware orientation
- Emergency response protocols (RTH, Emergency Stop) introduced

### Chapter 7: Radio Communication Procedures

**FAA Test Coverage:** Phonetic alphabet, CTAF, UNICOM, MULTICOM, traffic advisory broadcasts at non-towered airports, aircraft call signs.

**Key Topics:**
- ICAO phonetic alphabet (Alpha through Zulu)
- Remote pilots are NOT required to transmit but should monitor frequencies near airports
- CTAF (Common Traffic Advisory Frequency): used at non-towered airports
- UNICOM (122.8 typical), MULTICOM (122.9 for airports without FSS/UNICOM)
- Standard position broadcasts: 10-mile inbound, entering pattern, base, final, clear of runway
- Aircraft call signs: N-number pronunciation, manufacturer name in call sign
- Only transmit on aviation frequency in an emergency

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 3.2](units/unit-3-2-flight-ops/) — Weeks 22–25
- Phonetic alphabet practice
- Monitoring CTAF during flight operations near Centerburg Airport

**Standards:** 7.7.6 (VHF/UHF radio bands), 7.7.7 (radio communication, phraseology, light signals)

### Chapter 8: Determining the Performance of Small Unmanned Aircraft

**FAA Test Coverage:** Effect of temperature and humidity on air density; using manufacturer performance data; density altitude effects on performance.

**Key Topics:**
- Air density decreases as temperature increases (same pressure)
- Moist air is less dense than dry air (water vapor is lighter)
- Humidity + temperature = density altitude calculation
- Use manufacturer data for performance; if unavailable, use data from similar platform operators
- Takeoff performance affected by: elevation, temperature, humidity, headwind/tailwind, surface type

**Primary Coverage:**
- [Unit 1.2](units/unit-1-2-aerodynamics/) — Density altitude and performance effects (Weeks 3–4)
- [Unit 2.2](units/unit-2-2-preflight/) — Performance calculations in preflight workflow (Weeks 13–15)
- [Unit 2.1](units/unit-2-1-meteorology/) — Weather effects on performance reinforced

### Chapter 9: Physiological Factors (Including Drugs and Alcohol)

**FAA Test Coverage:** IMSAFE checklist; hyperventilation; stress (acute/chronic); fatigue (acute/chronic/skill); dehydration; heatstroke; drug and alcohol regulations; vision and scanning.

**Key Topics:**
- **IMSAFE:** Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion
- **Drug/alcohol rules (§107.27):** No operation within 8 hours of alcohol; BAC < 0.04%; no impairing drugs
- **Hyperventilation:** excess CO₂ loss; symptoms; treatment (slow breathing, paper bag, talk aloud)
- **Fatigue:** acute vs. chronic vs. skill fatigue; only cure for acute is rest
- **Dehydration:** headache/dizziness; drink 2–4 qt/day; limit caffeine/alcohol
- **Heatstroke:** inability to regulate temperature; carry water even if not thirsty
- **Vision scanning:** scan right-to-left or left-to-right; 30° wide stops; 2–3 seconds per stop; 10° overlap

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 3.3](units/unit-3-3-emergency/) — Weeks 26–27
- IMSAFE self-check introduced as pre-flight protocol
- Drug/alcohol regulations linked to §107.27
- Fatigue and dehydration in context of long outdoor flight days

**Standards:** 7.8.1 (human factors in accidents), 7.8.2 (hazardous attitudes), 7.8.3 (aviation physiology)

### Chapter 10: Aeronautical Decision-Making and Judgment

**FAA Test Coverage:** ADM definition, history, risk management (PAVE/5P), hazardous attitudes (5 types + antidotes), DECIDE model, situational awareness, CRM/SRM.

**Key Topics:**
- **ADM:** systematic approach to best course of action in aviation
- **~80% of aviation accidents** related to human factors; most in takeoff/landing
- **5 Hazardous Attitudes:**
  - Anti-authority ("Don't tell me what to do") → *Follow the rules*
  - Impulsivity ("Do something now!") → *Not so fast, think first*
  - Invulnerability ("It won't happen to me") → *It could happen to me*
  - Macho ("I can do it") → *Taking chances is foolish*
  - Resignation ("What's the use?") → *I'm not helpless*
- **PAVE checklist:** Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External pressures
- **5P checklist:** Plan, Plane, Pilot, Passengers, Programming
- **DECIDE model:** Detect, Estimate, Choose, Identify, Do, Evaluate
- **Situational Awareness (SA):** perceive → comprehend → project; loss of SA precedes most accidents
- **Risk management:** IMSAFE + PAVE + go/no-go decision chain
- **CRM:** effective use of all available resources (human, hardware, information)

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 3.3](units/unit-3-3-emergency/) — Weeks 26–27
- All 5 hazardous attitudes + antidotes
- PAVE/DECIDE model applied to UAS scenarios
- Situational awareness discussion

**Secondary:** [Unit 3.2](units/unit-3-2-flight-ops/) — ADM in real-time flight context

**Standards:** 7.8.1, 7.8.6 (decision-making process), 7.8.9 (NTSB role in investigations)

### Chapter 11: Airport Operations

**FAA Test Coverage:** Types of airports, sources for airport data (Chart Supplement, sectional charts), latitude/longitude, antenna towers.

**Key Topics:**
- Types: towered vs. non-towered; controlled vs. uncontrolled; public-use vs. private
- Chart Supplement U.S. (formerly Airport/Facility Directory): runway info, frequencies, services
- Reading airport data on a sectional chart: symbol types, runway numbers, frequencies
- Latitude and longitude: meridians (lon, N/S) vs. parallels (lat, E/W)
- Antenna towers: depicted on sectional charts; must check prior to low-altitude flights near structures

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 2.2](units/unit-2-2-preflight/) — Weeks 13–15
- Airport types and chart reading in preflight planning workflow

**Standards:** 7.5.1–7.5.5 (airport types, towered/non-towered, features, runway markings, lighting, weather)

### Chapter 12: Maintenance and Preflight Inspection Procedures

**FAA Test Coverage:** FAA-accepted maintenance procedures, logbooks, airworthiness inspection, inspection checklists.

**Key Topics:**
- Remote PIC must ensure aircraft is in safe operating condition before each flight
- Inspection checklist: frame/arms, propellers (cracks, nicks, secure), battery (charge, swelling, connector), camera/gimbal, RC link, GPS status
- Manufacturer's maintenance schedule
- Logbook requirements: recording flights, maintenance, incidents
- Airworthiness standards: if doubt about airworthiness, do not fly
- Modifications: remote PIC remains responsible for airworthiness after any modification

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 2.2](units/unit-2-2-preflight/) — Weeks 13–15
- Pre-flight inspection checklist (DJI Mavic 3 specific + general Part 107 checklist)

**Preview:** [Unit 1.4](units/unit-1-4-safety-bpa/) — Week 9
- Hardware orientation: propeller inspection, battery safety, RC 2 pairing introduced

**Standards:** 7.11.2 (airworthiness), 7.11.13 (operation of systems), 7.11.14 (preflight assessment), 7.11.18 (before takeoff check)

### Appendix 2: Registration and Marking Requirements

**FAA Test Coverage:** When registration is required, how to register, required markings.

**Key Topics:**
- Registration required for all UAS 0.55 lbs – 55 lbs for commercial operations
- Exception: UAS under 0.55 lbs used recreationally (no registration for hobbyist below 0.55 lbs)
- Remote ID must be displayed during flight
- Marking: FAA registration number displayed externally on aircraft
- Registration via FAA DroneZone (faa.gov/uas)

**Primary Coverage:** [Unit 1.3](units/unit-1-3-regulations/) — Weeks 5–7
