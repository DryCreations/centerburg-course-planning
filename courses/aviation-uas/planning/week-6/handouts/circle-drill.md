# The Circle Drill

**Aviation UAS | Thursday Sep 24**

A box is four straight lines and three stops. You can fly a box by pushing one stick at a time.

**You cannot fly a circle one stick at a time.** A circle is two inputs at once, both of them changing,
the whole way around. That is the reason for this drill. It is not about circles.

## Warm-up: the box, once

**Every pilot flies one box pattern first**, exactly as on Tuesday's sheet. 15 feet, four corners, 5
second holds.

This is not busywork. It is the check that says you are in control today before you start combining
inputs. **If your box is sloppy, stay on the box.** There is no prize for moving on early and a sloppy
circle teaches you nothing.

---

## The drill

Set a cone, a bag, or a marker on the ground. **That is the center.** You are going to fly a circle
around it.

```
                    . - - - - .
                 .               .
               .                   .
              .         [X]         .        [X] = the marker
               .                   .
                 .               .
                    . - - - - .

                        ^
                    you, here, outside the circle
```

| | |
|---|---|
| **Altitude** | **15 feet.** Same the whole way around |
| **Radius** | About **20 feet** from the marker |
| **Nose** | **Pointed the same direction the entire circle.** Do not yaw. Not yet |
| **Speed** | Slow. Slower than you think. A slow round circle beats a fast oval |
| **Direction** | One full lap each way. Clockwise, then counterclockwise |

### Why the nose stays fixed

With the nose locked in one direction, flying a circle means **continuously mixing right stick left/right
with right stick forward/back**, and the mix changes every second. At the near side you are sliding
sideways. At the far side you are sliding the other way. Between them you are doing both at once.

**That mixing is the skill.** Locking the yaw is what forces you to learn it instead of steering with the
nose.

---

## What is actually being judged

Three things. All of them are about smoothness, none of them are about speed.

| | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| **Round** | It is a circle, not an oval and not a square with soft corners. Watch the gap to the marker: it should not change |
| **Level** | The altitude at the far side is the altitude at the near side. Drifting down as you go away is the most common miss |
| **Continuous** | No stopping and restarting. A circle flown as eight little moves is eight little moves |

> **A slow, round, level circle is a pass. A fast lopsided one is not.** Nobody is timing you.

---

## The thing that will get you: orientation

When the drone is facing **away** from you, left stick is left, right is right. Everything matches.

When it comes around and faces **toward** you, **your left is its right.** The stick that moved it away
from the marker now moves it toward the marker.

**This is the single hardest part of flying a drone and it is why we are doing this.**

| What to do about it | |
|---|---|
| **Slow down before the far side** | Mistakes at walking speed are recoverable |
| **Make small inputs** | If you have to fix it, you overcorrected |
| **Pick a reference and say it out loud** | "Its nose is pointed at the fence, so its right is my left" |
| **When you lose it, stop** | Let go, let it hover, look at the nose, figure out the orientation, then move again |

**Letting go and hovering is a correct response.** It is not giving up. Panicking and pushing a stick is
what puts a drone in a tree.

---

## Progression

Go in order. **Do not skip ahead, and do not start at step 4 because it looks more impressive.**

| Step | What |
|---|---|
| **1** | One box pattern, clean. The gate |
| **2** | **Half circle**, nose fixed, near side only. Get the mixing feel |
| **3** | **Full circle clockwise**, nose fixed, 15 feet, steady |
| **4** | **Full circle counterclockwise.** It will feel worse than clockwise. Everyone has a good direction and a bad one |
| **5** | **Both directions without landing between them** |
| **6** | **Nose-in circle:** yaw so the nose stays pointed at the marker the whole way. Now you are mixing three inputs. This is real |

**Most people will get to 3 or 4 today. That is a fine day.** Step 6 is genuinely hard and nobody should
attempt it until 5 is comfortable.

You are not standing around. Pick one:

  no risk
- **Study.** The quiz is tomorrow. Bring the readiness check and the regulations practice set out with
  you

## Why this drill and not something more interesting

Because a circle is the smallest thing that forces you to stop thinking in single stick movements.

Everything you will actually be asked to do, an orbit around a building, following a subject, a smooth
reveal, is this. **Photographers and inspectors fly orbits constantly.** The circle is not a warm-up for
the real skill. It is the real skill, at 15 feet with nothing to hit.

And the tests you take at the end of this are about rules, not stick work. **This part is not on the
quiz. It is the part that makes you a pilot.**
