# Coordinate Bell Ringers

**Aviation UAS, Week 6. Teacher reference.** A short daily warm-up: a coordinate goes on the board, students
find it on SkyVector, and report the airspace over that spot.

**Five minutes.** It is a warm-up, not a lesson.

---

## The routine

**On the board:**

```
BELL RINGER

  39.9980 N,  -82.8919 W

1. Find it on SkyVector
2. What airspace class are you in at ground level?
3. What is its FLOOR and its CEILING?

Finished? Do every layer above that spot, floor and ceiling for each.
```

**That is the whole thing.** The extension is what keeps the fast students busy without a second task.

---

## Finding a coordinate on SkyVector

Walk this once on Monday, then never again.

1. Go to **skyvector.com**
2. The **search box** is at the top
3. Paste the coordinate and press enter
4. The map jumps there. **Zoom in** until you can read the airspace lines and numbers

**If the search box will not take the format**, two fallbacks that always work:

- **Use the airport identifier instead.** `KCMH` jumps straight there
- **Pan manually** to the area, which is slower but fine for a warm-up

> **Try your format before class.** Coordinate entry is the one step that can eat the whole five minutes if
> it does not behave.

---

## What a complete answer looks like

For a spot under a Class C shelf:

| Layer | Floor | Ceiling |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Class G | Surface | 700 ft AGL |
| Class E | 700 ft AGL | 4,000 ft MSL |
| Class C shelf | 4,000 ft MSL | 7,000 ft MSL |

**The answer to question 2 is Class G**, because that is what you are standing in. Everything above it is the
extension.

**Push for "how did you know."** Which color, which line style, which numbers. A right answer with no reason
is a guess that landed.

**Build the week in difficulty order:** start in Class G where the answer is simple, then a Class D ring, then
somewhere under a Class C shelf where there are several layers stacked.

---

## Five that build

| Day | Aim for | Why |
|-----|---------|-----|
| 1 | **Class G**, nothing overhead | Confidence. The answer is short and correct |
| 2 | Inside a **Class D** ring | One ring, surface to a ceiling. Introduces reading the number |
| 3 | Under a **Class C** shelf | Now there are layers, and the extension question has teeth |
| 4 | Near a **Class B** | The wedding cake. Being under it is the whole point |
| 5 | Their choice: **they pick a coordinate** and trade with a partner | They have to know the answer to write the question |

**Day 5 is the best one.** A student who can build a good question understands the map.

---

## Grading

**Do not grade these.** They are warm-ups, and grading them turns five minutes into fifteen.

Take answers out loud, correct the reasoning, move on. **What you are listening for is the "how did you
know," not the class letter.**

---

## The recurring corrections

| They say | The correction |
|----------|----------------|
| The class of the airspace **above** them | Question 2 is what you are standing in. Work up from the ground |
| A ceiling in AGL when the chart says MSL | Chart numbers on rings are **MSL** unless marked otherwise |
| "Class E" for anything magenta | Only a **dashed** magenta ring is Class E at the surface. A faded band starts at 700 |
| Reading 25 as 25 feet | Hundreds of feet. **25 means 2,500** |
| Forgetting Class G exists | If nothing is drawn over you, you are in G, and that is a real answer |
