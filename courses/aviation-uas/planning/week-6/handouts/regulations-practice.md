# Regulations Practice: On Your Own

**Aviation UAS | Week 6** | Optional. Not collected, not graded.

Module 1 covers the rules, and the rules are the part of Part 107 you can actually study for. **This is
extra reps if you want them.** Answers are at the bottom. Cover them, work the whole set, then check.

Do not read the answers first. Getting one wrong now is free.

---

## Part A: Limits and numbers

1. Maximum groundspeed for a small UAS under Part 107?
2. Maximum altitude, and the one exception?
3. Minimum flight visibility?
4. Minimum distance from clouds, below and horizontally?
5. At what weight must a UAS be registered?
6. How long is a remote pilot certificate valid before you must complete recurrent training?
7. What is the minimum age to hold a remote pilot certificate?

## Part B: When you can and cannot fly

8. Can you fly at night under Part 107?
9. Can you fly over people who are not part of your operation?
10. Can you fly from a moving vehicle?
11. Can one person operate two aircraft at the same time?
12. Can you fly beyond visual line of sight if you have a spotter with binoculars?
13. Can you carry cargo for compensation?

## Part C: Airspace and authorization

14. You want to fly in Class D. What do you need first?
15. What is LAANC and what does it actually give you?
16. What does a LAANC grid ceiling of 100 mean for that square?
17. Do you need authorization to fly in Class G?
18. A temporary flight restriction is in effect over your site. May you fly?

## Part D: Responsibility and paperwork

19. Who is ultimately responsible for whether a flight is safe and legal?
20. Can you fly if you are taking a medication that makes you drowsy?
21. How soon must a serious injury or significant property damage be reported to the FAA?
22. What is the difference between TRUST and a Part 107 certificate?
23. Can you deviate from a Part 107 rule in an in-flight emergency?

---

## Answers

**Part A**

1. **100 miles per hour** (87 knots)
2. **400 feet AGL**, unless you are within **400 feet of a structure**, in which case you may fly up to
   400 feet above that structure's uppermost limit
3. **3 statute miles**
4. **500 feet below** and **2,000 feet horizontally**
5. **0.55 pounds** (250 grams) and above
6. **24 calendar months**
7. **16 years old**

**Part B**

8. **Yes**, with anti-collision lighting visible for 3 statute miles, and the night training completed
9. **Only under the Operations Over People categories**, based on the aircraft's category and weight.
   Not by default
10. **Yes in a sparsely populated area.** Not over a densely populated one
11. **No.** One remote pilot, one aircraft at a time
12. **No.** Visual line of sight means unaided vision, other than corrective lenses. Binoculars do not
    count
13. **Yes**, within the weight limit and not across state lines under Part 107
    
**Part C**

14. **Prior authorization from ATC**
15. **Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability.** Near real-time authorization to fly in
    controlled airspace up to the published grid ceiling
16. You can be approved to fly up to **100 feet AGL** in that square without a further request
17. **No.** Class G is uncontrolled, though every other Part 107 rule still applies
18. **No**, unless you hold specific authorization to operate in it

**Part D**

19. **The remote pilot in command.** Always. Not the person who owns the drone, not the client
20. **No.** You may not operate under the influence of anything, including medication, that affects
    safety
21. **Within 10 calendar days**, for a serious injury, loss of consciousness, or property damage of at
    least $500 to repair or replace, other than the aircraft
22. **TRUST** is the free recreational safety test that lets you fly for fun. **Part 107** is the
    certificate that lets you fly for work or any non-recreational purpose. They are not
    interchangeable
23. **Yes.** In an in-flight emergency you may deviate as needed to meet it, and you must send a written
    report to the FAA if asked

## What to do with the ones you missed

Go back to the module section that covers it. **Do not just read the answer and move on.** The test asks
the same rule in different clothes, so the goal is to be able to state the rule cold, not to recognize
one sentence about it.

The numbers in Part A are worth memorizing outright. There are seven of them and they never change.
