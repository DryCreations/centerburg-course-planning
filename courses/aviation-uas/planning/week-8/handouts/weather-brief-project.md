# Section 1 (Monday): The site and the air

**Pick the site we fly from.** You are briefing this week, for real.

**1.** Today's METAR for the nearest reporting station. Paste it, then pull out: **wind, visibility,
ceiling, temperature, dew point, pressure.**

**2.** Temperature and dew point close together means the air is near saturation. **How close are they
today, and what does that tell you?**

### Why the air is doing anything at all

**3.** Warm air rises because it is less dense. **What has to happen to the air around it?** In one
sentence, what is wind?

**4.** Our site has pavement, grass, and buildings. **Which surfaces heat fastest?** Mark them on a rough
sketch of the site, on paper, and attach a photo.

**5.** Based on that sketch, **where over our site would you expect bumpy air on a sunny afternoon?**

### Density altitude

**Three things thin the air: heat, humidity, altitude.** Thin air gives your propellers less to push
against.

**6.** Using today's temperature and dew point, is the air thicker or thinner than a standard cool dry
day? **How do you know?**

**7.** Last week you learned a payload eats your margin. **Explain how today's air does the same thing**,
and say what you did not get to choose about it.

**8. Section 1 verdict.** In two sentences: is today's air working for you or against you, and why?

# Section 2 (Tuesday): The system moving through

**9.** Find the TAF for the nearest station. Paste it. **What does it say is changing, and when?**

**10.** Look at a weather map. **Is there a front near us?** Which kind, and which direction is it
moving?

### What each front brings

Fill this in as we cover it.

**11. Cold front:** what is moving, what the weather does, how fast it passes.

**12. Warm front:** the same three.

**13. Stationary front:** what is happening, and what it means for your week.

**14. Occluded front:** what has happened here.

**15.** Which front brings short violent weather? Which brings long dreary weather?

### Air masses

**16.** An air mass takes on the character of the ground it sat over. **Where did the air over us come
from**, and what does that explain about today?

**17.** Wind patterns come from pressure systems. **Which way does air circulate around a low? Around a
high?**

**18. Section 2 verdict.** In two sentences: what is coming, and does your flight window need to move?

# Section 3 (Wednesday): The hazard list

**This section is a list of things that could stop you.** Be specific and use numbers.

### Wind

**19. Crosswind.** What does the aircraft have to do to hold a straight line, and what does that cost
you?

**20. Tailwind.** Why is a tailwind on the way out a problem on the way back?

**21. Windshear.** A sudden change in wind speed or direction over a short distance. **Why is that worse
than steady strong wind?**

**22.** You fly beside the gym. Wind is steady at 10 mph. Coming around the corner the aircraft lurches.
**What happened, and what is that called?**

**23. Mountain wave.** Air flowing over a ridge makes waves downwind, with sinking air behind. **We have
no mountains. Where does a smaller version of this happen at our site?**

**24.** Your aircraft does 100 mph. Wind is 25 mph. **Why is that still a problem?**

### Turbulence

**25.** The four causes are **mechanical, thermal, frontal, windshear.** One line of example each, **at
our site**.

**26.** Which one do you get near a building? Which over pavement on a hot afternoon?

### Severe weather

**27.** Thunderstorm stages are **cumulus, mature, dissipating.** What is the air doing in each, and
which is most dangerous?

**28.** A thunderstorm is 15 miles out and you are ready to launch. **Is that far enough? Defend it.**

**29. Microburst.** A column of rapidly sinking air that spreads out violently at the ground. **Why is
this the worst item on your list for a small aircraft?** What might warn you one is near?

**30. Section 3 verdict: your go or no-go limits.** Write the actual numbers. Wind speed, gust,
visibility, ceiling, temperature, and anything else you will not fly past. **This is the list you bring
outside tomorrow.**

## What a finished brief looks like

Someone who has never seen our site should be able to read your four sections and know **whether to fly
today and why not.**

- Real numbers from real reports, not "it was windy"
- Your site specifically, not weather in general
- **A go or no-go call with the deciding number named**
- Honest about what you got wrong on Thursday

## Words

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Air mass** | A large body of air with consistent temperature and moisture |
| **Front** | The boundary where two air masses meet |
| **Dew point** | The temperature at which the air would saturate. Close to temperature means moist air |
| **Density altitude** | How thin the air behaves. Heat, humidity and altitude all raise it |
| **Crosswind** | Wind across your direction of travel |
| **Tailwind / headwind** | Wind from behind you / into your face |
| **Windshear** | A sudden change in wind speed or direction over a short distance |
| **Mountain wave** | Waves downwind of a ridge, with sinking air behind it |
| **Mechanical turbulence** | Air broken up by obstacles: buildings, trees, terrain |
| **Thermal turbulence** | Rising columns of air from unevenly heated ground |
| **Microburst** | A violent column of sinking air that spreads out at the surface |
| **Cumulus / mature / dissipating** | The three stages of a thunderstorm |
| **Personal minimums** | The limits you set for yourself, tighter than the legal ones |

## Grading

**On the four verdicts and the hazard numbers**, not on the definitions.

Questions 7, 8, 18, 30, 32 and 34 are the ones that show whether you can brief a flight rather than
recite weather facts. **Question 34 is worth as much as any of them**: a brief that admits what it missed
is more useful than one that pretends it was right.
