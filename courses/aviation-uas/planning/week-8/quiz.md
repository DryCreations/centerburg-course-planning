## The questions that tell you the most

- **"Faded magenta band, flying at 300 feet AGL, you are in:"** Class G, because that Class E starts at
  700 feet. **This is the single most useful chart fact for a drone pilot**: most of Ohio sits under a
  faded magenta band, and a student who thinks it means "controlled at the ground" will ask for
  authorization they do not need
- **"Class B shelf 80 over 30, ground at 1,000 MSL, drone at 400 AGL."** Below the shelf, about 1,600
  feet under it. **It needs three facts at once**: the numbers are hundreds of feet, they are MSL, and you
  convert your AGL to MSL by adding the ground. Whoever gets this has the whole chart
- **"An obstruction reads 1910 over (470)."** Go around it. The tempting wrong answer is "under 500, fly
  over," which is the confusion between the 400 foot limit and a round number
- **"When you push forward from a hover, the drone dips because:"** tilting turns part of the lift into
  thrust. The tempting wrong answer has the **back motors slowing down**, which is backwards: the back
  speeds up

## How the answer options were checked

- **One objectively correct answer**, each checked against what the Week 5 to 7 handouts taught
- **Option lengths are balanced.** The correct answer is the single longest option in 6 of 30, and no
  correct answer is more than 4 characters longer than every distractor
- **Every distractor is a real mistake** students make: AGL for MSL, counting from the wrong number, the
  400 versus 500 confusion, the back motors going the wrong way

## One thing in an earlier handout worth knowing

**The Week 5 `sectional-reading.md` handout describes the large grid-square number as magenta.** On FAA
sectionals that number, the Maximum Elevation Figure, is printed in **blue**. The quiz question avoids
color entirely and asks only what the number means, so nothing on the quiz depends on it. **That handout
is already out to students, so it was not changed**, but it is worth a sentence in class next time it
comes up.

## Alignment with ODOT

This bank was written against the class handouts, not the ODOT module transcripts. **When you run it
past the transcripts**, the places most likely to need a wording change are the airport text block and
the grid-square number, since those are the two most likely to be phrased differently in the online
course.
