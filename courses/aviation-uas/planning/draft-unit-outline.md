## Design Principles

- **Two-semester structure:** S1 (Weeks 1–18) = foundations, regulations, aerodynamics, preflight; S2 (Weeks 19–37) = FAA Part 107 exam prep + DJI Mavic 3 hands-on flight operations
- Enrollment: juniors and seniors only; no prior flight experience required
- Quarter structure aligned to BPA Competition Season Calendar and WebXam testing window
- BPA primary events: **#555 Presentation Individual** and **#505 Entrepreneurship** (co-equal; students select one at Week 8–9 event selection)
- Aviation UAS is Transportation Technologies — no direct BPA aviation events exist; BPA draws on Strand 1 (Business/Leadership) applied to aviation content
- Every competency in the standards appears at least once
- At least 1 major project per quarter; BPA presentation project spans Q2–Q3
- EOC weight is heavily Strand 7 Aviation (~83%) — instructional time follows

### Daily Class Structure

Every class period follows the same rhythm:

| Phase | Time | Details |
|-------|------|---------|
| **Bell Ringer** | First 5–7 min | One question from the current unit's `quiz.md` (projected on the board). Students respond on paper or digitally. Teacher cold-calls 2–3 students; class discusses briefly. Mon–Thu rotate DOK 1–2 vocab/recall; DOK 3–4 scenario questions (styled after FAA Part 107 ACS format where applicable) after at least one day of instruction. |
| **Instruction / Demo** | 10–15 min *(as needed)* | Mini-lesson, live demo, or clarifying review. Skipped or shortened on heavy work days. |
| **Work Time** | 25–35 min | Students work on the current **lab** or **project**. There is always an active lab or project — no dead time. Students who finish early move to the extension task in the lab or project document. |
| **Wrap-Up** | Last 5 min | Teacher circulates for quick status checks; students update progress log or answer one exit question. |
| **End-of-Week Quiz** | Friday *(last 15–20 min)* | 10–20 MCQ from the unit's `quiz.md`. 70% DOK 1–2 (vocab/recall), 30% DOK 3–4 (analysis/application). Modeled after WebXam and FAA Part 107 knowledge test format — scenario-based questions, aeronautical chart references where applicable. |

> **Always a project:** Students always have a multi-week project in progress. When a lab is finished early, the next action is always to work on the current project. Extension tasks inside labs also point forward to the next unit.

### Per-Unit Resources

Each unit has a dedicated folder under `planning/units/` containing:

| File | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| `vocab.md` | 15–25 vocabulary terms with definitions, standard codes, and DOK levels |
| `quiz.md` | 10–20 MCQ end-of-week quiz; includes answer key, DOK rating, and standard reference per question. Questions are styled after FAA Part 107 ACS format in addition to WebXam MCQ style. |

### Unit 1.0: WebXam Pretest & Course Orientation (Week 1)

**Focus:** Establish baseline. Students take the WebXam pretest under test-like conditions.

**Activities:**

- Day 3: **WebXam Pretest** — full-length practice under timed conditions

This drives pacing — if 80% of students already grasp basic aerodynamics but score 0% on meteorology, time can shift accordingly.

### Unit 3.2: Flight Operations — Takeoff, Landing & Maneuvers (Weeks 22–25)

**Focus:** Hands-on flight with DJI Mavic 3 drones.
**FAA Study Guide:** Ch. 6 (Crew Resource Management — SRM, VO coordination, task management); Ch. 7 (Radio Communication — phonetic alphabet, CTAF, UNICOM 122.8, MULTICOM 122.9, call signs); Ch. 12 (Maintenance and Preflight — post-flight inspection, flight logs) Covers 7.12 and 7.13 (~15% of EOC combined). School has 6 DJI Mavic 3 units; students fly in supervised groups of 2–3 per aircraft. This is the flagship S2 experience.

**Hardware:** DJI Mavic 3 (fleet of 6) with DJI RC 2 controller (built-in touchscreen — no phone or additional app required). Students were introduced to the hardware in Unit 1.4 (Week 9).

**Pre-flight safety primer (Day 1, before first flight):** Before any aircraft leaves the ground, review immediate response protocols: *fly-away response* — activate Return-to-Home (RTH) via the RC 2 RTH button immediately; press Emergency Stop as last resort; *GPS loss* — aircraft drops to ATTI mode (no position hold), land immediately while maintaining visual contact, do not chase; *control link failure* — aircraft executes preset failsafe (RTH or land), call out the situation to the visual observer, do not chase the aircraft; *battery critical alert* — land immediately, no mission extension. All students sign a flight operations agreement before taking the controls for the first time.

**Competencies Covered:**
- 7.12.1 Describe takeoffs, landings, go-arounds
- 7.12.2 Demonstrate normal takeoff and climb
- 7.12.3 Demonstrate normal approach and landing
- 7.12.10 Postflight procedures
- 7.13.2 Right of way and minimum safe altitude rules
- 7.13.3 Performance and ground reference maneuvers
- 7.13.9 Slow flight and stalls
- 7.13.10 Maneuvering during slow flight
- 7.13.11 Power-on and power-off stalls
- 7.13.14 Straight-and-level flight
- 7.13.15 Constant airspeed climbs
- 7.13.16 Constant airspeed descents
- 7.13.18 Recovery from unusual flight attitudes
- 7.13.20 Night operations challenges
- 7.7.6 VHF and UHF radio bands
- 7.7.7 Radio communication, phraseology, light signals
- 7.8.1 Human factors in aircraft accidents
- 7.8.2 Hazardous attitudes of flight
- 7.8.3 Aviation physiology problems
- 7.8.6 Decision-making process and breaking chain of poor judgement
- 7.8.9 Role of NTSB in accident investigations

**Weeks 22–23 — Ground Reference Maneuvers & Basic Flight:**
- Pre-flight inspection routine (DJI Mavic 3): aircraft frame and arms, propeller condition, battery charge and seating, RC 2 signal link confirmation, airspace check via B4UFLY/DroneZone
- Simulator warm-up if available (DJI Flight Simulator); otherwise hover exercises in the approved open area
- Basic flight sequence: power on → hover at 5 ft → climb to safe altitude → stationary hover → slow translation in cardinal directions → land
- Ground reference maneuvers: fly a rectangular pattern over a defined ground reference; maintain position awareness
- Radio communication: each student calls positions during practice using standard phraseology (7.7.7)
- Human factors debrief after each session: was there any hazardous attitude present? Complacency? Task saturation?

**Weeks 24–25 — Maneuvers, Emergency Patterns & Aerial Photo/Video:**
- Pattern work: establish departure, crosswind, downwind, base, final approach; go-around procedure
- Maneuvering: slow flight, altitude holds, recovery from unusual orientations (7.13.9–11, 7.13.18)
- Introduction to DJI intelligent flight modes (ActiveTrack, Hyperlapse) as cinematic tools — used after manual skills are established
- **Aerial photo/video production:** students plan and execute a simple aerial shot sequence (establishing wide shot, orbit, reveal); review footage for composition, horizon level, stability; export as a deliverable (this footage also supports BPA #420 Digital Media Production entry)
- Post-flight analysis: review flight telemetry logs on the RC 2 display; discuss any anomalies or deviations

**Major Project — "Flight Operations Log & Analysis":**
Complete a supervised flight skills progression across Weeks 22–25. Maintain a flight log documenting: date/conditions, pre-flight check status, skills practiced, performance self-rating, post-flight observations. Submit log plus a brief final reflection (1 page) applying at least one human factors concept to a decision made during flight practice.

**Optional Deliverable — "Aerial Showcase Video" (BPA #420 track):**
Film a 60–90 second aerial video sequence at the approved flight site: minimum 5 distinct shots (wide establishing, orbit, tracking, reveal, landing approach). Edit in any video editor; add music and titles. May substitute for or supplement the BPA presentation event. Teams of 1–2.

**Tools:** **Felt** (browser-based collaborative mapping — web GIS, no install required); **QGIS** (desktop GIS for analysis and cartographic output); **WebODM** (processes drone imagery into orthomosaics and point clouds on iMac); **RealityScan** (mobile photogrammetry for image capture — *requires student personal devices, iOS or Android; this portion is optional for students without a personal device*).

**Week 28 — GIS Foundations & Felt Lab:**
- GIS concepts: map projections, coordinate systems, data structures (vector, raster, TIN), scale and accuracy; discuss how UAS-collected data fits each structure type (7.10.4, 7.10.5)
- Felt orientation lab: import the school campus and approved flight site boundary; add markers, layers, and annotations; share the collaborative map with the class; discuss UAS application scenarios (agriculture NDVI, construction site monitoring, emergency response mapping, infrastructure inspection)
- Introduce remote sensing (7.10.2) and GIS applications for UAS (7.10.3); GPS positioning fundamentals (7.10.10)

**Week 29 — WebODM Processing & QGIS Introduction:**
- WebODM workshop: load drone imagery from Unit 3.2 flight sessions (or teacher-provided sample dataset if weather or scheduling prevented outdoor flight); run the photogrammetry pipeline; generate an orthomosaic and point cloud; review the processing quality report; interpret resolution and coverage statistics
- Introduction to QGIS: load the WebODM orthomosaic as a raster layer; apply color symbology; measure distances and areas; overlay vector boundary layers; integrate GPS-tagged flight log data into GIS (7.10.11)
- Spatial interpolation concepts (7.10.7) and digital elevation methods — DEM, GPS, TIN (7.10.6) — in context of the WebODM output; discuss sources of error and correction strategies (7.10.9)

**Week 30 — Spatial Analysis & GIS Mission Presentations:**
- Complete the GIS Mission project: finalize spatial analyses in QGIS; export annotated map products; identify 3+ error sources and document solutions; add final deliverables to the class Felt collaborative map
- Student presentations (5 min each): present annotated map and findings; explain application scenario; address one error source and how it was or could be corrected; field questions from peers and teacher

**Major Project — "UAS GIS Mission":**
Plan and execute (or simulate) a UAS mapping mission using the approved tool pipeline: capture imagery with the DJI Mavic 3 (or use provided sample imagery set) → optionally capture supplemental close-range imagery with RealityScan on personal devices → process in WebODM to generate an orthomosaic or point cloud → analyze and visualize spatial data in QGIS (desktop) and Felt (collaborative web map). Identify error sources, assess data quality, and present findings with annotated map products. Real-world application scenario: agriculture survey, construction site progress, or environmental monitoring.

### Unit 4.2: FAA Part 107 Prep & WebXam Review (Weeks 31–32)

**Focus:** Full-length FAA Part 107 practice test as a WebXam review anchor. Significant content overlap between Part 107 and the aviation WebXam (~80% of topics match); drilling Part 107 prep simultaneously sharpens WebXam readiness.

**FAA Study Guide:** All Chapters 1–12 + Appendix 2 — comprehensive review unit. High-priority chapters by test frequency: Ch. 2 (Airspace), Ch. 3a (Weather Sources), Ch. 1 (Regulations), Ch. 10 (ADM), Ch. 12 (Preflight), Ch. 9 (Physiological), Ch. 4 (Loading), Ch. 11 (Airport Ops), Ch. 8 (Performance), Ch. 5 (Emergency), Ch. 7 (Radio), Ch. 6 (CRM).

**Weeks 31–32 — FAA Part 107 Practice & WebXam Review:**
- Administer a full-length FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot practice exam under timed conditions (Week 31); review results by topic area
- EOC review keyed to blueprint weights (prioritize 7.11 Preflight 15%, 7.13 Flight Environment 10%, 7.4 Aerodynamics 9%, 7.9 sUAS 8%)
- Use FAASTeam and SUAS News practice question banks; work through incorrect answers with class discussion
- Sectional chart reading drills: airspace classes, TFR interpretation, decode METAR/TAF (required for both Part 107 and WebXam)
- Compare practice exam scores to pretest baseline from Week 1 — identify remaining weak areas for targeted remediation

**End of Week 32 — Initial WebXam:**
- **⚑ WebXam Initial Test — administer by April 23**
- Makeups for absent students must happen within the same week to preserve the 30-day retake window
- After test: students receive preliminary scores

> **Part 107 Exam (optional, strongly encouraged):** Any student who demonstrates readiness (scoring 70%+ on both the Part 107 practice exam and the WebXam) is encouraged to register for the official FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Knowledge Test at a PSI testing center (~$175 fee). This is a genuine commercial pilot credential. Teacher can write a letter of recommendation for any student who chooses to attempt it.

### Unit 4.3: UAS Business Capstone + Remediation & Retake (Weeks 33–37)

**Focus:** Large summative project completed during the 30-day retake window.
**FAA Study Guide:** Applied review — students reference relevant chapters when building compliance manual (Ch. 1, 5), mission portfolio (Ch. 2, 3a, 12), and financial projections. No new primary content — synthesis unit. All students develop a comprehensive UAS business and mission portfolio; students who need to retake also receive embedded remediation.

**Competencies Covered:**
- 1.1.6 Work ethic, accountability
- 1.1.8 Emotions, behavior, appearance — professionalism
- 1.1.10 Coping skills for workplace demands
- 1.1.11 Cultural beliefs and practices
- 1.1.12 Healthy lifestyles
- 1.5.1–1.5.8 Cultural understanding, intelligence, intercultural communication
- 1.6.1 Business opportunities
- 1.6.5 Organizational structure, chain of command
- 1.6.9 Employee/department/organization performance assessment
- 1.6.11 Business activities within budget parameters
- 1.6.12 Employee benefits, rights, deductions, compensations
- 1.7.10 Techniques for experience (apprenticeship, co-op, internship, job shadowing)
- 1.8.4 Alternative actions when goals not met
- 1.8.8 Routine facility/equipment maintenance
- 1.8.10 Business and environmental management systems
- 1.10.4 Company policies for product/service improvements
- 1.10.5 Customer satisfaction measurement
- 1.10.8 Promotional techniques to maximize sales
- 1.10.9 Product mix for sales/market share/profit
- 1.10.10 Sales techniques
- 1.3.7 Labor laws affecting employment
- 7.10 GIS competencies (applied in mission portfolio)
- 7.11 Preflight competencies (applied in mission planning)
- 7.9 sUAS competencies (applied in regulatory compliance plan)

**Major Project — "UAS Drone Services Business Plan & Mission Portfolio" (Summative):**
Develop a complete business plan and mission portfolio for a drone services startup. Deliverables:
1. **Business plan** — company name, target market (agriculture, real estate, inspection, filmmaking), pricing model, equipment inventory, regulatory compliance strategy (Part 107, airspace authorization), marketing plan
2. **Sample mission portfolio** — 3 documented missions (real or simulated): preflight checklist, flight plan, weather briefing, flight log, GIS/data products, post-flight analysis
3. **Financial projections** — startup costs, operating budget, break-even analysis, pricing schedule
4. **Safety & compliance manual** — emergency procedures, maintenance schedule, crew roles, insurance requirements
5. **Pitch presentation** — present the complete package to class and/or BAC panel as a business pitch

> **BPA Option — #505 Entrepreneurship:** Students who declared this event during the Week 8–9 selection period use this capstone as their competition entry. Structure the business plan and pitch to match the WSAP Entrepreneurship scoring rubric. No separate project needed — this IS the BPA entry. The pitch presentation fulfills the live-to-judges requirement. See [bpa-events.md](bpa-events.md).

**Weekly Structure (Weeks 33–37):**

| Day | All Students | Retake Students (additional) |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Capstone project work | — |
| Tue | Capstone project work | 30 min targeted WebXam review (weak areas from initial test) |
| Wed | Capstone project work | — |
| Thu | Peer critique / check-in | 30 min targeted WebXam review |
| Fri | Capstone project work | — |

**Week-by-Week Milestones:**
- **Week 33:** Market research, company concept, target market analysis; document first 2 sample missions (preflight checklist, flight plan, weather brief)
- **Week 34:** Third mission portfolio entry (GIS data products + post-flight analysis); financial projections, pricing model, equipment list. **NLC May 6–10** — state qualifiers travel to Nashville
- **Week 35:** Safety manual, regulatory compliance documentation. NLC debrief.
- **Week 36:** Pitch presentation development, final revisions. **⚑ WebXam Retake — available 30 days after initial test (by May 23)**
- **Week 37:** Final business pitch presentations to class/BAC panel. Portfolio compilation (flight logs + GIS products + business plan). Course evaluation. Career reflection — pathways (Part 107, college aviation programs, military).

> **Retake policy note:** WebXam requires a minimum 30-day gap between attempts. Students who test April 23 are eligible to retake May 23. Students who test earlier can retake sooner. Schedule makeups promptly to preserve the full retake window before school ends May 27.
>
> **Differentiation:** Students who passed the WebXam on the first attempt spend full periods on the business capstone and may take on stretch goals (competitive analysis, investor deck, video marketing reel). Students who need to retake dedicate ~30 minutes twice per week to targeted review on their weakest blueprint areas (preflight, flight environment, aerodynamics, regulatory concepts), using the remaining time on the capstone.
