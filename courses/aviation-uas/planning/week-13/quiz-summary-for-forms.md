```
Create a multiple-choice quiz in Google Forms for a high school Unmanned Aircraft Systems class. 15 questions, 4 options each, one correct answer, marked in the answer key, 1 point each.

Rules for the questions:
- Test the content from ODOT Module 3 (UAS Operations): preflight, flight safety, emergencies, and postflight. No questions about BPA competitions.
- Write most questions as short situations ("Your drone loses GPS signal...") rather than definitions.
- Every question has one objectively correct answer, based on FAA Part 107 and standard UAS practice.
- Write all four options in the same form and about the same length. The correct answer should NOT usually be the longest option.
- No "all of the above" or "none of the above."
- Plain, short wording for high school students.
- Call the course and the aircraft by their real names: Unmanned Aircraft Systems, small unmanned aircraft (sUAS), remote pilot in command.

Mix: about 5 preflight, about 4 flight safety and human factors, about 3 emergencies, about 3 postflight.

PREFLIGHT (about 5 questions)
- Airworthiness: the aircraft is in condition for safe operation. The remote pilot in command (RPIC) must check it before every flight and makes the final go/no-go decision. Any doubt means no-go.
- Preflight checklist: propellers (no cracks or chips, tight), arms locked, body undamaged, battery charged and seated, gimbal cover off, controller charged, firmware current.
- Before takeoff check: GPS lock, home point recorded, Return to Home (RTH) altitude set above the tallest obstacle, area clear of people.
- Airspace: check B4UFLY; controlled airspace needs LAANC authorization; check NOTAMs and TFRs (Temporary Flight Restrictions, for example VIP visits, wildfires, stadium events).
- Weather and performance: hot, high, and humid air is thin (high density altitude), so less lift and shorter flights. Cold batteries lose capacity fast. Wind uses more battery, especially flying back into a headwind.

FLIGHT SAFETY AND HUMAN FACTORS (about 4 questions)
- Part 107 limits: 400 feet above ground level, 100 mph maximum, 3 statute miles visibility, stay 500 feet below and 2,000 feet horizontally from clouds.
- Visual line of sight: the RPIC or a visual observer must see the aircraft at all times with their own eyes.
- Yield right of way to all manned aircraft.
- Crew roles: RPIC (responsible, final authority), visual observer (watches the aircraft and airspace), person manipulating the controls.
- IMSAFE self-check: Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion (or Eating).
- Alcohol: no flying within 8 hours of drinking, and blood alcohol must be below 0.04.
- Hazardous attitudes and antidotes: anti-authority ("follow the rules"), impulsivity ("not so fast"), invulnerability ("it could happen to me"), macho ("taking chances is foolish"), resignation ("I'm not helpless").
- PAVE risk check: Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External pressures.

EMERGENCIES (about 3 questions)
- Lost link: the aircraft loses connection to the controller. Most aircraft Return to Home automatically, which is why RTH altitude is set before takeoff.
- Flyaway: the aircraft will not respond. Keep trying to regain control, note its last position and direction, and warn anyone in its path.
- Lost GPS: the aircraft switches to ATTI mode, holds altitude but drifts with the wind, and RTH may not work. The pilot flies it back manually.
- In an emergency, the RPIC may deviate from Part 107 rules as needed to respond.
- Accident reporting: report to the FAA within 10 days if there is serious injury, loss of consciousness, or more than $500 of damage to property other than the aircraft.

POSTFLIGHT (about 3 questions)
- Power down the aircraft before the controller.
- Postflight inspection: propellers, motors, arms, body, and gimbal for damage; motors warm but not hot.
- Batteries: let them cool before charging; a swollen or puffy LiPo battery is not used again and is disposed of properly; store batteries at storage charge, not full, if not flying soon.
- Flight log: date, location, pilot, aircraft, flight time, and any problems or maintenance needed.
```
