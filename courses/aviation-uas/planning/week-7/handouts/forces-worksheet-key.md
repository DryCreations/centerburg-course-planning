# Forces Worksheet: Key

**Aviation UAS | Unit 1.2** | **Teacher only**

**Runs Tuesday and Wednesday.** Parts 1 to 3 Tuesday, 4 to 6 Wednesday. Longer than one period on
purpose.

**Digital, make-a-copy. Drawings on paper**, photographed and attached.

---

## Part 1

**1.** Lift up, weight down, thrust in the direction of travel, drag opposite it.

**2.** Lift: props pushing air down. Weight: gravity on aircraft, battery, payload. Thrust: tilting so
part of the lift points sideways. Drag: air resistance.

**3.** Hover `=`, climb `>`, descend `<`.

**4.** The forces are equal and opposite, so they **cancel**. A hover is balance, not absence.
**Catch this one:** students write "no forces are acting on it."

**5.** Three diagrams, arrow lengths meaningful.

---

## Part 2

**6.** 1,000 g lift, weight 1,000 g. **Hovering.**

**7.** 1,200 g. **Climbing.**

**8.** New weight **1,200 g**. Each motor needs **300 g**. Spare is **100 g**, down from 150 g.

**9.** Weight costs you **margin**: the spare power to climb, fight wind, or recover from a mistake.
Accept any wording for reserve or headroom.

**This is the point of Part 2. Teach it as safety, not arithmetic.**

---

## Part 3

**10.** The props throw air **down**, the air pushes the aircraft **up**.

**11.** The blade is an airfoil. Air over the curved top moves faster, so lower pressure than
underneath. The difference makes lift.

**12.** They describe the **same event** from two directions. Newton accounts for the air's momentum,
Bernoulli for the pressure difference that moved it. **Students will want one to be the real one.** Say
explicitly that neither is.

**13.** Leading edge, trailing edge, chord line, camber, angle of attack.

---

## Part 4

**14.** Diagonal pairs match. Front-left and back-right one way, front-right and back-left the other.

**15.** The torques would add instead of cancelling. **It would spin continuously** with no way to stop
it.

**Ask this before explaining.** Let a student get there. Forty seconds, worth more than telling them.

**16.** The twisting reaction on the airframe from a spinning prop. Newton's third law, rotating.

**17.** Two each way means the torques are equal and opposite, so they cancel and it holds a heading.

---

## Part 5

**18.** Pitch: nose up and down, lateral axis. Roll: tilt left and right, longitudinal axis. Yaw: nose
left and right while level, vertical axis.

**19.**
- Climb: all four speed up
- Pitch forward: **back two speed up**, front two slow
- Roll left: **right two speed up**, left two slow
- Yaw right: the two **counterclockwise** speed up, the two clockwise slow

**Watch for reversed pitch and roll answers.** Students expect "go forward, front motors work harder."
It is the opposite: the back lifts, the nose drops, the lift tilts forward.

**20.** **Leftover torque**, not tilt. It stops cancelling its own torque on purpose.

---

## Part 6

**These matter most.** They connect physics to something students have felt.

**21.** Pushing forward **tilts** the aircraft, so the lift tilts too. Less points straight up, so the
vertical part is now smaller than the weight and it sinks until power is added.

**22.** Yaw runs on leftover torque, a **much smaller force** than the lift differential driving pitch
and roll.

**23.** From **tilting the lift.** There is no separate thrust source.
