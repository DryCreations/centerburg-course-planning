# How To Study This Class

**Aviation UAS | Week 7**

The last quiz showed something specific. **The questions people missed were not the hard ones.** They
were the regulation numbers from Module 1: altitudes, distances, weights, timeframes.

Those are not questions you can reason your way to. **They are memorization, and almost nobody in here is
actually studying.** Re-reading the module is not studying. Here is what is.

---

## Why re-reading does not work

When you read something a second time it **feels** familiar, and your brain reads that familiarity as
knowing it. It is not the same thing.

> **Recognizing an answer when you see it is not the same as producing it when you do not.**

The quiz asks you to produce it. So practice producing it.

---

## The method: retrieval practice

**Close the book and try to answer. Then check.** That is the whole thing, and it beats every other
method that has been tested.

| Do this | Not this |
|---|---|
| Cover the answer, say it out loud, then check | Read the page again |
| Write the number from memory, then verify | Highlight the number |
| Have someone quiz you | Watch a video about it |
| Do practice questions cold | Look at practice questions with answers visible |

**Getting it wrong while studying is the point.** A wrong answer you correct sticks far harder than a
right answer you read.

---

## Spacing: fifteen minutes, four nights

**Four fifteen-minute sessions beat one hour the night before.** This is not an opinion, it is one of the
most reliably reproduced findings in learning research.

| Night | Do |
|---|---|
| **1** | All the numbers. Get them wrong. Correct them |
| **2** | Only the ones you missed on night 1 |
| **3** | All of them again, cold |
| **4** | Only what is still shaky |

**Fifteen minutes.** Set a timer. When it goes off, stop, even if you are not finished.

---

## The numbers you have to know cold

There are not many. That is the thing worth realizing: **this is a short list, and people are failing it
anyway because they never sat down with it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Maximum altitude | **400 ft AGL**, or 400 ft above a structure when within 400 ft of it |
| Maximum groundspeed | **100 mph** |
| Minimum visibility | **3 statute miles** |
| Cloud clearance | **500 ft below, 2,000 ft horizontal** |
| Registration required at | **0.55 lb** (250 g) |
| Certificate valid for | **24 calendar months** before recurrent training |
| Minimum age | **16** |
| Accident report window | **10 calendar days** |
| Reportable damage | **$500** or more, other than the aircraft |

**Nine items.** Cover the right column right now and see how many you get. That took thirty seconds and
you now know exactly what to study.

---

## Make the list smaller

**Only study what you get wrong.** Most people spend most of their study time on things they already
know, because it feels good.

1. Go through all nine cold. Mark the misses
2. **Study only the misses**
3. Go through all nine again
4. Repeat until there are no misses two sessions in a row

If you know six of nine, you have **three things to learn**, not nine. That is a ten minute problem.

---

## Tricks that actually help for pure numbers

- **Say it out loud.** Silent reading does not encode as well
- **Write it by hand**, from memory, not copied
- **Connect it to something physical.** 400 feet is about a third of the height of a large building. 3
  statute miles is the drive to somewhere you know
- **Group them.** The cloud clearance pair is always 500 and 2,000, and **below is the smaller number**
- **Put the shaky three on your phone lock screen** for a week

---

## For the concept questions, different method

Airspace classes, sectional symbols, and weather are not memorization in the same way. **Those you should
be able to explain**, which is a different test.

**Try this:** explain it out loud to somebody who is not in this class, or to nobody. If you get stuck
partway through, that is exactly the part you do not understand, and now you know where to look.

**This is the single most useful study technique there is** and almost nobody uses it, because it feels
strange and because getting stuck feels bad. Getting stuck is the information.

---

## What this is really about

You are going to sit a real FAA exam at the end of this course if you want the certificate. **It costs
$175 and it is entirely this kind of material.** Nobody talks their way through it.

The habit is worth more than the content. Learning how to make yourself learn something boring on purpose
is a genuinely useful skill and most people never acquire it.

---

## Start now, in class

1. Cover the numbers table. Write all nine from memory
2. Check. **Circle your misses**
3. Those are your study list. Put them somewhere you will see them
4. Do it again tomorrow at the start of class

That is it. **Four minutes a day beats an hour on Thursday night**, every single time.
