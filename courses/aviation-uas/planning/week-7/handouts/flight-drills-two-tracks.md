# Flight Drills: Two Tracks

**Aviation UAS | Wednesday and Thursday** | Teacher reference

**Pilots are not in the same place and pretending otherwise wastes both groups' time.** Assign a track.
Do it by name, quietly, at the preflight briefing.

---

## The gate, unchanged

- [ ] METAR pulled and read out loud
- [ ] Compared against personal minimums
- [ ] **Go or no-go call, with the deciding number named**
- [ ] Airspace confirmed
- [ ] Preflight walked out loud, item by item
- [ ] Roles: **Pilot**, **Visual Observer**, **Logger**
- [ ] Everyone else behind the spectator line

**No preflight, no flight.** A no-go is a real outcome and it gets logged.

## Assigning the track

Use last week's figure eight log. **One question decides it:**

> **Did the aircraft stop turning at the crossover, or did it wander through it?**

| | Track |
|---|---|
| Wandered, lost orientation, or had to reset mid-figure | **Track A** |
| Flew both loops with a clean crossover, twice | **Track B** |

**Do not announce the tracks as levels.** They are different drills, both with a real bar. A Track A
pilot who gets it clean moves up the same day.

---

## Track A: Own the figure eight

**The problem is almost never the loops. It is the crossover**, where the nose changes which way it is
pointed relative to you and every input reverses.

### Break it apart

| Step | Drill |
|------|-------|
| **A1** | **Two cones, 20 feet apart.** Fly a single circle around the left cone. Just one. Land |
| **A2** | Single circle around the right cone. Land. **Notice which direction felt worse** |
| **A3** | **The crossover only.** Fly the S between the two cones, straight through the middle. No loops. Back and forth ten times |
| **A4** | Left circle, then the crossover, then **stop and hover** in the middle of the right circle |
| **A5** | The full figure eight, **slowly**. Slower than feels right |
| **A6** | Two consecutive figure eights without stopping |

**Most pilots who cannot fly a figure eight can fly A3 fine.** Isolating the crossover is the fix, and
it is usually a fifteen minute fix once it is isolated.

### What to say when they lose it

- **"Let go."** Let it hover. Find the nose. Then move
- **"Where is its nose pointed?"** Not "which way is it going"
- **"Slower."** Every orientation error is recoverable at walking speed
- **"Smaller inputs."** If they are correcting hard, they overcorrected first

## Track B: Maneuverability

For pilots who own the figure eight. **Each one adds a real constraint.**

### B1: Nose-in circle

Circle the marker with the **nose pointed at it the whole way.** Now they are mixing roll, pitch, and
yaw continuously, and the controls reverse through the entire far half.

**This is genuinely hard.** It is also the single best orientation drill there is.

### B2: The square with yaw

Fly a square. **At each corner, yaw 90 degrees** so the nose always points along the direction of
travel.

Teaches flying "as the aircraft sees it" instead of "as I see it," which is how every real orbit and
follow shot is flown.

### B3: Altitude changes inside a pattern

Figure eight, but **one loop at 15 feet and the other at 25 feet**, transitioning on the crossover.

Adds a third input to a pattern they already own.

### B4: The orbit with a target

Circle a person or object, nose held on it, **at a constant radius.** Have the Visual Observer call out
whenever the distance visibly changes.

**This is the actual job.** Photographers and inspectors fly this constantly.

### B5: Precision landing

From 25 feet, descend and land with a skid inside a marked 2-foot square. **Three times in a row.**

Unglamorous and it is what separates a pilot from someone who can make a drone move.

## What gets judged, both tracks

Same three things, always. **None of them are speed.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Smooth** | Continuous, not a series of corrections |
| **Level** | Altitude at the far side equals altitude at the near side |
| **Deliberate** | The aircraft went where they meant it to, not where it drifted |

> **A slow clean maneuver is a pass. A fast sloppy one is not. Nobody is timing you.**

## Connect it back to Tuesday

**This is the week's real payoff** and it takes ten seconds at a time.

| When they | Say |
|---|---|
| Sink while pushing forward | "Where did your lift go? You tilted it" |
| Yaw slowly | "That is torque. It is a much smaller force than roll" |
| Drift after letting go | "Newton's first law. Nothing took the momentum away yet" |
| Fight a gust | "Drag, and the aircraft tilting to hold position against it" |
| Struggle nose-in | "Roll, pitch and yaw at once, and two of them are reversed" |

**They spent Tuesday being told a drone has no steering, only four motors at different speeds. Out here
they can feel it.** Naming it in the moment is worth more than any amount of review.

## Waiting for a turn

Nobody stands around.

- **Watch the pilot's hands**, not the aircraft
- **Call the orientation out loud** as it flies. Which way is its right? Free practice, no risk
- **Log for someone.** The logger role is real work
- Study. `vocab.md` and the physics handout are the ground school for all of this
