# Flight: Orbit and Figure Eight

**Aviation UAS | Thursday** | Teacher reference

**Two drills, assigned by where the pilot actually is.** Both demonstrate accurate turning. One gives a
visual reference to hold onto, the other does not.

---

## The gate, unchanged

- METAR read out loud, compared against personal minimums
- **Go or no-go call, with the deciding number named**
- Airspace confirmed, preflight walked out loud, item by item
- Roles: **Pilot, Visual Observer, Logger**. Everyone else behind the line

**No preflight, no flight.** A no-go is a real outcome and gets logged.

---

## Assigning the drill

**One question, off last week's log:**

> **Can they fly a clean figure eight without losing orientation at the crossover?**

| | Drill |
|---|---|
| Not yet, or shaky | **Orbit a fixed object** |
| Yes, twice | **Figure eight in open air** |

**Do not call them levels.** They are two different demonstrations of the same skill. A pilot who orbits
cleanly moves to the figure eight the same day.

---

## Drill A: Orbit a fixed object

**Pick a real target.** A tree, a pole, a cone, a backstop. Something with a trunk or a base so the
distance to it is obvious from the pilot's position.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Altitude** | 15 feet, same all the way around |
| **Radius** | About 20 feet. **Constant** is what matters, not the exact number |
| **Nose** | Pointed the same direction the whole lap. **Do not yaw yet** |
| **Speed** | Slow. A slow round orbit beats a fast oval |
| **Laps** | One each direction |

### Why a target helps

**The object gives them something to measure against.** In open air a pilot has no idea whether their
circle is round. With a tree in the middle, **the gap between the drone and the trunk is visible from
the ground**, and when it changes, everyone can see it.

That makes the Visual Observer useful: **have them call out "closer" and "wider"** as it goes around.
Immediate feedback, no instruments.

### Judged on

- **Round:** the gap to the object does not change
- **Level:** the far side is the same height as the near side
- **Continuous:** no stopping and restarting

### If they lose it

- **"Let go."** Let it hover. Find the nose. Then move
- **"Where is its nose pointed?"** Not "which way is it going"
- **"Slower."** Every orientation error is recoverable at walking speed

### When the orbit is clean

**Add the yaw.** Nose stays pointed at the object the whole way around. Now they are mixing roll, pitch
and yaw continuously, and the controls reverse through the far half.

**This is genuinely hard.** It is also the single best orientation drill there is, and it is the real
job: photographers and inspectors fly this constantly.

## Drill B: Figure eight in open air

**No target, no markers.** Two imagined loops, crossing in the middle.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Altitude** | 15 feet, constant through both loops |
| **Size** | Loops roughly even. If one is visibly bigger, that is the note |
| **Nose** | Fixed, not yawing |
| **Crossover** | **This is the whole drill** |

### What you are looking for

**The crossover.** That is where the nose changes which way it points relative to the pilot and every
input reverses.

| Good | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **The aircraft stops turning** at the crossover and goes straight for a beat before the next loop | |
| **Both loops are about the same size** | |
| **Altitude does not sag** on the far side | |

**A figure eight flown as two circles that happen to touch is not a figure eight.** The straight segment
through the middle is the part that demonstrates control.

### If it is clean, twice

- **Reverse it.** Start the other direction. Everyone has a good side and a bad side
- **Shrink it.** Same pattern, tighter loops
- **Add altitude change:** one loop at 15 feet, the other at 25, transitioning at the crossover

---

## Pilot log

For each pilot: **drill, laps completed, round or even, level, continuous, and one thing to fix.**

**The last column is the point.** "Drifted low on the far side" is useful. "Did fine" is not.

## Connect it to Tuesday

Ten seconds each time, and this is the week's real payoff.

| When they | Say |
|---|---|
| Sink while pushing forward | "Where did your lift go? You tilted it" |
| Complain yaw is slow | "That is torque. Much smaller force than roll" |
| Drift after letting go | "Newton's first law. Nothing took the momentum away yet" |
| Struggle with the nose-in orbit | "Roll, pitch and yaw at once, and two of them are reversed" |

---

## Waiting for a turn

- **Watch the pilot's hands**, not the aircraft
- **Call the orientation out loud** as it flies. Which way is its right? Free practice, no risk
- **Log for someone.** The logger role is real work
