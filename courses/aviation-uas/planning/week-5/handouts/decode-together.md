# Decode Together: Board Practice

**Aviation UAS, Week 5. Teacher reference.** Codes to put on the board for group decoding, ordered easiest to
hardest. Use them for turn and talk: put one up, give them ninety seconds with a partner, take answers, then
reveal.

**Run the first few with the decoder handout open, then take it away.** The goal is fluency, and fluency
comes from repetition with the reference before it comes from memory.

---

## Round 1: one field at a time (key allowed)

Put just the fragment on the board. Partners work out what it says.

| Fragment | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| `27012KT` | From 270 degrees at 12 knots |
| `10SM` | Visibility 10 statute miles |
| `OVC007` | Overcast at 700 feet |
| `SCT045` | Scattered at 4,500 feet |
| `24/13` | 24 C, dew point 13 C |
| `A2998` | Altimeter 29.98 inches of mercury |
| `18015G25KT` | From 180 at 15 knots, gusting 25 |
| `BKN012` | Broken at 1,200 feet |
| `1/2SM` | Visibility half a statute mile |
| `M02/M05` | Minus 2 C, dew point minus 5 C |

**Questions to ask as you go:**
- "Which direction is the wind actually coming from? Point at the wall."
- "How many feet? Say the trick out loud." (add two zeros)
- "Is that a ceiling?" (only BKN and OVC)

## Round 2: short strings (key allowed)

| String | The story |
|--------|-----------|
| `KOSU 141953Z 00000KT 10SM CLR 26/11 A3001` | Calm, ten miles, clear, 26 degrees. **A perfect day.** |
| `KCMH 142151Z 09008KT 10SM FEW060 22/09 A3005` | Light easterly, ten miles, a few clouds high up. **Fine.** |
| `KTZR 150053Z 21014G23KT 7SM SCT035 20/14 A2991` | **Gusting 23.** Everything else is fine. The gust is the problem. |

**The question for every one:** "Which single number would decide this for you?"

## Round 3: no key

Take the handout away. Partners work it cold.

| String | What they should catch |
|--------|------------------------|
| `KCMH 150151Z 18015G25KT 5SM -RA BKN015 OVC030 19/17 A2985` | Gusting 25, **light rain**, ceiling 1,500. Temp and dew point 2 apart. **No-go on several counts.** |
| `KTZR 150253Z VRB04KT 1/2SM FG OVC002 16/16 A2992` | **Half a mile in fog**, overcast at 200 feet. 16/16 is exactly saturated, which is why. **No-go, far below minimums.** |
| `KOSU 151853Z 31018G29KT 9SM BKN025 18/06 A2975` | Clear enough to see, but **gusting 29**. Visibility is a distraction here. |
| `KCMH 152053Z 00000KT 2SM BR CLR 21/20 A2999` | Calm and clear, **but 2 statute miles in mist.** Below the 3 SM minimum. **The only bad number is the one that matters.** |

## Round 4: the trap questions

These are the ones that separate reading from understanding.

**1.** `27012G22KT` and `27022KT`. Which is worse for our aircraft, and why?

> Arguably the **gusting** one. A steady 22 can be leaned into and held. A gust from 12 to 22 is a repeated
> shove, and the aircraft has to fight back to position every time.

**2.** A METAR reads `18/17`. Nothing else looks bad. What are you worried about?

> One degree apart means the air is nearly saturated. **Expect mist or fog**, possibly soon. Visibility is
> about to become the problem.

**3.** `BKN012` versus `SCT012`. Same height. Does it matter?

> Yes. **BKN is a ceiling**, SCT is not. Broken means 5 to 7 eighths covered, scattered is 3 to 4.

**4.** A METAR is timestamped `141851Z` and it is now 4:30 PM local. Is this report useful?

> `1851Z` is 2:51 PM local. **The report is over 90 minutes old.** Pull a fresh one.

**5.** `09003KT 10SM CLR 31/12 A2996`. Nothing here is close to a limit. Anything to think about?

> **31 C is hot.** Hot air is thinner, so the aircraft has less lift to work with and will perform worse than
> it would on a cool day. Not a no-go, but plan for it.

## Round 5: build one

Hand groups a scenario and have them **write the METAR** that would describe it.

- "Perfect flying weather at our field"
- "Legal, but you would not want to"
- "Fog rolled in"
- "Clear and sunny but absolutely not flyable" → gusts are the answer

Writing one backwards proves they can read one forwards.

---

## Live data beats all of this

**Pull real METARs on the projector every day.** `KCMH`, `KOSU`, `KTZR` are close and reliable.

When local weather is boring, go hunting: coastal fields, mountain airports, anywhere with a storm. Students
remember `1/2SM FG OVC002` far better than anything invented, and it is genuinely more interesting to decode
a sky that actually exists right now.
