# Reading a Sectional

**Aviation UAS, Week 5.** Everything on a sectional is a symbol. This page teaches you to look things up on
the legend and read what you find, which is the actual skill. Nobody memorizes a sectional.

**You need:** a sectional viewer you can pan and zoom, and the chart legend.

---

## Part 1: Get oriented

Open your viewer and find an airport you recognize. Then:

- [ ] **Zoom out** until you can see several airports at once. Notice the rings and colors
- [ ] **Zoom in** on one airport until you can read the small text next to it
- [ ] Find the **legend** and keep it open. You will use it constantly

> **Every symbol on the chart is in the legend.** Reading a sectional is a lookup skill, not a memory test.

---

## Part 2: Airspace, by color and line

This is the part that decides whether you may fly.

| What you see | Class | Authorization |
|--------------|-------|---------------|
| Solid **blue** rings, nested and wide | B | Required |
| Solid **magenta** rings, usually two | C | Required |
| **Dashed blue** ring | D | Required |
| **Dashed magenta** ring | E at the surface | Required |
| Faded **magenta** band, soft edge | E starting at 700 ft | Usually not at the ground |
| No ring at all | G | Not required |

**The pattern:** blue for the big controlled airspace, magenta for the smaller. **Solid** for the large
layered ones, **dashed** where it starts at the ground around a smaller field.

### Find each one

- [ ] Find a **Class B** airport. Follow one ring all the way around
- [ ] Find a **Class C** airport. How many rings?
- [ ] Find a **Class D** airport. Dashed blue, usually a single ring
- [ ] Find a **dashed magenta** ring. That is Class E at the surface
- [ ] Find somewhere with **no rings at all.** That is Class G, and most of the map is this

### The stacked numbers

On a ring you will see numbers like a fraction:

```
 70        top = ceiling
---        bottom = floor
 25        both in hundreds of feet
```

That piece runs from **2,500 to 7,000 feet.** Below 2,500 you are underneath it.

- [ ] Find a set of stacked numbers and say out loud what altitudes that piece covers
- [ ] Find one where the bottom is **SFC**. That means surface, so it goes all the way to the ground

> **SFC is the one that matters to you.** Airspace that starts at the surface is airspace you are standing
> in. Airspace with a floor of 2,500 is not.

## Part 3: Airports

| Symbol | Means |
|--------|-------|
| **Magenta** airport symbol | No control tower |
| **Blue** airport symbol | Has a control tower |
| Circle with runways drawn in | Hard-surfaced runway |
| Small tick marks around the circle | Fuel available |
| **R** in a box | Private, permission required |

Next to each airport is a block of text: the name, the identifier, the field elevation, the runway length,
and radio frequencies.

- [ ] Find an airport and read its **identifier**, the three or four letters
- [ ] Find its **field elevation**, the number after the name
- [ ] Find a **magenta** airport and a **blue** one. Which has a tower?

---

## Part 4: Obstructions

The symbols you care about most, because they are in the air where you fly.

| Symbol | Means |
|--------|-------|
| A small tower shape | An obstruction under 1,000 ft AGL |
| A taller tower shape with a wider base | An obstruction 1,000 ft AGL or higher |
| A tower shape with lightning-bolt marks | Lit at night |
| Several drawn together | A group of obstructions |

Next to each one, two numbers:

```
1549        the taller number = height above SEA LEVEL (MSL)
(451)       the number in parentheses = height above the GROUND (AGL)
```

**The number in parentheses is the one that matters to you**, because you fly in feet above the ground.

- [ ] Find an obstruction and read both numbers
- [ ] Find one taller than 400 feet AGL. **That is taller than you are allowed to fly**
- [ ] Find one that is lit

---

## Part 5: Other things you will run into

| Symbol | Means |
|--------|-------|
| Blue flag | A visual checkpoint, a landmark pilots navigate by |
| Blue hashed line around an area | Special use airspace: restricted, warning, or alert |
| **R-** or **MOA** labels | Restricted area or Military Operations Area |
| Thin blue or magenta lines with numbers | Airways, routes between navigation aids |
| Contour shading and numbers | Terrain elevation |
| A large **magenta** number in a grid square | The highest terrain or obstacle in that square, in thousands and hundreds of feet |

## Part 8: Two to answer in writing

**1.** You are standing somewhere with **no rings on the chart at all.** What class is that, and name four
rules that still apply to you.

**2.** You find an obstruction marked `(620)`. You want to fly nearby at 300 feet AGL. What do you need to
think about?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**1. Class G, uncontrolled.** No authorization needed. Still applies: 400 ft AGL maximum, 3 statute miles
visibility, visual line of sight at all times, daylight or civil twilight with anti-collision lighting, not
over people outside your operation, one aircraft at a time, registration over 0.55 lb, no careless or
reckless operation.

**2.** That tower is **620 feet above the ground**, so it is more than 200 feet taller than your legal
ceiling. You are flying below its top. It is a physical obstacle, its guy-wires may extend well out from the
base, and you need to stay clear of it laterally rather than thinking about going over it.

</details>

## The lookup habit

**You are not supposed to know every symbol.** Real pilots check the legend constantly.

The skill is: **see something unfamiliar, find it on the legend, read what it means, decide whether it
affects you.** That is what this activity is practicing.
