# METAR & Weather Reading: Resources

**Aviation UAS · Week 4.** Real, standard references for reading aviation weather. Use these to check today's
actual conditions and to practice decoding, not just the examples in `vocab.md`.

## Where to pull a real, current METAR

- **aviationweather.gov** (the National Weather Service Aviation Weather Center): the standard source pilots
  actually use for current METARs, forecasts (TAFs), and weather charts. Search by the nearest airport
  identifier.
- Any nearby airport's **identifier** (a three or four letter code, for example an Ohio regional airport code)
  works as the search term on that site.

## Reference for decoding

- The FAA's **Aviation Weather Handbook** (FAA-H-8083-28) is the standard FAA reference for how METARs, TAFs,
  and other aviation weather products are built and read. It is long; the METAR chapter alone is enough for
  this class.
- The general decoding pattern to teach: **station identifier, date and time (UTC), wind (direction and speed,
  plus gusts), visibility, weather phenomena if any, cloud layers and heights, temperature and dew point,
  altimeter setting.**

## Practice pattern for class

1. Pull today's real METAR for the nearest airport on aviationweather.gov.
2. Decode it live as a group, field by field.
3. Apply Part 107 limits to it (3 statute miles visibility minimum, cloud clearance rules) and make the
   go/no-go call for a hypothetical flight right now.
4. Repeat with an older or different METAR so students see more than one weather picture.
