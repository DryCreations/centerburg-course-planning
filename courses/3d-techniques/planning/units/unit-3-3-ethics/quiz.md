## Questions

| # | Question | A | B | C | D | Answer | DOK | Standard |
|---|----------|---|---|---|---|--------|-----|---------|
| 1 | Which type of license protects an original creative work automatically at the moment it is created? | Trademark | Patent | Copyright | DRM | C | 1 | 1.3.8 |
| 2 | An artist downloads a 3D model from Sketchfab labeled "CC BY-NC." They want to use it in a paid client project. Why is this problematic? | CC BY-NC means the model cannot be used in any project | NC means Non-Commercial — paid client work is commercial use, which this license prohibits | CC licenses always require notification to the creator | The artist must convert it to a different file format first | B | 2 | 1.3.8 |
| 3 | Blender is licensed under the GPL. What does this primarily mean? | Blender requires a monthly subscription | Blender's source code is open and any distributed version must remain open-source | Blender cannot be used commercially | Blender can only be used by students | B | 2 | 9.6.1 |
| 4 | A 3D artist creates a character model while employed at a game studio. The standard employment agreement says all work created on company time belongs to the company. When the artist leaves, who owns the model? | The artist — they created it | The game studio — work-for-hire doctrine means the employer owns the work product | It is shared equally | Neither — the model enters the public domain | B | 2 | 1.3.7 |
| 5 | Which of the following is an example of an ethical issue that is NOT necessarily illegal? | Copying a paid font and distributing it | Claiming your teammate's contribution as your own on a professional resume | Using a student discount for personal commercial work | All of the above are illegal | B | 2 | 1.1.6 |
| 6 | A student creates a highly detailed 3D scan of a trademarked product (a consumer electronics device) and posts it for free download on Sketchfab. This potentially violates: | Color theory guidelines | The owner's intellectual property rights — the product's design is protected and replication may infringe even if given away free | Open-source licensing | Blender's GPL | B | 2 | 9.6.1 |
| 7 | A professional design brief is most valuable to a client because: | It makes the project more expensive | It documents the agreed scope, deliverables, and constraints — preventing scope creep and disputes later | It ensures faster rendering | It removes the need for a contract | B | 2 | 1.2.12 |
| 8 | **[HOT]** A freelance 3D artist signs a work-for-hire contract with a film studio for a 3-month project. After the project, the artist wants to include the renders she created in her personal portfolio to get future clients. Can she? | Yes — she created them so she owns them | Possibly — it depends on whether the contract explicitly grants or denies portfolio rights; she must check the contract; if unclear, she should request written permission | No — work-for-hire always prohibits all portfolio use | Yes — renders are always exempt from work-for-hire | B | 3 | 1.3.7 |
| 9 | **[HOT]** A student uses a paid texture pack purchased "for personal use only" in a BPA competition entry that will be publicly screened at state competition. Is this within the license? | Yes — educational use is always exempt | No — BPA competitions involve public performance/display, which likely goes beyond "personal use"; the student should use textures with a license that permits educational/competition use | Yes — the texture was purchased, so it can be used anywhere | No — BPA requires only original content | B | 3 | 1.3.8 |
| 10 | **[HOT]** An employer tells a new 3D artist: "We move fast here — verbal approvals are fine, just get it done." What is the professional risk of relying on verbal approvals? | None — verbal contracts are just as binding as written ones | Verbal agreements are unenforceable in most professional situations — scope, payment, and ownership disputes become impossible to resolve without written documentation | Verbal approvals are illegal | Only contracts over $500 need to be written | B | 4 | 1.2.12 |
