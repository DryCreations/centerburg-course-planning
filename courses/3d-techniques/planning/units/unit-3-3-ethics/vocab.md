## Terms

| Term | Definition | Related Standard | DOK |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| **Intellectual property (IP)** | Legal rights over creations of the mind — writings, artworks, inventions, and designs | 1.7.13 | 1 |
| **Copyright** | Automatic legal protection that gives a creator exclusive rights to reproduce, distribute, and display their original work | 1.3.8 | 1 |
| **Trademark** | A symbol, word, or phrase legally registered to identify a product or brand; protects brand identity | 9.6.1 | 1 |
| **DRM (Digital Rights Management)** | Technology that restricts how digital content is used, copied, or shared (e.g., software license keys, streaming protections) | 9.6.1 | 2 |
| **Creative Commons license** | A flexible licensing system that allows creators to share work with specific permissions — some allow commercial use; some allow modification; some require attribution | 1.3.8 | 2 |
| **Attribution** | Credit given to the original creator, usually as required by a Creative Commons or open-source license | 1.3.8 | 1 |
| **Work-for-hire** | A legal doctrine where work created by an employee or contractor becomes the property of the employer or client, not the creator | 1.3.7 | 2 |
| **GPL (GNU General Public License)** | An open-source software license (used by Blender itself) that requires distributed software to remain open-source and credits contributors | 9.6.1 | 2 |
| **Ethics** | The principles and values that guide professional behavior — honesty, fairness, accountability, respect | 1.1.6 | 1 |
| **Work ethic** | The personal standard of effort, reliability, and quality a person applies to their work | 1.1.6 | 1 |
| **Accountability** | Taking responsibility for your actions and their consequences — including missed deadlines and quality failures | 1.1.6 | 2 |
| **Labor law** | Legal regulations governing employment relationships — wages, hours, contracts, and worker protections | 1.3.7 | 1 |
| **Contractor** | A self-employed professional hired for a specific project, not a permanent employee; usually retains ownership of their tools and process | 1.3.7 | 2 |
| **Portfolio ownership** | The right to use work samples from past employment — governed by work-for-hire agreements and company policy | 1.1.5 | 3 |
| **Design brief** | A professional document specifying a project's scope, deliverables, and constraints — the technical writing form for client projects | 1.2.12 | 2 |
| **Technical writing** | Writing intended to document a process, specification, or decision in clear, precise, unambiguous language | 1.2.12 | 2 |
| **Consumer protection law** | Legal regulations that protect buyers from deceptive or fraudulent business practices | 1.3.4 | 1 |

## Key Distinctions

| Term 1 | Term 2 | Key Difference |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| Copyright | Trademark | Copyright protects creative work automatically at creation; trademark protects brand identity and must be registered |
| Work-for-hire | Contractor | Work-for-hire means the employer owns the output; contractor work is owned by the contractor unless otherwise agreed |
| GPL | Creative Commons | GPL = open-source software license (code must stay open); Creative Commons = flexible content license (photos, models, media) |
| Ethics | Law | Law defines what is legally required; ethics defines what is right, even when not legally required |

## Bell Ringer Prompts

1. You download a 3D model from Sketchfab labeled "CC BY." What are you allowed to do with it, and what must you do?
2. You work at a game studio for 2 years and create 20 character models. When you leave, can you include them in your portfolio? What determines this?
3. What is the difference between a syntax error (from Unit 3.2) and an ethical error (from this unit)?
4. A client asks you to create a 3D replica of a copyrighted toy. They say "it's for internal use only." Is this acceptable? Why or why not?
5. Why does a professional always document project decisions in writing, even for verbal agreements?
