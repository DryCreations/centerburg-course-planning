## Student Overview

### What Is This Unit About?

3D printing and digital fabrication raise questions that don't have obvious answers: Who owns a design that anyone can copy? Is printing a replacement part ethical? What's the environmental cost of additive manufacturing? This unit explores the ethical, legal, and environmental dimensions of 3D fabrication and the open-source design ecosystem.

### What You'll Be Able to Do

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

- Explain the ethical implications of 3D printing in at least 3 domains: IP, weapons, medical, counterfeiting
- Analyze the environmental impact of FDM printing: material, energy, and waste
- Describe how open-source design platforms (Thingiverse, Printables, GitHub) operate and their license models
- Write a structured ethics reflection and sustainability analysis

## Teacher Reference

### Pacing Guide

| Day | Activity | Notes |
|-----|----------|-------|
| Mon, Wk 26 | Vocab intro + ethics overview | Start with "Is it wrong to 3D print a fidget spinner you could buy?" — surface easy vs. hard cases |
| Tue, Wk 26 | Lab: ethics case study analysis | 4 cases: IP violation, medical device, weapon, counterfeit goods — is each ethical? under what circumstances? |
| Wed, Wk 26 | Open-source design platform tour | Thingiverse, Printables, GitHub — what license types exist? what can users do with each? |
| Thu, Wk 26 | Lab: open-source license comparison | Students find 3 models and classify the license type and what it permits |
| Fri, Wk 26 | Quiz | Ethics vocabulary and license types |
| Mon, Wk 27 | Environmental impact of FDM | Material lifecycle (PLA vs. ABS vs. PETG), energy use, failed print waste |
| Tue, Wk 27 | Lab-2: sustainability analysis | Calculate approximate filament waste from this course; compare materials by end-of-life |
| Wed–Thu, Wk 27 | Lab-2: ethics reflection (4 topics, 2–3 sentences each) | IP, weapons, medical, environmental — each with a specific real case |
| Fri, Wk 27 | Quiz + submission + portfolio | Artifact #9 |

### Common Issues & Responses

- **Ethics reflection is opinion without evidence:** Require each of the 4 topics to reference a real case, regulation, or published standard.
- **Sustainability analysis just lists materials:** Require a comparison across 2 materials showing why one is more sustainable than the other.

### Differentiation Notes

- **Students who need more support:** Provide a reflection sentence frame for each of the 4 ethics topics.
- **Students who finish early:** Extension in lab-2.md — research one manufacturer's sustainability initiative for 3D printing materials and evaluate whether it's meaningful.

### BPA Connections

Ethics and sustainability knowledge supports the written analysis section of BPA V25 and any competition entry using AI or fabrication tools.
